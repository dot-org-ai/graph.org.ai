# Petrochemicals

Petrochemicals are chemical products derived from petroleum (crude oil) and natural gas. They serve as the fundamental building blocks for the vast majority of synthetic chemicals, plastics, fibers, rubbers, and countless other products essential to modern life.

## Overview

The petrochemical industry represents the critical link between:
- **Upstream**: Oil and gas production and refining
- **Downstream**: Chemicals, plastics, fibers, pharmaceuticals, and consumer products

**Global petrochemical production**: 400+ million tons/year of primary petrochemicals
**Market value**: $500+ billion globally
**Growth rate**: 3-4% annually, driven by plastics and specialty chemicals demand
**Energy intensity**: Accounts for 8-10% of global oil and gas consumption

The industry is characterized by:
- Capital-intensive mega-scale facilities ($5-20 billion investment)
- Tight integration with oil refineries and natural gas processing
- Regional competitiveness based on feedstock availability and cost
- Cyclical profitability tied to crude oil prices and supply-demand balance
- Critical role in virtually every manufacturing sector

## Petrochemical Feedstocks

### Petroleum-Derived Feedstocks

**Crude Oil Fractions**
- **Naphtha** (C₅-C₁₁): Primary feedstock for steam crackers globally
  - Boiling range: 30-200°C
  - Produced in atmospheric distillation of crude oil
  - Composition: Paraffins, naphthenes, aromatics
  - Regional use: Europe, Asia (naphtha crackers)

- **Gas Oil**: Heavier cracker feedstock
  - Vacuum gas oil (VGO) or atmospheric gas oil (AGO)
  - Higher ethylene yield vs. light feeds but more by-products

- **Fuel Oil**: Residual oil cracking in some regions

**Aromatic-Rich Streams**
- **Reformate**: From catalytic reforming of naphtha
  - High benzene, toluene, xylene content
  - Source for BTX extraction

- **Pyrolysis Gasoline (Pygas)**: From steam cracking
  - Rich in aromatics
  - Requires hydrogenation before BTX recovery

### Natural Gas Liquids (NGL)

**Ethane (C₂H₆)**
- Most selective feedstock for ethylene production
  - Ethylene yield: 80-82%
  - Minimal by-products (propylene, C4s)
- Source: Natural gas processing, particularly shale gas
- Regional advantage: North America (Marcellus, Permian shale), Middle East

**Propane (C₃H₈)**
- Ethylene yield: 40-45%
- Propylene yield: 15-17%
- Flexible feedstock, can adjust for ethylene or propylene optimization

**Butane (C₄H₁₀)**
- Less common cracker feed
- Higher butadiene and C4 yields

**Natural Gas Liquids Advantage**
- Lower cost in gas-rich regions
- Cleaner processing (lower sulfur)
- Challenges: Propylene deficit (must be supplemented)

### Regional Feedstock Economics

**North America**
- Shale gas revolution: Abundant low-cost ethane
- Ethane crackers dominate new capacity (2010s wave)
- Cost advantage: $100-200/ton ethylene production cost vs. $500-700 naphtha-based

**Middle East**
- Natural gas and ethane from oil fields
- Government subsidies and low feedstock cost
- Large-scale integrated complexes (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Iran)

**Asia**
- Primarily naphtha crackers (Japan, South Korea, China, Taiwan, Singapore)
- Dependent on imported crude oil
- Higher production costs but proximity to demand

**Europe**
- Naphtha crackers historically
- Declining competitiveness vs. Middle East and North America
- Some LPG and ethane imports

## Primary Petrochemicals

### Olefins (Alkenes)

Light unsaturated hydrocarbons with C=C double bonds, produced primarily by steam cracking.

#### Ethylene (C₂H₄) - 200M+ tons/year

**The King of Petrochemicals** - highest volume organic chemical

**Production: Steam Cracking (Pyrolysis)**
1. **Feedstock vaporization**: Ethane, propane, naphtha, or gas oil heated to gas phase
2. **Cracking furnace**:
   - Temperature: 750-950°C
   - Steam dilution: 0.3-0.5 kg steam/kg feed
   - Residence time: 0.1-0.5 seconds
   - Rapid quenching to stop reactions
3. **Separation**:
   - Compression (cracked gas compressor)
   - Acid gas removal (CO₂, H₂S)
   - Drying
   - Cryogenic separation (demethanizer, deethanizer, depropanizer)
   - Ethylene recovery: 99.9+% purity

**Yields by Feedstock**:
- Ethane: 80-82% ethylene
- Propane: 40-45% ethylene, 15-17% propylene
- Naphtha: 30-35% ethylene, 15-18% propylene, 5-7% butadiene, 8-12% aromatics
- Gas oil: 25-30% ethylene, higher BTX and fuel oil

**Energy Intensity**: 10-15 GJ/ton ethylene produced

**Major Derivatives** (% of ethylene consumption):
1. **Polyethylene** (LDPE, LLDPE, HDPE): 60%
2. **Ethylene Oxide** → Ethylene Glycol: 20%
   - EG for polyester (PET), antifreeze
3. **Ethylene Dichloride (EDC)** → Vinyl Chloride Monomer (VCM) → PVC: 12%
4. **Ethylbenzene** → Styrene → Polystyrene: 5%
5. **Ethanol** (ethylene hydration): 2%
6. **Alpha Olefins** (LAO): Comonomers, detergent alcohols

**Ethylene Cracker Economics**:
- World-scale: 1-2 million tons/year capacity
- Capital cost: $1-3 billion
- Operating cost: 70% feedstock, 15% energy, 10% labor/maintenance, 5% other
- Margin volatility: Tight correlation with crude oil and natural gas prices

#### Propylene (C₃H₆) - 130M+ tons/year

**Second most important olefin**, growing demand due to polypropylene

**Production Routes**:

1. **Steam Cracker Co-Product** (60% of supply)
   - By-product of ethylene production
   - Yield depends on feedstock and cracking severity
   - Separated in depropanizer column

2. **Fluid Catalytic Cracking (FCC) - Refinery** (30%)
   - Gasoline production unit in refineries
   - Propylene in C3/C4 streams
   - Lower purity (refinery-grade propylene)
   - Chemical-grade requires additional processing

3. **Propane Dehydrogenation (PDH)** (10% and growing)
   - **On-purpose propylene production**
   - C₃H₈ → C₃H₆ + H₂
   - Catalysts: Pt-based (Oleflex by UOP), Cr-based (Catofin by CB&I)
   - Temperature: 550-650°C
   - Conversion: 40-60% per pass
   - Addresses propylene deficit from ethane crackers
   - Economics: Competitive when propane is cheap

4. **Metathesis** (emerging)
   - Ethylene + butylenes → propylene
   - OCT process (Olefins Conversion Technology)
   - Flexible production based on C2/C4 availability

5. **Methanol to Olefins (MTO)** (China)
   - Methanol (from coal or natural gas) → ethylene + propylene
   - Zeolite catalysts (SAPO-34)
   - China-specific due to coal resources

**Propylene Derivatives**:
1. **Polypropylene**: 65%
2. **Propylene Oxide** → Polyurethanes, propylene glycol: 10%
3. **Acrylonitrile** (via ammoxidation) → ABS, acrylic fibers: 10%
4. **Cumene** (via alkylation with benzene) → Phenol + Acetone: 8%
5. **Isopropanol**: 3%
6. **Acrylic Acid**: 3%
7. **Oligomers**: Detergent alcohols, plasticizers

#### Butadiene (C₄H₆) - 15M+ tons/year

**Production**:
- **Steam cracker by-product**: C4 fraction from naphtha cracking
  - Extractive distillation to separate from butenes and butane
  - Acetonitrile, DMF, or NMP as solvent
- **Ethanol to butadiene**: Russia, bio-based routes being developed
- **On-purpose production**: Oxidative dehydrogenation of butenes (emerging)

**Applications**:
1. **Synthetic Rubber**: 70%
   - SBR (styrene-butadiene rubber): Tires
   - Polybutadiene rubber (BR): Tires
   - NBR (nitrile rubber): Oil-resistant applications
2. **ABS Plastics** (acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene): 15%
3. **SBR Latex**: Carpet backing, paper coating
4. **Adiponitrile** → Nylon 6,6 (via hydrocyanation): 5%

**Butadiene Extraction Challenge**:
- Ethane crackers produce minimal butadiene
- Naphtha crackers: 5-7% butadiene yield
- Supply tightness with shift to lighter feeds

#### Higher Olefins (C₄-C₈)

**Butylenes (C₄H₈)**: 1-Butene, 2-butene, isobutylene
- MTBE (methyl tert-butyl ether): Gasoline octane booster (declining)
- Polyisobutylene: Lubricant additives, sealants
- ETBE (ethyl tert-butyl ether)
- Maleic anhydride (via oxidation)

**Linear Alpha Olefins (LAO)**: 1-hexene, 1-octene
- Comonomers for LLDPE
- Detergent alcohols (via hydroformylation → oxo alcohols)
- Production: Ethylene oligomerization

### Aromatics (BTX)

Cyclic unsaturated hydrocarbons with benzene ring structure.

#### Benzene (C₆H₆) - 50M+ tons/year

**Production**:

1. **Catalytic Reforming** (50% of supply)
   - Naphtha reforming in refineries
   - Platinum-rhenium catalysts on alumina
   - Dehydrogenation and cyclization of paraffins/naphthenes
   - Temperature: 450-550°C, Pressure: 3-25 bar
   - Aromatics-rich reformate (50-70% aromatics)
   - Extraction with solvent (sulfolane, NMP, tetraethylene glycol)

2. **Toluene Disproportionation and Transalkylation** (30%)
   - **Disproportionation**: 2 Toluene → Benzene + Xylene
   - **Transalkylation**: Toluene + C9+ aromatics → Benzene + Xylene
   - Zeolite catalysts
   - Adjust BTX ratios to meet market demand

3. **Pyrolysis Gasoline (Pygas) Hydrogenation** (20%)
   - Aromatic-rich gasoline from steam crackers
   - Hydrogenate olefins (avoid gum formation)
   - Extract benzene via extractive distillation or liquid-liquid extraction

**Benzene Derivatives**:
1. **Ethylbenzene** → **Styrene** (50%)
   - Polystyrene, ABS, SBR
   - Alkylation: Benzene + Ethylene → Ethylbenzene
   - Dehydrogenation: Ethylbenzene → Styrene + H₂

2. **Cumene** → **Phenol + Acetone** (20%)
   - Cumene process (Hock process)
   - Alkylation: Benzene + Propylene → Cumene
   - Oxidation: Cumene → Cumene hydroperoxide
   - Cleavage: Cumene hydroperoxide → Phenol + Acetone

3. **Cyclohexane** → **Nylon Intermediates** (15%)
   - Hydrogenation: Benzene + H₂ → Cyclohexane
   - Oxidation: Cyclohexane → Cyclohexanone + Cyclohexanol (KA oil)
   - Caprolactam (Nylon 6) or adipic acid (Nylon 6,6)

4. **Aniline** (5%)
   - Nitration: Benzene + HNO₃ → Nitrobenzene
   - Hydrogenation: Nitrobenzene + H₂ → Aniline
   - Applications: MDI (polyurethanes), rubber chemicals, dyes

5. **Alkylbenzenes** (LAB - Linear Alkylbenzenes) (5%)
   - Detergent feedstock
   - Alkylation with C10-C13 olefins
   - Sulfonation to LAS (linear alkylbenzene sulfonate)

6. **Chlorobenzenes**: Solvents, intermediates (declining due to environmental concerns)

7. **Maleic Anhydride**: Via benzene oxidation (alternative to butane oxidation)

#### Toluene (C₇H₈) - 30M+ tons/year

**Production**:
- Catalytic reforming: Largest source
- Pyrolysis gasoline
- Coal tar (minor, declining)

**Toluene Derivatives and Uses**:
1. **Benzene Production** (via hydrodealkylation or disproportionation): 40%
   - Thermal hydrodealkylation (HDA): Toluene + H₂ → Benzene + Methane
   - Catalytic hydrodealkylation: Lower temperature, catalyst

2. **Gasoline Blending**: 30%
   - High octane number
   - Aromatics content in gasoline

3. **Toluene Diisocyanate (TDI)** (10%)
   - Dinitration → diamine → phosgene reaction
   - Flexible polyurethane foams (furniture, bedding)

4. **Solvents** (10%)
   - Paints, coatings, adhesives
   - Chemical processing

5. **Xylene Production** (disproportionation): 5%

6. **Benzoic Acid**: Preservatives, caprolactam production

#### Xylenes (C₈H₁₀) - 50M+ tons/year

**Isomers**:
- **p-Xylene** (para): 60-70% of xylene demand
- **o-Xylene** (ortho): 10-15%
- **m-Xylene** (meta): 10-15%
- **Ethylbenzene**: Mixed in C8 aromatics stream

**Production**:
- Catalytic reforming: Primary source
- Toluene disproportionation
- Mixed xylenes separated by:
  - **Crystallization**: p-xylene (highest melting point, 13°C)
  - **Adsorption** (Parex process): p-xylene and o-xylene
  - **Distillation**: o-xylene (lowest boiling point)
  - **Isomerization**: Convert m-xylene and o-xylene to equilibrium mixture, recycle

**p-Xylene Derivatives** (critical for polyester):
1. **Purified Terephthalic Acid (PTA)** (95%)
   - Oxidation: p-Xylene + O₂ → Terephthalic acid
   - Mid-Century or Amoco process
   - Cobalt-manganese-bromide catalyst, acetic acid solvent
   - Polyester (PET) production: PTA + Ethylene glycol → PET

2. **Dimethyl Terephthalate (DMT)** (5%)
   - Alternative route to PET
   - Esterification of terephthalic acid

**o-Xylene Derivatives**:
1. **Phthalic Anhydride** (100% of o-xylene use)
   - Oxidation: o-Xylene + O₂ → Phthalic anhydride
   - Applications:
     - Plasticizers (phthalates for PVC) - 50% - declining
     - Unsaturated polyester resins - 30%
     - Alkyd resins (paints) - 15%

**m-Xylene**:
- Isophthalic acid (via oxidation)
- Limited market, mostly isomerized to p-xylene

### Other Key Petrochemicals

#### Methanol (CH₃OH) - 110M+ tons/year

Not strictly a petrochemical (can be from natural gas, coal, or biomass), but critical intermediate.

**Production**: Synthesis gas (syngas) route
1. **Steam methane reforming (SMR)**: CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂
2. **Partial oxidation (POX)** or **autothermal reforming (ATR)**: Alternative syngas routes
3. **Methanol synthesis**: CO + 2H₂ → CH₃OH
   - Copper-zinc oxide catalyst on alumina
   - Pressure: 50-100 bar, Temperature: 200-300°C

**Methanol Derivatives**:
1. **Formaldehyde**: 30%
2. **Acetic acid** (methanol carbonylation): 10%
3. **MTBE/MTAE** (fuel additives): 10% (declining in some regions)
4. **MTO (Methanol-to-olefins)**: 10% (China)
5. **MTP (Methanol-to-propylene)**: Emerging
6. **Dimethyl ether (DME)**: Fuel, aerosol propellant
7. **Methyl methacrylate (MMA)**: Acrylic plastics
8. **Solvents and chemicals**: Various

```
    ↓

    ↓
Ethylene
    ↓
├─→ Polyethylene (LDPE, LLDPE, HDPE) → Packaging, films, pipes, containers

├─→ Ethylbenzene → Styrene → Polystyrene, ABS plastics
└─→ Ethanol, Alpha olefins
```

```
Crude Oil / Natural Gas / Propane
    ↓
[Steam Cracking / FCC / PDH]
    ↓
Propylene
    ↓
├─→ Polypropylene → Automotive, packaging, textiles, consumer goods
├─→ Propylene Oxide → Polyurethanes, propylene glycol
├─→ Acrylonitrile → ABS, acrylic fibers, nylon
├─→ Cumene → Phenol + Acetone → Polycarbonate, epoxies, resins
├─→ Acrylic Acid → Superabsorbent polymers, acrylic esters
└─→ Isopropanol
```

## Integrated Petrochemical Complexes

### Advantages of Integration

1. **Feedstock synergy**: Share refinery streams
2. **Energy integration**: Heat exchange between units
3. **By-product utilization**: One unit's waste is another's feedstock
4. **Logistics efficiency**: Minimize transportation
5. **Capital efficiency**: Shared utilities and infrastructure
6. **Economies of scale**: World-scale facilities

### Example: Fully Integrated Complex

**Upstream**:
- Crude oil refinery
- Natural gas processing

**Primary Units**:
- Steam cracker (ethylene, propylene, C4s, pygas)
- Aromatics complex (benzene, toluene, xylenes)

**Derivative Units**:
- Polyethylene (HDPE, LDPE, LLDPE)
- Polypropylene
- Ethylene oxide / Ethylene glycol
- Styrene monomer
- Monoethylene glycol (MEG) for PET

**Support Infrastructure**:
- Hydrogen plant
- Utilities (steam, power, cooling water)
- Wastewater treatment
- Storage and logistics

### Major Integrated Sites

- **Jurong Island, Singapore**: Multiple petrochemical complexes
- **Saudi Arabia**: Jubail, Yanbu (SABIC, Aramco, joint ventures)
- **U.S. Gulf Coast**: Texas, Louisiana (ExxonMobil, Dow, Shell, etc.)
- **China**: Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Guangdong provinces
- **South Korea**: Yeosu, Ulsan
- **Taiwan**: Mailiao complex

## Regional Dynamics and Trade

### Feedstock-Based Competitive Advantage

**Middle East**:
- Low-cost ethane and LPG
- Government subsidies
- Export-oriented (80% of production)
- Dominant in commodity polymers

**North America**:
- Shale gas revolution (2010s)
- Ethane cracker wave
- Ethylene export capability
- Polyethylene export growth

**Asia**:
- Naphtha-based (higher cost)
- Import feedstock and export finished goods
- Value-add focus (specialty polymers, compounding)
- Strong domestic demand

**Europe**:
- Mature, high-cost region
- Naphtha crackers
- Declining capacity
- Focus on specialty chemicals

### Petrochemical Trade Flows

**Major Exporters**:
- Middle East: PE, PP, MEG
- North America: PE, PP (growing)
- Northeast Asia: PVC, PET, synthetic fibers

**Major Importers**:
- China: PE, PP, ethylene glycol
- India: Polymers and intermediates
- Southeast Asia: Polymers
- Europe: Ethylene, propylene

## Environmental and Sustainability Challenges

### Carbon Footprint

**Greenhouse Gas Emissions**:
- Petrochemical industry: 1-2% of global CO₂ emissions
- Steam cracking: Energy-intensive (CO₂ from furnaces)
- Potential reductions:
  - Electrification of crackers (electric furnaces)
  - Renewable hydrogen
  - Carbon capture and storage (CCS)
  - Bio-based feedstocks

### Circular Economy

**Chemical Recycling**:
- Pyrolysis of plastic waste → naphtha → crackers
- Closed-loop sustainability
- Quality equivalent to virgin materials

**Renewable Feedstocks**:
- Bio-naphtha from biomass
- Bio-ethylene from ethanol (sugarcane, corn)
- Drop-in replacements in existing infrastructure

### Flaring Reduction

- Zero routine flaring commitments
- Flare gas recovery systems
- Minimize upsets and startups

### Water and Wastewater

- High water consumption (cooling, steam generation)
- Wastewater treatment (biological, chemical)
- Water recycling and closed-loop systems

## Technology Trends

### Cracking Technology

- **Millisecond furnaces**: Shorter residence time, higher selectivity
- **Catalytic cracking**: Lower temperature, higher yields
- **Plasma cracking**: Electric arc, no combustion

### On-Purpose Production

- **Propane dehydrogenation (PDH)**: Address propylene gap
- **Ethane oxidative dehydrogenation**: Lower temperature ethylene
- **Methanol-to-olefins (MTO)**: Coal or gas to chemicals (China)

### Digital and AI

- Advanced process control (APC)
- Machine learning for yield optimization
- Predictive maintenance
- Digital twins

### Modular and Distributed

- Smaller-scale units closer to feedstock or demand
- Shale gas sites (North America)
- Stranded gas monetization
