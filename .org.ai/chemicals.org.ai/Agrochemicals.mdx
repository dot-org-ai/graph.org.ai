# Agrochemicals (NAICS 3253)

Agrochemicals are chemical products used in agriculture to protect crops from pests and diseases, enhance plant growth, and improve agricultural productivity. This sector encompasses fertilizers for plant nutrition and pesticides for crop protection.

## Overview

The global agrochemicals industry:
- **Market size**: $280+ billion globally
- **Fertilizers**: $180 billion market
- **Crop protection**: $70+ billion market
- **Growth driver**: Feeding growing global population (10 billion by 2050)
- **Arable land**: Limited and declining per capita
- **Yield improvement**: Critical for food security
- **Sustainability pressure**: Reduce environmental impact while increasing productivity

Key trends:
- Precision agriculture and targeted application
- Biological alternatives to synthetic chemicals
- Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
- Regulatory pressures and product withdrawals
- Resistance management
- Digital agriculture and AI-driven recommendations

## NAICS 3253 Subsectors

### 32531 - Fertilizer Manufacturing

Fertilizers provide essential nutrients for plant growth, primarily nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K).

#### Nitrogen Fertilizers ($80+ billion globally)

**Ammonia (NH₃)** - 180M tons/year
- Foundation of nitrogen fertilizers (82% of N-fertilizer production)
- Direct application (anhydrous ammonia injection)
- Feedstock for other N-fertilizers
- Production: Haber-Bosch process
  - N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃
  - Natural gas reforming for H₂ (95% of production)
  - Energy-intensive: 30-40 GJ/ton NH₃
  - Accounts for 1-2% of global energy consumption

**Urea [CO(NH₂)₂]** - 180M tons/year
- Highest nitrogen content (46% N)
- Most widely used N-fertilizer globally
- Applications:
  - Granular fertilizer
  - Urea-ammonium nitrate (UAN) solutions
  - Diesel exhaust fluid (DEF/AdBlue)
  - Animal feed supplement
- Production: Ammonia + CO₂ → urea + H₂O
  - 0.58 tons NH₃ per ton urea
  - High pressure (140-250 bar), temperature (170-200°C)
- Forms:
  - Prilled urea (2-4mm spheres)
  - Granular urea (1-4mm)
  - Urea formaldehyde (slow-release)

**Ammonium Nitrate (AN)** - 20M tons/year
- 34% N content
- Highly soluble, fast-acting
- Applications:
  - Agriculture (declining due to explosive concerns)
  - Explosives (ANFO - ammonium nitrate fuel oil)
- Production: NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃
- Security concerns: Heavily regulated post-9/11
- Calcium ammonium nitrate (CAN): Safer formulation with limestone

**Ammonium Sulfate [(NH₄)₂SO₄]** - 20M tons/year
- 21% N, 24% S
- By-product of caprolactam (nylon) production
- Acidifying effect on soil (benefits for alkaline soils)
- Sulfur provides secondary nutrient

**UAN Solutions** - 15M+ tons/year
- Urea-ammonium nitrate liquid fertilizer
- Formulations: 28% N, 30% N, 32% N
- Advantages:
  - Easy handling and application
  - Uniform field distribution
  - Can be mixed with herbicides
- Used extensively in North American corn production

**Calcium Nitrate [Ca(NO₃)₂]**
- 15.5% N, 19% Ca
- Specialty fertilizer for high-value crops
- Greenhouse and hydroponic applications
- Prevents blossom-end rot in tomatoes

#### Phosphate Fertilizers ($30+ billion globally)

**Phosphate Rock Mining** - 240M tons/year
- Sedimentary deposits (Florida, Morocco, China)
- Igneous deposits (Russia, South Africa)
- Morocco holds 70% of world reserves

**Single Superphosphate (SSP)**
- 16-20% P₂O₅ available
- Production: Phosphate rock + sulfuric acid
- Also provides sulfur and calcium
- Declining use due to low P content

**Triple Superphosphate (TSP)**
- 44-48% P₂O₅
- Production: Phosphate rock + phosphoric acid
- Higher P concentration than SSP

**Monoammonium Phosphate (MAP)** - 20M tons/year
- 11% N, 52% P₂O₅
- High P analysis
- Water-soluble
- Production: NH₃ + H₃PO₄
- Starter fertilizer (band application)

**Diammonium Phosphate (DAP)** - 40M tons/year
- 18% N, 46% P₂O₅
- Most widely used P-fertilizer
- Production: 2NH₃ + H₃PO₄
- Alkaline reaction in soil

**Phosphoric Acid (H₃PO₄)** - 45M tons/year
- Intermediate for MAP and DAP
- Wet process: Ca₃(PO₄)₂ + 3H₂SO₄ → 2H₃PO₄ + 3CaSO₄
  - Produces gypsum as by-product
  - Merchant grade (50-54% P₂O₅)
- Thermal process: Higher purity for food/industrial use
  - Elemental P combustion

#### Potash Fertilizers ($20+ billion globally)

**Potassium Chloride (KCl) - Muriate of Potash** - 40M tons/year
- 60-62% K₂O
- Most common K fertilizer (95% of K-fertilizer market)
- Mined from underground deposits or evaporation ponds
  - Saskatchewan, Canada (largest producer)
  - Russia, Belarus
- Soluble, readily available to plants

**Potassium Sulfate (K₂SO₄) - Sulfate of Potash** - 6M tons/year
- 50% K₂O, 18% S
- Chloride-free (preferred for chloride-sensitive crops)
- Premium pricing
- Crops: Tobacco, fruits, vegetables, nuts
- Production:
  - Mannheim process: 2KCl + H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + 2HCl
  - Natural brines (Great Salt Lake, Dead Sea)

**Potassium Nitrate (KNO₃)**
- 13% N, 44% K₂O
- Specialty fertilizer
- Greenhouse, fertigation, high-value crops
- Production: KCl + NaNO₃ exchange

#### NPK Compound Fertilizers

**Blended Fertilizers (Bulk Blends)**
- Physical mixture of granular fertilizers
- Custom formulations (e.g., 19-19-19, 10-20-10)
- Regional blending plants
- Lower cost than chemically combined

**Chemically Combined NPK**
- Granulation or prilling of NPK slurry
- Uniform nutrient content in each granule
- Common grades: 16-16-16, 15-15-15, 20-20-20

#### Micronutrients and Specialty Fertilizers

**Micronutrients**
- Zinc (Zn): Zinc sulfate, zinc oxide
- Iron (Fe): Ferrous sulfate, chelated iron
- Manganese (Mn): Manganese sulfate
- Copper (Cu): Copper sulfate
- Boron (B): Borax, boric acid
- Molybdenum (Mo): Sodium molybdate

**Controlled/Slow-Release Fertilizers**
- **Sulfur-coated urea (SCU)**: Elemental S coating
- **Polymer-coated urea (PCU)**: Resin coating, controlled diffusion
- **Urea-formaldehyde (UF)**: Slow N release
- **Isobutylidene diurea (IBDU)**: Slow hydrolysis
- Benefits: Reduced leaching, fewer applications, environmental protection

**Organic and Bio-Based Fertilizers**
- Compost and manure
- Bone meal, blood meal, feather meal
- Seaweed extracts
- Biochar

### 32532 - Pesticide and Agricultural Chemical Manufacturing

Crop protection chemicals to control pests, diseases, and weeds.

#### Herbicides ($30+ billion globally)

Herbicides account for 45% of global pesticide market.

**Glyphosate** - 825,000 tons/year
- Broad-spectrum, non-selective herbicide
- Systemic absorption and translocation
- Mode of action: EPSP synthase inhibitor (amino acid synthesis)
- Post-emergence application
- Glyphosate-resistant crop varieties (GMO soybeans, corn, cotton)
- Formulations: Isopropylamine salt, potassium salt
- Trade names: Roundup (original), numerous generics
- Patent expiration: 2000 (Monsanto)
- Controversy: IARC classification, litigation

**2,4-D (2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid)**
- Selective herbicide (broadleaf weeds)
- Synthetic auxin (plant growth regulator mimic)
- Post-emergence systemic
- Cereal crops, turf, pasture
- Oldest synthetic herbicide (introduced 1945)
- Formulations: Amine, ester
- Resistance management tool

**Atrazine**
- Triazine herbicide
- Photosynthesis inhibitor (PSII)
- Pre- and post-emergence
- Primarily corn (maize)
- Banned in EU, widely used in U.S.
- Groundwater contamination concerns

**Paraquat**
- Contact herbicide (non-selective)
- Photosystem I electron diversion
- Fast-acting (hours)
- Highly toxic to humans (no antidote)
- Banned in EU and 60+ countries
- Still used in developing countries

**Acetochlor**
- Chloroacetanilide herbicide
- Pre-emergence grass and broadleaf control
- Corn and soybeans
- Cell division inhibitor

**Dicamba**
- Benzoic acid herbicide
- Selective systemic
- Dicamba-tolerant crops (Xtend soybeans/cotton)
- Volatility and drift issues

**Pendimethalin**
- Dinitroaniline herbicide
- Pre-emergence
- Microtubule assembly inhibitor
- Broad crop use

**Modern Herbicide Classes**
- **ALS inhibitors** (sulfonylureas, imidazolinones): Acetolactate synthase
- **ACCase inhibitors**: Acetyl CoA carboxylase (grass control)
- **HPPD inhibitors** (isoxaflutole, mesotrione): Carotenoid biosynthesis
- **PPO inhibitors**: Protoporphyrinogen oxidase
- **Synthetic auxins**: Hormone mimics

#### Insecticides ($17+ billion globally)

**Neonicotinoids** - Largest insecticide class
- Imidacloprid, thiamethoxam, clothianidin
- Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (nAChR) agonists
- Systemic (seed treatment, soil drench)
- Broad-spectrum insect control
- Pollinator concerns: EU restrictions, neonicotinoid bans
- Bee colony collapse disorder (CCD) association

**Pyrethroids**
- Synthetic analogues of natural pyrethrins
- Sodium channel modulators
- Permethrin, cypermethrin, deltamethrin, lambda-cyhalothrin
- Broad-spectrum, fast knockdown
- Low mammalian toxicity
- Photolabile (limited residual)
- Resistance issues in many pests

**Organophosphates** - Declining use
- Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors
- Chlorpyrifos, malathion, diazinon, acephate
- Neurotoxic mode of action
- High mammalian toxicity
- Being phased out or restricted

**Carbamates** - Declining use
- Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (reversible)
- Carbaryl, methomyl, aldicarb
- Moderate to high mammalian toxicity

**Diamides**
- Chlorantraniliprole, flubendiamide, cyantraniliprole
- Ryanodine receptor modulators
- Lepidoptera (caterpillar) control
- Excellent safety profile
- Newer chemistry (2000s)

**Spinosyns**
- Spinosad, spinetoram
- Natural products (fermentation of Saccharopolyspora spinosa)
- Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor allosteric modulators
- Organic farming approved
- Lepidoptera, thrips, leafminers

**Avermectins**
- Abamectin, emamectin benzoate
- Natural products (Streptomyces avermitilis fermentation)
- Glutamate-gated chloride channel activators
- Miticides and insecticides

**Insect Growth Regulators (IGRs)**
- **Chitin synthesis inhibitors**: Diflubenzuron, lufenuron
- **Juvenile hormone mimics**: Pyriproxyfen, methoprene
- **Ecdysone agonists**: Methoxyfenozide, tebufenozide
- Disrupt molting and metamorphosis
- Selective for insects

#### Fungicides ($16+ billion globally)

**Triazoles** - Largest fungicide class
- Tebuconazole, propiconazole, cyproconazole, difenoconazole
- Sterol biosynthesis inhibitors (SBI) - Demethylation inhibitors (DMI)
- Ergosterol biosynthesis disruption
- Systemic, broad-spectrum
- Cereal diseases (rusts, powdery mildew, Septoria)
- Resistance management: Rotation required

**Strobilurins (QoI)**
- Azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin, trifloxystrobin
- Complex III respiration inhibitors (Qo site)
- Systemic, translaminar
- Broad-spectrum activity
- Cereal, fruit, vegetable diseases
- Resistance risk: Single-site mode of action

**SDHI Fungicides**
- Succinate dehydrogenase inhibitors (Complex II)
- Boscalid, fluxapyroxad, benzovindiflupyr, pydiflumetofen
- Respiration inhibitors
- Broad spectrum
- Excellent residual activity

**Chloronitriles**
- Chlorothalonil
- Multi-site mode of action
- Non-systemic, protectant
- Low resistance risk
- Vegetables, turf, ornamentals
- Under regulatory review (banned in EU)

**Dithiocarbamates**
- Mancozeb, maneb, thiram, zineb
- Multi-site activity
- Protectant fungicides
- Low resistance
- Widely used, low cost

**Carboxamides (non-SDHI)**
- Metalaxyl, mefenoxam (phenylamides)
- Systemic, oomycete-specific
- Downy mildew, Phytophthora, Pythium
- Resistance issues

**Benzimidazoles**
- Carbendazim, benomyl, thiophanate-methyl
- Tubulin assembly inhibitors
- Systemic
- Widespread resistance

**Copper-Based**
- Copper sulfate, copper hydroxide, copper oxychloride
- Multi-site activity
- Organic farming approved
- Bordeaux mixture (historic)

**Biopesticides**
- **Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt)**: Bacterial insecticide
- **Beauveria bassiana**: Entomopathogenic fungus
- **Trichoderma spp.**: Biocontrol fungi
- **Bacillus subtilis**: Biofungicide
- **Plant extracts**: Neem oil, pyrethrin

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Holistic approach to pest management:

### IPM Principles
1. **Pest identification and monitoring**: Scouting, traps, thresholds
2. **Prevention**: Cultural practices, resistant varieties
3. **Economic thresholds**: Treat when pest levels justify cost
4. **Multiple control tactics**: Chemical, biological, cultural, mechanical
5. **Evaluation**: Assess effectiveness, adjust strategies

### IPM Tactics
- **Cultural control**: Crop rotation, tillage, planting dates
- **Biological control**: Natural enemies, parasitoids, predators
- **Mechanical control**: Trapping, barriers, cultivation
- **Chemical control**: Selective pesticides, spot treatment, rotation
- **Host plant resistance**: Breeding, biotechnology (Bt crops)

## Formulation Technologies

### Formulation Types

**Emulsifiable Concentrates (EC)**
- Active ingredient dissolved in organic solvent + emulsifier
- Dilute with water for application
- Good coverage, phytotoxicity risk

**Suspension Concentrates (SC)**
- Solid active ingredient suspended in water
- Dispersants and thickeners
- Reduced organic solvent

**Wettable Powders (WP)**
- Solid formulation, mix with water
- Tank mixing flexibility
- Inhalation exposure during handling

**Water Dispersible Granules (WG/WDG)**
- Granules disperse in water
- Reduced dust compared to WP
- Improved safety

**Granules (GR)**
- Ready-to-use, no mixing
- Soil application, seed furrow
- Reduced applicator exposure

**Microencapsulation**
- Active ingredient encapsulated in polymer shell
- Controlled release
- Reduced volatility and odor
- Environmental safety

**Oil Dispersions (OD)**
- Suspension in oil rather than water
- Rainfast, penetration, adjuvancy

## Mode of Action and Resistance Management

### Herbicide Resistance Action Committee (HRAC)
- Classification by mode of action
- Site of action groups (1-29+)
- Rotation recommendations to prevent resistance

### Insecticide Resistance Action Committee (IRAC)
- Mode of action groups (1-30+)
- Resistance monitoring
- Best management practices

### Fungicide Resistance Action Committee (FRAC)
- Target site groups
- Multi-site vs. single-site fungicides
- Mixture and rotation strategies

### Resistance Management Strategies
- **Rotation**: Alternate modes of action
- **Mixtures**: Tank-mixes of different modes
- **Refuges**: Non-treated areas (insect resistance management)
- **Monitoring**: Track resistance development
- **Integrated approaches**: Reduce selection pressure

## Regulatory Environment

### Registration and Approval
- **EPA (U.S.)**: FIFRA registration, re-registration
- **EU**: Approval under Regulation 1107/2009
- **WHO**: Pesticide classification (Ia, Ib, II, III, U)
- **Data requirements**:
  - Toxicology (acute, chronic, carcinogenicity)
  - Environmental fate (soil, water persistence)
  - Ecotoxicology (birds, fish, bees, non-targets)
  - Residue studies (crop metabolism, storage stability)
  - Efficacy trials

### Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs)
- Codex Alimentarius international standards
- Regional limits (EPA tolerance, EU MRLs)
- Import/export trade considerations

### Restricted Use and Bans
- Paraquat: Banned in 60+ countries
- Neonicotinoids: EU outdoor use restrictions
- Chlorpyrifos: Banned for food use in many countries
- Atrazine: Banned in EU since 2004
- DDT: Banned 1972 (U.S.), limited malaria use allowed

### Worker Safety
- Re-entry intervals (REI)
- Personal protective equipment (PPE)
- Restricted entry signs
- Applicator training and certification

## Application Technologies

### Ground Application
- **Boom sprayers**: Tractor-mounted, field crops
- **Air-blast sprayers**: Orchards, vineyards
- **Backpack sprayers**: Small farms, spot treatment
- **Granular applicators**: In-furrow, broadcast

### Aerial Application
- Fixed-wing aircraft: Large fields
- Helicopters: Forests, rice, terrain
- Drones (UAVs): Precision application, emerging technology

### Precision Agriculture
- **GPS guidance**: Reduce overlaps
- **Variable rate application (VRA)**: Match application to need
- **Weed mapping**: Spot spraying
- **Sensor technology**: Detect disease/pests
- **Decision support systems**: Optimize timing and rates

### Adjuvants
- **Surfactants**: Improve spreading and wetting
- **Oils**: Penetration, rainfastness
- **Drift control agents**: Reduce off-target movement
- **pH buffers**: Optimize pesticide stability
- **Deposition aids**: Improve retention

## Environmental and Health Concerns

### Water Quality
- **Runoff**: Surface water contamination
- **Leaching**: Groundwater contamination
- **Buffer zones**: Protect waterways
- **Integrated pest management**: Reduce use

### Pollinator Health
- **Neonicotinoid restrictions**: Seed treatment bans
- **Spray timing**: Avoid blooming periods
- **Habitat conservation**: Hedgerows, wildflower strips
- **Pollinator testing**: Regulatory requirements

### Soil Health
- **Microbiome impacts**: Soil bacteria and fungi
- **Earthworm toxicity**: Non-target effects
- **Organic matter**: Adsorption and degradation
- **Residual activity**: Crop rotation restrictions

### Human Health
- **Acute toxicity**: Poisoning incidents
- **Chronic exposure**: Applicator health
- **Endocrine disruption**: Hormonal effects
- **Carcinogenicity**: Long-term cancer risk
- **Dietary exposure**: Residues in food

### Generic vs. Originator
- Patent expiration: 20 years from filing

- High attrition rate (1 in 10,000 compounds)
- Discovery, development, registration
