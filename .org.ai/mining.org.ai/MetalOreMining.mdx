# Metal Ore Mining

Metal ore mining (NAICS 2122) encompasses the extraction and primary processing of metallic mineral ores, including ferrous metals (iron, steel-making materials), precious metals (gold, silver, platinum), base metals (copper, lead, zinc, nickel), and specialty metals (uranium, rare earth elements). This sector supplies essential raw materials for manufacturing, construction, electronics, energy, and defense industries.

## Overview

Metal mining operations vary dramatically in scale, technology, and economics, from massive open-pit copper mines moving millions of tons of material daily to selective underground gold mines targeting high-grade ore bodies. The sector is characterized by:

- **Capital Intensity**: Billion-dollar investments for major projects
- **Long Development Cycles**: 7-15 years from discovery to production
- **Global Markets**: Commodity prices set on international exchanges
- **Resource Depletion**: Declining ore grades necessitating technological innovation
- **Environmental Scrutiny**: Tailings management, water quality, ecosystem impacts
- **Strategic Importance**: Critical minerals for national security and clean energy

### U.S. Metal Mining Industry Scale

- **Employment**: ~50,000 direct mining jobs
- **Production Value**: $30+ billion annually
- **Major Mining States**: Nevada, Arizona, Utah, Minnesota, Alaska, Missouri
- **Active Operations**: ~150 metal mines
- **Exploration Spending**: $1-2 billion/year

## Iron Ore Mining (NAICS 212210)

### U.S. Iron Ore Production

The United States produces approximately 50 million metric tons of usable iron ore annually, primarily from Minnesota's Mesabi Iron Range and Michigan's Marquette Range. Production is dominated by low-grade taconite ores requiring extensive beneficiation to produce pellets suitable for steelmaking.

**Major Producers**:
- Cleveland-Cliffs (formerly ArcelorMittal USA)
- U.S. Steel (Keetac, Minntac)
- Magnetation LLC

**Key Facilities**:
- Minnesota: Hibbing Taconite, United Taconite, Northshore Mining, Mesabi Nugget
- Michigan: Empire Mine, Tilden Mine

### Taconite Processing

**Geology**: Banded iron formation (BIF) containing 20-30% iron as magnetite (Fe3O4) and hematite (Fe2O3) with silica, requiring grinding and separation.

**Mining**:
- Open-pit operations with 40-60 ft benches
- Rotary blast-hole drilling (12-15 inch diameter)
- ANFO or emulsion blasting
- Electric rope shovels (30-100 cubic yard capacity)
- Haul trucks (240-400 ton capacity)

**Processing Steps**:

1. **Crushing**: Gyratory and cone crushers reduce ore to <1 inch
2. **Grinding**: Semi-autogenous (SAG) mills and ball mills grind to ~200 mesh (74 microns)
3. **Magnetic Separation**: Wet magnetic separators concentrate magnetite
4. **Flotation**: Silica flotation removes remaining gangue
5. **Pelletizing**: Iron ore concentrate (65-70% Fe) mixed with bentonite binder
6. **Induration**: Pellets fired in traveling grate furnace at 2,300°F
7. **Shipping**: Great Lakes ore boats to steel mills (20,000-70,000 ton capacity)

**Product Specifications**:
- Iron content: 63-67% Fe
- Silica: <6%
- Phosphorus: <0.04%
- Pellet size: 9-16 mm
- Crush strength: >500 lbs

**Economics**:
- Capital cost: $2-4 billion for new facility
- Operating cost: $40-60/ton concentrate
- Stripping ratio: 0.5-1.5:1 (waste to ore)
- Processing recovery: 85-90%

### Direct Shipping Ore (DSO)

High-grade iron ore (>60% Fe) requiring minimal processing is increasingly rare in the U.S. Historical DSO production from Minnesota and Michigan natural ore deposits has been largely exhausted, with remaining production focused on taconite beneficiation.

## Copper Mining (NAICS 212230)

### Porphyry Copper Deposits

Porphyry copper deposits account for ~60% of global copper production and dominate U.S. copper mining. These large, low-grade (0.2-1.0% Cu) disseminated deposits formed by hydrothermal fluids associated with intrusive igneous rocks.

**Major U.S. Copper Districts**:
- **Arizona**: Morenci, Bagdad, Sierrita, Ray, Mission, Miami
- **Utah**: Bingham Canyon (Kennecott), Lisbon Valley
- **New Mexico**: Chino, Tyrone, Cobre
- **Nevada**: Robinson, Yerington

**U.S. Production**: ~1.3 million metric tons copper/year (~6% of global production)

### Bingham Canyon Mine (Kennecott Utah Copper)

The world's largest man-made excavation and most productive copper mine in U.S. history.

**Dimensions**:
- Depth: 4,000 feet
- Width: 2.5 miles
- Vertical benches: 50 feet
- Operating since: 1906

**Operations**:
- Material movement: 450,000 tons/day
- Ore production: 150,000 tons/day @ 0.6% Cu
- Stripping ratio: 2:1
- Haul trucks: 400-ton payload (CAT 797F)
- Shovels: 73 cubic yard electric rope shovels
- Blasting: 900,000 lbs explosives/day

**Processing**:
- Crushing: Gyratory crusher (60x110 inch)
- Grinding: SAG and ball mills
- Flotation: Copper sulfide concentration to 28% Cu
- Smelting: Flash furnace producing copper matte
- Converting: Peirce-Smith converters to blister copper (98.5% Cu)
- Electrorefining: Cathode copper (99.99% Cu)

**Production**:
- Copper: 180,000 tons/year
- Molybdenum: 5,000 tons/year (byproduct)
- Gold: 400,000 oz/year (byproduct)
- Silver: 3 million oz/year (byproduct)

**Mine Life**: Reserves to support mining through 2030s

### Copper Processing Technology

**Sulfide Ore Processing**:

1. **Comminution**:
   - SAG mills: 36-40 ft diameter, 11,000-17,000 HP
   - Ball mills: 22-28 ft diameter
   - Target grind: 60% passing 150 mesh (100 microns)

2. **Flotation**:
   - Collectors: Xanthates (potassium amyl xanthate)
   - Frothers: MIBC, pine oil
   - Rougher, scavenger, and cleaner circuits
   - Concentrate grade: 25-35% Cu

3. **Thickening and Filtration**:
   - High-rate thickeners: 300-500 ft diameter
   - Pressure filters or vacuum filters
   - Concentrate moisture: 8-10%

4. **Pyrometallurgical Extraction**:
   - Flash smelting: Oxygen-enriched air, autogenous (self-heating)
   - Matte: 45-75% Cu
   - Converting: Oxidizing iron and sulfur
   - Anode casting: 99.5% Cu
   - Electrorefining: Copper sulfate electrolyte, cathode deposition
   - Cathode copper: 99.99% Cu (LME Grade A)

**Oxide Ore Processing (SX-EW)**:

For oxide and secondary sulfide ores unsuitable for flotation:

1. **Heap Leaching**:
   - Ore stacked on lined pads (200-300 ft lifts)
   - Dilute sulfuric acid solution (5-20 g/L H2SO4)
   - Drip irrigation: 0.005-0.02 gpm/ft²
   - Leach cycle: 60-365 days
   - Pregnant leach solution (PLS): 2-10 g/L Cu

2. **Solvent Extraction (SX)**:
   - Organic extractant: LIX 984N or similar
   - Mixer-settlers: 3-4 extraction stages, 2-3 stripping stages
   - Loaded organic + acid stripping = rich electrolyte
   - Electrolyte: 40-50 g/L Cu, 150-200 g/L H2SO4

3. **Electrowinning (EW)**:
   - Permanent stainless steel or lead-alloy cathodes
   - Insoluble lead-alloy anodes
   - Current density: 250-350 A/m²
   - Cathode cycle: 5-10 days
   - Cathode copper: 99.99% Cu

**Advantages**: Lower capital cost, suitable for low-grade ores (0.1-0.5% Cu), lower metallurgical recovery (~70-90%)

### Copper Concentrate Marketing

- Sold to smelters on long-term contracts
- Treatment charges (TC) and refining charges (RC)
- Typical TC/RC: $50-100/ton and 5-10 cents/lb
- Penalties for impurities (arsenic, bismuth, mercury)
- Payment for byproducts (gold, silver, molybdenum)

## Gold and Silver Mining (NAICS 212220)

### U.S. Gold Production

The United States is the 4th largest gold producer globally (~200 metric tons/year), primarily from Nevada's Carlin Trend and other disseminated gold deposits.

**Major Districts**:
- **Nevada**: Carlin Trend, Cortez, Round Mountain, Lone Tree, Goldstrike
- **Alaska**: Fort Knox, Pogo, Donlin Gold
- **South Dakota**: Homestake (historic), Wharf
- **California**: Mesquite, Castle Mountain (reactivation)

**Mine Types**:
- Open-pit heap leach: 60% of production
- Underground: 25% of production
- Open-pit milling: 15% of production

### Carlin-Type Gold Deposits

Unique sediment-hosted, disseminated gold deposits discovered in Nevada in 1961. World-class district with >75 million ounces gold produced.

**Geology**:
- Sedimentary host rocks (limestone, siltstone)
- Microscopic gold (<5 microns) in arsenian pyrite, arsenopyrite
- Low-grade (0.02-0.15 oz/ton)
- Large tonnage (10-100+ million tons)
- Refractory (not amenable to simple cyanide leaching)

**Mining**:
- Large open-pit operations (Goldstrike, Cortez)
- Blast-hole pattern: 20-30 ft spacing
- Waste rock management: Hundreds of millions of tons overburden
- Ore stockpiling: Multiple grade categories

### Gold Heap Leaching

**Process**:

1. **Ore Preparation**:
   - Primary crushing: Jaw or gyratory crusher
   - Agglomeration: Mixing with cement, lime, or curing with acid
   - Stacking: Conveyor or truck placement, 15-30 ft lifts

2. **Leaching**:
   - Sodium cyanide solution: 200-500 mg/L NaCN
   - pH control: Lime addition (pH 10-11)
   - Application rate: 0.005-0.02 gpm/ft²
   - Drip emitters or sprinklers
   - Leach cycle: 60-365 days
   - Gold extraction: 60-85%

3. **Pregnant Solution Processing**:
   - Pregnant solution: 0.5-5 mg/L gold
   - Activated carbon columns (Merrill-Crowe alternatively)
   - Gold adsorption onto carbon
   - Carbon elution: Caustic cyanide solution
   - Electrowinning: Gold deposition on steel wool cathodes
   - Smelting: Induction furnace
   - Doré bars: 85-95% gold, 5-15% silver

**Environmental Controls**:
- Double-lined pads: HDPE geomembrane
- Leak detection systems
- Solution ponds: Bird netting, cyanide destruction
- Closure: Rinsing, detoxification (SO2, H2O2, Caro's acid)

### Refractory Gold Processing

For Carlin-type and other refractory ores where gold is locked in sulfide minerals:

**Oxidation Pretreatment**:

1. **Roasting**:
   - Multiple-hearth or fluidized-bed roasters
   - 500-700°C
   - Oxidize sulfides to oxides
   - Sulfur dioxide emissions: Acid plant integration

2. **Pressure Oxidation (Autoclave)**:
   - High pressure (400-500 psi), high temperature (200-230°C)
   - Oxygen injection
   - Oxidize sulfides in aqueous slurry
   - No SO2 emissions

3. **Biological Oxidation**:
   - Bacteria (Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans)
   - Ambient temperature, atmospheric pressure
   - 5-10 day retention
   - Lower capital cost but slower

**Cyanide Leaching**:
- Oxidized ore + NaCN solution in agitated tanks
- 24-48 hours leaching
- Carbon-in-pulp (CIP) or carbon-in-leach (CIL)
- Gold recovery: 85-95%

### Underground Gold Mining

**Methods**:
- **Cut-and-Fill**: Narrow vein deposits, selective mining
- **Longhole Open Stoping**: Larger ore bodies, mechanized drilling
- **Sublevel Caving**: Large, low-grade deposits (rare for gold)

**Equipment**:
- Jumbo drills: Single or multi-boom
- Load-haul-dump (LHD) units: 6-14 yard capacity
- Haul trucks: 40-60 ton underground trucks
- Bolters, shotcrete for ground support
- Ventilation: 50,000-500,000 cfm

**Mining Costs**: $75-150/ton (higher than open-pit)

**Ore Grades**: 0.15-0.50 oz/ton (higher than open-pit)

### Silver Mining

Silver is primarily produced as a byproduct of copper, lead-zinc, and gold mining. Pure silver mines are rare in the U.S.

**Byproduct Sources**:
- Porphyry copper: 1-10 oz silver/ton ore
- Lead-zinc deposits: 2-20 oz silver/ton
- Gold deposits: Gold:silver ratio 1:5 to 1:50

**Primary Silver Deposits**:
- Coeur d'Alene District (Idaho): Historic major producer
- Epithermal deposits: Nevada, Colorado

## Rare Earth Element (REE) Mining

### Critical Minerals for Clean Energy and Defense

Rare earth elements consist of 17 elements (15 lanthanides plus scandium and yttrium) essential for permanent magnets, batteries, electronics, and defense systems.

**Light REEs**: Lanthanum (La), Cerium (Ce), Praseodymium (Pr), Neodymium (Nd), Samarium (Sm)

**Heavy REEs**: Europium (Eu), Gadolinium (Gd), Terbium (Tb), Dysprosium (Dy), Holmium (Ho), Erbium (Er), Thulium (Tm), Ytterbium (Yb), Lutetium (Lu), plus Yttrium (Y)

**Applications**:
- Permanent magnets (Nd, Dy): Wind turbines, EV motors, hard drives
- Batteries (La, Ce): NiMH batteries, catalysts
- Phosphors (Eu, Tb, Y): LED lighting, displays
- Polishing compounds (Ce): Glass, semiconductors
- Defense (Nd, Dy, Er): Guidance systems, lasers, radar

### Mountain Pass Mine (California)

**History**: Discovered 1949, operated 1952-2002, restarted 2012

**Current Operator**: MP Materials (formerly Molycorp)

**Geology**: Carbonatite intrusion with bastnaesite (Ce,La,Nd,Pr)CO3F

**Mining**:
- Open-pit operation
- Ore grade: 7-9% rare earth oxides (REO)
- Production: 15,000-50,000 tons/year REO concentrate

**Processing**:
- Crushing and grinding
- Flotation: Bastnaesite concentrate (60% REO)
- Rare earth chloride production
- Solvent extraction separation (planned)
- Neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr) oxide production

**Challenges**:
- Thorium radioactivity management
- Complex REE separation chemistry
- Chinese market dominance (80% global supply)
- U.S. downstream processing limited

### Other U.S. REE Prospects

- **Bear Lodge (Wyoming)**: Carbonatite deposit, development stage
- **Round Top (Texas)**: Heavy REE-enriched rhyolite, development stage
- **Bokan Mountain (Alaska)**: Heavy REE prospect

**Strategic Initiatives**:
- Department of Defense funding for domestic supply
- USGS critical minerals mapping
- Recycling from magnets, batteries, electronics

## Lead and Zinc Mining (NAICS 212230)

### Mississippi Valley-Type (MVT) Deposits

**Viburnum Trend (Missouri)**:
- Historic lead-zinc district (Southeast Missouri Lead District)
- Sediment-hosted sulfide deposits (galena PbS, sphalerite ZnS)
- Underground room-and-pillar mining
- Declining production, environmental legacy issues

**Current U.S. Lead-Zinc Production**: Minimal primary production, mostly recycling (lead-acid batteries)

## Uranium Mining (NAICS 212291)

### In-Situ Recovery (ISR)

**Principle**: Oxidizing solution injected into sandstone aquifer dissolves uranium, extracted via production wells.

**Process**:

1. **Wellfield Development**:
   - Injection and production well patterns (5-spot, 7-spot)
   - Well spacing: 30-100 ft
   - Screened interval: Uranium-bearing sandstone

2. **Lixiviant Injection**:
   - Oxygen + carbon dioxide or sodium bicarbonate
   - Oxidizes uranium (U+4 → U+6)
   - Uranyl carbonate complexes (soluble)

3. **Production and Processing**:
   - Pregnant solution: 20-200 mg/L uranium
   - Ion exchange resin columns
   - Elution with brine or acid
   - Precipitation as yellow cake (U3O8)

4. **Groundwater Restoration**:
   - Flushing with clean water
   - Reducing agents (H2S)
   - Reverse osmosis
   - Target: Baseline water quality

**U.S. ISR Operations**:
- Wyoming: Powder River Basin, Great Divide Basin
- Texas: South Texas uranium district
- Nebraska: Crow Butte, North Platte

**Advantages**: Low surface disturbance, lower cost than conventional mining

**Challenges**: Groundwater impacts, restoration to baseline, excursions (solution migration)

**Regulation**: Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) and EPA

### Conventional Uranium Mining

**Historic Methods**:
- Open-pit (Grants Mineral Belt, New Mexico)
- Underground (Colorado Plateau)

**Current Status**: No active conventional uranium mining in U.S. (2024)

**Legacy Sites**: Extensive remediation under CERCLA and UMTRCA

## Mineral Processing and Beneficiation

### Comminution Fundamentals

**Energy Consumption**: 30-50% of mining operating costs, 2-3% of global electricity

**Grinding Laws**:
- **Rittinger's Law**: Energy proportional to new surface area created
- **Kick's Law**: Energy proportional to reduction ratio
- **Bond's Law**: Most widely used, empirical work index

**Equipment Selection**:
- **Jaw Crushers**: Primary crushing, feed 1,000-1,500 mm, product 100-300 mm
- **Gyratory Crushers**: Primary crushing, higher capacity than jaw
- **Cone Crushers**: Secondary/tertiary crushing, feed 50-300 mm, product 10-50 mm
- **SAG Mills**: Semi-autogenous, ore + steel balls, 20-40 ft diameter
- **Ball Mills**: Fine grinding, steel balls, 12-28 ft diameter
- **High-Pressure Grinding Rolls (HPGR)**: Energy-efficient alternative

### Flotation Chemistry

**Principle**: Hydrophobic mineral particles attach to air bubbles, float to froth layer

**Reagents**:
- **Collectors**: Xanthates, dithiophosphates (sulfide minerals)
- **Frothers**: MIBC, pine oil (bubble stability)
- **Depressants**: Lime, sodium cyanide (selective flotation)
- **Activators**: Copper sulfate (zinc activation)
- **pH Modifiers**: Lime, sulfuric acid

**Applications**:
- Copper, lead, zinc, nickel sulfides
- Molybdenum
- Iron ore (reverse flotation)
- Phosphate
- Rare earths

**Recovery**: 85-95% for base metals

### Gravity Separation

**Principle**: Density difference between minerals

**Equipment**:
- **Jigs**: Pulsating water, dense minerals settle
- **Shaking Tables**: Inclined table with riffles, water flow
- **Spirals**: Gravity and centrifugal force on helical trough
- **Centrifugal Concentrators**: Enhanced gravity (Knelson, Falcon)

**Applications**:
- Gold, tin, tantalum, tungsten
- Coal cleaning
- Iron ore

### Magnetic Separation

**Principle**: Magnetic susceptibility differences

**Equipment**:
- **Low-Intensity Magnetic Separators (LIMS)**: Iron ore (magnetite)
- **High-Intensity Magnetic Separators (HIMS)**: Weakly magnetic minerals
- **Rare Earth Drum Magnets**: Rare earths, ilmenite

### Tailings Management

**Tailings**: Fine waste slurry from mineral processing (90-99% of mined material)

**Storage Methods**:

1. **Conventional Impoundment**:
   - Tailings slurry (20-30% solids)
   - Tailings dams (upstream, downstream, centerline)
   - Supernatant water pond
   - Water recycling to mill

2. **Thickened Tailings**:
   - Increased solids (45-60%)
   - High-rate thickeners
   - Reduced dam height
   - Less segregation

3. **Paste Tailings**:
   - Very high solids (65-75%)
   - Non-segregating
   - Surface stacking or underground backfill
   - Higher energy for pumping

4. **Filtered (Dry Stack) Tailings**:
   - Pressure filtration to 80-85% solids
   - Conveyor transport
   - Compacted stacking
   - No pond, smallest footprint
   - Highest capital and operating cost

**Risks**:
- Dam failure: Catastrophic environmental damage (Brumadinho 2019, Mount Polley 2014)
- Acid rock drainage: Sulfide oxidation
- Seepage: Groundwater contamination
- Long-term stability: Perpetual care

**Regulation**: State dam safety, federal (MSHA if mineral), EPA, local

## Economics and Markets

### Commodity Pricing

**Exchanges**:
- **London Metal Exchange (LME)**: Copper, lead, zinc, nickel, aluminum
- **COMEX (CME)**: Gold, silver, copper
- **London Bullion Market (LBMA)**: Gold, silver

**Price Drivers**:
- Supply-demand balance
- Economic growth (industrial metals)
- Currency fluctuations (USD)
- Geopolitical events
- Inventory levels
- Speculation

**Price Volatility**: Boom-bust cycles, long lead times for new supply

### Mine Development Economics

**Typical Capital Costs** (Greenfield):
- Small underground gold: $100-300 million
- Open-pit copper: $1-5 billion
- World-class copper: $5-10+ billion

**Development Timeline**: 7-15 years (exploration to production)

**Operating Costs**:
- Gold (heap leach): $600-900/oz
- Gold (underground): $900-1,400/oz
- Copper (porphyry): $1.50-2.50/lb (C1 cash cost)
- Iron ore (taconite): $40-70/ton

**Economic Metrics**:
- Net Present Value (NPV) at discount rate (5-10%)
- Internal Rate of Return (IRR): >15% for approval
- Payback period: <7 years preferred
- Sensitivity to metal prices, operating costs, capital
