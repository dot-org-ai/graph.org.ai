# Aquaculture (NAICS 1125)

Aquaculture is the farming of aquatic organisms including fish, mollusks, crustaceans, and aquatic plants. This sector encompasses establishments primarily engaged in farm-raising finfish, shellfish, or any other kind of aquatic animal or plant life.

## Overview

The global aquaculture industry:
- **Global production**: 120+ million tons/year (exceeds wild-caught fisheries)
- **Market value**: $250+ billion globally
- **U.S. production**: 630,000 tons/year (7% of global)
- **Growth rate**: 5-6% annually (fastest-growing food production sector)
- **Major producers**: China (60% of global), Indonesia, India, Vietnam, Bangladesh
- **U.S. consumption**: 90% of seafood is imported
- **Protein efficiency**: Fish have feed conversion ratios of 1.1-1.5 (highly efficient)

Key trends:
- Recirculating Aquaculture Systems (RAS) for land-based production
- Offshore and open-ocean aquaculture expansion
- Integrated Multi-Trophic Aquaculture (IMTA)
- Genetics and selective breeding programs
- Disease management and biosecurity
- Sustainable feed development (reducing fishmeal dependency)
- Traceability and certification (ASC, BAP)

## NAICS 1125 Subsectors

### 11251 - Finfish Farming and Fish Hatcheries

Production of finfish for food, sport, and ornamental purposes.

#### Catfish Farming ($380 million U.S.)

**Channel Catfish (Ictalurus punctatus)** - Dominant U.S. aquaculture species
- **Production**: 300+ million lbs/year (live weight)
- **Primary region**: Mississippi Delta (88% of U.S. production)
  - Mississippi, Alabama, Arkansas
- **Pond culture system**:
  - Earthen ponds (10-20 acres typical)
  - 4-6 feet depth
  - Aeration (paddle wheels, propeller-aspirators)
  - Continuous oxygen monitoring
- **Stocking density**: 5,000-8,000 fish/acre
- **Feed**: Floating pellets (32-36% protein)
  - Soybean meal, corn, fish meal
  - Feed conversion ratio: 1.6-2.0
- **Grow-out period**: 18-24 months to 1.5 lbs market size
- **Harvest**: Seine nets, draining ponds
- **Processing**: Live-haul to processing plants
  - Filleting, freezing, value-added products
- **Market**: Food service, retail, export (minimal)

**Hybrid Catfish**:
- Channel catfish (female) x Blue catfish (male)
- Improved growth rate, disease resistance
- Better feed conversion
- Increasing adoption

#### Trout Farming ($100+ million U.S.)

**Rainbow Trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss)**
- **Production**: 55+ million lbs/year
- **Primary states**: Idaho (73% of U.S.), North Carolina, Pennsylvania, California
- **Raceway systems**:
  - Concrete raceways with flowing water
  - Spring water or river water (cold, clean, high oxygen)
  - High density: 0.5-1.0 lbs/gallon
- **Feed**: High protein pellets (40-50% protein)
  - Fishmeal-based, sustainable alternatives emerging
  - Feed conversion ratio: 1.1-1.3
- **Grow-out**: 12-18 months to 1-2 lbs
- **Markets**:
  - Fee-fishing/pay-to-fish operations (stocking)
  - Food fish (fresh, frozen, smoked)
  - U.S. Department of Interior stocking programs (hatcheries)

**Other Trout Species**:
- Brook trout, Brown trout (smaller scale)

#### Salmon Farming ($1.5+ billion U.S.)

**Atlantic Salmon (Salmo salar)** - Not native to Pacific, but farmed globally
- **Global production**: 2.7 million tons/year
- **Major producers**: Norway (50%), Chile, Scotland, Canada, Faroe Islands
- **U.S. production**: Maine (net pen aquaculture)
- **Net pen systems**:
  - Marine net pens in coastal waters
  - Circular pens (100-200 ft diameter)
  - Submerged to 30-50 feet depth
- **Grow-out**: 18-24 months to 10-12 lbs
- **Feed**: Fishmeal, fish oil, soy, vegetable oils
  - Feed conversion ratio: 1.2-1.4
- **Challenges**:
  - Sea lice (parasitic copepods)
  - Escapes (genetic interaction with wild fish concerns)
  - Disease (infectious salmon anemia, Piscirickettsia)
  - Environmental impact (nutrient loading, benthic impacts)
- **Innovations**:
  - Land-based RAS salmon farms (Atlantic Sapphire, Nordic Aquafarms)
  - Offshore submersible pens
  - Closed containment systems

**Coho Salmon, Chinook Salmon**:
- Limited farming, mostly Pacific hatcheries for enhancement

#### Tilapia Farming ($100+ million U.S.)

**Nile Tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus)** - "Aquatic chicken"
- **Global production**: 6+ million tons/year
- **Major producers**: China, Indonesia, Egypt, Thailand
- **U.S. production**: Limited (imports dominate market)
  - Some production in geothermal facilities, indoor RAS
- **Production systems**:
  - Pond culture (tropical/subtropical climates)
  - Tank culture with water recirculation
  - Cage culture in reservoirs
- **Temperature**: Tropical species (75-85°F optimal)
- **Feed**: Omnivorous, accept plant-based feeds well
  - Feed conversion ratio: 1.5-1.8
- **Grow-out**: 6-9 months to 1.5-2 lbs
- **Market**: Whole fish, fillets (mild white meat)

**Other Tilapia Species**:
- Blue tilapia, Mozambique tilapia, hybrids

#### Striped Bass and Hybrid Striped Bass

**Hybrid Striped Bass** - Striped bass x White bass
- **Production**: 10+ million lbs/year
- **States**: North Carolina, California, Massachusetts
- **Pond and tank culture**
- **High-value food fish**
- **Sportfish stocking**

#### Recirculating Aquaculture Systems (RAS)

**Land-Based Indoor Fish Farming**
- **Water recirculation**: 90-99% water reuse
- **Biosecurity**: Controlled environment, disease exclusion
- **Location flexibility**: Near urban markets
- **Year-round production**: Climate-independent
- **Species**: Salmon, barramundi, tilapia, trout, yellowtail

**RAS Components**:
- **Culture tanks**: Circular for self-cleaning water flow
- **Solids removal**: Drum filters, settling basins, clarifiers
- **Biofiltration**: Nitrifying bacteria convert ammonia → nitrite → nitrate
  - Moving bed biofilm reactors (MBBR)
  - Fluidized sand filters
- **Oxygenation**: Pure oxygen injection, low-head oxygenators (LHO)
- **Degassing**: CO₂ stripping towers
- **UV sterilization**: Pathogen control
- **Temperature control**: Heating/cooling systems
- **pH and alkalinity management**: Lime dosing

**Advantages**:
- Biosecurity and disease control
- Water conservation
- No escapes or interactions with wild fish
- Proximity to markets (freshness)

**Challenges**:
- High capital costs ($10,000+/ton capacity)
- Energy costs (pumping, aeration, temperature control)
- Technical expertise required
- Waste disposal (sludge management)

#### Ornamental Fish Farming

**Tropical Fish**:
- Guppies, tetras, barbs, cichlids, goldfish, koi
- Florida is major production center
- Greenhouse and pond culture
- Breeding for color and fin varieties

#### Baitfish Farming

**Golden Shiners, Fathead Minnows**:
- Arkansas is leading producer
- Pond culture
- Live bait market for recreational fishing

### 11252 - Shellfish Farming

Production of mollusks and crustaceans.

#### Oyster Farming ($200+ million U.S.)

**Eastern Oyster (Crassostrea virginica)** - East Coast
**Pacific Oyster (Crassostrea gigas)** - West Coast
- **U.S. production**: 40+ million lbs/year (meat weight)
- **Primary states**: Washington, Louisiana, Virginia, Maryland, California
- **Production methods**:
  - **Bottom culture**: Oysters grow on natural or prepared substrate on bottom
  - **Off-bottom culture**:
    - Bags or cages on racks
    - Floating bags or cages
    - Longline systems
  - **Intertidal culture**: Exposed at low tide (faster growth, easier harvest)
- **Hatchery production**:
  - Spawn oysters in controlled conditions
  - Larval rearing (2-3 weeks)
  - Setting on cultch (shell) or remote setting
  - Nursery rearing to seed size
- **Grow-out**: 18-36 months to market size (3 inches)
- **Triploid oysters**:
  - Sterile oysters (chromosome manipulation)
  - Faster growth, better summer meat quality
  - Year-round harvest (no spawning season)
- **Benefits**:
  - Water filtration (each oyster filters 30-50 gallons/day)
  - Habitat creation
  - Nutrient removal (nitrogen, phosphorus sequestration)
- **Markets**: Half-shell (raw), shucked (meat), specialty varieties

**Oyster Varieties and Appellations**:
- Kumamoto, Olympia (West Coast)
- Blue Point, Wellfleet, Chesapeake (East Coast)
- Terroir concept (flavor profiles based on location)

#### Clam Farming ($100+ million U.S.)

**Hard Clam (Mercenaria mercenaria)** - "Quahog"
- **Production**: Florida, Virginia, Massachusetts
- **Sizes**: Littleneck, topneck, cherrystone, chowder
- **Culture methods**:
  - Hatchery spawning and larval rearing
  - Nursery grow-out
  - Field planting in intertidal or subtidal areas
  - Protection nets (predator exclusion)
  - Harvest by raking, hydraulic dredging
- **Grow-out**: 2-3 years to littleneck size

**Manila Clam (Venerupis philippinarum)**:
- Pacific Northwest
- Intertidal beach culture

**Geoduck Clam (Panopea generosa)**:
- Washington State
- High-value export market (Asia)
- 5-7 years to market size

#### Mussel Farming ($50+ million U.S.)

**Blue Mussel (Mytilus edulis)**
- **Production**: Maine, Washington, California
- **Culture methods**:
  - **Raft culture**: Ropes suspended from floating rafts
  - **Longline culture**: Ropes suspended from horizontal longlines
  - **Bouchot culture**: Poles in intertidal zone (Europe)
- **Hatchery or wild seed collection**:
  - Seed collection on ropes
  - Mussel spatfall (larval settlement)
- **Grow-out**: 12-24 months
- **Harvest**: Stripping ropes, mechanical declumping
- **Processing**: Debearding, grading, packaging
- **Market**: Live, frozen, canned

#### Abalone Farming

**Red Abalone (Haliotis rufescens)**
- **Production**: California
- **Tank culture, flow-through or recirculating systems**
- **Feed**: Kelp (macroalgae)
- **Grow-out**: 3-4 years to market size
- **High-value product** ($15-30/lb)

#### Scallop Farming

**Bay Scallop, Sea Scallop**:
- Limited U.S. production
- Lantern nets, ear-hanging culture
- China is major producer

### Crustacean Aquaculture

#### Shrimp Farming ($50 million U.S., $30+ billion globally)

**Pacific White Shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei)** - Dominant species
- **Global production**: 5+ million tons/year
- **Major producers**: Ecuador, India, Vietnam, Indonesia, Thailand, China
- **U.S. production**: Limited (Texas, Florida, Hawaii)
  - Indoor RAS systems emerging (Iowa, Indiana)
- **Production systems**:
  - **Ponds**: Extensive, semi-intensive, intensive
    - Earthen ponds, 1-10 acres
    - Aeration (paddle wheels)
    - Stocking density: 10-100/m²
  - **Biofloc systems**: High density, zero water exchange
    - Heterotrophic bacteria consume waste
    - Biofloc as supplemental feed
  - **RAS systems**: Indoor tanks, biosecure
- **Hatchery production**:
  - Broodstock maturation
  - Spawning, larval rearing (nauplii → zoea → mysis → post-larvae)
  - 21-day hatchery cycle
- **Grow-out**: 3-4 months to 15-25g market size
- **Feed**: Pelleted feeds (35-40% protein)
  - Fishmeal, soy, other protein sources
  - Feed conversion ratio: 1.4-1.8
- **Diseases**:
  - White Spot Syndrome Virus (WSSV)
  - Early Mortality Syndrome (EMS/AHPND)
  - Specific Pathogen Free (SPF) and Specific Pathogen Resistant (SPR) stocks
- **Sustainability concerns**:
  - Mangrove destruction (historical)
  - Effluent discharge
  - Disease outbreaks
  - Certification programs (BAP, ASC)

#### Freshwater Prawn Farming

**Giant River Prawn (Macrobrachium rosenbergii)**
- **Production**: Asia (Thailand, China, India)
- **U.S. production**: Minimal (warm climates only)
- **Pond culture**
- **Grow-out**: 5-6 months

### 11253 - Aquaculture (except Animal)

#### Seaweed and Algae Farming

**Kelp Farming** (Sugar kelp, Bull kelp)
- **Growing interest in U.S.**: Maine, Alaska, Connecticut
- **Longline culture**: Spores seeded on ropes
- **Winter/spring growth** (cold water)
- **Harvest**: Spring (before water warms)
- **Uses**: Food (salads, snacks), animal feed, fertilizer, biofuel potential
- **Ecosystem benefits**: Nutrient removal, carbon sequestration, habitat

**Gracilaria, Eucheuma** (Tropical seaweeds):
- Agar and carrageenan production (hydrocolloids)
- Asia-Pacific is major producer

**Spirulina (Cyanobacteria)**:
- Dietary supplement, health food
- Raceway ponds, photobioreactors
- Hawaii, California production

### Integrated Multi-Trophic Aquaculture (IMTA)

**Concept**: Combine species from different trophic levels
- **Example**:
  - Finfish (fed species, nutrient output)
  - Shellfish (filter feeders, consume organic particles)
  - Seaweed (absorb dissolved nutrients)
- **Benefits**:
  - Nutrient recycling
  - Waste reduction
  - Diversified products and income
  - Environmental sustainability

## Aquaculture Production Systems

### Pond Culture

**Earthen Ponds**:
- Low-cost construction
- Natural productivity (phytoplankton, zooplankton)
- Fertilization to enhance natural food
- Aeration for oxygen management
- Challenges: Predators, disease, water quality variability

### Flow-Through Systems

**Raceways**:
- Continuous water flow from spring, river, or well
- High density possible with good water quality
- High water use
- Temperature-dependent (cold water for trout)

### Net Pen and Cage Culture

**Marine Net Pens**:
- Salmon, cobia, pompano, yellowtail
- Coastal locations with good water quality and currents
- Concerns: Escapes, disease, waste accumulation

**Freshwater Cages**:
- In reservoirs, lakes
- Tilapia, catfish, trout

### Recirculating Aquaculture Systems (RAS)

- See detailed coverage under Finfish Farming section above

### Biofloc Technology (BFT)

**Zero-Exchange Systems**:
- Minimal water use
- Heterotrophic bacteria dominate (not nitrifying bacteria)
- Carbon:Nitrogen ratio management (add molasses, sugar)
- Aeration and mixing critical
- Used for shrimp, tilapia

## Feed and Nutrition

### Aquaculture Feed

**Feed Ingredients**:
- **Protein sources**:
  - Fishmeal (anchovy, menhaden, herring) - declining use
  - Fish protein concentrate
  - Soy protein concentrate
  - Insect protein (black soldier fly larvae)
  - Single-cell proteins (yeast, bacteria)
  - Rendered animal proteins (poultry by-product meal)
- **Lipids**:
  - Fish oil (EPA, DHA omega-3s)
  - Algal oil (alternative omega-3 source)
  - Vegetable oils (canola, soy)
- **Carbohydrates**: Wheat, corn (for binding and energy)
- **Vitamins and minerals**: Premixes

**Feed Formulation**:
- Species-specific nutrient requirements
- Protein: 28% (tilapia) to 50% (salmon, shrimp)
- Lipid: 5-25%
- Digestibility and water stability
- Floating vs. sinking pellets

**Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR)**:
- Feed required per unit of fish weight gain
- Salmon: 1.2-1.4
- Trout: 1.1-1.3
- Tilapia: 1.5-1.8
- Shrimp: 1.4-1.8
- Catfish: 1.6-2.0

**Sustainable Feed Development**:
- Reducing fishmeal and fish oil dependency
- Plant-based and insect-based proteins
- Functional feeds (immune support, disease resistance)

## Health Management

### Common Diseases

**Bacterial Diseases**:
- Columnaris (Flavobacterium columnare) - catfish, tilapia
- Streptococcus - tilapia, trout
- Vibriosis - marine fish, shrimp
- Edwardsiellosis - catfish, tilapia
- Furunculosis (Aeromonas) - trout, salmon

**Viral Diseases**:
- Infectious Salmon Anemia (ISA)
- Infectious Hematopoietic Necrosis (IHN) - salmon, trout
- Viral Hemorrhagic Septicemia (VHS)
- White Spot Syndrome Virus (WSSV) - shrimp

**Parasitic Diseases**:
- Ichthyophthirius (Ich) - freshwater fish
- Sea lice (Lepeophtheirus salmonis) - salmon
- Myxosporean parasites

**Fungal Diseases**:
- Saprolegnia - water mold

### Biosecurity

**Disease Prevention**:
- All-in/all-out stocking
- Disinfection between crops
- Quarantine new stocks
- Water filtration and sterilization (UV, ozone)
- Certified disease-free stocks (SPF)
- Vaccination programs

**Treatments**:
- Antibiotics (FDA-approved for aquaculture: oxytetracycline, florfenicol, Aquaflor)
- Antiparasitic drugs (formalin, hydrogen peroxide)
- Probiotics and immunostimulants
- Vaccination (salmon, trout, tilapia)

### Water Quality Management

**Critical Parameters**:
- **Dissolved oxygen**: >5 mg/L (critical for survival)
  - Aeration, pure oxygen injection
- **Ammonia (NH₃)**: Toxic at >0.02 mg/L (un-ionized)
  - Biofiltration, water exchange
- **Nitrite (NO₂⁻)**: Toxic at >0.5 mg/L
  - Salt (chloride) addition reduces toxicity
- **pH**: 6.5-9.0 (species-dependent)
- **Alkalinity**: >50 mg/L (buffering capacity)
- **Temperature**: Species-dependent, affects metabolism and oxygen requirements
- **Salinity**: Freshwater, brackish, or marine (species-specific)

**Monitoring**:
- Continuous oxygen monitoring
- Daily water quality testing
- Automated alarm systems

## Sustainability and Environmental Considerations

### Environmental Impacts

**Nutrient Loading**:
- Nitrogen and phosphorus from feed and waste
- Eutrophication in receiving waters
- Mitigation: IMTA, settling ponds, constructed wetlands

**Escapes**:
- Genetic interactions with wild fish
- Disease transmission
- Competition for resources
- Mitigation: Stronger nets, inspections, sterile fish

**Disease Transmission**:
- Pathogens spreading to wild populations
- Mitigation: Biosecurity, vaccination, siting away from wild fish migration routes

**Habitat Impacts**:
- Benthic impacts below net pens
- Mangrove destruction (shrimp farming)
- Mitigation: Site rotation, fallow periods, inland systems

### Certification and Eco-Labels

**Aquaculture Stewardship Council (ASC)**:
- Third-party certification
- Species-specific standards (salmon, shrimp, tilapia, etc.)

**Best Aquaculture Practices (BAP)**:
- Global Aquaculture Alliance certification
- Hatchery, farm, feed mill, processing plant standards

**Organic Certification**:
- USDA Organic standards for aquaculture
- Organic feed requirements

## Economic and Market Trends

### U.S. Seafood Consumption

- **Per capita consumption**: 19+ lbs/year
- **Top seafood**: Shrimp, salmon, tuna, tilapia, cod
- **Import dependency**: 90% of seafood consumed is imported

### Aquaculture Growth Drivers

- **Declining wild fisheries**: Overfishing, stock depletion
- **Demand for seafood**: Health benefits (omega-3s, protein)
- **Population growth**: Protein demand increasing
- **Technology**: RAS and offshore systems enabling expansion

### Challenges

- **Regulatory complexity**: Federal, state, local permits
  - USDA, NOAA, EPA, Army Corps of Engineers
- **Competition from imports**: Low-cost production abroad
- **Capital intensity**: High startup costs for RAS and offshore systems
- **Feed costs**: Major operating expense (50-70% of costs)
- **Market access**: Consumer perceptions, traceability

### Opportunities

- **Domestic production**: Reduce imports, improve food security
- **RAS near urban markets**: Freshness, reduced transportation
- **Offshore aquaculture**: Expansion into federal waters (NOAA initiatives)
- **Species diversification**: High-value species (sablefish, yellowtail, barramundi)
- **Value-added products**: Smoked, marinated, ready-to-eat

## Innovations and Future Directions

### Genomics and Selective Breeding

- **Selective breeding programs**: Improved growth, disease resistance
- **Genomic selection**: DNA markers for desirable traits
- **Gene editing (CRISPR)**: Disease resistance, sterility, faster growth
- **Triploid fish**: Sterile (prevent reproduction), faster growth

### Alternative Feeds

- **Insect protein**: Black soldier fly larvae, mealworms
- **Single-cell proteins**: Yeast, bacteria from methane or hydrogen
- **Algae and microalgae**: Omega-3 production, protein source
- **Food waste conversion**: Circular economy approach

### Offshore and Open-Ocean Aquaculture

- **Submersible pens**: Withstand storms and currents
- **Spar platforms**: Deep-water structures
- **Federal waters expansion**: Beyond 3-mile state waters
- **Species**: Sablefish, yellowtail, cobia, moi

### Automation and Sensors

- **Automated feeding systems**: Feed optimization, reduce waste
- **Underwater cameras**: Behavior monitoring, biomass estimation
- **Sensors**: Real-time water quality, fish health monitoring
- **AI and machine learning**: Predictive analytics, disease detection

### Cellular Aquaculture (Cultured Seafood)

- **Lab-grown seafood**: Cell culture technology
- **Companies**: BlueNalu, Finless Foods, Wildtype
- **Regulatory pathway**: FDA oversight
- **Potential**: Sustainability, no fishing or farming needed
