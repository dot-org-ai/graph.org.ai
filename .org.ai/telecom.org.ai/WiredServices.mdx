# Wired Services

Wired telecommunications services encompass fixed-line voice, broadband internet, and data services delivered through physical network infrastructure including fiber optic cables, coaxial cable, copper telephone lines, and enterprise Ethernet networks. This sector provides the high-capacity backbone that supports residential broadband, business connectivity, and internet infrastructure.

## Overview

Wired telecommunications infrastructure forms the foundation of modern digital connectivity, providing the high-bandwidth, low-latency connections essential for cloud computing, video streaming, remote work, and digital transformation. The industry is undergoing massive transformation as fiber optic networks replace legacy copper infrastructure, cable companies upgrade to DOCSIS 4.0, and enterprise networks migrate to software-defined architectures.

**NAICS Code**: 517110 - Wired Telecommunications Carriers

**Market Size**: Global fixed broadband revenue exceeds $400 billion annually, with fiber deployments accelerating worldwide.

## Subsectors (6-digit NAICS)

### 517110 - Wired Telecommunications Carriers

**Incumbent Local Exchange Carriers (ILECs)**

Traditional telephone companies providing legacy voice and modern broadband:

- **Regional Bell Operating Companies (RBOCs)**: AT&T, Verizon, Lumen (formerly CenturyLink)
- **Independent LECs**: Frontier, Windstream, Consolidated Communications
- **Services**: POTS (plain old telephone service), DSL, fiber internet, enterprise data
- **Last-Mile Infrastructure**: Extensive copper plant being selectively upgraded to fiber

**Cable Operators**

MSOs (Multiple System Operators) providing broadband via hybrid fiber-coaxial (HFC) networks:

- **Major MSOs**: Comcast, Charter, Cox, Altice, Mediacom
- **Services**: Cable internet, video, voice (triple play)
- **Technology**: DOCSIS 3.1 (up to 10 Gbps), DOCSIS 4.0 deployment beginning
- **Network Architecture**: Fiber backbone with coax distribution to homes

**Fiber Overbuilders**

Pure-play fiber providers building competitive fiber-to-the-home (FTTH) networks:

- **Municipal Fiber**: City-owned fiber networks (Chattanooga EPB, Longmont NextLight)
- **Electric Cooperatives**: Rural electric coops adding fiber service
- **Independent Fiber**: Google Fiber, Ting, Sonic, MetroNet
- **Wholesale Fiber**: Dark fiber and carrier-neutral infrastructure providers

**Competitive Local Exchange Carriers (CLECs)**

Facilities-based and resale competitors to ILECs:

- **Facilities-Based**: Own infrastructure in select markets
- **UNE-Based**: Lease unbundled network elements from ILECs
- **Services**: Business voice and data, wholesale services
- **Focus**: Enterprise and commercial customers primarily

**Enterprise Network Providers**

Carriers focused on business connectivity and managed services:

- **Tier 1 Providers**: AT&T, Verizon Business, Lumen, GTT
- **Regional Providers**: Zayo, Lightpath, Granite, Windstream Enterprise
- **Services**: MPLS, Ethernet, wavelength, SD-WAN, cloud connectivity
- **Global Reach**: International private line and data networks

## Network Technologies

### Fiber Optic Networks

**Fiber-to-the-Home (FTTH) / Fiber-to-the-Premises (FTTP)**

Direct fiber connections to individual buildings:

- **PON (Passive Optical Network)**: Point-to-multipoint architecture
  - **GPON (Gigabit PON)**: 2.5 Gbps downstream, 1.25 Gbps upstream, up to 32 subscribers per PON
  - **XG-PON**: 10 Gbps downstream, 2.5 Gbps upstream
  - **XGS-PON**: Symmetrical 10 Gbps, most common for new deployments
  - **NG-PON2**: Wavelength division multiplexing, 40+ Gbps aggregate
  - **25G/50G PON**: Next generation for 2025+ deployments

- **Active Ethernet**: Point-to-point or switched Ethernet
  - Dedicated fiber per subscriber
  - Higher cost but greater flexibility and bandwidth
  - Common in MDU (multi-dwelling unit) deployments

- **Architecture Components**:
  - **OLT (Optical Line Terminal)**: Central office aggregation equipment
  - **Splitters**: Passive optical splitters (1:4, 1:8, 1:16, 1:32)
  - **ONT/ONU**: Optical network terminal at customer premises
  - **ODN (Optical Distribution Network)**: Fiber cables and splitters

**Fiber-to-the-Node/Curb (FTTN/FTTC)**

Fiber to neighborhood cabinets with copper or coax to homes:

- **VDSL2**: Very-high-bit-rate DSL over short copper loops
- **G.fast**: Ultra-high-speed DSL (up to 1 Gbps over <100m copper)
- **Use Case**: Incremental upgrade path for legacy infrastructure
- **Limitations**: Speed degrades with distance from node

**Metro and Long-Haul Fiber**

High-capacity backbone and intercity networks:

- **DWDM (Dense Wavelength Division Multiplexing)**:
  - 40, 80, 96+ wavelengths per fiber
  - 100 Gbps, 200 Gbps, 400 Gbps per wavelength
  - Total capacity: Terabits per second per fiber

- **OTN (Optical Transport Network)**:
  - Digital wrapper for wavelengths
  - ODU (Optical Data Unit) framing
  - Forward error correction (FEC)
  - Protection switching and restoration

- **Submarine Cables**:
  - Transoceanic fiber systems
  - Branching units for multiple landing points
  - Erbium-doped fiber amplifiers (EDFA) for regeneration
  - 99.999% availability requirements

### Cable Networks (HFC)

**Hybrid Fiber-Coaxial (HFC) Architecture**

Fiber backbone with coaxial distribution:

- **Headend**: Video and internet service origination
- **Fiber Nodes**: Optical-to-electrical conversion, serving 100-500 homes
- **Coax Distribution**: RG-6 or RG-11 coax to homes
- **Node Segmentation**: Splitting large nodes to reduce shared bandwidth

**DOCSIS (Data Over Cable Service Interface Specification)**

Cable modem technology evolution:

- **DOCSIS 3.1** (current generation):
  - Up to 10 Gbps downstream, 1-2 Gbps upstream
  - OFDM/OFDMA modulation
  - Active Queue Management (AQM) for low latency
  - 96% of US cable subscribers have access

- **DOCSIS 4.0** (deploying 2024+):
  - Full Duplex DOCSIS (FDX): Symmetrical multi-gigabit
  - Extended Spectrum DOCSIS (ESD): Using spectrum up to 1.8 GHz
  - 10 Gbps+ symmetrical capabilities
  - Incremental upgrade from DOCSIS 3.1

**Distributed Access Architecture (DAA)**

Pushing functions deeper into HFC networks:

- **Remote PHY**: Moving physical layer to fiber nodes
- **Remote MACPHY**: Distributed MAC and PHY processing
- **Benefits**: Simplified headend, improved scalability, lower latency
- **Node+0**: Fiber directly to amplifiers, eliminating cascaded amplifiers

### DSL (Digital Subscriber Line)

**Legacy Copper-Based Broadband**

Internet service over telephone lines:

- **ADSL2+**: Up to 24 Mbps downstream, 1-3 Mbps upstream
- **VDSL2**: Up to 100 Mbps over short loops (<500m)
- **G.fast**: 500 Mbps to 1 Gbps over very short copper (<100m)
- **Bonding**: Combining multiple copper pairs for higher speeds
- **Vectoring**: Crosstalk cancellation for improved performance

**Limitations and Sunset**

- Distance-dependent speeds (degrades beyond 3-5km)
- Cannot compete with cable and fiber speeds
- Many carriers phasing out DSL investments
- Copper plant maintenance increasingly expensive
- Regulatory obligations keeping DSL in service in rural areas

### Enterprise Networking

**Ethernet Services**

Dedicated high-speed business connectivity:

- **Ethernet Private Line (EPL)**: Point-to-point dedicated bandwidth
- **Ethernet Virtual Private Line (EVPL)**: Point-to-point with VLANs
- **E-LAN (Ethernet LAN)**: Multipoint-to-multipoint any-to-any
- **E-Tree**: Point-to-multipoint hub-and-spoke
- **Speeds**: 10 Mbps to 100 Gbps, typically 100 Mbps to 10 Gbps for enterprise

**MPLS (Multiprotocol Label Switching)**

Layer 3 VPN technology for enterprise WANs:

- **Label-Based Forwarding**: Fast packet switching through provider network
- **Traffic Engineering**: Explicit path control and QoS
- **VPN Services**: Layer 3 VPN (IP), Layer 2 VPN (Ethernet)
- **Use Cases**: Branch connectivity, data center interconnect
- **Being Displaced**: SD-WAN replacing MPLS for many use cases

**Dark Fiber**

Unlit fiber infrastructure leased to customers:

- **Flexibility**: Customer controls transmission equipment and protocols
- **Unlimited Bandwidth**: Can upgrade speeds by changing optics
- **Cost Model**: Fixed monthly fee regardless of bandwidth used
- **Use Cases**: Data center interconnects, campus networks, research networks

**Wavelength Services**

Dedicated optical wavelengths on shared fiber infrastructure:

- **Lambda Services**: Customer owns a dedicated wavelength
- **Speeds**: Typically 10 Gbps, 100 Gbps, 400 Gbps
- **Protocols**: Support any Layer 1/2 protocol (Ethernet, Fibre Channel, etc.)
- **Protected Services**: Diverse routing and automatic failover

**SD-WAN (Software-Defined WAN)**

Application-aware, policy-based wide area networking:

- **Hybrid Connectivity**: Combine MPLS, internet, LTE, 5G
- **Application Steering**: Route traffic based on application and performance
- **Zero-Touch Deployment**: Automated branch configuration
- **Security Integration**: Integrated firewall, VPN, threat protection
- **Major Vendors**: Cisco (Viptela), VMware (VeloCloud), Fortinet, Palo Alto

## Service Offerings

### Residential Services

**Internet Access**

Broadband connectivity for home use:

- **Fiber Internet**:
  - Symmetrical speeds: 100/100 Mbps to 10/10 Gbps
  - Low latency (<10ms)
  - Highly reliable
  - Pricing: $50-150/month depending on speed

- **Cable Internet**:
  - Asymmetric speeds: 100-1000+ Mbps down, 10-50 Mbps up
  - Shared bandwidth in neighborhood
  - Widely available in urban/suburban areas
  - Pricing: $40-100/month depending on speed

- **DSL**:
  - Legacy service: 10-100 Mbps depending on distance
  - Declining availability as carriers exit DSL
  - Lower cost: $30-60/month
  - Often bundled with phone service

**Voice Services**

Traditional and VoIP telephone service:

- **POTS (Plain Old Telephone Service)**: Analog phone service over copper
- **Digital Voice**: VoIP delivered over broadband connection
- **Features**: Caller ID, voicemail, call waiting, three-way calling
- **E911**: Enhanced 911 with automatic location
- **Number Portability**: Keep phone number when switching providers

**Video Services**

Multi-channel television and streaming:

- **Linear TV**: Traditional scheduled channel lineup
- **Cloud DVR**: Network-based recording and time-shifting
- **On-Demand**: VOD library of movies and shows
- **IP Video**: IPTV delivered over fiber infrastructure
- **Cord-Cutting**: Shift to streaming services reducing video subscribers

**Bundled Services (Triple Play/Quad Play)**

- **Triple Play**: Internet + video + voice
- **Quad Play**: Add wireless service (cable companies)
- **Discounts**: Bundling typically 20-30% cheaper than individual services
- **Simplified Billing**: Single bill for all services

### Business Services

**Dedicated Internet Access (DIA)**

Symmetrical, guaranteed-bandwidth internet connectivity:

- **Speeds**: 10 Mbps to 10 Gbps (typical: 100 Mbps to 1 Gbps)
- **SLA (Service Level Agreement)**: 99.9%+ uptime guarantee
- **Dedicated Bandwidth**: Not shared with other customers
- **Static IP Addresses**: Typically 5-13 IP addresses included
- **Low Latency**: <10ms to internet backbone
- **Pricing**: $200-5000+/month depending on speed and location

**MPLS and Private Networks**

Site-to-site connectivity for multi-location businesses:

- **MPLS VPN**: Layer 3 any-to-any connectivity
- **Point-to-Point**: Dedicated circuits between two locations
- **Managed Services**: Carrier manages routing and troubleshooting
- **Class of Service (CoS)**: QoS for voice, video, and data prioritization
- **Pricing**: $300-3000+/month per site depending on bandwidth

**Cloud Connectivity**

Direct connections to public cloud providers:

- **AWS Direct Connect**: Private connection to Amazon Web Services
- **Azure ExpressRoute**: Dedicated Microsoft Azure connectivity
- **Google Cloud Interconnect**: Direct access to Google Cloud Platform
- **Oracle FastConnect**: Oracle Cloud dedicated connectivity
- **Benefits**: Lower latency, improved security, potentially lower egress costs

**Unified Communications (UC)**

Integrated voice, video, and collaboration:

- **Hosted PBX**: Cloud-based phone systems
- **SIP Trunking**: VoIP connectivity to on-premises PBX
- **UC Platforms**: Microsoft Teams, Cisco Webex, Zoom Phone
- **Features**: Auto-attendant, call routing, voicemail-to-email, mobile apps
- **Pricing**: $20-40/user/month for hosted PBX

**Managed Services**

Comprehensive IT infrastructure management:

- **Managed Router/Firewall**: Carrier manages customer premises equipment
- **Security Services**: Firewall, IDS/IPS, DDoS protection
- **Managed Wi-Fi**: Wireless access point deployment and management
- **SD-WAN**: Managed software-defined networking
- **NOC/SOC**: 24x7 monitoring and incident response

### Wholesale Services

**Carrier Ethernet**

High-capacity Ethernet services for carrier and enterprise customers:

- **Network-to-Network Interfaces (NNI)**: Carrier interconnection
- **E-Line Services**: Point-to-point Ethernet private line
- **E-LAN Services**: Multipoint Ethernet connectivity
- **Speeds**: 1 Gbps to 100 Gbps typically

**Dark Fiber IRU (Indefeasible Right of Use)**

Long-term fiber lease agreements:

- **20-30 Year Terms**: Effectively permanent fiber rights
- **Route Diversity**: Geographically diverse paths for redundancy
- **Colocation**: Access to carrier-neutral data centers
- **Use Cases**: Mobile backhaul, data center interconnect, content delivery

**Transit and Peering**

Internet backbone connectivity:

- **IP Transit**: Full internet routing table and connectivity
- **Peering**: Direct interconnection between networks
- **Pricing**: Per-Mbps or per-Gbps commit with 95th percentile billing
- **Internet Exchanges**: Public peering at IXPs (DE-CIX, AMS-IX, etc.)

## Network Architecture

### Access Network

**Outside Plant (OSP)**

Physical infrastructure from central office to customer:

- **Aerial Plant**: Fiber/copper on utility poles
- **Underground Plant**: Buried conduit and handholes
- **Splicing**: Fiber fusion splicing in splice enclosures
- **Distribution**: Fiber distribution hubs (FDH) and terminals (FDT)

**Central Office (CO) / Point of Presence (POP)**

Carrier facilities housing network equipment:

- **OLT Chassis**: GPON/XGS-PON optical line terminals
- **BRAS/BNG**: Broadband Remote Access Server for subscriber authentication
- **Routers and Switches**: IP routing and Ethernet switching
- **Voice Switches**: Class 5 switches for POTS service (legacy)
- **Power Systems**: Battery backup (4-8 hours) and generator

### Aggregation Network

**Metro Ethernet**

Layer 2 aggregation within metropolitan areas:

- **Carrier Ethernet 2.0**: MEF (Metro Ethernet Forum) standards
- **Provider Backbone Bridge (PBB)**: MAC-in-MAC encapsulation for scalability
- **VPLS (Virtual Private LAN Service)**: Multipoint layer 2 VPN
- **G.8032 (ERPS)**: Ethernet ring protection for rapid failover

**IP Aggregation**

Layer 3 routing and aggregation:

- **PE (Provider Edge) Routers**: Customer-facing routers
- **P (Provider) Routers**: Core transit routers
- **Route Reflectors**: BGP scaling for large networks
- **MPLS Core**: Label switching for traffic engineering and VPNs

### Core Network

**Backbone Infrastructure**

Long-haul and intercity connectivity:

- **DWDM Systems**: Dense wavelength division multiplexing
- **OTN**: Optical transport network for management and protection
- **Core Routers**: Terabit-scale IP routers (Cisco CRS, Juniper PTX)
- **Redundancy**: N+1 or 1+1 protection, diverse routing

**Internet Peering and Transit**

External connectivity:

- **Tier 1 Networks**: Settlement-free global peering
- **Regional Peering**: Peering at internet exchanges
- **Content Peering**: Direct interconnection with CDNs (Netflix, Google, etc.)
- **DDoS Mitigation**: Scrubbing centers for attack mitigation

## Business Models and Economics

### Revenue Streams

**Residential ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)**

- **Broadband-Only**: $50-80/month (fiber/cable), $40-60/month (DSL)
- **Triple Play Bundle**: $100-150/month
- **Video Cord-Cutting Impact**: Declining video subscribers reducing bundle revenue
- **Speed Tier Migration**: Upselling to higher speeds increasing ARPU

**Business Revenue**

- **Small Business**: $100-500/month (internet and phone)
- **Mid-Market**: $1K-10K/month (dedicated internet, MPLS, cloud connectivity)
- **Enterprise**: $50K-1M+/month (national networks, wavelengths, managed services)
- **Wholesale**: $10K-100K+/month per high-capacity circuit

**Churn and Customer Lifetime Value**

- **Residential Churn**: 1-2% per month
- **Business Churn**: 0.5-1% per month (lower due to contracts)
- **Customer Lifetime**: 3-5 years residential, 5-10 years business
- **Acquisition Cost**: $200-400 residential, $1K-5K business

### Capital Expenditure (CapEx)

**Fiber Deployment Costs**

- **Aerial Fiber**: $10K-30K per mile (using existing poles)
- **Underground Fiber**: $50K-150K per mile (trenching and boring)
- **FTTH Construction**: $500-1500 per home passed
- **MDU (Multi-Dwelling Unit)**: $200-500 per unit (shared infrastructure)

**Network Equipment**

- **OLT Port Costs**: $50-150 per GPON port
- **Core Routers**: $500K-2M+ per chassis
- **DWDM Systems**: $50K-500K+ per node depending on capacity
- **Customer Premises Equipment**: $50-300 per ONT/modem

**ROI Considerations**

- **Take Rate**: 30-50% of homes passed subscribe to fiber
- **Payback Period**: 7-12 years typical for FTTH deployment
- **Funding Sources**: Internal cash, debt, government subsidies
- **Operating Leverage**: High fixed costs, low marginal cost per subscriber

### Operating Expenditure (OpEx)

**Network Operations**

- **Transport and Backhaul**: Wavelength and dark fiber leases
- **Facilities**: Central office leases, power, cooling
- **Maintenance**: Field technicians, repair trucks, spare parts
- **Customer Installations**: Truck rolls for new connects and service calls

**Customer Operations**

- **Call Centers**: Customer service and technical support
- **Billing Systems**: OSS/BSS software licenses and maintenance
- **Bad Debt**: 1-3% of revenue typically
- **Marketing**: Customer acquisition campaigns

## Technology Trends

### Fiber Expansion and Densification

**FTTH Acceleration**

- Government broadband funding (US Infrastructure Bill: $42B+)
- Electric cooperatives expanding into fiber
- Municipal and community fiber networks
- Incumbent telcos expanding FTTH footprints (AT&T Fiber, Verizon Fios)

**PON Evolution**

- XGS-PON (10G symmetrical) becoming standard
- 25G and 50G PON standards ratified for future deployment
- Coherent PON research for 100G+ per wavelength
- Wavelength tunable optics for flexible capacity allocation

### Network Virtualization and Disaggregation

**Virtual BNG (vBNG)**

Virtualized broadband network gateway replacing hardware BRAS:

- **Cloud-Native Architecture**: Containerized network functions
- **Scalability**: Elastic scaling based on subscriber demand
- **Automation**: Zero-touch provisioning and service activation
- **Orchestration**: Kubernetes-based lifecycle management

**Disaggregated Cell Site Gateway (DCSG)**

Open, disaggregated mobile backhaul infrastructure:

- **White Box Hardware**: Merchant silicon-based switches and routers
- **Open Source Software**: FRR, DANOS, SONiC network operating systems
- **Cost Reduction**: Lower CapEx than proprietary solutions
- **Flexibility**: Faster innovation and feature velocity

**Access Transformation**

- Software-defined access networks (SDAN)
- Network disaggregation (hardware and software separation)
- Open optical and packet transport (Open ROADM, Transponder API)
- Intent-based automation and orchestration

### Convergence and Multi-Gig Services

**10G Platform**

Cable industry initiative for multi-gigabit services:

- **DOCSIS 4.0**: 10 Gbps symmetrical over HFC
- **Fiber Deep**: Reducing coax cascade and node sizes
- **Wi-Fi 6E/7**: Multi-gigabit home wireless
- **Low Latency**: <10ms for gaming and real-time applications

**XGS-PON Deployments**

10 Gbps symmetrical fiber access becoming standard for new builds:

- **Future-Proof**: Excess capacity for 10+ years
- **Service Flexibility**: Support for residential, business, mobile backhaul
- **Wavelength Stacking**: Additional wavelengths for capacity expansion
- **Cost Parity**: XGS-PON approaching GPON equipment costs

### Edge Computing and Network APIs

**Telco Edge Cloud**

Distributed computing at network edge:

- **MEC (Multi-Access Edge Computing)**: Ultra-low latency compute at aggregation sites
- **Use Cases**: Cloud gaming, AR/VR, AI inference, IoT processing
- **Integration**: Tight coupling with network for QoS and session awareness
- **Partners**: Hyperscalers (AWS Wavelength, Azure Edge Zones, Google Anthos)

**Network-as-a-Service (NaaS)**

API-driven network provisioning and management:

- **Self-Service Portals**: Customer-initiated provisioning and changes
- **Dynamic Bandwidth**: On-demand capacity adjustments
- **Programmable Networks**: API integration with cloud and applications
- **Usage-Based Billing**: Pay-per-use models for elasticity

## Industry Challenges

### Infrastructure Competition

**Overbuilding and Market Saturation**

- Multiple fiber providers competing in same neighborhoods
- Municipal fiber challenging incumbents
- Fixed wireless access (5G/LTE) providing alternative to wired
- Difficulty achieving sustainable market share in competitive markets

**Universal Service and Rural Broadband**

- High cost to serve low-density rural areas
- Government subsidies required for economic viability (RDOF, BEAD, CAF)
- Fixed wireless and satellite emerging as cost-effective alternatives
- Regulatory obligations requiring service to unprofitable areas

### Technology Transitions

**Copper Network Retirement**

- Aging copper plant requiring expensive maintenance
- POTS sunset reducing phone service revenue
- Regulatory hurdles for discontinuing legacy services
- Last-mile copper to fiber migration over 10-20 year timeframe

**Cable Network Evolution**

- DOCSIS 4.0 upgrades requiring node splits and amplifier replacement
- Fiber deep initiatives pushing fiber closer to customers
- Competition from FTTH providers with superior upload speeds
- Balancing investment in HFC upgrades vs. fiber overbuilds

### Market Dynamics

**Cord-Cutting Acceleration**

- Video subscriber losses of 5-10% annually
- Streaming services cannibalizing linear TV
- Broadband becoming standalone service for many customers
- Reduced bundle revenue and ARPU pressure

**Enterprise SD-WAN Adoption**

- SD-WAN replacing MPLS for branch connectivity
- Commoditization of internet access vs. managed MPLS
- Shift from CapEx (owned circuits) to OpEx (managed services)
- Need for value-added services beyond basic connectivity

### Regulatory and Policy

**Net Neutrality**

- Debates over paid prioritization and traffic management
- State-level regulations creating patchwork compliance burden
- Impact on network investment incentives

**Broadband Regulation**

- Proposals to classify broadband as Title II common carrier service
- Universal service contribution obligations
- Open access requirements and infrastructure sharing
- Privacy regulations and data usage transparency

## Future Outlook

**Residential 10G Services**

- Fiber and cable delivering 10 Gbps+ to homes by late 2020s

**Symmetrical Bandwidth**
