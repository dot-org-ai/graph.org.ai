# Crop Production (NAICS 111)

Crop production encompasses the cultivation of plants for food, fiber, and other uses. This sector includes establishments primarily engaged in growing crops, plants, vines, or trees and their seeds.

## Overview

The global crop production industry:
- **Market size**: $3+ trillion globally
- **U.S. cropland**: 390+ million acres
- **Primary crops**: Corn, soybeans, wheat, cotton, rice, vegetables, fruits
- **Employment**: Millions of agricultural workers worldwide
- **Yield improvement**: Continuous innovation in genetics, agronomy, and technology
- **Climate impact**: Agriculture accounts for 10-12% of global greenhouse gas emissions
- **Food security**: Critical for feeding global population of 8+ billion

Key trends:
- Precision agriculture and data-driven farming
- Climate-smart agriculture practices
- Genetic engineering and CRISPR gene editing
- Sustainable intensification
- Water use efficiency and irrigation technology
- Integrated pest management
- Carbon farming and sequestration

## NAICS 111 Subsectors

### 1111 - Oilseed and Grain Farming

Production of crops for oil extraction and grain products.

#### 11111 - Soybean Farming ($50+ billion U.S.)

**Soybeans (Glycine max)** - 120M+ tons/year (U.S.)
- **Uses**:
  - Soybean oil (cooking oil, biodiesel)
  - Soybean meal (animal feed, protein source)
  - Food products (tofu, soy milk, edamame)
  - Industrial applications (plastics, lubricants)
- **Major producers**: U.S., Brazil, Argentina (90% of global production)
- **Planting**: Late April to early June (U.S. Midwest)
- **Harvest**: September to November
- **Yield**: 50-60 bushels/acre (U.S. average)
- **Rotation**: Often rotated with corn for nitrogen fixation
- **GMO adoption**: 94% of U.S. soybeans (herbicide-tolerant)
- **Exports**: China is largest importer (60% of global trade)

**Agronomic practices**:
- Row spacing: 7.5-30 inches
- Seeding rate: 100,000-200,000 seeds/acre
- Nitrogen fixation: Symbiotic relationship with Bradyrhizobium
- Weed control: Pre-emergence and post-emergence herbicides
- Disease management: Sudden death syndrome, soybean cyst nematode, white mold
- Irrigation: Mostly rainfed, supplemental irrigation in drought years

#### 11112 - Oilseed (except Soybean) Farming

**Canola/Rapeseed** - 20M+ tons/year (global)
- High-quality vegetable oil (low saturated fat)
- Meal for livestock feed
- Biodiesel feedstock
- Canada is leading producer
- Spring and winter varieties

**Sunflower** - 50M+ tons/year (global)
- Oil-type and confection varieties
- Russia, Ukraine, EU are major producers
- Drought-tolerant crop

**Peanuts (Groundnuts)** - 2.5M tons/year (U.S.)
- Legume crop grown for oil and direct consumption
- Rotation benefits (nitrogen fixation)
- Georgia, Alabama, Florida are top U.S. states

**Other oilseeds**: Cottonseed, flaxseed, safflower

#### 11115 - Corn Farming ($70+ billion U.S.)

**Corn (Maize, Zea mays)** - 380M+ tons/year (U.S.)
- **Uses**:
  - Animal feed (40% of U.S. production)
  - Ethanol (40% of U.S. production)
  - Food products (corn starch, corn syrup, sweeteners)
  - Exports (10% of U.S. production)
  - Industrial products (biodegradable plastics)
- **U.S. Corn Belt**: Iowa, Illinois, Nebraska, Minnesota, Indiana
- **Planting**: April to early June
- **Harvest**: September to November
- **Yield**: 170-180 bushels/acre (U.S. average, increasing)
- **GMO adoption**: 92% (insect-resistant Bt, herbicide-tolerant)
- **Hybrids**: F1 hybrids dominate (heterosis/hybrid vigor)

**Agronomic practices**:
- Population density: 30,000-38,000 plants/acre
- Row spacing: 30 inches standard, narrow rows (15-20") increasing
- Nitrogen requirements: 150-200 lbs N/acre
- Irrigation: Common in Western states, expanding in Eastern states
- Pest management:
  - European corn borer (Bt trait)
  - Corn rootworm (Bt trait, crop rotation)
  - Fall armyworm
- Disease management: Gray leaf spot, northern corn leaf blight, Goss's wilt

**Corn production innovations**:
- High-density planting for increased yield
- Variable rate seeding based on soil productivity zones
- Split application of nitrogen (side-dressing, in-season)
- Fungicide application at tasseling (VT stage) for disease control
- Hybrid selection based on maturity, disease package, stress tolerance

#### 11116 - Rice Farming

**Rice (Oryza sativa)** - 10M tons/year (U.S.)
- Arkansas, California, Louisiana, Missouri, Texas
- Flooded paddy production system
- Long-grain, medium-grain, short-grain varieties
- 50% exported (major global supplier)
- Water-intensive crop (1,000+ gallons per pound)

#### 11119 - Other Grain Farming

**Wheat** - 50M tons/year (U.S.)
- Winter wheat (planted fall, harvested early summer)
- Spring wheat (planted spring, harvested fall)
- Durum wheat (pasta production)
- Kansas, North Dakota, Montana are top producers
- Classes: Hard red winter, hard red spring, soft red winter, white wheat

**Barley** - 3.5M tons/year (U.S.)
- Malting barley (beer production)
- Feed barley (livestock)
- Montana, Idaho, North Dakota

**Sorghum (Milo)** - 10M tons/year (U.S.)
- Drought-tolerant grain crop
- Animal feed, ethanol production
- Kansas, Texas are top producers

**Oats, Rye, Millet**: Smaller acreage specialty grains

## Crop Production Practices

### Soil Management

**Soil Health Principles**:
- Minimize soil disturbance (reduced tillage, no-till)
- Maximize soil cover (cover crops, residue retention)
- Maximize biodiversity (crop rotation, intercrops)
- Maintain living roots (cover crops, perennials)
- Integrate livestock (grazing, manure application)

**Tillage Systems**:
- **Conventional tillage**: Moldboard plow, disking
- **Reduced tillage**: Chisel plow, field cultivator
- **No-till**: Direct seeding into residue
- **Strip-till**: Tillage only in seed row

**Cover Crops**:
- Winter cover crops (rye, wheat, vetch, radishes)
- Prevent erosion, suppress weeds, improve soil structure
- Nitrogen fixation (legumes)
- Scavenge nutrients, prevent leaching

### Nutrient Management

**The 4Rs of Nutrient Stewardship**:
1. **Right Source**: Match fertilizer type to crop needs
2. **Right Rate**: Apply appropriate amount (soil testing)
3. **Right Time**: Apply when crop needs nutrients
4. **Right Place**: Position nutrients where roots access

**Soil Testing**:
- pH, organic matter, macro and micronutrients
- Recommendations for lime and fertilizer
- Variable rate application based on soil maps

**Nitrogen Management**:
- Soil tests, plant tissue tests
- Side-dress application, split applications
- Nitrification inhibitors (reduce N loss)
- Legume rotation for biological N fixation

**Phosphorus and Potassium**:
- Band application with starter fertilizer
- Broadcast application based on soil test

### Water Management

**Irrigation Systems**:
- **Center pivot**: Large-scale, automated, corn/soybeans
- **Drip irrigation**: High-value crops (vegetables, fruits, nuts)
  - Water use efficiency 90%+
  - Fertigation (fertilizer through irrigation)
- **Furrow irrigation**: Traditional, less efficient
- **Micro-sprinkler**: Orchards, vineyards
- **Subsurface drip**: Permanent installation

**Water Conservation**:
- Soil moisture sensors
- Evapotranspiration (ET) based scheduling
- Deficit irrigation strategies
- Rainwater harvesting
- Aquifer recharge and management

### Pest Management

**Integrated Pest Management (IPM)**:
- Scouting and monitoring
- Economic thresholds
- Cultural practices (rotation, resistant varieties)
- Biological control (beneficial insects, pathogens)
- Chemical control (targeted, least toxic)

**Weed Management**:
- Pre-emergence herbicides (residual)
- Post-emergence herbicides (contact, systemic)
- Mechanical cultivation
- Cover crops for weed suppression
- Herbicide resistance management (rotation, mixtures)

**Insect Management**:
- Bt crops (corn, cotton, soybeans)
- Insecticide seed treatments (neonicotinoids)
- Foliar insecticides (pyrethroids, diamides)
- Biological control (parasitoid wasps, predatory beetles)

**Disease Management**:
- Resistant varieties
- Crop rotation
- Fungicide application (preventative, curative)
- Seed treatments

### Harvest and Post-Harvest

**Harvest Methods**:
- **Combine harvest**: Grains, oilseeds (threshing and separating)
- **Mechanical harvest**: Cotton pickers, tomato harvesters
- **Hand harvest**: High-value fruits and vegetables
- **Timing**: Physiological maturity, moisture content

**Grain Drying and Storage**:
- Natural air drying
- High-temperature drying
- Aeration systems
- Moisture monitoring (prevent mold, spoilage)
- Pest control (fumigation, temperature management)

**Cold Storage**:
- Fruits and vegetables (extend shelf life)
- Controlled atmosphere (CA) storage (apples)
- Temperature and humidity control

## Precision Agriculture Technologies

### Data Collection and Sensing

**Satellite Imagery**:
- Multispectral and hyperspectral imaging
- Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI)
- Crop health monitoring, biomass estimation

**Drone (UAV) Imagery**:
- High-resolution field imagery
- Early disease and pest detection
- Variable rate application mapping

**Soil Sensors**:
- Electrical conductivity (EC) mapping
- Soil moisture sensors
- Nutrient sensors (N, P, K)

**Yield Monitors**:
- Combine-mounted sensors
- Real-time yield mapping
- Harvest data for future planning

### Variable Rate Technology (VRT)

**Variable Rate Seeding**:
- Adjust planting population based on soil productivity
- Higher populations in high-yield zones

**Variable Rate Fertilization**:
- Apply nutrients based on soil test maps
- Zone management or grid sampling

**Variable Rate Spraying**:
- Herbicide application based on weed maps
- Reduce chemical use, target problem areas

### Farm Management Software

**Data Platforms**:
- John Deere Operations Center
- Climate FieldView (Bayer)
- AgLeader SMS
- Integration of equipment, imagery, soil data

**Decision Support**:
- Planting recommendations
- Nitrogen recommendations
- Pest and disease forecasting

## Climate and Sustainability

### Climate-Smart Agriculture

**Mitigation Strategies**:
- Reduced tillage and no-till (carbon sequestration)
- Cover crops (reduce emissions)
- Precision nitrogen management (reduce N₂O emissions)
- Renewable energy (solar, wind on farms)
- Biofuel production (ethanol, biodiesel)

**Adaptation Strategies**:
- Drought-tolerant crop varieties
- Heat-tolerant varieties
- Diversified cropping systems
- Irrigation expansion
- Crop insurance and risk management

### Carbon Farming

**Carbon Sequestration**:
- Soil organic carbon storage
- Conservation tillage practices
- Cover crops and perennial crops
- Agroforestry

**Carbon Credits**:
- Voluntary carbon markets
- Nori, Indigo Ag carbon programs
- Ecosystem services payments

### Water Quality and Conservation

**Best Management Practices (BMPs)**:
- Nutrient management plans
- Buffer strips along waterways
- Constructed wetlands
- Tile drainage management (controlled drainage)
- Precision application to reduce runoff
