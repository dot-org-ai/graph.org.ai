# Animal Production (NAICS 112)

Animal production encompasses the raising of animals for food, fiber, and other products. This sector includes establishments primarily engaged in raising livestock, producing dairy products, operating feedlots, and hatching poultry.

## Overview

The global animal production industry:
- **Market size**: $1.5+ trillion globally
- **U.S. livestock inventory**: 94M cattle, 75M hogs, 9B+ chickens
- **Protein production**: Beef, pork, poultry, eggs, dairy products
- **Employment**: Millions employed in animal agriculture
- **Feed consumption**: 60% of U.S. corn and soybean production
- **Land use**: Grazing land and confined animal feeding operations
- **Exports**: Major U.S. export sector (beef, pork, poultry, dairy)

Key trends:
- Consolidation and vertical integration
- Animal welfare and housing reforms
- Antibiotic-free and organic production
- Alternative proteins and cellular agriculture
- Precision livestock farming technologies
- Sustainability and environmental stewardship
- Traceability and food safety systems

## NAICS 112 Subsectors

### 1121 - Cattle Ranching and Farming

#### 11211 - Beef Cattle Ranching and Farming ($80+ billion U.S.)

**U.S. Beef Cattle Inventory**: 94 million head
- **Cow-calf operations**: 730,000+ operations
  - Breeding herds (beef cows and bulls)
  - Calving season (spring or fall)
  - Weaning at 6-8 months (500-600 lbs)
  - Pasture-based, extensive grazing
- **Stocker/backgrounding**:
  - Growing phase (600-900 lbs)
  - Grazing wheat pasture, crop residues
  - Preconditioning for feedlot
- **Feedlot finishing**:
  - High-energy grain diet (corn, soybean meal)
  - 120-180 days on feed
  - Finished weight: 1,200-1,400 lbs
  - Fed cattle marketed to packers

**Major Cattle States**:
- Texas (5.0M+ head)
- Oklahoma (2.2M head)
- Missouri, Nebraska, South Dakota, Kansas

**Beef Breeds**:
- Angus (most popular, marbling genetics)
- Hereford, Simmental, Charolais
- Brahman (heat tolerance)
- Crossbreeding for hybrid vigor

**Cow-Calf Management**:
- Breeding season: Natural service or AI (artificial insemination)
- Calving management: Assistance, colostrum intake
- Vaccinations: Clostridial diseases, respiratory diseases
- Parasite control: Dewormers, fly control
- Mineral supplementation: Salt, trace minerals
- Estrous synchronization for timed AI

**Grazing Management**:
- Rotational grazing systems
- Stocking rate optimization
- Forage species: Tall fescue, bermudagrass, switchgrass
- Hay production for winter feeding
- Water access and fencing infrastructure

**Feedlot Operations**:
- Capacity: 1,000 to 100,000+ head
- High Plains region (Kansas, Nebraska, Texas, Colorado)
- Ration formulation: Corn, distillers grains, silage, hay
- Growth implants: Hormones for improved feed efficiency
- Feed additives: Ionophores (Rumensin, Bovatec)
- Pen management: Morbidity monitoring, treatment protocols

**Beef Quality and Grading**:
- USDA quality grades: Prime, Choice, Select
- Marbling score (intramuscular fat)
- Yield grades (cutability)
- Age verification (A-maturity)
- Branded beef programs (Certified Angus Beef)

**Market Channels**:
- Live cattle futures (CME)
- Cash cattle negotiations
- Formula pricing (grid pricing based on quality)
- Four major packers (Tyson, JBS, Cargill, National Beef)

#### 11212 - Dairy Cattle and Milk Production ($40+ billion U.S.)

**U.S. Dairy Herd**: 9.4 million cows
- **Milk production**: 226 billion lbs/year
- **Average per cow**: 24,000 lbs/year (increasing)
- **Major dairy states**:
  - California (1.7M cows, largest producer)
  - Wisconsin (1.3M cows, "America's Dairyland")
  - Idaho, New York, Texas, Michigan, Pennsylvania

**Dairy Breeds**:
- **Holstein**: 90%+ of U.S. dairy cows (high milk volume)
- **Jersey**: High butterfat and protein content
- **Brown Swiss, Guernsey, Ayrshire**: Specialty breeds

**Dairy Farm Systems**:
- **Conventional tie-stall/stanchion barns**: Smaller herds, older facilities
- **Freestall barns**: Cows free to move, individual lying stalls
  - Sand or mattress bedding
  - Concrete alleys (scraped or flushed)
- **Compost-bedded pack barns**: Open bedded area, composting manure
- **Pasture-based**: Grazing dairies (organic, smaller scale)
- **Robotic milking**: Automated milking systems (AMS)
  - Cows enter voluntarily, milked by robot
  - 2.5-3 milkings/day
  - Increasing adoption in Europe and U.S.

**Dairy Nutrition**:
- Total Mixed Ration (TMR): Forage, grain, protein, minerals mixed
- Forage base: Corn silage, alfalfa hay/haylage, grass
- Concentrate: Corn, soybean meal, cottonseed, distillers grains
- Ration formulation software: Maximize milk production, minimize cost
- Feed additives: Bypass fat, yeast, buffers

**Dairy Cow Management**:
- **Lactation cycle**: 305-day lactation, 60-day dry period
- **Breeding**: AI with sexed semen (heifer calves for replacements)
  - Genomic selection (sires with high Predicted Transmitting Ability)
- **Calving**: Year-round or seasonal calving
  - Colostrum feeding critical (passive immunity)
  - Calf hutches or group housing
- **Milking routine**: 2x or 3x daily
  - Milking parlor: Herringbone, parallel, rotary
  - Milking equipment: Vacuum system, pulsators, milk meters
  - Milk quality: Somatic cell count (SCC), bacteria count
  - Cooling: Bulk tank refrigeration (38-40°F)
  - Daily milk pickup by tanker trucks

**Dairy Cow Health**:
- **Mastitis**: Udder infection (Staph aureus, Strep, coliforms)
  - Hygiene, teat dipping, dry cow therapy
  - Somatic cell count monitoring
- **Lameness**: Claw health, hoof trimming
- **Metabolic diseases**: Ketosis, milk fever, displaced abomasum
- **Reproductive efficiency**: Heat detection, timed AI protocols
  - Pregnancy rate, calving interval targets

**Milk Processing and Marketing**:
- Cooperative milk marketing (Land O'Lakes, Dairy Farmers of America)
- Federal Milk Marketing Orders (pricing system)
- Fluid milk, cheese, butter, yogurt, ice cream
- Milk protein concentrate, whey protein

**Dairy Farm Sizes**:
- Small farms (<100 cows): Declining
- Medium farms (100-500 cows): Stable
- Large farms (500-5,000 cows): Growing
- Mega-dairies (5,000+ cows): California, Texas, New Mexico

### 1122 - Hog and Pig Farming

#### 11221 - Hog and Pig Farming ($25+ billion U.S.)

**U.S. Hog Inventory**: 75 million head
- **Major pork states**:
  - Iowa (23M head, largest producer)
  - North Carolina, Minnesota, Illinois, Indiana, Nebraska
- **Production cycles**:
  - Farrow-to-finish (complete cycle)
  - Segregated (farrow-to-wean, nursery, finishing)
  - Wean-to-finish operations

**Swine Breeds**:
- Yorkshire, Landrace, Duroc, Hampshire, Berkshire
- Crossbreeding programs for heterosis
- Genetic companies: PIC, Genesus, Hypor, Fast Genetics

**Swine Production Phases**:

**1. Breeding/Gestation**:
- Sow farms with breeding herds
- Artificial insemination standard practice
- Gestation period: 114 days (3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days)
- Group housing systems (gestation stalls being phased out)
- Farrowing crates for birthing and lactation

**2. Farrowing and Lactation**:
- Litter size: 10-14 piglets born alive
- Colostrum intake critical
- Piglet processing: Ear notching, castration, tail docking, iron injection
- Lactation length: 21-28 days
- Sows bred post-weaning (5-7 days)
- Target: 2.4-2.5 litters/sow/year

**3. Nursery (Wean-to-Feeder)**:
- 12-15 lbs at weaning to 50-60 lbs
- 6-8 weeks duration
- Specialized nutrition (milk replacers, highly digestible)
- Climate control (warm, dry environment)

**4. Finishing (Grow-Finish)**:
- 50-60 lbs to market weight (280-300 lbs)
- 16-20 weeks duration
- Corn-soybean meal diet (feed conversion ratio 2.8-3.0)
- Large confinement barns (1,000-2,500 head per barn)

**Pork Quality and Marketing**:
- Live weight: 280-300 lbs
- Dressing percentage: 74-75%
- Carcass grading: Lean percentage, loin depth
- Packer premiums: Lean, consistent quality
- Major packers: Smithfield (WH Group), Tyson, JBS, Hormel
- Lean Hog Futures (CME)

**Swine Health Management**:
- Biosecurity: Shower-in/shower-out, downtime, sanitation
- Disease prevention: PRRS, PED, swine influenza, circovirus
- Vaccination programs
- Antibiotic use reduction initiatives
- All-in/all-out production flows

**Environmental Management**:
- Lagoon and spray field systems (waste management)
- Covered lagoons with biogas capture
- Solid separation and composting
- Nutrient management plans

**Industry Structure**:
- Vertical integration (Smithfield Foods)
- Contract production (integrator owns pigs, grower provides labor and facilities)
- Independent farrow-to-finish operations declining

### 1123 - Poultry and Egg Production

#### 11231 - Chicken Egg Production ($10+ billion U.S.)

**U.S. Layer Inventory**: 380 million laying hens
- **Egg production**: 110+ billion eggs/year
- **Per capita consumption**: 290+ eggs/year
- **Major egg states**: Iowa, Ohio, Indiana, Pennsylvania, Texas

**Egg Production Systems**:
- **Conventional cage** (battery cage): 67-76 sq in/bird
  - Historically dominant system
  - Being phased out in many states
- **Enriched colony cages** (furnished cages): 116+ sq in/bird
  - Nest boxes, perches, scratch area
  - EU standard, increasing in U.S.
- **Cage-free** (barn systems):
  - Floor housing with litter, perches, nest boxes
  - 1-1.5 sq ft/bird indoor space
  - Increasing market demand
- **Free-range**: Outdoor access required
- **Pasture-raised**: Significant outdoor space
- **Organic**: Organic feed, outdoor access, no antibiotics

**Layer Breeds**:
- White Leghorn (white eggs, most common)
- Rhode Island Red, Plymouth Rock (brown eggs)
- Specialty breeds for blue/green eggs (Araucana, Ameraucana)

**Layer Management**:
- Pullet rearing: 0-16 weeks
- Point of lay: 18-20 weeks
- Peak production: 24-28 weeks
- Laying cycle: 60-80 weeks
- Molting (induced or natural) for second cycle
- Spent hen disposal/processing

**Egg Grading and Marketing**:
- USDA grades: AA, A, B
- Size: Jumbo, Extra Large, Large, Medium, Small
- Candling for interior quality
- Automated collection, washing, grading, packaging
- Shell eggs vs. breaking stock (liquid eggs)

#### 11232 - Broiler Chicken Production ($30+ billion U.S.)

**U.S. Broiler Production**: 9+ billion birds/year
- **Live weight production**: 60+ billion lbs/year
- **Major broiler states**:
  - Georgia, Arkansas, Alabama, North Carolina, Mississippi
  - Vertically integrated production

**Broiler Production Cycle**:
- Grow-out period: 42-56 days
- Market weight: 6.0-6.5 lbs live weight
- Feed conversion ratio: 1.7-1.9 (highly efficient)
- 5.5-6.5 flocks/year per house

**Broiler Breeds**:
- Cornish Cross hybrids (fast growth, breast meat)
- Genetics companies: Cobb-Vantress, Aviagen (Ross), Hubbard

**Broiler Housing**:
- Long, narrow barns (40 ft x 400-600 ft)
- 20,000-30,000 birds per house
- Climate control: Tunnel ventilation, evaporative cooling
- Automatic feeders and drinkers
- Litter floor: Wood shavings, rice hulls

**Broiler Nutrition**:
- Starter, grower, finisher rations
- Corn-soybean meal base
- High protein (18-24% protein depending on phase)
- No hormones (prohibited in poultry)
- Coccidiostats (anticoccidial drugs)
- Antibiotic-free production growing (NAE - No Antibiotics Ever)

**Processing**:
- Live haul to processing plant
- Stunning, slaughter, defeathering, evisceration
- Air chilling or water chilling
- Cut-up (parts) or whole bird
- Further processing (nuggets, tenders, patties)
- Major integrators: Tyson, Pilgrim's Pride, Perdue, Sanderson Farms (now Wayne Farms)

**Vertical Integration**:
- Integrator owns breeder flocks, hatcheries, feed mills, processing plants
- Contract growers raise birds (grower provides housing and labor)
- Tournament system compensation

#### 11233 - Turkey Production

**U.S. Turkey Production**: 220+ million birds/year
- **Major turkey states**: Minnesota, North Carolina, Arkansas, Indiana, Missouri
- **Seasonal demand**: Thanksgiving, Christmas
- **Live weight**: 28-40 lbs (toms), 14-16 lbs (hens)
- **Grow-out**: 14-18 weeks

**Turkey Breeds**:
- Broad Breasted White (99% of commercial production)
- Heritage breeds: Bourbon Red, Narragansett (small-scale)

#### 11234 - Poultry Hatcheries

**Hatchery Operations**:
- Egg collection from breeder flocks
- Incubation: 21 days (chickens), 28 days (turkeys)
- Temperature and humidity control
- Automated turning
- Hatching and chick processing (sexing, vaccination)
- Transport to farms

### 1124 - Sheep and Goat Farming

#### 11241 - Sheep Farming

**U.S. Sheep Inventory**: 5.2 million head
- **Lamb and mutton production**: Small compared to beef, pork, poultry
- **Wool production**: 24 million lbs/year (declining)
- **Major sheep states**: Texas, California, Wyoming, Colorado, Utah

**Sheep Production Systems**:
- Range sheep operations (Western states)
- Farm flocks (Midwest, Eastern states)
- Lambing season: Spring or fall
- Market lambs: 120-150 lbs, 6-8 months old

**Sheep Breeds**:
- Meat breeds: Suffolk, Hampshire, Dorset, Texel
- Wool breeds: Merino, Rambouillet
- Hair sheep: Katahdin, Dorper (low maintenance, no shearing)

#### 11242 - Goat Farming

**U.S. Goat Inventory**: 2.7 million head
- **Meat goats** (Boer, Kiko, Spanish)
  - Growing ethnic market demand
  - Chevon/cabrito production
- **Dairy goats** (Nubian, Saanen, Alpine, LaMancha)
  - Artisan cheese production
  - Goat milk yogurt, ice cream
- **Fiber goats** (Angora for mohair, Cashmere)

## Feed and Nutrition

### Animal Feed Industry

**U.S. Feed Production**: 280+ million tons/year
- Cattle feed, swine feed, poultry feed
- Pet food (separate but related industry)

**Feed Ingredients**:
- **Energy sources**: Corn, sorghum, wheat, barley
- **Protein sources**: Soybean meal, canola meal, distillers grains
- **Forages**: Alfalfa hay, grass hay, corn silage
- **By-products**: Distillers grains, wheat midds, soybean hulls, cottonseed meal
- **Minerals**: Limestone, salt, trace mineral premixes
- **Vitamins**: A, D, E, B-complex

**Feed Formulation**:
- Least-cost formulation software
- Nutrient requirements (NRC - National Research Council)
- Digestibility and bioavailability
- Palatability and intake

**Feed Additives**:
- **Growth promoters**: Ionophores, beta-agonists (ractopamine)
- **Antibiotics**: Sub-therapeutic use (being phased out)
  - Veterinary Feed Directive (VFD) regulations
- **Enzymes**: Phytase (phosphorus release), xylanase
- **Probiotics and prebiotics**: Gut health

### Grazing and Pasture Management

**Forage Species**:
- Cool-season grasses: Tall fescue, orchardgrass, timothy, bromegrass
- Warm-season grasses: Bermudagrass, switchgrass, big bluestem
- Legumes: Alfalfa, clover, birdsfoot trefoil

**Grazing Systems**:
- Continuous grazing
- Rotational grazing (paddock rotation)
- Intensive rotational grazing (management-intensive grazing)
- Strip grazing

**Pasture Improvement**:
- Fertilization (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium)
- Overseeding
- Weed control
- Irrigation

## Animal Health and Welfare

### Disease Management

**Biosecurity Protocols**:
- Limiting farm access
- Sanitation and disinfection
- Quarantine for new animals
- Pest control (rodents, birds)

**Vaccination Programs**:
- Cattle: IBR, BVD, BRSV, PI3, Clostridial diseases
- Swine: PRRS, Mycoplasma, circovirus, Lawsonia
- Poultry: Marek's disease, Newcastle disease, infectious bronchitis

**Parasite Control**:
- Cattle: Internal parasites (roundworms), external (flies, lice, ticks)
- Sheep: Gastrointestinal nematodes, liver flukes
- Strategic deworming, rotational grazing

### Animal Welfare Standards

**Industry Guidelines**:
- National Pork Board Animal Care Program
- National Chicken Council Animal Welfare Guidelines
- Beef Quality Assurance (BQA)
- United Egg Producers Certified Program

**Housing Reforms**:
- Gestation crate phase-out (sows)
- Battery cage phase-out (layers)
- Enriched housing environments
- California Proposition 12 (farm animal confinement standards)

**Handling and Transport**:
- Low-stress handling techniques (Temple Grandin principles)
- Transport regulations (28-hour law)
- Humane slaughter (Humane Methods of Slaughter Act)

**Third-Party Audits**:
- Global Animal Partnership (GAP) - 5-Step Rating
- Certified Humane
- Animal Welfare Approved

## Environmental Management

### Manure Management

**Manure Production**:
- Dairy cow: 120 lbs/day
- Beef cattle: 50-60 lbs/day
- Hog: 15-20 lbs/day
- Chicken: 0.2 lbs/day

**Manure Systems**:
- **Liquid/Slurry**: Lagoons, storage pits
  - Pumping and land application
  - Irrigation/spray systems
- **Solid**: Composting, stockpiling
  - Spreading with manure spreaders
- **Bedded pack**: Compost barns (aerobic decomposition)

**Nutrient Management**:
- Nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium in manure
- Soil testing and application rates
- Crop nutrient uptake calculations
- Environmental regulations (CAFO permits)

### Greenhouse Gas Emissions

**Methane (CH₄)**:
- Enteric fermentation (ruminants)
- Manure storage
- Mitigation: Feed additives (3-NOP), improved genetics

**Nitrous Oxide (N₂O)**:
- Manure management
- Mitigation: Covered lagoons, anaerobic digestion

**Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)**:
- Energy use in barns, feed production

### Air Quality

**Ammonia (NH₃)**:
- Manure decomposition
- Odor and respiratory irritant

**Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S)**:
- Manure pits and lagoons
- Safety hazard (toxic gas)

**Particulate Matter**:
- Dust from feed, bedding, animal movements

### Anaerobic Digesters

**Biogas Production**:
- Manure as feedstock
- Methane capture for electricity or pipeline injection
- Digestate (effluent) as fertilizer
- Renewable natural gas (RNG) credits
