# Corporate Training

Business schools, computer training, and management education providing professional development and workplace learning (NAICS 6114).

## Overview

Corporate training encompasses organizations delivering business education, computer and technology training, and management development for working professionals and organizations. This includes standalone business schools (non-degree), computer training centers, management consulting education, and corporate learning and development (L&D) functions. The sector focuses on practical skills application, career advancement, and organizational performance improvement.

## NAICS 6114 Breakdown

| Code | Industry | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| **611410** | Business and Secretarial Schools | Management training, business skills, office administration |
| **611420** | Computer Training | IT certification, programming, software training |
| **611430** | Professional and Management Development Training | Leadership, executive education, organizational development |

### Sector Segments

- **Corporate Universities**: Internal training academies (e.g., McDonald's Hamburger University)
- **Business Schools**: Executive education, non-degree management programs
- **Computer Training Centers**: IT certifications, programming bootcamps
- **Management Consulting Firms**: Training as service line (McKinsey, BCG programs)
- **Professional Development Providers**: Project management, leadership, soft skills
- **Industry Associations**: Professional certification and continuing education

## Corporate Learning Landscape

### In-House L&D Functions

#### Corporate Learning & Development Department

Typical organizational structure within medium-to-large enterprises:

- **Chief Learning Officer (CLO)**: Executive leader for talent development
- **Learning Strategy**: Aligning training to business goals and competency models
- **Instructional Design**: Course development, curriculum, assessment design
- **Delivery Team**: Facilitators, trainers, e-learning specialists
- **Learning Technology**: LMS administration, learning experience platform management
- **Evaluation**: Kirkpatrick levels, ROI measurement, analytics

#### Corporate L&D Responsibilities

- **Onboarding**: New hire orientation, role-specific training
- **Compliance Training**: Mandated training (e.g., anti-harassment, data security, safety)
- **Skills Development**: Technical and functional skill building
- **Leadership Development**: Management training, succession planning
- **Sales Enablement**: Product knowledge, selling skills, customer service
- **Change Management**: Training to support organizational transformation
- **Culture and Values**: Reinforcing corporate culture and expected behaviors

### External Training Providers

Organizations often supplement internal L&D with external providers:

#### Executive Education Programs

- **University-Based**: Harvard Business School, Wharton, Stanford GSB, Kellogg, MIT Sloan
- **Program Types**: Open enrollment, custom corporate programs, executive MBA
- **Duration**: 1-week intensives to year-long leadership programs
- **Topics**: Strategy, leadership, finance, innovation, digital transformation

#### Management Consulting Education

- **McKinsey Academy**: Digital learning, leadership, agile transformation
- **BCG U**: Strategy execution, change leadership
- **Deloitte University**: Campus in Texas, client and employee training

#### Professional Training Firms

## Training Delivery Methods

### Instructor-Led Training (ILT)

Traditional classroom-based training:

- **In-Person Workshops**: Multi-day facilitated sessions
- **Conferences and Seminars**: Industry events with learning tracks
- **On-Site Training**: Customized delivery at client location
- **Public Enrollment**: Open courses with participants from multiple companies

### Virtual Instructor-Led Training (VILT)

Live online training sessions:

- **Synchronous**: Real-time interaction with instructor and peers
- **Platforms**: Zoom, WebEx, Teams, Adobe Connect
- **Breakout Rooms**: Small group activities and discussions
- **Polls and Q&A**: Interactive engagement tools
- **Recording**: On-demand access after live session

### E-Learning / Asynchronous

Self-paced digital learning:

- **Video-Based**: Recorded lectures, demonstrations, interviews
- **Interactive Modules**: Branching scenarios, simulations, knowledge checks
- **SCORM Packages**: Standards-compliant content for LMS
- **Microlearning**: 5-10 minute bite-sized lessons
- **Mobile Learning**: Optimized for smartphone access

### Blended Learning

Combination of delivery methods for optimal outcomes:

- **Flipped Classroom**: Pre-work (video, reading) + live application session
- **Cohort-Based**: Group progresses together through multi-week program
- **70-20-10 Model**: 70% on-the-job, 20% social learning, 10% formal training

### On-the-Job Training (OJT)

Learning in the context of work:

- **Job Shadowing**: Observing experienced colleague
- **Mentoring**: One-on-one guidance from senior employee
- **Stretch Assignments**: Projects beyond current role for development
- **Action Learning**: Real business problems with facilitated reflection

### Coaching

Individualized development conversations:

- **Executive Coaching**: One-on-one with certified coach for senior leaders
- **Manager as Coach**: Managers trained to coach direct reports
- **Peer Coaching**: Colleagues supporting each other's development
- **Internal Coaching**: Certified coaches on staff

## Instructional Design for Corporate Training

### Adult Learning Principles (Andragogy)

Key principles for training adult learners (Malcolm Knowles):

- **Self-Direction**: Adults want control over their learning
- **Experience**: Leverage prior knowledge and experience
- **Relevance**: Learning must apply to real problems and goals
- **Problem-Centered**: Focus on solving practical challenges
- **Motivation**: Intrinsic motivation (career, satisfaction) drives learning

### ADDIE Model

Systematic instructional design process:

- **Analysis**: Training needs assessment, learner analysis, gap analysis
- **Design**: Learning objectives, assessment strategy, content outline
- **Development**: Create materials, build courses, pilot testing
- **Implementation**: Deliver training, facilitate learning
- **Evaluation**: Measure effectiveness, gather feedback, iterate

### Kirkpatrick's Four Levels of Evaluation

Measuring training effectiveness:

| Level | Measure | Question | Methods |
|-------|---------|----------|---------|
| **1 - Reaction** | Satisfaction | Did they like it? | Smile sheets, surveys |
| **2 - Learning** | Knowledge/Skills | Did they learn? | Tests, assessments, demonstrations |
| **3 - Behavior** | Application | Are they using it? | Observation, 360 feedback, manager assessment |
| **4 - Results** | Business Impact | Did it matter? | KPIs, ROI, business metrics |

### Training Needs Assessment

Identifying training requirements:

- **Organizational Analysis**: Strategic goals, performance gaps, priorities
- **Task Analysis**: Job responsibilities, required competencies, standards
- **Person Analysis**: Individual skill gaps, performance issues, career goals
- **Data Sources**: Performance reviews, surveys, interviews, focus groups

## Compliance and Mandated Training

### Workplace Compliance Training

Required training to meet legal and regulatory obligations:

#### Common Compliance Topics

- **Anti-Harassment**: Sexual harassment prevention, discrimination awareness
- **Diversity, Equity, Inclusion (DEI)**: Unconscious bias, inclusive behaviors
- **Data Security**: Cybersecurity awareness, phishing, password management
- **Code of Conduct**: Ethics, conflicts of interest, gifts and entertainment
- **Anti-Corruption**: FCPA, anti-bribery, international compliance
- **Workplace Safety**: OSHA requirements, emergency procedures, ergonomics
- **Privacy**: GDPR, CCPA, data handling, consent

#### Compliance Training Characteristics

- **Frequency**: Often annual refresh requirements
- **Tracking**: Completion records for audit purposes
- **Acknowledgment**: Signed attestations of understanding
- **Testing**: Knowledge checks to verify comprehension
- **Consequences**: Disciplinary action for non-completion

### Industry-Specific Mandates

- **Healthcare**: HIPAA, infection control, bloodborne pathogens
- **Financial Services**: Anti-money laundering, securities compliance, fiduciary duties
- **Manufacturing**: Safety training (OSHA), hazardous materials (HAZMAT)
- **Transportation**: DOT regulations, hours of service, vehicle inspection

## Market Trends and Challenges

### Current Trends

- **Skills-Based Talent Management**: Competency frameworks, skill adjacencies
- **Reskilling and Upskilling**: Addressing automation and digital transformation
- **Learning in the Flow of Work**: Embedded, just-in-time learning (Microsoft Viva)
- **Personalization**: AI-powered recommendations, adaptive learning paths
- **Microlearning**: Short, focused content for busy professionals
- **Social and Collaborative Learning**: Communities of practice, peer-to-peer
- **VR/AR Training**: Immersive simulations for high-risk or complex tasks

### Challenges

- **Budget Constraints**: L&D often cut during economic downturns
- **Time**: Employees too busy for training, competing priorities
- **Engagement**: Low completion rates, especially for compliance e-learning
- **Transfer**: Learning doesn't translate to on-the-job behavior change
- **Measurement**: Difficulty proving ROI and business impact
- **Technology Adoption**: Resistance to new platforms, change management

### Future Directions

- **AI Tutors and Chatbots**: Personalized coaching, instant answers
- **Adaptive Learning**: Content adjusts to learner proficiency in real-time
- **Skills Inference**: AI identifies skill gaps from work behaviors
- **Learning Experience Platform (LXP) Growth**: Replacing traditional LMS
- **Democratized Content Creation**: Subject matter experts creating courses
- **Blockchain Credentials**: Verified, portable learning records

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

### Activity Metrics

- Training hours per employee per year (industry benchmark: 30-40 hours)
- Percentage of employees completing required training
- Course completion rates
- LMS login frequency and engagement

### Quality Metrics

- Learner satisfaction scores (Level 1)
- Knowledge assessment scores (Level 2)
- Manager-reported behavior change (Level 3)
- Certification pass rates

### Business Impact Metrics

- Time to productivity for new hires
- Performance improvement post-training
- Employee engagement and retention rates
- Revenue per employee (productivity)
- Safety incident rates (for safety training)

### Financial Metrics

- L&D spend as percentage of payroll (typical: 1-3%)
- Cost per learning hour delivered
- Return on investment (ROI) for major programs
- Internal vs. external training costs
