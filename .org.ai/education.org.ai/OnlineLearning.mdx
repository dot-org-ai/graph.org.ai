# Online Learning

Digital education delivery through online platforms, MOOCs, e-learning systems, and distance education technologies.

## Overview

Online learning encompasses educational experiences delivered primarily or exclusively through digital platforms. This includes fully online degree programs, massive open online courses (MOOCs), corporate e-learning, K-12 virtual schools, and supplemental digital learning tools. The sector has experienced explosive growth driven by technology advancement, accessibility demands, cost pressures, and accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Sector Landscape

### Online Learning Categories

| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Online Degree Programs** | Fully online bachelor's, master's, doctoral degrees from accredited institutions | WGU, ASU Online, SNHU, Purdue Global |
| **MOOCs** | Massive open online courses, free or low-cost, certificate options | Coursera, edX, Udacity, FutureLearn |
| **Corporate E-Learning** | Workplace training and professional development platforms | LinkedIn Learning, Udemy Business, Skillsoft, Pluralsight |
| **K-12 Virtual Schools** | Online schools for K-12 students | K12 Inc, Connections Academy, state virtual schools |
| **Bootcamps** | Intensive skills training in tech, data, design | General Assembly, Flatiron School, Lambda School, Springboard |
| **OPM Partnerships** | Online program management for universities | 2U, Academic Partnerships, Wiley, Noodle, Coursera for Campus |
| **Learning Management Systems** | Platforms hosting course content | Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle, Google Classroom |

### Market Segments

- **Higher Education Online**: Undergraduate and graduate online degrees
- **Professional Certification**: Industry credentials and continuing education
- **K-12 Virtual**: Full-time online schools and supplemental courses
- **Corporate Training**: Employee skill development and compliance
- **Personal Enrichment**: Hobbies, languages, creative skills

## MOOC Platforms

### Major Providers

#### Coursera

- Founded 2012 by Stanford professors
- 100M+ learners, 300+ university partners
- Model: Free courses, paid certificates and degrees
- Offerings: Specializations, professional certificates, online degrees
- Enterprise: Coursera for Business, Coursera for Government

#### edX

- Founded 2012 by MIT and Harvard
- 40M+ learners, acquired by 2U in 2021
- Model: Free courses, paid verified certificates
- MicroMasters, MicroBachelors, online master's degrees
- Open edX platform for self-hosted solutions

#### Udacity

- Founded 2011, initially Stanford CS courses
- Focus: Tech skills (AI, data science, programming, cloud)
- Nanodegrees with project-based learning
- Industry partnerships (Google, AWS, Meta)
- Enterprise upskilling solutions

#### Other Platforms

- **FutureLearn**: UK-based, social learning focus
- **Udemy**: Marketplace model, instructor-created courses (200K+ courses)
- **LinkedIn Learning**: Professional skills, integrated with LinkedIn
- **Skillshare**: Creative skills and entrepreneurship
- **Khan Academy**: Free K-12 and test prep

### MOOC Business Models

- **Freemium**: Free course access, paid certificates and graded assessments
- **Subscription**: Monthly/annual unlimited access (LinkedIn Learning, Skillshare)
- **Marketplace**: Instructor revenue share (Udemy, Teachable)
- **Degree Programs**: Full online degrees at scale with university partners
- **Enterprise**: B2B corporate training and upskilling

## Online Degree Programs

### Institutional Models

#### University Online Divisions

Traditional universities operating online programs:

- **Arizona State University Online**: 200+ degree programs, Starbucks partnership
- **Penn State World Campus**: 150+ programs, established 1998
- **University of Florida Online**: Reduced tuition for in-state online students
- **Georgia Tech OMSCS**: $7,000 master's in computer science via edX

#### Online-First Universities

Institutions designed for online delivery:

- **Western Governors University (WGU)**: Competency-based, flat-rate tuition, 300K+ students
- **Southern New Hampshire University (SNHU)**: 160K+ online students, aggressive growth
- **Purdue Global**: Acquired from Kaplan University, competency-based options
- **University of Phoenix**: For-profit pioneer, declining enrollment

### OPM (Online Program Management) Partners

Universities partner with OPM companies for online program development and marketing:

#### Major OPM Providers

| Company | Model | Market Position |
|---------|-------|-----------------|
| **2U** | Revenue share (60-75%), full-service marketing and tech | Premium programs, acquired edX |
| **Academic Partnerships** | Revenue share, regional focus | 300+ programs, strong in nursing/education |
| **Wiley Education Services** | Revenue share, acquired mStoner | Mid-market universities |
| **Noodle Partners** | Lower revenue share, transparency focus | Alternative to 2U model |
| **Coursera for Campus** | Platform + marketplace model | University + Coursera content access |

#### OPM Services

- Market research and program development
- Student recruitment and lead generation
- Instructional design and faculty training
- Learning platform and technical support
- Student support services
- Performance analytics and optimization

#### Controversies

- High revenue share (50-70% of tuition) reducing institutional margins
- Lead generation practices and aggressive marketing
- Bundling of services limiting institutional control
- Regulatory scrutiny and new regulations (2023 Dear Colleague letter)

#### Higher Education

| Platform | Market Share | Key Features |

| **Canvas (Instructure)** | 30%+ | Modern UI, mobile-first, robust API ecosystem |
| **Blackboard** | 25% | Legacy leader, mature features, Learn Ultra redesign |

#### K-12

- **Canvas**: Growing K-12 adoption
- **Schoology**: Acquired by PowerSchool, K-12 focused features
- **Seesaw**: Elementary grades, parent communication

#### Corporate

- **Cornerstone OnDemand**: Enterprise learning and talent management
- **SAP SuccessFactors**: Integrated with HR systems
- **Docebo**: AI-powered, learning in the flow of work
- **Absorb LMS**: Mid-market focus, user-friendly

### LMS Core Features

- **Content Management**: Upload files, videos, SCORM packages, embed content
- **Assessment**: Quizzes, assignments, rubrics, grading workflows
- **Communication**: Announcements, discussion forums, messaging
- **Gradebook**: Grade calculation, standards-based grading, analytics
- **Integration**: SSO, SIS sync, video conferencing, plagiarism detection
- **Mobile**: Native apps for iOS and Android
- **Accessibility**: WCAG compliance, screen reader support

### LMS Trends

- **Learning Experience Platforms (LXP)**: Netflix-style content discovery (Degreed, EdCast)
- **Competency-Based**: Mastery tracking, personalized pathways
- **AI-Powered**: Adaptive learning, chatbots, automated feedback
- **Analytics**: Predictive models, early alert, engagement tracking
- **Interoperability**: LTI (Learning Tools Interoperability) standards, integration ecosystems

## K-12 Virtual Schools

### Full-Time Online Schools

#### K12 Inc (Stride)

- Largest K-12 online provider, publicly traded
- Operates 100+ schools in 30+ states
- Curriculum, technology platform, teacher support
- Mix of virtual charter schools and private pay options

#### Connections Academy (Pearson)

- Virtual charter and private schools
- 70+ schools in 30 states
- Live online classes, certified teachers
- Parent as learning coach model

#### State Virtual Schools

- **Florida Virtual School (FLVS)**: Largest state virtual school, 200K+ enrollments
- **Michigan Virtual**: Supplemental and full-time options
- **North Carolina Virtual Public School**: Supplemental courses for brick-and-mortar students

### Supplemental Online Courses

- Credit recovery for students behind on graduation
- Advanced courses not offered at local schools
- Flexible scheduling for athletes, performers, working students
- Summer school and acceleration

### K-12 Virtual School Characteristics

- **Enrollment**: Often open enrollment, sometimes lottery
- **Instruction**: Mix of synchronous (live classes) and asynchronous (self-paced)
- **Learning Coach**: Parent role supporting student, especially elementary
- **Curriculum**: Often proprietary, aligned to state standards
- **Accountability**: Subject to state testing and school ratings

## Instructional Design for Online Learning

### Online Course Design Principles

- **Backward Design**: Start with learning outcomes, design assessments, then instruction
- **Chunking**: Break content into manageable modules and lessons
- **Multimedia**: Video, audio, interactive elements, varied formats
- **Active Learning**: Quizzes, discussions, projects, simulations (not just reading)
- **Scaffolding**: Build complexity gradually with support
- **Accessibility**: Captions, transcripts, alt text, screen reader compatibility

### Quality Standards

#### Quality Matters (QM)

- Peer review rubric for online course design
- 8 general standards, 42 specific review standards
- Certification for courses and programs
- Professional development for faculty

#### WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines)

- International standards for web accessibility
- WCAG 2.1 AA compliance typical requirement
- Perceivable, operable, understandable, robust (POUR principles)

### Instructional Design Models

- **ADDIE**: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation
- **SAM (Successive Approximation Model)**: Iterative, agile approach
- **Backwards Design**: Outcomes-focused (Wiggins & McTighe)
- **Community of Inquiry**: Social, cognitive, teaching presence (Garrison)

## Student Success in Online Learning

### Challenges

- **Self-Regulation**: Requires motivation, time management, self-discipline
- **Isolation**: Limited peer interaction and social learning
- **Technology Barriers**: Access to devices, internet, digital literacy
- **Learning Preferences**: Not ideal for hands-on or interpersonal learning styles
- **Persistence**: Higher attrition rates than face-to-face programs

### Success Factors

- **Orientation**: Pre-course readiness assessment and training
- **Clear Expectations**: Syllabi, schedules, grading policies, communication norms
- **Regular Interaction**: Faculty presence, timely feedback, discussion facilitation
- **Peer Connection**: Group projects, peer review, study groups
- **Support Services**: Online tutoring, advising, technical help, mental health
- **Early Alert**: Monitoring engagement and reaching out to struggling students

### Online Student Support

- **Technical Support**: 24/7 help desk for LMS and technology issues
- **Academic Advising**: Virtual appointments, degree planning, transfer credit
- **Tutoring**: Online tutoring platforms (Paper, Tutor.com), embedded tutors
- **Library**: Remote access to databases, research help via chat/video
- **Disability Services**: Accommodations for online assessments and content
- **Career Services**: Resume review, interview prep, job search via video

## Market Dynamics and Business Models

### Pricing Models

| Model | Description | Examples |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Tuition-Based** | Per-credit or per-program pricing | Traditional university online programs |
| **Subscription** | Monthly/annual unlimited access | LinkedIn Learning, Skillshare, MasterClass |
| **Freemium** | Free content with paid upgrades | Coursera, edX, Khan Academy |
| **Marketplace** | Pay-per-course, instructor revenue share | Udemy, Teachable |
| **Competency-Based** | Flat-rate tuition, progress at own pace | WGU, Capella FlexPath |
| **Employer-Paid** | B2B enterprise licenses | Corporate LMS, Udemy Business |

### Regulatory Environment

#### Accreditation for Online Programs

- Same regional accreditation as campus programs
- Additional requirements for distance education
- State authorization for out-of-state students (SARA agreements)
- Programmatic accreditation for professional programs

#### Federal Regulations

- **Title IV Financial Aid**: Regular and substantive interaction requirements
- **State Authorization Reciprocity Agreement (SARA)**: Multi-state authorization
- **Consumer Protection**: Gainful employment, borrower defense, program disclosures

### Market Trends

- **Growth**: Online enrollment growing while overall higher ed enrollment declines
- **Employer-Paid**: Companies investing in upskilling (Amazon, Walmart programs)
- **Micro-Credentials**: Certificates, badges, stackable credentials
- **Competency-Based**: Credit for prior learning, self-paced progression
- **Global Expansion**: International students in online programs, cross-border delivery

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

### Enrollment Metrics

- Online enrollment headcount and FTE
- Online as percentage of total enrollment
- Enrollment by program and modality
- Lead-to-enrollment conversion rate

### Student Success Metrics

- Course completion rates (vs. face-to-face)
- Retention rates (term-to-term, year-to-year)
- Graduation rates for online cohorts
- Time to degree completion

### Engagement Metrics

- LMS login frequency and session duration
- Discussion post participation rates
- Assignment submission timeliness
- Video view completion rates

### Quality Metrics

- Student satisfaction (course evaluations)
- Faculty satisfaction with online teaching
- Quality Matters certification rates
- Accessibility audit scores

### Financial Metrics

- Revenue per online student
- Cost per online completion
- Marketing cost per acquisition (CPA)
- Return on OPM investment (if applicable)
