# Litigation Services

Litigation services encompass legal representation of clients in disputes resolved through court proceedings, arbitration, mediation, and other forms of dispute resolution. Litigators advocate for clients in pre-trial proceedings, trials, appeals, and settlement negotiations across diverse practice areas including commercial litigation, intellectual property disputes, employment litigation, and product liability.

Litigation is adversarial by nature, requiring strategic thinking, advocacy skills, factual investigation, legal research, and persuasive communication. The practice ranges from routine collection matters to bet-the-company cases with billions of dollars at stake.

## Overview

Litigation practice encompasses multiple dispute resolution forums:

**Court Litigation**:
- Federal courts (district courts, courts of appeals, Supreme Court)
- State courts (trial courts, intermediate appellate courts, supreme courts)
- Specialized courts (bankruptcy, tax, claims, family courts)
- Administrative tribunals and agency proceedings

**Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)**:
- Arbitration (binding and non-binding)
- Mediation and facilitated settlement
- Neutral evaluation and early neutral evaluation
- Mini-trials and summary jury trials

**Types of Cases**:
- Commercial and business disputes
- Intellectual property litigation
- Employment and labor disputes
- Products liability and mass torts
- Securities and shareholder litigation
- Antitrust and competition law
- Environmental litigation
- Class actions and multi-district litigation
- White-collar criminal defense

## Litigation Process

### Case Assessment and Strategy

**Initial Evaluation**:
- Facts investigation and witness interviews
- Legal research on claims and defenses
- Damages quantification and analysis
- Likelihood of success assessment
- Forum analysis (federal vs. state, jurisdiction, venue)
- Cost-benefit analysis of litigation vs. settlement

**Strategic Planning**:
- Litigation objectives and success metrics
- Budget and resource allocation
- Discovery strategy and plan
- Motion practice strategy
- Expert witness needs and selection
- Settlement posture and negotiation strategy

**Parties and Jurisdiction**:
- Identifying proper plaintiffs and defendants
- Personal jurisdiction and minimum contacts
- Subject matter jurisdiction (diversity, federal question)
- Venue selection and forum shopping
- Impleader, interpleader, and joinder of parties

### Pleadings and Pre-Trial Motions

**Complaint**:
- Jurisdiction and venue allegations
- Statement of facts (notice pleading vs. fact pleading)
- Legal claims with elements
- Prayer for relief (damages, injunction, declaratory relief)
- Demand for jury trial

**Answer and Defenses**:
- Admissions and denials of allegations
- Affirmative defenses
- Counterclaims against plaintiff
- Cross-claims against co-defendants
- Third-party complaints (impleader)

**Motion Practice**:
- **Motion to Dismiss (Rule 12(b))**: Jurisdiction, venue, service, failure to state claim
- **Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings**: No dispute on facts, movant entitled to judgment
- **Motion to Strike**: Improper allegations or defenses
- **Motion for More Definite Statement**: Complaint too vague to respond

**Amended Pleadings**:
- Amendments as of right (before answer or 21 days after service)
- Amendments by leave of court (good cause required)
- Relation back of amendments to original pleading date

### Discovery

Discovery is the fact-finding phase where parties exchange information:

**Written Discovery**:
- **Interrogatories**: Written questions requiring sworn written answers (limited to 25 in federal court)
- **Requests for Production**: Demands for documents, ESI, and tangible things
- **Requests for Admission**: Statements to be admitted or denied, narrowing disputed facts
- **Subpoenas Duces Tecum**: Third-party document production

**Depositions**:
- Oral examination of witnesses under oath
- Fact witness depositions
- Expert witness depositions (after report exchange)
- 30(b)(6) depositions of corporate representatives
- Deposition used for impeachment at trial

**Physical and Mental Examinations**:
- Independent medical examinations (IME) in personal injury cases
- Mental examinations in appropriate cases (rare)
- Good cause and court order required

**Discovery Disputes**:
- Meet and confer requirements before motion
- Motions to compel discovery responses
- Protective orders for confidential information
- Sanctions for discovery abuse or spoliation
- Privilege logs and assertion of privileges

**Electronic Discovery (eDiscovery)**:
- Identification and preservation of electronically stored information (ESI)
- ESI protocol and search terms negotiation
- Collection, processing, and review of electronic data
- Technology-assisted review (TAR) and predictive coding
- Production format (native, TIFF, PDF) and metadata
- Clawback agreements for inadvertent privilege production

### Motion for Summary Judgment

**Standard**:
- No genuine dispute of material fact
- Movant entitled to judgment as a matter of law
- Court views evidence in light most favorable to non-movant
- Partial summary judgment on particular claims or issues

**Briefing**:
- Movant's motion, statement of undisputed facts, supporting brief
- Non-movant's opposition, dispute of facts, opposing brief
- Movant's reply brief
- Supporting declarations and exhibits (authenticated evidence)

**Hearing and Decision**:
- Oral argument (discretionary in many courts)
- Court's written order and opinion
- Certification for interlocutory appeal (if partial summary judgment)

### Trial Preparation

**Pretrial Conference**:
- Simplify and clarify issues for trial
- Scheduling and trial management
- Settlement discussions
- Pretrial order governing trial conduct

**Motions in Limine**:
- Exclude or admit evidence before trial
- Prevent prejudicial evidence or arguments
- Resolve evidentiary issues in advance
- Strategic advantage by limiting opponent's evidence

**Trial Brief**:
- Statement of facts and legal issues
- Legal arguments on contested issues
- Proposed jury instructions
- Exhibit and witness lists

**Jury Selection (Voir Dire)**:
- Jury panel questioning
- Challenges for cause (bias, relationship to party)
- Peremptory challenges (limited number, no reason required)
- Jury composition and demographics

**Witness Preparation**:
- Review of anticipated testimony
- Preparation for direct and cross-examination
- Exhibits and demonstratives review
- Courtroom demeanor and credibility

**Exhibits and Demonstratives**:
- Authentication and foundation for admission
- Demonstrative exhibits (charts, timelines, animations)
- Technology for presentation (trial software, video)
- Exhibit notebooks and organization

### Trial

**Opening Statements**:
- Roadmap of case and what evidence will show
- Themes and narrative structure
- Not argument but preview of evidence
- Plaintiff/prosecutor goes first, defendant responds

**Plaintiff/Prosecution Case-in-Chief**:
- Presentation of evidence through witnesses
- Direct examination of witnesses
- Introduction of exhibits
- Expert testimony
- Burden of proof (preponderance for civil, beyond reasonable doubt for criminal)

**Defense Case**:
- Motion for judgment as a matter of law (directed verdict)
- Defense witnesses and evidence
- Cross-examination of plaintiff's witnesses
- Challenging credibility and reliability

**Rebuttal and Surrebuttal**:
- Plaintiff's rebuttal evidence
- Defense surrebuttal (discretionary)
- Limited to responding to defense case

**Closing Arguments**:
- Summary of evidence presented
- Argument on facts and law
- Jury instructions and burden of proof
- Plaintiff goes first, defense responds, plaintiff rebuttal

**Jury Instructions and Deliberations**:
- Court's instructions on applicable law
- Verdict forms (general or special verdict)
- Jury deliberations (confidential and sealed)
- Jury questions and supplemental instructions
- Deadlocked jury and Allen charge

**Verdict and Judgment**:
- Jury verdict reading in open court
- Polling of jury (if requested)
- Post-trial motions (judgment as a matter of law, new trial)
- Entry of judgment
- Costs and attorneys' fees (if applicable)

### Post-Trial and Appeals

**Post-Trial Motions**:
- **Judgment as a Matter of Law (JMOL)**: Insufficient evidence for verdict
- **Motion for New Trial**: Errors in trial, verdict against weight of evidence
- **Motion to Alter or Amend Judgment**: Correct errors or reconsider
- **Motion for Attorneys' Fees and Costs**: If authorized by statute or contract

**Appeals**:
- Notice of appeal filing (strict deadlines)
- Record on appeal (transcript, exhibits, docket)
- Appellant's opening brief
- Appellee's response brief
- Appellant's reply brief
- Oral argument before appellate panel
- Appellate decision (affirm, reverse, remand)
- Petition for rehearing or en banc review
- Certiorari petition to higher court

**Standards of Review**:
- **De Novo**: Legal issues reviewed without deference
- **Abuse of Discretion**: Evidentiary and procedural rulings
- **Substantial Evidence**: Factual findings and jury verdicts
- **Plain Error**: Issues not preserved but seriously affecting rights

### Enforcement of Judgments

**Collection**:
- Post-judgment discovery of assets
- Writ of execution on personal property
- Levy on bank accounts and receivables
- Wage garnishment
- Liens on real property
- Fraudulent transfer claims

**Contempt Proceedings**:
- Civil contempt for failure to comply with court orders
- Coercive sanctions to compel compliance
- Compensatory sanctions for damages from non-compliance

## Types of Litigation

### Commercial Litigation

**Contract Disputes**:
- Breach of contract claims
- Interpretation of contract terms
- Damages (expectation, reliance, restitution)
- Specific performance and injunctive relief
- Third-party beneficiary claims

**Business Torts**:
- Fraud and fraudulent inducement
- Tortious interference with contract or business relations
- Unfair competition and trade secrets misappropriation
- Breach of fiduciary duty (partners, officers, directors)
- Unjust enrichment and quantum meruit

**Shareholder and Partnership Disputes**:
- Shareholder derivative actions
- Oppression and freeze-out claims
- Breach of fiduciary duty by controlling shareholders
- Partnership dissolution and accounting
- LLC member disputes

**Bankruptcy Litigation**:
- Adversary proceedings in bankruptcy court
- Preference and fraudulent transfer actions
- Dischargeability of debts
- Plan confirmation disputes
- Creditor claims and objections

### Intellectual Property Litigation

**Patent Litigation**:
- Patent infringement claims (utility, design, plant patents)
- Claim construction (Markman hearings)
- Invalidity defenses (anticipation, obviousness, indefiniteness)
- Damages (lost profits, reasonable royalty, willful infringement)
- Declaratory judgment actions of non-infringement or invalidity

**Trademark Litigation**:
- Trademark infringement and dilution
- False advertising (Lanham Act Section 43(a))
- Counterfeiting and gray market goods
- Trade dress infringement
- Domain name disputes (UDRP, ACPA)

**Copyright Litigation**:
- Copyright infringement claims
- Fair use defense
- DMCA safe harbor for online service providers
- Statutory damages and attorneys' fees
- Registration as prerequisite to suit

**Trade Secrets Litigation**:
- Misappropriation under Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA) and state UTSA
- Ex parte seizure orders for irreparable harm
- Injunctive relief and damages
- Employee mobility and non-compete enforcement

### Employment Litigation

**Discrimination and Harassment**:
- Title VII (race, color, religion, sex, national origin)
- Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA)
- Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)
- State fair employment laws
- Sexual harassment and hostile work environment

**Wage and Hour**:
- Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) collective actions
- Misclassification (employee vs. independent contractor, exempt vs. non-exempt)
- Unpaid overtime and minimum wage
- Meal and rest break violations (state law)
- Off-the-clock work and donning/doffing

**Wrongful Termination**:
- Retaliation for protected activity
- Breach of employment contract or implied covenant
- Public policy violations
- Whistleblower protections
- WARN Act mass layoff notice violations

**Restrictive Covenants**:
- Non-compete agreement enforcement
- Non-solicitation of customers and employees
- Confidentiality and trade secret protection
- Inevitable disclosure doctrine
- Preliminary injunctions and temporary restraining orders

### Products Liability

**Theories of Liability**:
- Strict liability for defective products
- Negligence in design or manufacturing
- Breach of warranty (express and implied)
- Failure to warn and inadequate warnings
- Successor liability and market share liability

**Types of Defects**:
- Manufacturing defects (departure from design)
- Design defects (risk-utility test, consumer expectations test)
- Warning defects (failure to warn of risks)

**Mass Torts and Class Actions**:
- Pharmaceutical and medical device litigation
- Toxic torts (asbestos, environmental contamination)
- Consumer products (automobiles, appliances)
- Coordinated proceedings and multidistrict litigation (MDL)
- Class certification and mass joinder

### Securities Litigation

**Federal Securities Claims**:
- **Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5**: Fraud and misrepresentation in securities transactions
- **Section 11**: Misstatements in registration statements
- **Section 12**: Unlawful sales and prospectus violations
- **Section 14**: Proxy fraud and false proxy statements
- **Section 16(b)**: Short-swing profits by insiders

**Shareholder Class Actions**:
- Securities fraud class actions
- Lead plaintiff selection (Private Securities Litigation Reform Act)
- Loss causation and damages
- Scienter requirement (intent to deceive)
- Settlement and approval process

**Derivative Actions**:
- Shareholder derivative suits for corporate harm
- Demand on board or demand futility
- Special litigation committees
- Settlement and corporate governance reforms

**Enforcement Actions**:
- SEC enforcement proceedings
- Insider trading investigations
- Accounting fraud and financial reporting
- FINRA arbitrations for broker-dealer disputes

### White-Collar Criminal Defense

**Types of Cases**:
- Fraud (wire fraud, mail fraud, bank fraud, securities fraud)
- Embezzlement and theft
- Money laundering and structuring
- Tax evasion and tax fraud
- Bribery and public corruption
- Healthcare fraud and False Claims Act
- Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) violations

**Investigation Phase**:
- Grand jury investigations and subpoenas
- Internal investigations and voluntary disclosure
- Proffer and cooperation agreements
- Asserting Fifth Amendment privilege
- Document production and privilege claims

**Charging and Plea Negotiations**:
- Indictment vs. information
- Arraignment and bail hearing
- Plea bargaining and cooperation
- Deferred prosecution agreements (DPA)
- Non-prosecution agreements (NPA)

**Trial and Sentencing**:
- Criminal trial procedure and constitutional rights
- Jury trial and proof beyond a reasonable doubt
- Federal Sentencing Guidelines
- Downward departures and variances
- Restitution and forfeiture

### Class Actions and Mass Torts

**Class Certification**:
- **Rule 23(a) Requirements**: Numerosity, commonality, typicality, adequacy of representation
- **Rule 23(b) Types**: (b)(1) prejudice, (b)(2) injunctive relief, (b)(3) predominance and superiority
- Class definition and ascertainability
- Notice to class members
- Opt-out rights for (b)(3) classes

**Settlement**:
- Settlement class certification
- Fairness hearing and objections
- Notice and claims administration
- Cy pres awards to charitable organizations
- Attorneys' fees (percentage of fund or lodestar)

**Multidistrict Litigation (MDL)**:
- Transfer to single district for coordinated pretrial proceedings
- Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation (JPML)
- Coordinated discovery and motions
- Bellwether trials and settlement negotiations
- Remand to originating courts for trial

**Mass Tort Aggregation**:
- State court mass tort litigation
- Coordinated proceedings and judicial cooperation
- Common benefit funds for attorneys' fees
- Inventory settlements and matrix resolutions

## Alternative Dispute Resolution

### Arbitration

**Types of Arbitration**:
- Binding vs. non-binding arbitration
- Institutional rules (AAA, JAMS, CPR) vs. ad hoc
- Domestic vs. international arbitration
- Industry-specific arbitration (labor, construction, securities)

**Arbitration Agreements**:
- Enforceability under Federal Arbitration Act (FAA)
- Scope of arbitration clause
- Class action waivers in arbitration
- Unconscionability and adhesion contract challenges
- Delegation clauses (arbitrator decides arbitrability)

**Arbitration Process**:
- Demand for arbitration and selection of arbitrators
- Preliminary hearing and scheduling
- Discovery (limited compared to litigation)
- Hearing and presentation of evidence
- Post-hearing briefs
- Award and confirmation

**Advantages and Disadvantages**:
- **Advantages**: Speed, cost, privacy, expert arbitrators, finality
- **Disadvantages**: Limited discovery, limited appeal rights, potentially high arbitrator fees, perceived bias

### Mediation

**Mediation Process**:
- Selection of mediator (retired judge, experienced attorney, professional mediator)
- Pre-mediation statements and briefs
- Opening joint session with all parties
- Private caucuses with each party
- Shuttle diplomacy and settlement negotiations
- Mediated settlement agreement (binding if signed)

**Mediator's Role**:
- Neutral facilitator, not decision-maker
- Evaluate strengths and weaknesses of each side
- Reality testing and risk assessment
- Propose creative solutions and compromise
- Maintain confidentiality of discussions

**Timing of Mediation**:
- Early mediation before significant costs
- After discovery to assess case
- Mandatory settlement conferences ordered by court
- Mediation clauses in contracts requiring pre-suit mediation

**Success Factors**:
- Party and attorney preparation
- Client attendance with settlement authority
- Good faith participation
- Mediator skill and credibility
- Timing and readiness to settle

## Litigation Economics

### Cost Structures

**Attorney Time**:
- Partner, associate, paralegal hourly rates
- Typical hourly rates: $200-$1,000+ depending on location and firm
- Billable hour requirements (1,700-2,000 hours annually)
- Staffing leverage (partners vs. associates)

**Litigation Costs**:
- Court filing fees
- Service of process
- Deposition transcripts
- Expert witness fees (consultative and testimonial)
- E-discovery costs (collection, processing, hosting, review)
- Trial exhibits and demonstratives
- Travel and accommodation for out-of-town matters
- Jury consultants and mock trials

**Fee Arrangements**:
- **Hourly Billing**: Traditional time-based fees
- **Contingency Fees**: Percentage of recovery (typically 25%-40%)
- **Hybrid Arrangements**: Reduced hourly rate plus success fee
- **Fixed Fees**: Set fee for defined scope (e.g., arbitration, appeal)
- **Defense-Side Contingency**: Fee reduction if result better than benchmark

### Litigation Funding

**Third-Party Litigation Funding**:
- Non-recourse financing for litigation costs
- Funder receives portion of recovery
- Used in commercial litigation, antitrust, patent cases
- Disclosure of funding to opposing party (varies by jurisdiction)
- Ethical considerations and attorney independence

**Insurance Coverage**:
- Directors and officers (D&O) insurance
- Errors and omissions (E&O) / professional liability
- Employment practices liability insurance (EPLI)
- Commercial general liability (CGL)
- Coverage disputes and reservation of rights

**Budgeting and Cost Control**:
- Litigation budgets and cost estimates
- Alternative fee arrangements
- Early case assessment and cost-benefit analysis
- Proportionality in discovery
- Settlement vs. trial economics
