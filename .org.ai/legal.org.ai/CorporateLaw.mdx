# Corporate Law

Corporate law encompasses legal services for businesses including entity formation, governance, commercial transactions, mergers and acquisitions, securities offerings, and regulatory compliance. Corporate lawyers serve as strategic advisors to companies, helping them navigate complex legal and business issues from inception through growth, transactions, and sometimes dissolution.

Corporate law is one of the most significant practice areas by revenue in the legal industry, particularly at large law firms serving public companies and private equity firms. The practice requires deep understanding of business operations, financial structures, regulatory frameworks, and transactional mechanics.

## Overview

Corporate legal practice covers the full lifecycle of business entities:

**Formation and Structure**:
- Entity selection and formation (corporations, LLCs, partnerships)
- Organizational documents (articles of incorporation, bylaws, operating agreements)
- Capitalization and ownership structures
- Foreign entity qualification and registration
- Conversion and restructuring of existing entities

**Ongoing Corporate Governance**:
- Board of directors governance and compliance
- Shareholder rights and meetings
- Officer responsibilities and authority
- Corporate record-keeping and minute books
- Subsidiary governance and intercompany matters

**Commercial Transactions**:
- Contract drafting, negotiation, and review
- Strategic partnerships and joint ventures
- Licensing and distribution agreements
- Supply chain and vendor agreements
- Terms of service and customer agreements

**Regulatory Compliance**:
- Corporate governance standards
- Securities law compliance (public and private companies)
- Industry-specific regulations
- Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) compliance
- Corporate transparency and beneficial ownership reporting

**Strategic Advisory**:
- Business strategy and legal risk assessment
- Transaction structuring and tax efficiency
- Corporate reorganizations and restructurings
- Succession planning for closely-held businesses
- Crisis management and special situations

## Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A)

M&A represents a major component of corporate practice:

### Types of Transactions

**Acquisitions**:
- **Stock Purchase**: Buyer acquires target company's shares
- **Asset Purchase**: Buyer acquires specific assets and assumes selected liabilities
- **Merger**: Combination of two companies into one legal entity
- **Reverse Merger**: Private company merges into public shell company
- **Tender Offer**: Direct offer to shareholders to purchase shares

**Divestitures**:
- Asset sales and carve-outs
- Spin-offs and split-offs
- Equity carve-outs and tracking stocks

**Strategic Transactions**:
- Joint ventures and strategic alliances
- Minority investments and preferred stock deals
- Rollups and consolidation strategies

### Transaction Process

**Pre-Transaction Phase**:
- Strategic planning and target identification
- Preliminary valuation and deal structure
- Confidentiality agreements (NDAs)
- Initial term sheet or letter of intent (LOI)
- Antitrust and regulatory pre-clearance assessment

**Due Diligence**:
- Legal due diligence on target company
- Review of contracts, litigation, compliance, IP, employment, real estate
- Financial and tax due diligence (coordinated with accountants)
- Environmental and regulatory assessments
- Data room management and Q&A process

**Definitive Agreement Negotiation**:
- Purchase agreement drafting and negotiation
- Representations and warranties
- Covenants (pre-closing and post-closing)
- Indemnification provisions and escrows
- Conditions to closing (including regulatory approvals)
- Termination rights and break-up fees

**Regulatory Approvals**:
- Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) antitrust filings
- Foreign investment reviews (CFIUS, EU merger control)
- Industry-specific regulatory approvals
- State-level approvals and consents

**Closing and Post-Closing**:
- Closing conditions satisfaction
- Closing deliverables and documentation
- Funds transfer and escrow arrangements
- Post-closing integration and transition
- Earnout administration and dispute resolution

### Deal Structures

**Cash Transactions**:
- All-cash consideration at closing
- Funded by buyer's cash, debt financing, or equity raise
- Simplest structure with immediate liquidity to sellers

**Stock Transactions**:
- Stock-for-stock exchanges (tax-free reorganizations possible)
- Valuation and exchange ratio negotiations
- Registration rights for buyer's stock
- Lock-up and leak-out provisions

**Mixed Consideration**:
- Combination of cash, stock, and other consideration
- Earnouts based on future performance
- Seller financing and promissory notes
- Contingent value rights (CVRs)

**Leveraged Buyouts (LBOs)**:
- Acquisition financed primarily with debt
- Target's assets and cash flow secure financing
- Private equity sponsored transactions
- Management buyouts (MBOs) and employee buyouts

## Securities Law

Corporate lawyers advise on compliance with securities laws and capital formation:

### Private Offerings

**Regulation D Offerings**:
- **Rule 504**: Up to $10 million offerings to any investors
- **Rule 506(b)**: Unlimited amount to accredited investors (no general solicitation)
- **Rule 506(c)**: Unlimited amount to verified accredited investors (general solicitation allowed)
- Form D filing with SEC and state securities regulators
- Investor accreditation verification

**Regulation A (Mini-IPOs)**:
- **Tier 1**: Up to $20 million offerings (state review required)
- **Tier 2**: Up to $75 million offerings (SEC-only review)
- Offering circular and ongoing reporting requirements
- Testing the waters and general solicitation permitted

**Regulation Crowdfunding**:
- Up to $5 million raised from general public
- Conducted through registered funding portals
- Individual investment limits based on income/net worth
- Ongoing reporting obligations

**Simple Agreement for Future Equity (SAFE)**:
- Convertible security for early-stage companies
- No interest, maturity date, or repayment obligation
- Converts to equity upon qualified financing or exit
- Y Combinator-developed instrument

### Public Offerings

**Initial Public Offering (IPO)**:
- Registration statement (Form S-1) preparation and SEC review
- Underwriter selection and syndicate formation
- Due diligence and disclosure preparation
- Roadshow and investor presentations
- Pricing and allocation
- Listing on stock exchange (NYSE, NASDAQ)

**Follow-On Offerings**:
- Secondary offerings by public companies
- Shelf registrations (Form S-3) for well-known seasoned issuers
- At-the-market (ATM) offerings
- Rights offerings to existing shareholders

**Special Purpose Acquisition Companies (SPACs)**:
- IPO of blank-check company
- Search for and merger with target company (de-SPAC)
- PIPE financing concurrent with business combination
- Shareholder approval and redemption rights

### Public Company Compliance

**Periodic Reporting**:
- Annual reports (Form 10-K)
- Quarterly reports (Form 10-Q)
- Current reports (Form 8-K) for material events
- Proxy statements (Schedule 14A) for shareholder meetings
- Beneficial ownership reporting (Sections 13 and 16)

**Corporate Governance**:
- Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) compliance
- Board independence requirements
- Audit committee and compensation committee charters
- Internal controls over financial reporting (ICFR)
- Disclosure controls and procedures

**Insider Trading Compliance**:
- Trading windows and blackout periods
- Pre-clearance procedures
- Rule 10b5-1 trading plans
- Insider trading policies and training

## Corporate Governance

Legal advice on board and management responsibilities:

### Board of Directors

**Composition and Structure**:
- Board size and director qualifications
- Independent directors and lead director
- Committee structure (audit, compensation, nominating/governance)
- Director elections and term limits
- Board diversity and skills matrix

**Duties and Responsibilities**:
- **Duty of Care**: Informed decision-making and oversight
- **Duty of Loyalty**: Acting in company's best interests, avoiding conflicts
- **Duty of Good Faith**: Honest and lawful conduct
- Reliance on experts and advisors
- Business judgment rule protection

**Board Processes**:
- Meeting frequency and agendas
- Executive sessions of independent directors
- Board materials and information access
- Board evaluations and director education
- Succession planning for CEO and key officers

**Special Situations**:
- Sale of company and Revlon duties (maximizing shareholder value)
- Conflicts of interest and special committees
- Derivative litigation and demand requirements
- Hostile takeover defense and board response

### Stockholder Rights

**Voting Rights**:
- Common stock voting (one share, one vote)
- Preferred stock voting rights
- Dual-class and multi-class structures
- Cumulative voting and class voting

**Information Rights**:
- Inspection of books and records
- Financial statements and reporting
- Shareholder proposals (Rule 14a-8)
- Proxy access and director nominations

**Economic Rights**:
- Dividends and distributions
- Liquidation preferences and priorities
- Preemptive rights and anti-dilution protection
- Appraisal rights in mergers

**Stockholder Activism**:
- Proxy contests and director nominations
- Shareholder proposals on governance and policy
- Engagement with institutional investors
- Activist campaigns and defense strategies
