# Performing Arts

The performing arts sector encompasses live theatrical productions, musical performances, dance, comedy, and other forms of live entertainment. This industry is characterized by creative expression, audience engagement, and the ephemeral nature of live performance.

## Overview

Performing arts represent one of humanity's oldest forms of cultural expression, evolving from ancient rituals to contemporary multimedia experiences. The sector includes:

- **Theater Companies**: Dramatic productions, musicals, experimental theater
- **Music Groups**: Orchestras, bands, chamber ensembles, solo artists
- **Dance Companies**: Ballet, modern, contemporary, cultural dance
- **Comedy**: Stand-up, improv, sketch comedy troupes
- **Opera Companies**: Classical and contemporary opera productions
- **Performance Art**: Experimental and interdisciplinary performances

## Business Models

### Production Companies
Traditional model where organizations produce and present performances:
- **Repertory Companies**: Maintain rotating selection of productions
- **Touring Companies**: Travel to venues across regions/nations
- **Resident Companies**: Perform at dedicated venue(s)
- **Festival Companies**: Seasonal productions and events

**Revenue Streams**:
- Ticket sales (primary income)
- Subscriptions and memberships
- Corporate sponsorships
- Government grants (NEA, state/local arts councils)
- Private donations and philanthropy
- Merchandise sales
- Educational programs and workshops

### Independent Artists
Solo performers and small ensembles:
- **Touring Musicians**: Concert tours, festival appearances
- **Session Musicians**: Recording studio work
- **Street Performers**: Busking and public performances
- **Tribute Acts**: Performances honoring established artists

**Revenue Streams**:
- Performance fees
- Recording royalties (mechanical, performance, sync)
- Merchandise (albums, posters, apparel)
- Teaching and workshops
- Crowdfunding and patronage (Patreon, etc.)
- Licensing and sync deals

### Promoters and Venues
Organizations that present performances but don't produce them:
- **Concert Halls**: Symphony halls, opera houses
- **Theaters**: Broadway, regional theaters, black box venues
- **Arenas and Amphitheaters**: Large-scale concerts and events
- **Clubs and Bars**: Intimate live music venues
- **Festival Organizers**: Multi-day, multi-artist events

**Revenue Streams**:
- Ticket sales (often split with artists)
- Facility rental fees
- Food and beverage sales
- Parking
- Sponsorships
- VIP packages and hospitality

## Industry Practices

### Production Process

#### Pre-Production
1. **Concept Development**: Script selection, artistic vision
2. **Casting**: Auditions, callbacks, negotiations
3. **Design**: Sets, costumes, lighting, sound design
4. **Rehearsals**: Table reads, blocking, technical rehearsals
5. **Marketing**: Publicity, advertising, social media campaigns

#### Production
1. **Preview Performances**: Soft openings for refinement
2. **Opening Night**: Official launch
3. **Run**: Series of performances
4. **Matinees and Special Performances**: Student shows, accessibility performances

#### Post-Production
1. **Strike**: Dismantling sets and equipment
2. **Archive**: Documentation, recordings, press materials
3. **Assessment**: Reviews, audience surveys, financial analysis

### Rights and Licensing

#### Performance Rights
- **Grand Rights**: Full theatrical productions (musicals, operas)
- **Small Rights**: Individual songs, concert performances
- **Mechanical Rights**: Sound recordings
- **Synchronization Rights**: Use in film, TV, advertising

#### Licensing Organizations
- **ASCAP**: American Society of Composers, Authors, and Publishers
- **BMI**: Broadcast Music, Inc.
- **SESAC**: Performance rights organization
- **MTI**: Music Theatre International (theatrical licensing)
- **Samuel French**: Play licensing
- **Dramatists Play Service**: Play licensing

### Union and Guild Representation

#### Major Unions
- **Actors' Equity Association (AEA)**: Stage actors and stage managers
- **American Federation of Musicians (AFM)**: Musicians
- **American Guild of Musical Artists (AGMA)**: Opera singers, dancers
- **Society of Stage Directors and Choreographers (SDC)**: Directors and choreographers
- **International Alliance of Theatrical Stage Employees (IATSE)**: Technical crew

**Union Benefits**:
- Minimum wage standards
- Health insurance and pension
- Safe working conditions
- Overtime and break requirements
- Rehearsal and performance pay structures

## Market Segments

### Broadway and Commercial Theater
- **Broadway**: 41 designated theaters in NYC (500+ seats)
- **Off-Broadway**: 100-499 seats, often more experimental
- **Off-Off-Broadway**: Under 100 seats, developmental work
- **Regional Theater**: Professional theaters outside NYC
- **Community Theater**: Amateur and semi-professional productions

**Economics**:
- Average Broadway musical budget: $10-20M
- Average ticket price: $100-150 (premium: $300+)
- Long runs possible with hit shows (years to decades)
- Recoupment period: 1-3 years for successful shows

### Classical Music
- **Symphony Orchestras**: Major, regional, metropolitan
- **Opera Companies**: Full productions with orchestra
- **Chamber Music**: Small ensembles, intimate venues
- **Early Music**: Period instruments, historical performance
- **Contemporary Classical**: New compositions, avant-garde

**Challenges**:
- Aging audience demographics
- High operating costs (100+ musicians for major orchestras)
- Competition from recorded music
- Need for continuous fundraising
- Educational outreach to develop new audiences

### Popular Music
- **Concert Tours**: Arena tours, club tours, festival circuits
- **Residencies**: Extended runs in single venue (Las Vegas model)
- **Corporate Events**: Private performances, weddings
- **Music Festivals**: Multi-day events (Coachella, Bonnaroo, Lollapalooza)

**Touring Economics**:
- Ticket sales (60-80% of revenue)
- Merchandise (20-30% of revenue)
- Sponsorships and partnerships
- VIP packages and meet-and-greets
- Live album/video recordings

### Dance
- **Ballet Companies**: Classical and neo-classical ballet
- **Modern Dance**: Graham, Cunningham traditions
- **Contemporary Dance**: Fusion of styles
- **Cultural Dance**: Traditional forms from various cultures
- **Dance Theater**: Narrative-driven movement

**Funding Model**:
- Heavy reliance on donors and grants
- Education programs and workshops
- Collaborations with other arts organizations
- International touring

### Comedy
- **Stand-Up Comedy**: Solo performances, comedy clubs
- **Improv**: Improvisational theater (Second City, UCB)
- **Sketch Comedy**: Written comedic scenes
- **Comedy Festivals**: Just for Laughs, Edinburgh Fringe

**Career Path**:
1. Open mics and showcases
2. Club regular/paid spots
3. Headliner at clubs
4. Theater tours
5. TV specials and streaming deals
6. Arena tours (elite tier)

## Economic Impact

### Direct Impact
- Box office revenue: $15B+ annually (US)
- Employment: 500,000+ performers and production staff
- Touring revenue: $10B+ annually
- Government funding: $150M+ (NEA + state/local)

### Indirect Impact
- Restaurant and hospitality spending
- Tourism and destination appeal
- Urban revitalization (theater districts)
- Education and cultural enrichment
- Real estate values near cultural venues

### Multiplier Effect
Every dollar spent on performing arts generates approximately:
- $1.70 in local economic activity
- $0.43 in government revenue (taxes)
- Support for adjacent industries (printing, catering, transportation)

## Challenges and Opportunities

### Current Challenges
- **Economic Volatility**: Susceptible to recessions and crises
- **Aging Infrastructure**: Historic theaters need renovation
- **Audience Development**: Attracting younger, diverse audiences
- **Competition**: Streaming entertainment, gaming, social media
- **Funding Cuts**: Reduced government and corporate support
- **Geographic Inequality**: Concentration in urban centers

### COVID-19 Impact
The pandemic devastated live performance:
- Complete shutdown March 2020 - varies by region
- Broadway closed 18 months (March 2020 - September 2021)
- Estimated losses: $30B+ globally
- Permanent closure of many small venues
- Shift to outdoor and digital performances

### Recovery and Innovation
- **Hybrid Models**: In-person + streaming options
- **Outdoor Venues**: Open-air performances
- **Flexible Spacing**: Reconfigurable seating
- **Enhanced Safety**: Air filtration, health protocols
- **New Works**: Addressing contemporary issues and diverse voices

### Opportunities
- **Demographic Shifts**: Growing Hispanic and Asian audiences
- **Technology Integration**: Enhanced experiences without replacing live element
- **Alternative Venues**: Non-traditional performance spaces
- **International Collaboration**: Cross-cultural productions and tours
- **Immersive Theater**: Audience participation, site-specific work
- **Education Partnerships**: School and university collaborations

## Career Paths

### Performance
- **Actor**: Stage, musical theater
- **Musician**: Orchestral, chamber, solo
- **Dancer**: Company member, principal, choreographer
- **Singer**: Opera, musical theater, concert
- **Comedian**: Stand-up, improv, character work

- **Director**: Artistic vision and leadership
- **Choreographer**: Movement design
- **Music Director/Conductor**: Musical leadership
- **Stage Manager**: Production coordination
- **Technical Director**: Scenic, lighting, sound design
- **Costume Designer**: Wardrobe creation
- **Makeup and Hair**: Character design

### Business and Administration
- **Artistic Director**: Organizational vision and programming
- **Managing Director**: Business operations
- **Development Director**: Fundraising and grants
- **Marketing Director**: Promotion and sales
- **House Manager**: Front-of-house operations
