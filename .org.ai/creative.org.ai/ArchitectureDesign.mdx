# Architecture Design

Architecture is both the art and science of designing buildings and other physical structures. It encompasses the planning, design, and oversight of construction for residential, commercial, institutional, and public structures, balancing aesthetics, functionality, safety, and sustainability.

## Overview

Architectural practice integrates creative vision with technical expertise to shape the built environment. The field includes:

- **Architectural Design**: Buildings, structures, spaces
- **Urban Planning**: City and regional development
- **Landscape Architecture**: Outdoor spaces and site design
- **Interior Architecture**: Spatial planning within buildings
- **Historic Preservation**: Restoration and adaptive reuse
- **Sustainable Design**: Green building and environmental performance
- **Urban Design**: Public spaces, streetscapes, master planning
- **Parametric Design**: Computational and algorithmic design

## Design Process

### Phase 1: Pre-Design / Programming (5-10% of fee)

#### Client Discovery
- **Initial Consultations**: Understanding vision and goals
- **Site Analysis**: Context, topography, climate, zoning
- **Program Development**: Space requirements, adjacencies
- **Budget Establishment**: Construction budget and project budget
- **Feasibility Study**: Is the project viable?

#### Research
- **Code Research**: Zoning, building codes, regulations
- **Precedent Studies**: Similar projects for reference
- **Site Survey**: Existing conditions, property boundaries
- **Geotechnical**: Soil conditions (if available)

**Deliverables**:
- Program document (space list with square footages)
- Site analysis report
- Feasibility study
- Preliminary budget
- Project schedule

**Duration**: 2-6 weeks

### Phase 2: Schematic Design (SD) (15% of fee)

#### Concept Development
- **Massing Studies**: Building form and volume
- **Site Planning**: Building placement, access, parking
- **Floor Plans**: Room layouts and relationships
- **Elevations**: Building appearance and materials
- **Concept Presentation**: 2-3 design directions

#### Refinement
- **Client Feedback**: Selecting preferred direction
- **Design Development**: Refining chosen concept
- **Preliminary Systems**: HVAC, structural approach

**Deliverables**:
- Site plan
- Floor plans (all levels)
- Elevations (all sides)
- Sections (key cuts)
- 3D renderings or physical model
- Preliminary material selections
- Updated budget estimate

**Duration**: 4-12 weeks depending on complexity

### Phase 3: Design Development (DD) (20% of fee)

#### Detailed Design
- **Refined Plans**: Dimensions, room names, annotations
- **Building Sections**: Interior relationships, ceiling heights
- **Exterior Elevations**: Materials, windows, details
- **Structural System**: Coordination with structural engineer
- **MEP Coordination**: Mechanical, electrical, plumbing systems
- **Interior Finishes**: Materials, colors, fixtures
- **Specifications**: Material standards and quality

#### Consultant Coordination
- **Structural Engineer**: Foundation, framing, lateral systems
- **MEP Engineer**: Systems sizing and layout
- **Civil Engineer**: Site work, drainage, utilities
- **Landscape Architect**: Site plantings, hardscape
- **Other Specialists**: Acoustics, lighting, facade, etc.

**Deliverables**:
- 1/4" or 1/8" scale drawings
- Building sections and details
- Preliminary specifications
- Consultant drawings integrated
- Updated cost estimate (more accurate)
- Materials and finishes board

**Duration**: 6-16 weeks

### Phase 4: Construction Documents (CD) (40% of fee)

#### Permit Drawings
- **Comprehensive Drawings**: All information for construction
- **Specifications**: Written requirements (Division 00-33)
- **Details**: Enlarged drawings showing assembly
- **Schedules**: Doors, windows, finishes, equipment
- **Code Compliance**: Meeting all regulations

#### Documentation
- **Architectural Drawings**: A-series (A101, A201, etc.)
  - Cover sheet, site plan, floor plans, RCPs, elevations, sections, details, schedules
- **Structural Drawings**: S-series
- **MEP Drawings**: M, E, P series
- **Civil Drawings**: C-series
- **Landscape Drawings**: L-series

**Drawing Count**: 20-200+ sheets depending on project size

**Deliverables**:
- Complete construction document set
- Technical specifications (300-800 pages)
- Permit application package
- Final cost estimate
- Project manual

**Duration**: 8-24 weeks

### Phase 5: Bidding/Negotiation (5% of fee)

#### Contractor Selection
- **Bidding**: Soliciting competitive bids from contractors
- **Bid Documents**: Issuing CD set to bidders
- **Pre-Bid Meeting**: Site visit, Q&A
- **Addenda**: Answers to contractor questions
- **Bid Opening**: Receiving and reviewing bids
- **Recommendation**: Advising client on contractor selection

**Deliverables**:
- Bid documents
- Addenda
- Bid tabulation
- Contractor recommendation

**Duration**: 4-8 weeks

### Phase 6: Construction Administration (CA) (20% of fee)

#### Oversight
- **Submittals Review**: Approving contractor product selections
- **RFIs (Requests for Information)**: Answering contractor questions
- **Site Visits**: Regular inspections (weekly or bi-weekly)
- **Change Orders**: Evaluating and documenting changes
- **Payment Applications**: Certifying contractor payments
- **Shop Drawings**: Reviewing fabrication drawings

#### Quality Control
- **Conformance**: Ensuring work matches drawings
- **Problem Solving**: Addressing field conditions
- **Punch List**: Final items to complete
- **Substantial Completion**: Project ready for occupancy
- **Final Completion**: All work finished

**Deliverables**:
- Meeting minutes
- Site observation reports
- RFI responses
- Submittal reviews
- Change order documentation
- Punch list
- Certificate of substantial completion

**Duration**: 6-36+ months (project dependent)

## Architectural Specializations

### Residential Architecture

#### Single-Family Homes
- **Custom Homes**: One-of-a-kind, high-end ($500K-$10M+ construction)
- **Production Homes**: Repeated designs, volume builders
- **Renovations**: Additions, remodels, restorations
- **Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)**: Backyard cottages, in-law units

**Design Considerations**:
- Lifestyle and family needs
- Site orientation (solar, views)
- Indoor-outdoor relationships
- Privacy and acoustics
- Future flexibility

**Fee Structure**: 8-15% of construction cost typical

#### Multi-Family Housing
- **Apartments**: Market-rate, affordable, mixed-income
- **Condominiums**: Ownership units
- **Senior Living**: Independent, assisted, memory care
- **Student Housing**: Dorms, university housing

**Design Considerations**:
- Unit mix and layouts
- Common amenities
- Parking (structured, surface)
- Code compliance (egress, accessibility)
- Construction cost per unit

**Fee Structure**: 5-10% of construction cost, varies by complexity

### Commercial Architecture

#### Office Buildings
- **Corporate Headquarters**: Brand identity, workplace culture
- **Spec Office**: Flexible layouts for multiple tenants
- **Co-Working**: Shared workspace, amenities
- **Adaptive Reuse**: Converting old buildings (warehouses, etc.)

**Trends**:
- Open office to activity-based working
- Wellness (natural light, air quality, biophilia)
- Flexibility for hybrid work
- Collaboration spaces

#### Retail
- **Shopping Centers**: Malls, strip centers, lifestyle centers
- **Standalone Stores**: Flagship locations, boutiques
- **Restaurants**: Dining spaces, commercial kitchens
- **Mixed-Use**: Retail + residential/office

**Design Focus**:
- Street presence and visibility
- Customer flow and experience
- Merchandising and display
- Brand expression

#### Hospitality
- **Hotels**: Full-service, limited-service, boutique
- **Resorts**: Destination properties
- **Restaurants and Bars**: Dining experiences
- **Entertainment Venues**: Theaters, clubs, event spaces

**Design Considerations**:
- Guest experience and journey
- Operational efficiency
- Brand standards (chain hotels)
- Local character (boutique properties)

### Institutional Architecture

#### Educational
- **K-12 Schools**: Elementary, middle, high schools
- **Higher Education**: Classrooms, labs, libraries, student centers
- **Specialized**: Trade schools, performing arts schools

**Design Principles**:
- Daylighting and acoustics
- Security and supervision
- Flexibility for changing pedagogy
- Community connection

**Market**: Large, stable (public funding)

#### Healthcare
- **Hospitals**: Acute care, specialized (children's, cancer)
- **Outpatient Clinics**: Primary care, specialty
- **Medical Office Buildings**: Physician practices
- **Senior Care**: Assisted living, skilled nursing, memory care

**Complexity**: High (codes, infection control, MEP)

**Design Focus**:
- Evidence-based design (healing environments)
- Infection control and cleanability
- Wayfinding and orientation
- Staff efficiency
- Patient safety and privacy

**Licensing**: Often requires healthcare design experience

#### Cultural
- **Museums**: Art, natural history, science centers
- **Performing Arts**: Theaters, concert halls, opera houses
- **Libraries**: Public, academic, special collections
- **Religious**: Churches, synagogues, mosques, temples

**Design Emphasis**:
- Public space and experience
- Symbolism and meaning
- Specialized systems (lighting, acoustics)
- Collections care (museums)

### Civic and Public Architecture

#### Government
- **City Halls**: Administrative centers
- **Courthouses**: Judicial facilities
- **Fire/Police Stations**: Emergency services
- **Transportation**: Airports, train stations, transit hubs

**Requirements**:
- Public process (community input)
- Accessibility (ADA compliance)
- Security (especially post-9/11)
- Durability and low maintenance
- Civic presence and symbolism

#### Infrastructure
- **Airports**: Terminals, concourses
- **Transit**: Subway stations, bus terminals
- **Parking Structures**: Multi-level, automated
- **Utilities**: Water treatment, power plants

**Scale**: Very large, complex, multi-year projects

### Sustainable Architecture

#### Certification Programs
- **LEED**: Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design (USGBC)
  - Certified, Silver, Gold, Platinum levels
- **WELL**: Health and wellness focus
- **Passive House**: Ultra-low energy standard
- **Living Building Challenge**: Net-positive performance
- **BREEAM**: UK-based green building (international)

#### Strategies
- **Passive Design**: Orientation, shading, natural ventilation
- **High-Performance Envelope**: Insulation, air sealing, windows
- **Efficient Systems**: HVAC, lighting, water heating
- **Renewable Energy**: Solar, geothermal, wind
- **Water Conservation**: Low-flow fixtures, rainwater harvesting
- **Healthy Materials**: Low-VOC, sustainable sourcing
- **Daylighting**: Natural light, views, circadian rhythms

#### Net-Zero Buildings
- **Net-Zero Energy**: Produces as much energy as consumed (annually)
- **Net-Zero Carbon**: No greenhouse gas emissions (operational)
- **Strategies**: Super-efficient + renewable energy
- **Growing Trend**: California requires by 2030 for new buildings

## Business of Architecture

### Fee Structures

#### Percentage of Construction Cost
Traditional method:
- Residential: 8-15% (higher for smaller/custom projects)
- Commercial: 5-10% (varies by type)
- Institutional: 7-12%
- Complex/specialized: 10-15%+

**Pros**: Scales with project size, covers scope changes
**Cons**: Penalizes efficiency, client may question motivation

#### Fixed Fee (Lump Sum)
Agreed total for defined scope:
- Requires clear scope definition
- Risk transfer to architect
- Incentivizes efficiency

**Typical Use**: Well-defined projects, experienced clients

#### Hourly Billing
Time and materials:
- Hourly rates by staff level ($50-$300+)
- Good for small projects, consultations
- Requires time tracking

**Multiplier Method**: Staff salary × 2.5-3.5 = billing rate
(Covers overhead, profit, benefits)

#### Hybrid Models
- Fixed fee for design phases
- Hourly for construction administration
- Not-to-exceed with hourly billing
- Value-based pricing (rare in architecture)

### Project Delivery Methods

#### Design-Bid-Build (Traditional)
Sequential process:
1. Architect designs
2. Contractors bid
3. Selected contractor builds

**Pros**: Competitive pricing, clear roles
**Cons**: Slow, adversarial, change orders common

#### Design-Build
Single entity responsible:
- One contract for design + construction
- Architect may be prime or sub

**Pros**: Faster, single point of responsibility
**Cons**: Less client control, potential quality concerns

#### Construction Manager at Risk (CMAR)
Contractor involved during design:
- CM provides cost input during design
- Guaranteed maximum price (GMP)
- CM self-performs or subcontracts

**Pros**: Cost certainty, constructability input
**Cons**: CM selection before full design

#### Integrated Project Delivery (IPD)
Collaborative approach:
- Shared risk and reward
- All parties involved from start
- Co-location, BIM, target cost

**Pros**: Alignment, innovation, efficiency
**Cons**: Requires cultural change, legal complexity

### Business Operations

#### Overhead Costs
- **Salaries and Benefits**: 50-60% of revenue
- **Rent and Utilities**: 5-10%
- **Software and Technology**: 3-5%
  - Revit/AutoCAD: $2,500-$5,000/seat/year
  - Rendering: $1,000-$2,000/seat/year
  - Project management: $500-$1,000/seat/year
- **Insurance**: 2-4%
  - Professional liability (E&O): $10K-$100K+
  - General liability, workers comp
- **Marketing and Business Development**: 5-10%
- **Professional Development**: 1-3%

**Target Profit Margin**: 10-20% (median ~12-15%)

#### Staffing Ratios
- 1 Principal : 5-10 staff (typical)
- 1 Project Manager : 2-5 project staff
- Mix of licensed architects, designers, technicians

**Staff Utilization Target**: 75-85% billable hours

### Licensing and Regulation

#### Architect Registration
- **Education**: Professional architecture degree (B.Arch or M.Arch)
  - NAAB-accredited program (National Architectural Accrediting Board)
- **Experience**: 3,740 hours documented (AXP - Architectural Experience Program)
  - Covers six practice areas
- **Examination**: ARE 5.0 (Architect Registration Examination)
  - 6 divisions, computer-based testing
- **State Licensure**: Register with state board
  - Reciprocity through NCARB certificate

**Timeline**: Typically 7-10 years from high school to license

**Title Protection**: "Architect" legally protected (varies by state)

#### Continuing Education
- Most states require CE hours (12-24/year typical)
- Health, safety, welfare (HSW) hours
- ADA, accessibility, building codes
- Sustainable design

#### Professional Organizations
- **AIA (American Institute of Architects)**:
  - ~95,000 members
  - Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct
  - Contracts and documents (AIA forms)
  - Continuing education
  - Advocacy
- **NCARB (National Council of Architectural Registration Boards)**:
  - Licensing coordination
  - Reciprocity between states
- **ACSA (Association of Collegiate Schools of Architecture)**: Academic focus
- **NAAB**: Accreditation of architecture programs

## Architectural Styles and Movements

### Historical Styles
- **Classical**: Greek, Roman orders and proportions
- **Gothic**: Pointed arches, verticality, ornament
- **Renaissance**: Revival of classical principles
- **Baroque**: Drama, movement, ornamentation
- **Neoclassical**: Return to classical ideals
- **Victorian**: Ornate, eclectic
- **Art Nouveau**: Organic forms, flowing lines
- **Art Deco**: Geometric, luxurious, modern
- **Beaux-Arts**: Grand, formal, classical

### Modern Movement (1920s-1960s)
- **Bauhaus**: Function, simplicity, industrial materials
- **International Style**: Glass boxes, no ornament, rational
- **Mid-Century Modern**: Integration with nature, open plans
- **Brutalism**: Exposed concrete, sculptural forms

**Key Figures**: Le Corbusier, Mies van der Rohe, Frank Lloyd Wright, Walter Gropius

### Contemporary Movements

#### Postmodernism (1970s-1990s)
Reaction against modernism:
- Historical references and ornament
- Symbolism and meaning
- Color and decoration
- Playfulness

**Key Figures**: Robert Venturi, Michael Graves, Philip Johnson (later work)

#### Deconstructivism (1980s-2000s)
Fragmented, non-rectilinear forms:
- Challenging assumptions
- Dynamic, unstable appearance
- Complex geometries

**Key Figures**: Frank Gehry, Zaha Hadid, Rem Koolhaas, Daniel Libeskind

#### Parametricism (2000s-present)
Algorithmic, complex geometries:
- Computer-generated forms
- Continuous variation
- Structural integration

**Key Figures**: Zaha Hadid, Patrik Schumacher

#### Critical Regionalism
Responding to local context:
- Climate-responsive
- Local materials and traditions
- Modernism + regional character

**Key Figures**: Glenn Murcutt, Rick Joy, Lake|Flato

#### Sustainable/Green Architecture
Environmental responsibility:
- Energy efficiency
- Passive strategies
- Renewable materials
- Regenerative design

**Key Figures**: William McDonough, Renzo Piano, Snøhetta

## Education and Career Path

### Academic Preparation

#### Bachelor of Architecture (B.Arch)
- 5-year professional degree
- NAAB-accredited
- Satisfies education requirement for licensure
- Intensive, studio-focused

#### Bachelor of Arts/Science in Architecture
- 4-year pre-professional degree
- Does not satisfy licensure requirement
- Prepares for M.Arch or related fields

#### Master of Architecture (M.Arch)
- **M.Arch I (First Professional)**: 2-3.5 years for non-architecture undergrads
- **M.Arch II (Post-Professional)**: 1-2 years for B.Arch holders
- NAAB-accredited programs satisfy licensure requirement
- Research and specialization opportunities

**Top Architecture Schools** (US):
- MIT, Harvard GSD, Princeton
- Columbia, Yale, UC Berkeley
- Cornell, University of Pennsylvania, UCLA
- Rice, University of Michigan, Washington University in St. Louis
- Cooper Union, Pratt, RISD

- Recent graduate
- Learning practice
- Documentation support
- Salary: $45K-$60K

#### Architectural Designer (3-6 years)
- Design development
- Construction documents
- Some client interaction
- Salary: $60K-$80K

- Leading projects
- Client presentations
- Consultant coordination

#### Senior Project Manager (10-15 years)
- Multiple projects
- Business development

- Leadership role

- Salary: $120K-$180K

#### Principal/Partner (15-30+ years)
- Firm ownership
- Strategic direction
- Major client relationships
- Salary + profit sharing: $150K-$500K+
