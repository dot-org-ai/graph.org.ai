# Broadcasting

Broadcasting encompasses the transmission of audio and video content to distributed audiences via over-the-air, cable, satellite, and internet streaming platforms.

## Overview

Broadcasting has evolved from one-way mass communication to a sophisticated multi-platform ecosystem. Traditional linear broadcasting (scheduled programming) now coexists with on-demand streaming, time-shifting, and interactive experiences. The industry spans free over-the-air television, subscription cable and satellite, and internet-based streaming services.

**NAICS Code**: 515 - Broadcasting (except Internet)

**Note**: Internet-only broadcasting is classified under NAICS 519130 (Internet Publishing and Broadcasting)

## Subsectors (4-digit)

### 5151 - Radio Broadcasting

**51511 - Radio Broadcasting Stations**

Over-the-air radio transmission:

- **AM Radio** - Amplitude modulation (talk, news, sports)
- **FM Radio** - Frequency modulation (music, variety)
- **Public Radio** - NPR, community stations
- **College Radio** - Educational stations
- **Low-Power FM (LPFM)** - Community broadcasters

**Formats**:
- News/Talk
- Music (Top 40, Country, Rock, Urban, Classical)
- Sports
- Religious
- Foreign language

**Business Models**:
- Commercial advertising
- Public funding (donations, grants)
- Subscription (satellite radio)
- Sponsorships and underwriting

### 5152 - Television Broadcasting

**51521 - Television Broadcasting Stations**

Over-the-air television transmission:

- **Network Affiliates** - ABC, NBC, CBS, FOX
- **Independent Stations** - Non-network
- **Public Television** - PBS stations
- **Religious Broadcasting** - Faith-based content
- **Educational TV** - Instructional programming

**Revenue Models**:
- Local and national advertising
- Retransmission consent fees (from cable/satellite)
- Network compensation
- Digital spectrum leasing

### 5152 - Cable and Other Subscription Programming

**Satellite TV**:
- Direct-to-home service

**IPTV (Internet Protocol TV)**:
- TV over broadband
- On-demand integration
- Interactive features

### Audience Trends

**Linear TV Decline**:
- Cord-cutting acceleration
- Average age of broadcast viewers: 60+
- Prime time viewing down 10-20% annually
- DVR time-shifting prevalent

**Streaming Growth**:
- 80% of US households have streaming service
- Live TV streaming (vMVPD) growing
- FAST channels (free ad-supported TV) emerging
- Younger audiences exclusively streaming

**Radio Resilience**:
- 90% weekly reach in US
- In-car listening dominant
- AM/FM competing with streaming (Spotify, Apple Music)
- Podcast growth impacting time spent

### Competitive Landscape

**Traditional Broadcasters vs. Streaming**:
- Networks launching streaming services (Peacock, Paramount+)
- Simultaneous release strategies
- Exclusive streaming content
- Sports as key differentiator

**Consolidation Trends**:
- Vertical integration (content + distribution)
- Station group roll-ups
- Network ownership changes
- Regulatory approval required

## Programming Strategies

### Daypart Programming

**Television Dayparts**:
- Early morning (5-9am): News, morning shows
- Daytime (9am-4pm): Talk shows, court shows, soap operas
- Early fringe (4-7pm): Syndicated programming, local news
- Prime time (8-11pm): Network programming, highest CPM
- Late night (11pm-1am): Talk shows, news
- Overnight (1-6am): Infomercials, repeat programming

**Radio Dayparts**:
- Morning drive (6-10am): Peak listening
- Midday (10am-3pm): At-work listening
- Afternoon drive (3-7pm): Commute listening
- Evening (7pm-midnight): Specialized programming
- Overnight (midnight-6am): Automation/voice-tracking

### Content Strategies

**Network Strategies**:
- Tent-pole programming (major events)
- Lead-in strategy (flow from strong to new shows)
- Counterprogramming (different from competition)
- Stunt programming (special events)
- Seasonal scheduling

**Local Strategies**:
- Local news franchises
- Community engagement
- Weather coverage leadership
- Investigative journalism
- Sports partnerships
