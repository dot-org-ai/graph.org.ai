# Clinical Trials

Clinical trials are prospective biomedical research studies on human participants designed to answer specific questions about the safety, efficacy, and optimal use of new therapeutic interventions. They represent the critical bridge between preclinical drug discovery and regulatory approval for patient use.

## Overview

Clinical drug development is the most expensive, time-consuming, and high-risk phase of pharmaceutical R&D:

**Scale and Investment**
- **Duration**: 6-7 years from IND to NDA approval
- **Cost**: $1.4-2.0 billion per approved drug (including failures)
- **Success Rate**: Only 10% of drugs entering Phase I ultimately receive FDA approval
- **Patient Enrollment**: 1,000-10,000+ patients across all phases
- **Global Operations**: Trials conducted across 40+ countries

**Regulatory Framework**
- **Good Clinical Practice (GCP)**: ICH-GCP guidelines (E6)
- **FDA Regulations**: 21 CFR Parts 50, 54, 56, 312, 314
- **Institutional Review Boards (IRBs)**: Ethics review and oversight
- **Informed Consent**: Patient autonomy and risk disclosure
- **Data Integrity**: Source data verification, audit trails (21 CFR Part 11)

## Investigational New Drug (IND) Application

The IND submission to FDA marks the transition from preclinical to clinical development.

### IND Components

**Clinical Protocol(s)**
- Study objectives and design
- Patient population (inclusion/exclusion criteria)
- Dosing regimen and schedule
- Efficacy endpoints (primary, secondary)
- Safety monitoring plan
- Statistical analysis plan

**Investigator's Brochure (IB)**
- Comprehensive summary of nonclinical and clinical data
- Pharmacology and toxicology findings
- Known human safety data
- Risk/benefit assessment
- Updated throughout development

**Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Controls (CMC)**
- Drug substance (API) manufacturing
- Drug product (formulation) manufacturing
- Analytical methods and specifications
- Stability data supporting clinical use
- GMP compliance documentation

**Pharmacology and Toxicology**
- Preclinical pharmacology (mechanism, PK/PD)
- Toxicology studies (acute, repeat-dose, reproductive, genotoxicity)
- Safety pharmacology (cardiovascular, CNS, respiratory)
- Justification for starting dose and dose escalation

**Previous Human Experience** (if applicable)
- Prior clinical trials
- Foreign marketing experience
- Related compounds

### IND Review Process

**FDA Review Timeline**
- 30-day review period
- Clinical hold if safety concerns identified
- Allowance to proceed if no objections raised

**IND Types**
- **Commercial IND**: Sponsor intends to market the drug
- **Research IND**: Non-commercial investigator-sponsored trials
- **Emergency Use IND**: Single-patient compassionate use
- **Treatment IND**: Expanded access for serious/life-threatening diseases

**IND Amendments**
- Protocol amendments (new studies, design changes)
- Information amendments (new safety data)
- Annual reports (development progress)
- Safety reports (adverse events, IND safety reports)

## Phase I Clinical Trials

First-in-human studies evaluating safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics.

### Objectives

**Primary Objectives**
- Establish safety and tolerability profile
- Determine maximum tolerated dose (MTD) and dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs)
- Characterize pharmacokinetics (PK) in humans
- Identify recommended Phase II dose (RP2D)

**Secondary Objectives**
- Preliminary pharmacodynamics (PD) and target engagement
- Early efficacy signals (especially in oncology)
- Food effect on absorption (fed vs. fasted)
- Drug-drug interaction potential

### Study Design

**Single Ascending Dose (SAD)**
- Small cohorts (3-6 subjects per dose level)
- Sequential dose escalation in healthy volunteers
- Extensive PK sampling (0-72 hours)
- Safety monitoring between cohorts
- Dose escalation rules (e.g., modified Fibonacci, 100% increments initially, then 50%, 33%)

**Multiple Ascending Dose (MAD)**
- 7-14 day repeated dosing
- Assess accumulation and steady-state PK
- Longer-term safety evaluation
- PD biomarker assessment

**Special Populations** (late Phase I or Phase IIa)
- Hepatic impairment
- Renal impairment
- Elderly patients
- Drug-drug interaction (DDI) studies

### Dose Escalation Strategies

**3+3 Design** (Oncology)
- Enroll 3 patients at starting dose
- If 0/3 DLTs: escalate to next dose
- If 1/3 DLTs: enroll 3 more patients at same dose
  - If 1/6 total DLTs: escalate
  - If ≥2/6 DLTs: MTD exceeded, previous dose is MTD
- If ≥2/3 DLTs: MTD exceeded

**Continuous Reassessment Method (CRM)**
- Bayesian adaptive design
- Uses all accumulated data to estimate MTD
- More efficient than 3+3 design

**Accelerated Titration Designs**
- Faster escalation with single patients until toxicity observed
- Then switch to standard cohort expansion

### Phase I Populations

**Healthy Volunteers**
- Used for most non-oncology drugs
- Age 18-55, good health (normal labs, vital signs, ECG)
- Exclusion: medications, smoking, alcohol, recent trials
- Compensation provided for participation

**Patient Populations**
- Oncology drugs (cancer patients, not healthy volunteers)
- Antivirals (HIV, HCV patients)
- Rationale: Toxic therapies, ethical considerations

### Bioanalytical and Biomarker Assessments

**Pharmacokinetics**
- Serial blood sampling (pre-dose, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24, 48 hours)
- PK parameters: Cmax, Tmax, AUC, t½, CL, Vd
- Urine and feces collection for excretion studies
- Validated LC-MS/MS bioanalytical methods

**Pharmacodynamics**
- Target engagement biomarkers (receptor occupancy, pathway inhibition)
- Surrogate efficacy markers (tumor markers, disease biomarkers)
- Exploratory translational research (genomics, proteomics)

**Safety Assessments**
- Vital signs and physical exams
- ECGs (baseline, on-treatment, intensive monitoring for QT interval)
- Clinical laboratory tests (hematology, chemistry, urinalysis)
- Adverse event monitoring and grading (CTCAE)

### First-in-Human Study Conduct

**Sentinel Dosing**
- First 1-2 subjects dosed, observed for 24-48 hours
- Remaining cohort dosed if no safety signals

**Dose Escalation Meetings**
- Safety review committee evaluates data
- Decision to escalate, expand cohort, or de-escalate
- Typically 1-2 week intervals between cohorts

**Phase I Duration**
- 1-2 years for full Phase I program
- 6-12 months for initial SAD/MAD studies

## Phase II Clinical Trials

Proof-of-concept studies evaluating efficacy, optimal dosing, and safety in patients.

### Objectives

**Phase IIa (Proof-of-Concept)**
- Demonstrate preliminary efficacy in target patient population
- Optimize dose and schedule
- Further characterize safety profile
- Select endpoints for Phase III trials

**Phase IIb (Dose-Ranging)**
- Establish optimal dose(s) for Phase III
- Compare multiple dose levels
- Confirm efficacy and safety
- Refine patient selection criteria (enrichment biomarkers)

### Study Design

**Patient Population**
- 100-500 patients with target disease
- Well-characterized inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Disease stage and severity defined
- Biomarker-selected subpopulations (increasingly common)

**Randomization and Controls**
- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled (gold standard)
- Active comparator if placebo unethical
- Parallel group design (dose groups vs. control)
- Adaptive designs (dose selection, futility, enrichment)

**Endpoints**
- **Primary Endpoint**: Key efficacy measure (disease-specific, validated)
  - Oncology: Objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS)
  - Cardiovascular: Blood pressure reduction, lipid levels
  - Diabetes: HbA1c reduction
- **Secondary Endpoints**: Supporting efficacy, safety, QOL
- **Exploratory Endpoints**: Biomarkers, translational research

### Common Phase II Designs

**Traditional Fixed Design**
- Predetermined sample size (power calculation)
- Fixed dose levels tested
- Analysis after all patients complete

**Adaptive Designs**
- **Seamless Phase II/III**: Continuous enrollment from Phase II to Phase III
- **Adaptive Dose-Ranging**: Futility rules, dose selection based on interim analysis
- **Enrichment**: Biomarker-based patient selection mid-trial
- **Basket Trials**: Single drug, multiple tumor types (biomarker-defined)
- **Umbrella Trials**: Multiple drugs, single disease, biomarker allocation

**Platform Trials**
- Master protocol with multiple treatment arms
- Arms can be added or dropped over time
- Shared control group and infrastructure
- Examples: I-SPY2 (breast cancer), LUNGMAP (lung cancer)

### Go/No-Go Decision Criteria

**Efficacy Criteria**
- Statistical significance on primary endpoint (p < 0.05 or predefined threshold)
- Clinically meaningful effect size
- Consistency across subgroups
- Comparison to standard of care benchmarks

**Safety Criteria**
- Acceptable adverse event profile
- No unexpected serious adverse events
- Dose-toxicity relationship understood
- Manageable side effects

**Feasibility Criteria**
- Patient population size (prevalence)
- Enrollment rate projections
- Competitive landscape
- Commercial potential

**Phase II Success Rates**
- Overall: ~30% of Phase II drugs advance to Phase III
- Varies by indication (oncology ~35%, CNS ~20%)

## Phase III Clinical Trials

Large-scale pivotal trials demonstrating substantial evidence of safety and efficacy for regulatory approval.

### Objectives

**Primary Objective**
- Confirm efficacy in large, well-defined patient population
- Demonstrate clinically meaningful benefit over placebo or standard of care
- Provide substantial evidence for regulatory approval (FDA requires ≥2 adequate and well-controlled studies)

**Secondary Objectives**
- Comprehensive safety database (>1,500-3,000 patients for NDA)
- Dose-response relationship confirmation
- Durability of response (long-term efficacy)
- Effects on morbidity and mortality (hard endpoints)
- Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes
- Subgroup analyses (age, gender, race, disease severity)

### Study Design

**Patient Enrollment**
- 1,000-5,000+ patients per trial
- Multiple trials often conducted in parallel (geographical, population diversity)
- Multi-center (50-500 sites) and multi-national
- Enrollment period: 1-3 years

**Randomization and Blinding**
- Randomized, double-blind, controlled trials (RCTs)
- 1:1 or 2:1 randomization (drug:control)
- Placebo-controlled (if ethical) or active comparator
- Blinding maintained until database lock

**Trial Duration**
- Follow-up: 6 months to 5+ years (depending on disease and endpoints)
- Chronic diseases: Longer trials for long-term outcomes
- Acute conditions: Shorter trials acceptable

### Endpoints and Outcomes

**Primary Endpoints** (Disease-Specific)

**Oncology**
- **Overall Survival (OS)**: Gold standard, time from randomization to death
- **Progression-Free Survival (PFS)**: Time to disease progression or death
- **Objective Response Rate (ORR)**: Complete + partial response
- Surrogate endpoints accepted for accelerated approval

**Cardiovascular**
- **MACE (Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events)**: CV death, MI, stroke
- **Blood Pressure**: Mean reduction in systolic/diastolic BP
- **Lipids**: LDL-C reduction

**Diabetes**
- **HbA1c**: Glycemic control (target <7%)
- **Cardiovascular Outcomes**: MACE in CV outcomes trials (CVOT)

**Neurology**
- **Cognitive Function**: ADAS-Cog (Alzheimer's), MoCA
- **Disability Progression**: EDSS (multiple sclerosis)
- **Seizure Frequency**: Epilepsy trials

**Infectious Diseases**
- **Cure Rate/Viral Suppression**: HIV, HCV viral load
- **Clinical Cure**: Bacterial infections
- **Prevention of Infection**: Vaccines (efficacy %)

**Secondary Endpoints**
- Patient-reported outcomes (PROs): Quality of life, symptom scores
- Biomarker changes (mechanistic, not regulatory endpoints)
- Healthcare utilization (hospitalizations, ER visits)
- Economic endpoints (cost-effectiveness, for payers)

### Statistical Considerations

**Sample Size and Power**
- Powered to detect clinically meaningful difference (typically 80-90% power)
- Alpha level: 0.05 (two-sided), adjusted for interim analyses
- Expected effect size based on Phase II data
- Assumed dropout/loss to follow-up rate

**Interim Analyses**
- Independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC/DSMB)
- Pre-specified stopping rules:
  - Futility (unlikely to show efficacy)
  - Overwhelming efficacy (early success)
  - Safety concerns (harm)
- Alpha spending function (O'Brien-Fleming, Pocock boundaries)

**Subgroup Analyses**
- Pre-specified subgroups (age, gender, race, disease severity, biomarkers)
- Post-hoc exploratory analyses (hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory)
- Multiple testing adjustments (Bonferroni, Hochberg)

### Global Clinical Trials

**Multi-Regional Clinical Trials (MRCTs)**
- Conducted simultaneously in US, EU, Asia, Latin America
- ICH E17 guidance on MRCT planning
- Regional differences in standard of care
- Ethnic factors and intrinsic/extrinsic factors

**Regulatory Considerations**
- FDA: Accepts foreign data if conducted under GCP
- EMA: Centralized procedure for EU approval
- PMDA (Japan): Japanese patient data often required
- NMPA (China): Local clinical trial requirement

### Patient-Reported Outcomes (PROs)

**FDA PRO Guidance**
- Well-defined concepts (symptoms, functioning, QOL)
- Validated instruments (psychometric properties)
- Pre-specified in protocol
- Can support labeling claims if properly designed

**Common PRO Instruments**
- **Generic**: SF-36, EQ-5D (health status)
- **Disease-Specific**: EORTC QLQ-C30 (cancer), WOMAC (osteoarthritis)
- **Symptom Scales**: Pain VAS, Fatigue Severity Scale

### Phase III Success Rates

**Advancement to Approval**
- ~60% of drugs entering Phase III are ultimately approved
- Higher in some areas (infectious disease, endocrinology)
- Lower in others (neurology, psychiatry)

**Common Reasons for Phase III Failure**
- Lack of efficacy (60%)
- Safety issues (30%)
- Commercial/strategic reasons (10%)

## Regulatory Submission and Approval

### New Drug Application (NDA) or Biologics License Application (BLA)

**NDA/BLA Components**

**Module 1: Administrative and Prescribing Information**
- Forms (FDA 356h)
- Proposed labeling (package insert)
- Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) if applicable

**Module 2: Common Technical Document (CTD) Summaries**
- Clinical overview and summary
- Nonclinical overview and summary
- Quality overall summary

**Module 3: Quality (CMC)**
- Drug substance and drug product manufacturing
- Control of excipients and container closure
- Specifications and analytical methods
- Stability data

**Module 4: Nonclinical Study Reports**
- Pharmacology studies
- Pharmacokinetic studies
- Toxicology studies (GLP)

**Module 5: Clinical Study Reports**
- Clinical pharmacology studies (Phase I, PK, DDI)
- Clinical efficacy studies (Phase II, Phase III)
- Clinical safety studies
- Individual patient data listings

### FDA Review Process

**Review Timeline**
- **Standard Review**: 10 months from submission
- **Priority Review**: 6 months (serious conditions, significant improvement)
- **Breakthrough Therapy**: Intensive FDA guidance and expedited review
- **Accelerated Approval**: Based on surrogate endpoints, post-marketing confirmatory trials required

**FDA Review Team**
- Medical officer (clinical reviewer)
- Pharmacology/toxicology reviewer
- Chemistry reviewer (CMC)
- Statistical reviewer
- Clinical pharmacology reviewer
- Biopharmaceutics reviewer

**FDA Actions**
- **Approval**: Drug can be marketed
- **Complete Response Letter (CRL)**: Deficiencies identified, cannot approve in current form
  - Additional data required (new trials, analyses)
  - Manufacturing issues (facility inspections)
  - Labeling negotiations
- **Approvable Letter**: Minor issues, can be resolved without new trials

**Advisory Committee Meetings**
- Independent expert panel reviews application
- Votes on approvability and risk/benefit
- FDA not bound by recommendation but typically follows

**PDUFA (Prescription Drug User Fee Act)**
- Pharmaceutical companies pay fees to FDA for review
- Enables FDA to hire additional reviewers
- Established performance goals and timelines

### Post-Approval Requirements

**Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies (REMS)**
- Medication guides and patient package inserts
- Communication plans for healthcare providers
- Elements to Assure Safe Use (ETASU):
  - Prescriber certification
  - Pharmacy certification
  - Patient enrollment and monitoring
- REMS assessments submitted to FDA

**Post-Marketing Studies (Phase IV)**
- Post-approval commitments or requirements
- Long-term safety and efficacy
- Pediatric studies (Pediatric Research Equity Act)
- Confirmatory trials (for accelerated approvals)

## Phase IV (Post-Marketing) Studies

Studies conducted after regulatory approval to gather additional safety and efficacy data in real-world settings.

### Objectives

**Safety Surveillance**
- Detect rare adverse events (1 in 10,000 or less)
- Long-term safety (cardiovascular, cancer, infections)
- Safety in special populations (pregnancy, pediatrics, elderly)
- Drug interactions in polypharmacy settings

**Effectiveness in Real-World Use**
- Comparative effectiveness research
- Adherence and persistence
- Outcomes in routine clinical practice
- Health economics and cost-effectiveness

**Expanded Indications**
- New therapeutic uses
- Different patient populations
- Combination therapies
- Line of therapy changes (adjuvant, neoadjuvant)

### Study Types

**Post-Marketing Surveillance**
- Large observational cohort studies (10,000-100,000+ patients)
- Registry studies (disease registries, drug registries)
- Claims database analyses (insurance, electronic health records)

**Post-Approval Randomized Trials**
- Cardiovascular Outcomes Trials (CVOTs) for diabetes drugs
- Cancer screening and prevention trials
- Head-to-head comparator trials (rarely required, but commercially valuable)

**Pragmatic Trials**
- Real-world settings (community practices, not academic centers)
- Broad inclusion criteria
- Effectiveness (not efficacy) endpoints
- Electronic data capture from EHRs

## Clinical Trial Operations

### Study Start-Up

**Site Selection and Feasibility**
- Investigator identification (key opinion leaders, high-enrolling sites)
- Site feasibility questionnaires (patient population, enrollment capability)
- Budget negotiation (per-patient payments, overhead)

**Regulatory and Ethics Approval**
- Institutional Review Board (IRB) / Ethics Committee (EC) approval
- Informed consent form (ICF) approval
- Site-specific regulatory submissions

**Site Initiation**
- Site initiation visit (SIV)
- Protocol training for site staff
- GCP training
- eCRF (electronic Case Report Form) training
- Investigational product (IP) delivery and accountability

### Patient Recruitment and Enrollment

**Recruitment Strategies**
- Physician referrals
- Patient databases and registries
- Advertising (print, digital, social media)
- Patient advocacy group partnerships
- ClinicalTrials.gov listing

**Screening and Enrollment**
- Pre-screening for eligibility
- Informed consent process (detailed discussion, questions)
- Screening assessments (labs, imaging, medical history)
- Randomization (via Interactive Response Technology - IRT)

**Retention Strategies**
- Regular communication and engagement
- Minimize visit burden (home nursing, telemedicine)
- Patient stipends for travel and time
- Reminder calls and texts

### Clinical Trial Monitoring

**Monitoring Activities**
- Source data verification (CRF vs. source documents)
- Informed consent review
- Adverse event documentation and reporting
- Protocol compliance assessment
- Investigational product accountability

**Monitoring Approaches**
- **On-Site Monitoring**: Traditional, frequent site visits (monthly, quarterly)
- **Remote Monitoring**: Phone/video calls, electronic data review
- **Risk-Based Monitoring (RBM)**: Focus on critical data and high-risk sites
- **Centralized Monitoring**: Statistical and data analytics to identify outliers

**Clinical Research Associates (CRAs)**
- Monitor assigned sites
- Issue monitoring visit reports and query letters
- Ensure regulatory compliance

### Data Management

**Electronic Data Capture (EDC)**
- Web-based systems for eCRF completion (Medidata Rave, Oracle InForm, Veeva Vault)
- Real-time data entry and query resolution
- Audit trails for regulatory compliance (21 CFR Part 11)

**Data Validation and Cleaning**
- Automated edit checks (range, logic, consistency)
- Manual data review
- Query generation and resolution
- Medical coding (adverse events, medications, medical history)
  - MedDRA (Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities) for AEs
  - WHO Drug Dictionary for medications

**Database Lock**
- Data cleaning complete
- All queries resolved
- Database frozen for analysis
- Backup and archiving

### Safety Reporting

**Adverse Event (AE) Collection**
- Spontaneous reporting by patients
- Systematic assessment at study visits
- Grading per CTCAE (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events)

**Serious Adverse Events (SAEs)**
- Death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, congenital anomaly
- Expedited reporting to sponsor (24 hours)
- Sponsor assessment for causality and expectedness

**IND Safety Reporting**
- **7-Day Reports**: Fatal or life-threatening unexpected SAE
- **15-Day Reports**: Other unexpected SAEs
- Annual safety reports to FDA

**Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)**
- Independent committee reviewing unblinded safety and efficacy data
- Recommends trial continuation, modification, or termination
- Charter defining roles, meeting frequency, stopping rules

## Special Trial Designs and Populations

### Pediatric Trials

**Regulatory Requirements**
- Pediatric Research Equity Act (PREA): Pediatric studies required for drugs treating pediatric conditions
- Best Pharmaceuticals for Children Act (BPCA): Incentives for pediatric studies (6-month patent extension)

**Age Groups**
- Neonates (birth to 1 month)
- Infants (1 month to 2 years)
- Children (2 to 12 years)
- Adolescents (12 to 18 years)

**Challenges**
- Ethical considerations (informed assent, parental consent)
- Developmental pharmacology (different PK/PD than adults)
- Age-appropriate formulations (liquids, chewables)
- Small patient populations (rare pediatric diseases)

### Rare Disease and Orphan Drugs

**Orphan Drug Designation**
- Prevalence <200,000 patients in US (or no reasonable expectation of recovering development costs)
- Incentives: 7-year market exclusivity, tax credits, protocol assistance
- Expedited review pathways

**Trial Challenges**
- Limited patient populations
- Lack of natural history data
- No validated endpoints
- Placebo control ethical issues
- Patient heterogeneity

**Innovative Designs**
- N-of-1 trials (individual patients as own control)
- Basket trials (multiple rare diseases sharing biomarker)
- Adaptive enrichment
- External control arms (historical data, registries)

### Precision Medicine and Biomarker-Driven Trials

**Companion Diagnostics**
- Diagnostic test identifying patients likely to benefit
- Co-developed with drug
- FDA approval required for use with drug
- Examples: HER2 testing for trastuzumab, EGFR testing for erlotinib

**Biomarker-Stratified Trials**
- Enrichment: Enroll only biomarker-positive patients
- All-comers with biomarker analysis: Enroll all, analyze subgroups
- Adaptive signature design: Develop biomarker cut-point during trial

### Decentralized and Virtual Trials

**Decentralized Clinical Trials (DCTs)**
- Remote enrollment and consent (eConsent)
- Telemedicine visits
- Home health visits (mobile nurses, phlebotomy)
- Direct-to-patient drug shipment
- Wearable devices and sensors for data capture

**Benefits**
- Improved patient access and retention
- Reduced patient burden
- Faster enrollment
- More diverse populations

**Challenges**
- Regulatory acceptance (evolving)
- Data quality and monitoring
- Technology barriers for some patients

## Clinical Trial Costs and Economics

### Cost Breakdown

**Phase I**: $10-20 million
- 20-100 subjects
- Intensive monitoring and bioanalytical work

**Phase II**: $30-60 million
- 100-500 patients
- Multiple doses, longer follow-up

**Phase III**: $100-300+ million per trial
- Often 2-3 trials conducted
- 1,000-5,000+ patients per trial
- Long duration (2-5 years)
- Global operations

**Total Clinical Development**: $200-500 million per drug

### Major Cost Drivers

**Patient Costs**
- Per-patient costs: $10,000-50,000 (varies by indication and complexity)
- Oncology and rare diseases: $50,000-100,000+ per patient
- Site payments (investigator fees, overhead, procedures)
- Patient stipends (travel, time)

**Operational Costs**
- Clinical trial management (CROs, internal staff)
- Monitoring (CRAs, travel)
- Data management and biostatistics
- Regulatory affairs
- Drug supply (IMP manufacturing, labeling, distribution)

**Site Costs**
- Site identification and contracting
- IRB fees
- Site training and meetings
- Site retention and engagement

### Outsourcing to CROs

**Contract Research Organizations (CROs)**
- Full-service CROs: Conduct entire trial (Covance, IQVIA, PPD, Syneos, Parexel)
- Functional service providers (FSPs): Staff augmentation for specific functions
- Niche CROs: Specialty areas (oncology, rare disease, pediatrics)

**CRO Services**
- Clinical operations and monitoring
- Data management and biostatistics
- Regulatory affairs
- Medical writing
- Pharmacovigilance and safety

**Cost Considerations**
- 30-60% of clinical development budget outsourced
- CROs provide expertise and flexibility
- Oversight and vendor management required
