# Drug Discovery

Drug discovery is the process of identifying and optimizing novel therapeutic compounds that can safely and effectively treat disease. It represents the earliest and most innovative phase of pharmaceutical R&D, transforming biological insights into potential medicines through iterative cycles of design, synthesis, and testing.

## Overview

Modern drug discovery is a highly sophisticated, multidisciplinary endeavor combining:
- **Biological Sciences**: Disease mechanism understanding, target biology
- **Chemistry**: Compound design, synthesis, and optimization
- **Computational Sciences**: Molecular modeling, AI/ML, bioinformatics
- **Pharmacology**: ADME properties, safety assessment, efficacy testing
- **Translational Medicine**: Bridging basic research to clinical applications

**Discovery Timeline**: 3-6 years from target identification to clinical candidate
**Investment**: $100-500 million per successful drug candidate
**Success Rate**: 1 in 5,000-10,000 compounds screened becomes an approved drug
**Attrition**: 90% of candidates fail in clinical development

## Target Identification and Validation

The first critical step is identifying biological targets (proteins, genes, pathways) whose modulation can treat disease.

### Target Identification Approaches

**Genetics and Genomics**
- **Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS)**: Identify disease-associated genetic variants
- **Exome/Whole Genome Sequencing**: Discover causal mutations in rare diseases
- **Cancer Genomics**: Identify oncogenes and tumor suppressors (TCGA, COSMIC databases)
- **Human Genetic Validation**: Targets with human genetic evidence have 2x higher success rates
  - Examples: PCSK9 for hypercholesterolemia, SGLT2 for diabetes

**Proteomics and Functional Genomics**
- **Differential Expression Analysis**: Identify dysregulated proteins/genes in disease
- **Phosphoproteomics**: Map signaling pathway alterations
- **CRISPR Screens**: Systematically knock out genes to identify essential targets
- **RNAi Screens**: Gene silencing to validate target function
- **Single-Cell Sequencing**: Cell-type-specific target identification

**Pathway Analysis**
- Signaling pathway mapping (KEGG, Reactome)
- Network biology and systems pharmacology
- Pathway enrichment analysis
- Disease mechanism reconstruction

**Phenotypic Screening**
- Cell-based disease models
- Identification of targets after compound discovery
- Unbiased approach without prior target hypothesis

### Target Validation

Confirming that modulating a target will produce desired therapeutic effects without unacceptable toxicity.

**Genetic Validation**
- Knockout/knockdown models (CRISPR, siRNA)
- Transgenic animal models
- Patient genetic evidence (mutations, polymorphisms)

**Pharmacological Validation**
- Tool compounds and chemical probes
- Target engagement assays
- Proof-of-concept in disease models
- On-target vs. off-target effect differentiation

**Target Assessment Criteria**
- **Druggability**: Can small molecules or biologics modulate the target?
- **Disease Relevance**: Clear link between target and disease pathology
- **Safety**: Acceptable toxicity profile based on target biology
- **Efficacy Potential**: Magnitude of expected therapeutic benefit
- **Novelty**: First-in-class vs. best-in-class opportunities
- **Intellectual Property**: Patent landscape and freedom to operate

**Target Classes**
- **Enzymes** (30% of drugs): Kinases, proteases, oxidoreductases
- **G-Protein Coupled Receptors** (35%): Largest drug target family
- **Ion Channels** (5%): Voltage-gated, ligand-gated channels
- **Nuclear Receptors** (5%): Steroid hormone receptors
- **Transporters** (5%): ABC transporters, SLC family
- **Protein-Protein Interactions** (emerging): Challenging but high-value targets
- **Nucleic Acids** (emerging): RNA targets, gene therapy, antisense

## Hit Identification

Discovering initial compounds (hits) that interact with the validated target.

### High-Throughput Screening (HTS)

**Compound Libraries**
- **Size**: 1-5 million compounds screened
- **Diversity Libraries**: Structurally diverse compound collections
- **Focused Libraries**: Target-class enriched (kinase, GPCR, protease libraries)
- **Natural Product Libraries**: Plant extracts, microbial metabolites, marine organisms
- **Fragment Libraries**: Small molecular fragments (MW < 300 Da), 1,000-10,000 compounds
- **DNA-Encoded Libraries (DEL)**: Billions of compounds with DNA barcodes

**HTS Technologies**
- **Biochemical Assays**: Target protein activity measurement
  - Enzyme activity assays (fluorescence, luminescence)
  - Binding assays (FRET, HTRF, FP, TR-FRET)
  - Reporter gene assays
- **Cell-Based Assays**: Phenotypic or target-based in cells
  - High-content imaging
  - Reporter cell lines
  - Primary cell screening
- **Automation and Robotics**:
  - Liquid handling systems
  - Plate readers and detection systems
  - 384-well and 1536-well plate formats
  - Acoustic dispensing for miniaturization

**Hit Criteria**
- **Activity**: IC₅₀, EC₅₀, or Ki < 10 μM (threshold varies by assay)
- **Reproducibility**: Confirmed in replicate tests
- **Dose-Response**: Clear concentration-dependent activity
- **Counter-Screen Negative**: No activity against related targets or assay artifacts
- **Chemical Tractability**: Suitable for medicinal chemistry optimization

### Fragment-Based Drug Discovery (FBDD)

Screening small molecular fragments that bind weakly to targets, then growing/linking to create high-affinity leads.

**Fragment Characteristics**
- Molecular weight: 150-300 Da
- Heavy atom count: 10-20 atoms
- High ligand efficiency (LE > 0.3)
- "Rule of 3" compliance (MW < 300, LogP < 3, H-bond donors/acceptors ≤ 3)

**Fragment Screening Methods**
- **X-ray Crystallography**: Direct visualization of fragment binding
- **Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR)**:
  - STD-NMR (Saturation Transfer Difference)
  - WaterLOGSY
  - ¹H-¹⁵N HSQC for protein chemical shift perturbation
- **Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR)**: Label-free binding detection
- **Thermal Shift Assays (DSF)**: Protein stabilization by fragments
- **Mass Spectrometry**: Native MS, H/D exchange

**Fragment Evolution**
- **Fragment Growing**: Add substituents to increase affinity
- **Fragment Linking**: Connect two fragments that bind adjacent sites
- **Fragment Merging**: Combine pharmacophores from different fragments

**FBDD Success Stories**
- Vemurafenib (Zelboraf) - BRAF kinase inhibitor for melanoma
- Venetoclax (Venclexta) - BCL-2 inhibitor for leukemia
- Erdafitinib (Balversa) - FGFR inhibitor for bladder cancer

### Structure-Based Drug Design (SBDD)

Using 3D target structure to design optimized compounds computationally.

**Structural Biology Techniques**
- **X-ray Crystallography**:
  - Atomic resolution target structures
  - Co-crystal structures with ligands
  - Iterative structure-guided optimization
- **Cryo-Electron Microscopy (Cryo-EM)**:
  - Large complexes and membrane proteins
  - Near-atomic resolution achievable
  - Multiple conformational states
- **Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR)**:
  - Solution structure determination
  - Protein dynamics and flexibility
  - Fragment screening

**Computational Methods**
- **Molecular Docking**:
  - Virtual screening of compound libraries
  - Binding pose prediction
  - Programs: AutoDock, GOLD, Glide, DOCK
- **Pharmacophore Modeling**:
  - 3D arrangement of chemical features
  - Virtual screening filter
  - De novo design guide
- **Molecular Dynamics (MD) Simulations**:
  - Protein-ligand binding dynamics
  - Induced fit and conformational changes
  - Binding free energy calculations (FEP, TI)
- **De Novo Design**:
  - Computational generation of novel structures
  - Growing molecules in binding site
  - Programs: LigBuilder, SPROUT, AutoGrow

### Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

AI/ML is transforming drug discovery with unprecedented speed and efficiency.

**Machine Learning Applications**
- **Predictive QSAR Models**: Structure-activity relationship prediction
- **ADME Property Prediction**: Solubility, permeability, metabolism
- **Toxicity Prediction**: hERG liability, hepatotoxicity, mutagenicity
- **De Novo Molecular Design**: Generative models (VAE, GAN, transformers)
- **Retrosynthetic Planning**: Synthesis route prediction

**Deep Learning Platforms**
- **AlphaFold**: Protein structure prediction (DeepMind/Google)
- **Atomwise**: Deep learning for virtual screening
- **Exscientia**: AI-driven compound design (first AI-designed drug in trials)
- **Insilico Medicine**: Generative chemistry and target discovery
- **Recursion Pharmaceuticals**: Phenomics and cellular image analysis

**Data Sources for ML**
- ChEMBL: 2M+ compounds, 15M+ bioactivities
- PubChem: 100M+ chemical structures
- PDB (Protein Data Bank): 200,000+ protein structures
- BindingDB: 2.5M+ binding affinities
- Proprietary corporate data

## Hit-to-Lead Optimization

Refining initial hits into drug-like lead compounds suitable for further development.

### Medicinal Chemistry Strategies

**Structure-Activity Relationship (SAR) Exploration**
- Systematic modification of chemical scaffolds
- Identify key pharmacophore elements
- Explore substituent effects on potency
- Assess bioisosteres and scaffold hops

**Multi-Parameter Optimization (MPO)**
Balancing multiple objectives simultaneously:
- **Potency**: Target binding affinity (IC₅₀, Ki < 100 nM)
- **Selectivity**: Against related targets (>100-fold)
- **ADME Properties**: Absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion
- **Physicochemical Properties**: Solubility, lipophilicity, permeability
- **Safety**: Cytotoxicity, hERG inhibition, reactive metabolites
- **Synthesizability**: Practical synthetic routes

**Drug-Like Property Guidelines**

**Lipinski's Rule of 5** (Oral Bioavailability)
- Molecular weight ≤ 500 Da
- LogP ≤ 5
- Hydrogen bond donors ≤ 5
- Hydrogen bond acceptors ≤ 10
- Violations allowed for natural products and biologics

**Beyond Rule of 5 (bRo5)**
- Molecular weight: 500-1,000 Da
- Applicable to antibiotics, natural products, targeted protein degraders (PROTACs)

**Additional Property Metrics**
- **Ligand Efficiency (LE)**: Binding energy per heavy atom
- **Lipophilic Efficiency (LipE)**: Potency relative to lipophilicity
- **Polar Surface Area (PSA)**: < 140 Ų for BBB penetration, < 200 Ų for oral absorption
- **Rotatable Bonds**: < 10 for oral drugs

### ADME Profiling

Early assessment of pharmacokinetic properties to guide optimization.

**Absorption**
- **Solubility**: Thermodynamic and kinetic solubility (>10 μg/mL target)
- **Permeability**:
  - PAMPA (Parallel Artificial Membrane Permeability Assay)
  - Caco-2 cell monolayer permeability
  - MDCK cell permeability
- **Efflux**: P-glycoprotein (P-gp) substrate assessment

**Distribution**
- **Plasma Protein Binding**: Free fraction available for target engagement
- **Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB)**: For CNS drugs or BBB exclusion
- **Volume of Distribution (Vd)**: Tissue distribution prediction

**Metabolism**
- **Metabolic Stability**:
  - Liver microsome assays (human, mouse, rat)
  - Hepatocyte stability
  - Half-life (t₁/₂) > 30 min target
- **CYP450 Enzyme Interactions**:
  - CYP inhibition (drug-drug interaction potential)
  - CYP induction (enzyme upregulation)
  - Metabolite identification
- **Reactive Metabolite Formation**: Glutathione trapping assays

**Excretion**
- Renal clearance mechanisms
- Biliary excretion pathways

### In Vitro Safety Profiling

**Cardiac Safety**
- **hERG Channel Inhibition**: Measure IC₅₀ (>30 μM preferred, >10 μM acceptable)
- **Cardiac Ion Panel**: Nav1.5, Cav1.2, KCNQ1/KCNE1
- **Electrophysiology**: Patch-clamp assays

**General Cytotoxicity**
- Cell viability assays (MTT, ATP, LDH release)
- Multiple cell lines (HepG2, HEK293, primary hepatocytes)

**Genotoxicity**
- Ames test (bacterial mutagenicity)
- Micronucleus assay
- Chromosomal aberration test

**Off-Target Screening**
- Safety pharmacology panels (ion channels, GPCRs, enzymes)
- Kinome selectivity profiling (>400 kinases)
- Lead Profiling Screen (Eurofins Cerep, >100 targets)

## Lead Optimization

Advancing lead compounds toward clinical candidate selection through rigorous optimization.

### In Vivo Pharmacokinetics

Testing in animal models to predict human PK and optimize dosing.

**PK Studies in Rodents (Mice, Rats)**
- Intravenous (IV) dosing: Clearance, volume of distribution, half-life
- Oral (PO) dosing: Bioavailability, Cmax, Tmax, AUC
- Multiple time points (0-24 hours)
- Dose linearity assessment

**PK Parameters**
- **Clearance (CL)**: Rate of drug elimination (mL/min/kg)
- **Volume of Distribution (Vd)**: Extent of tissue distribution (L/kg)
- **Half-Life (t₁/₂)**: Time for plasma concentration to decrease by 50%
- **Bioavailability (F)**: Fraction of dose reaching systemic circulation (%)
- **AUC (Area Under Curve)**: Total drug exposure
- **Cmax**: Maximum plasma concentration
- **Tmax**: Time to reach Cmax

**Species Comparison**
- Mouse, rat, dog, non-human primate studies
- Allometric scaling to predict human PK
- Species differences in metabolism and clearance

### Pharmacodynamics and Efficacy Models

Demonstrating target engagement and therapeutic efficacy in disease models.

**Target Engagement**
- Ex vivo target occupancy in tissues
- Biomarker modulation (pharmacodynamic markers)
- PK/PD relationship establishment

**Disease Models**
- **Cell-Based Disease Models**: Patient-derived cells, iPSC-derived cells
- **Animal Models of Disease**:
  - Genetic models (transgenic, knockout mice)
  - Xenograft models (tumor implants)
  - Chemically-induced disease models
  - Spontaneous disease models
- **Efficacy Endpoints**: Disease-relevant readouts (tumor growth inhibition, disease scores, biomarkers)

**Proof-of-Concept (PoC)**
- Dose-response efficacy
- Comparison to standard of care
- Therapeutic window assessment (efficacious dose vs. toxic dose)

### Toxicology Studies

Assessing safety in animal models to support clinical development.

**Acute Toxicity**
- Single-dose toxicity in rodents
- Maximum tolerated dose (MTD)
- Dose-limiting toxicities

**Repeat-Dose Toxicity**
- 14-day and 28-day studies in rodents
- 90-day studies in rodents and non-rodents (dog, monkey)
- Histopathology and clinical chemistry
- Reversibility assessment

**Specialized Toxicology**
- **Reproductive Toxicology**: Fertility, embryo-fetal development, pre/postnatal development
- **Genotoxicity**: Ames, micronucleus, chromosomal aberration
- **Carcinogenicity**: 2-year rodent studies (for chronic use drugs)
- **Phototoxicity**: For drugs with UV absorption
- **Immunotoxicity**: Immune system effects

**GLP Toxicology Studies**
- Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) compliance
- IND-enabling toxicology packages
- Regulatory submission quality

## Clinical Candidate Selection

Choosing the optimal compound to advance into clinical development.

### Candidate Selection Criteria

**Efficacy**
- Robust in vivo proof-of-concept
- Clear PK/PD relationship
- Differentiation from existing therapies

**Safety and Tolerability**
- Adequate therapeutic index (TI > 10 preferred)
- Clean toxicology profile
- Manageable side effects
- Low DDI (drug-drug interaction) potential

**Pharmaceutical Properties**
- Chemical stability
- Formulation feasibility
- Scale-up synthesis viability
- Intellectual property position

**Developability**
- Manufacturing process defined
- Analytical methods established
- Regulatory pathway clear
- Commercial viability

### IND-Enabling Activities

Preparing for Investigational New Drug (IND) application to FDA.

**CMC (Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Controls)**
- GMP synthesis of API
- Formulation development
- Stability studies (ICH guidelines)
- Analytical method validation
- Release specifications

**Nonclinical Safety Package**
- GLP toxicology studies (2-week and 1-month in two species)
- Safety pharmacology (cardiovascular, CNS, respiratory)
- Genotoxicity battery
- Toxicokinetics

**IND Application Components**
- Investigator's Brochure
- Clinical protocol (Phase I design)
- CMC information
- Pharmacology and toxicology data
- Previous human experience (if applicable)

**Regulatory Interactions**
- Pre-IND meetings with FDA
- Orphan drug designation (if applicable)
- Fast track designation request
- Regulatory strategy alignment

## Emerging Technologies in Drug Discovery

### Targeted Protein Degradation (PROTACs)

**Mechanism**: Bifunctional molecules that recruit E3 ubiquitin ligases to target proteins, inducing ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation.

**Advantages**:
- Targets "undruggable" proteins without enzymatic activity
- Sub-stoichiometric catalytic mechanism
- Overcomes resistance from target overexpression

**Challenges**:
- Large molecular size (MW 700-1,500 Da)
- Oral bioavailability limitations
- Complex SAR (ternary complex formation)

**Clinical Examples**:
- Arvinas: ARV-110 (androgen receptor PROTAC), ARV-471 (estrogen receptor PROTAC)
- Kymera: KT-474 (IRAK4 degrader)

### Molecular Glues

Small molecules that induce novel protein-protein interactions, often leading to target degradation.

**Examples**:
- Thalidomide and IMiDs (lenalidomide, pomalidomide): CRBN-CK1α interaction
- Indisulam: CRBN-RBM39 degradation

### Nucleic Acid Therapeutics

**Antisense Oligonucleotides (ASOs)**
- Single-stranded DNA/RNA that bind target mRNA
- Mechanism: RNase H-mediated degradation or splice modulation
- Approved drugs: Nusinersen (Spinraza), Eteplirsen (Exondys 51)

**Small Interfering RNA (siRNA)**
- Double-stranded RNA inducing mRNA degradation via RNAi
- Delivery challenges (GalNAc conjugates for liver targeting)
- Approved drugs: Patisiran (Onpattro), Givosiran (Givlaari)

**Aptamers**
- RNA/DNA oligonucleotides binding proteins with antibody-like specificity
- Approved: Pegaptanib (Macugen) for macular degeneration

### Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs)

Targeted delivery of cytotoxic payloads using antibody specificity.

**Components**:
- Monoclonal antibody (tumor antigen targeting)
- Cytotoxic payload (auristatin, maytansinoid, topoisomerase inhibitor)
- Linker (cleavable or non-cleavable)

**Approved ADCs**:
- Trastuzumab emtansine (Kadcyla) - HER2+ breast cancer
- Enfortumab vedotin (Padcev) - bladder cancer
- Sacituzumab govitecan (Trodelvy) - triple-negative breast cancer

## Drug Discovery Metrics and Economics

### Timeline and Costs

**Typical Discovery Timeline**:
- Target identification and validation: 1-2 years
- Hit identification: 0.5-1 year
- Hit-to-lead: 1-2 years
- Lead optimization: 1-2 years
- Candidate selection and IND-enabling: 0.5-1 year
- **Total**: 3-6 years

**Investment**:
- Discovery research: $50-200 million per program
- Multiple programs required for portfolio (high attrition)
- Outsourcing vs. internal capabilities trade-offs

### Productivity Metrics

**Output Metrics**:
- Targets validated per year
- Screening campaigns completed
- Leads advanced to optimization
- Clinical candidates nominated
- INDs filed

**Quality Metrics**:
- Hit rate in HTS campaigns (0.1-1% typical)
- Lead compound properties (potency, selectivity, ADME)
- Clinical candidate success rate in development (10% overall)

### Outsourcing and Partnerships

**Contract Research Organizations (CROs)**:
- **Chemistry CROs**: WuXi AppTec, Pharmaron, ChemPartner
- **Biology CROs**: Charles River, Eurofins, Covance
- **ADME/Tox CROs**: Absorption Systems, Cyprotex, XenoTech

**Academic Collaborations**:
- Target discovery partnerships
- Structural biology collaborations
- Chemical probe development (SGC, Chemical Probes Portal)

**Technology Licensing**:
- Screening library access
- Computational platforms
- Assay technologies
