# pharma.org.ai

[![License: CC BY-SA 4.0](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-CC%20BY--SA%204.0-lightgrey.svg)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)

Ontology domain for the pharmaceutical and medicine manufacturing industry.

## Overview

The pharmaceutical industry is one of the most research-intensive and highly regulated manufacturing sectors, responsible for discovering, developing, manufacturing, and distributing medications that prevent, treat, and cure diseases. With global revenues exceeding $1.4 trillion annually and over 2 million employees worldwide, the industry plays a critical role in public health and healthcare systems.

Pharmaceutical manufacturing (NAICS 3254) is characterized by:
- **Intensive R&D investment**: 15-20% of revenue, with 10-15 year development timelines
- **Rigorous regulatory oversight**: FDA, EMA, and global regulatory agencies
- **Complex manufacturing**: From chemical synthesis to biological production
- **Stringent quality requirements**: Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and quality assurance
- **Patent-driven economics**: 20-year patent protection followed by generic competition
- **High risk/high reward**: 90% failure rate in drug development, but blockbuster potential

## Pharmaceutical Industry Landscape

### Market Overview

The global pharmaceutical market is diverse and dynamic:

- **Prescription Drugs**: $1.1 trillion global market
  - Small molecule drugs (traditional pharmaceuticals)
  - Biologics (proteins, antibodies, vaccines)
  - Specialty pharmaceuticals (oncology, rare diseases, complex conditions)

- **Generic Drugs**: $400+ billion market
  - Off-patent small molecules
  - Biosimilars (generic biologics)
  - 90% of prescriptions in developed markets

- **Over-the-Counter (OTC)**: $150+ billion market
  - Self-care medications
  - Rx-to-OTC switches

### Key Market Segments

**By Therapeutic Area** (Global Sales)
1. **Oncology**: $180+ billion (cancer treatments)
2. **Immunology**: $120+ billion (autoimmune diseases)
3. **Diabetes**: $70+ billion (insulin, GLP-1 agonists)
4. **Cardiovascular**: $60+ billion (statins, anticoagulants, antihypertensives)
5. **Respiratory**: $50+ billion (asthma, COPD)
6. **Anti-infectives**: $45+ billion (antibiotics, antivirals)
7. **Neurology**: $40+ billion (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, MS, epilepsy)
8. **Vaccines**: $40+ billion (preventive immunizations)

**By Product Type**
- **Small Molecules**: Traditional chemical drugs (tablets, capsules, liquids)
- **Biologics**: Large molecule drugs (proteins, antibodies, cell therapies)
- **Vaccines**: Preventive immunizations
- **Gene Therapies**: Genetic modification treatments
- **Cell Therapies**: Living cell-based treatments

## NAICS 3254 - Pharmaceutical and Medicine Manufacturing

The pharmaceutical manufacturing industry is classified under NAICS 3254, part of the broader Chemical Manufacturing sector (NAICS 325):

### 32541 - Pharmaceutical and Medicine Manufacturing

This subsector comprises establishments primarily engaged in one or more of the following:

1. **Manufacturing biological and medicinal products**
   - Vaccines, sera, and other biological products
   - Medicinal chemicals and botanical products
   - Pharmaceutical preparations intended for internal and external consumption

2. **Processing (grading, grinding, milling) botanical drugs and herbs**

3. **Isolating active medicinal principals from botanical drugs and herbs**

4. **Manufacturing pharmaceutical preparations**
   - In-vivo diagnostic substances
   - In-vitro diagnostic substances

### Industry Subsegments

While NAICS 3254 is a single category, the industry is functionally divided into:

| Segment | Description | Examples |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Innovative Pharmaceuticals** | Research-based branded drugs | Pfizer, Merck, Novartis, Roche, AstraZeneca |
| **Generic Drugs** | Off-patent medications | Teva, Sandoz, Mylan (Viatris), Dr. Reddy's |
| **Biosimilars** | Generic biologics | Amgen, Sandoz, Samsung Bioepis |
| **Biologics** | Biotechnology drugs | Genentech, Amgen, Biogen, Gilead |
| **Vaccines** | Preventive immunizations | Sanofi, GSK, Merck, Pfizer |
| **Contract Manufacturing (CMO/CDMO)** | Manufacturing for others | Catalent, Lonza, Patheon, Samsung Biologics |
| **Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (API)** | Drug substance production | BASF, Cambrex, Siegfried, Cipla |

## APQC Pharmaceutical Industry Extension

The APQC Process Classification Framework includes pharmaceutical-specific extensions that map to the unique processes and requirements of drug development and manufacturing:

### 1.0 Discover and Develop Pharmaceuticals

**1.1 Pharmaceutical Research and Development**
- 1.1.1 Identify therapeutic targets and disease mechanisms
- 1.1.2 Conduct drug discovery (screening, lead identification)
- 1.1.3 Optimize lead compounds (medicinal chemistry, ADME studies)
- 1.1.4 Conduct preclinical testing (in vitro, in vivo, toxicology)
- 1.1.5 File investigational new drug (IND) applications
- 1.1.6 Conduct clinical trials (Phase I, II, III, IV)
- 1.1.7 Manage regulatory submissions and interactions
- 1.1.8 File new drug applications (NDA, BLA, MAA)
- 1.1.9 Manage pharmaceutical intellectual property and patents

**1.2 Pharmaceutical Product Development**
- 1.2.1 Develop drug formulations (dosage forms, delivery systems)
- 1.2.2 Scale up manufacturing processes (tech transfer, process validation)
- 1.2.3 Develop analytical methods and specifications
- 1.2.4 Conduct stability studies and shelf-life determination
- 1.2.5 Design pharmaceutical packaging systems
- 1.2.6 Prepare regulatory dossiers (CMC sections)

### 2.0 Manufacture Pharmaceutical Products

**2.1 Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API) Manufacturing**
- 2.1.1 Synthesize chemical APIs (batch or continuous processes)
- 2.1.2 Produce biological APIs (fermentation, cell culture)
- 2.1.3 Isolate and purify APIs (crystallization, chromatography)
- 2.1.4 Test and release APIs (identity, purity, potency)
- 2.1.5 Manage API raw materials and intermediates

**2.2 Finished Dosage Form Manufacturing**
- 2.2.1 Manufacture solid dosage forms (tablets, capsules)
- 2.2.2 Manufacture liquid/semi-solid forms (solutions, suspensions, creams)
- 2.2.3 Manufacture sterile products (injectables, biologics)
- 2.2.4 Perform pharmaceutical packaging and labeling
- 2.2.5 Conduct in-process testing and quality control
- 2.2.6 Perform finished product testing and batch release

**2.3 Pharmaceutical Quality and Compliance**
- 2.3.1 Implement Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP)
- 2.3.2 Manage pharmaceutical quality systems (ICH Q10)
- 2.3.3 Conduct batch record review and disposition
- 2.3.4 Manage deviations, investigations, and CAPA
- 2.3.5 Perform pharmaceutical process validation
- 2.3.6 Manage change control for pharmaceutical processes
- 2.3.7 Conduct internal GMP audits and self-inspections
- 2.3.8 Manage regulatory inspections (FDA, EMA, etc.)
- 2.3.9 Ensure data integrity and electronic records (21 CFR Part 11)

### 3.0 Commercialize Pharmaceutical Products

**3.1 Pharmaceutical Marketing and Sales**
- 3.1.1 Develop pharmaceutical marketing strategies
- 3.1.2 Engage with healthcare providers and key opinion leaders
- 3.1.3 Conduct pharmaceutical sales operations
- 3.1.4 Provide medical information and drug safety information
- 3.1.5 Manage pharmaceutical pricing and reimbursement
- 3.1.6 Negotiate formulary access and payer contracts

**3.2 Pharmaceutical Distribution and Supply Chain**
- 3.2.1 Manage pharmaceutical inventory (serialization, track and trace)
- 3.2.2 Distribute pharmaceutical products (wholesalers, specialty pharmacy)
- 3.2.3 Manage cold chain logistics for temperature-sensitive products
- 3.2.4 Ensure pharmaceutical supply chain security (DSCSA)
- 3.2.5 Manage pharmaceutical recalls and market withdrawals
- 3.2.6 Handle returns and expired product management

### 4.0 Manage Pharmaceutical Safety and Compliance

**4.1 Pharmacovigilance and Drug Safety**
- 4.1.1 Monitor adverse drug reactions and events
- 4.1.2 Manage individual case safety reports (ICSRs)
- 4.1.3 Conduct signal detection and risk assessment
- 4.1.4 Prepare periodic safety reports (PSURs, PADERs)
- 4.1.5 Develop and implement risk management plans
- 4.1.6 Manage product safety communications and label changes

**4.2 Regulatory Affairs and Compliance**
- 4.2.1 Maintain regulatory registrations and licenses
- 4.2.2 Submit regulatory variations and supplements
- 4.2.3 Manage regulatory intelligence and policy tracking
- 4.2.4 Ensure pharmaceutical advertising and promotion compliance
- 4.2.5 Manage pharmaceutical compliance programs
- 4.2.6 Conduct regulatory audits and due diligence

**4.3 Clinical and Medical Affairs**
- 4.3.1 Design and conduct post-marketing studies (Phase IV)
- 4.3.2 Generate real-world evidence and outcomes research
- 4.3.3 Develop and communicate scientific evidence
- 4.3.4 Support healthcare provider education and training
- 4.3.5 Manage investigator-initiated trials
- 4.3.6 Conduct health economics and outcomes research (HEOR)

### 5.0 Manage Pharmaceutical Intellectual Property and Lifecycle

**5.1 Patent Strategy and IP Management**
- 5.1.1 Develop pharmaceutical patent strategies
- 5.1.2 File and prosecute pharmaceutical patents
- 5.1.3 Defend against patent challenges (Paragraph IV, IPR)
- 5.1.4 Manage patent term extensions and exclusivities
- 5.1.5 License pharmaceutical intellectual property

**5.2 Product Lifecycle Management**
- 5.2.1 Develop line extensions and new formulations
- 5.2.2 Pursue new indications and label expansions
- 5.2.3 Manage loss of exclusivity (LOE) planning
- 5.2.4 Optimize product portfolios and discontinuations
- 5.2.5 Plan and execute product launches

## Drug Development Process

The pharmaceutical R&D process is lengthy, expensive, and highly regulated:

### Discovery Phase (3-6 years)

**Target Identification and Validation**
- Disease mechanism research
- Genomics and proteomics studies
- Target validation in disease models
- Druggability assessment

**Lead Discovery**
- High-throughput screening (HTS) of compound libraries
- Fragment-based drug design
- Structure-based drug design
- Natural product screening
- AI/ML-driven compound design

**Lead Optimization**
- Medicinal chemistry modifications
- ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion) studies
- Preliminary toxicity screening
- Intellectual property landscape analysis

### Preclinical Development (1-2 years)

**In Vitro Studies**
- Target binding and potency
- Selectivity screening
- Mechanism of action studies
- ADME properties in cellular systems

**In Vivo Animal Studies**
- Pharmacokinetics (PK) in animals
- Pharmacodynamics (PD) and efficacy models
- Acute and chronic toxicology
- Reproductive toxicology
- Genotoxicity and carcinogenicity studies

**IND-Enabling Studies**
- GLP toxicology studies
- Safety pharmacology
- CMC development (synthesis, formulation, analytics)
- IND application preparation

### Clinical Development (6-7 years)

See [ClinicalTrials.mdx](./ClinicalTrials.mdx) for detailed coverage.

**Phase I** (1-2 years)
- First-in-human safety studies
- 20-100 healthy volunteers (or patients for oncology)
- Pharmacokinetics and dose escalation
- Maximum tolerated dose (MTD) determination

**Phase II** (2-3 years)
- Proof-of-concept and dose-finding
- 100-500 patients with target disease
- Efficacy signals and optimal dosing
- Safety profile expansion

**Phase III** (2-4 years)
- Pivotal efficacy and safety trials
- 1,000-5,000+ patients
- Multicenter, randomized, controlled trials
- Registration-enabling data

**NDA/BLA Submission and Review** (1-2 years)
- Regulatory dossier compilation
- FDA/EMA review process
- Advisory committee meetings
- Approval or complete response letter

**Phase IV** (Post-Marketing)
- Post-approval commitments
- Long-term safety monitoring
- Real-world evidence generation
- Expanded indications

### Development Economics

**Costs**
- Average cost to develop one approved drug: $2.6 billion
- Discovery and preclinical: $1.0 billion
- Clinical development: $1.4 billion
- Regulatory and approval: $200 million
- Includes cost of capital and failed programs

**Success Rates**
- Preclinical to approval: ~10% overall
- Phase I to approval: ~10%
- Phase II to approval: ~30%
- Phase III to approval: ~60%
- Varies significantly by therapeutic area

**Timelines**
- Discovery to IND: 3-6 years
- IND to NDA: 6-7 years
- NDA review: 1-2 years
- Total: 10-15 years

## Pharmaceutical Manufacturing

See [DrugManufacturing.mdx](./DrugManufacturing.mdx) for detailed coverage.

### Manufacturing Types

**Chemical Synthesis (Small Molecules)**
- Batch reactors for multi-step synthesis
- Continuous flow manufacturing (emerging)
- Purification and isolation
- Crystallization for API production

**Biological Manufacturing (Large Molecules)**
- Mammalian cell culture (CHO cells for antibodies)
- Bacterial fermentation (E. coli for proteins)
- Downstream processing (purification, formulation)
- Viral vector production (for gene therapies)

**Dosage Form Manufacturing**
- Solid oral (tablets, capsules): Granulation, compression, coating
- Injectables (sterile): Aseptic filling, lyophilization
- Biologics: Formulation, fill-finish in vials/syringes
- Advanced therapies: Cell processing, cryopreservation

### Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP)

**Quality System Requirements**
- 21 CFR Part 210/211 (U.S. FDA)
- ICH Q7 (API GMP)
- ICH Q10 (Pharmaceutical Quality System)
- EU GMP Annex 1 (Sterile Products)

**Key GMP Principles**
- Personnel qualification and training
- Premises and equipment qualification (IQ, OQ, PQ)
- Documentation and batch records
- Production and process controls
- Quality control testing and release
- Complaint handling and recalls
- Self-inspection and quality audits

## Pharmaceutical Regulations

The pharmaceutical industry is among the most heavily regulated globally:

### United States (FDA)

**Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER)**
- IND and NDA review
- Generic drug approvals (ANDAs)
- Postmarketing surveillance
- Manufacturing inspections

**Key Regulations**
- 21 CFR Part 11 - Electronic records and signatures
- 21 CFR Part 210/211 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice
- 21 CFR Part 312 - Investigational New Drug Application
- 21 CFR Part 314 - New Drug Application
- 21 CFR Part 320 - Bioavailability and bioequivalence

**FDA Programs**
- Fast Track - Expedited review for serious conditions
- Breakthrough Therapy - Significant improvement over existing therapies
- Accelerated Approval - Surrogate endpoints for serious diseases
- Priority Review - 6-month review timeline
- Orphan Drug Designation - Rare disease incentives

### European Union (EMA)

**European Medicines Agency**
- Centralized procedure (single authorization for EU)
- Mutual recognition procedure
- Decentralized procedure
- National procedures

**EU Directives and Regulations**
- Directive 2001/83/EC - Medicinal products for human use
- Regulation (EC) No 726/2004 - Authorization and supervision
- EU GMP Guidelines
- Falsified Medicines Directive

### International Harmonization

**ICH (International Council for Harmonisation)**
- ICH Q guidelines - Quality (Q1-Q14)
- ICH E guidelines - Efficacy (E1-E20)
- ICH S guidelines - Safety (S1-S12)
- ICH M guidelines - Multidisciplinary

- Medicare Part D (prescription drug coverage)
- Medicaid rebates and 340B pricing

- Average Wholesale Price (AWP)

**European Markets**
- Health Technology Assessment (HTA)
- Reference pricing and cost-effectiveness
- Price negotiations with national authorities
- Parallel trade considerations

**Emerging Markets**
- Tiered pricing strategies
- Local manufacturing requirements
- Technology transfer agreements
- Access programs and patient assistance

### Market Access Strategies

**Payer Access**
- Formulary placement and tier positioning
- Prior authorization requirements
- Step therapy protocols
- Quantity limits and utilization management

**Value Demonstration**
- Health economics and outcomes research (HEOR)
- Real-world evidence (RWE)
- Cost-effectiveness analysis
- Budget impact modeling
- Quality of life (QOL) studies
