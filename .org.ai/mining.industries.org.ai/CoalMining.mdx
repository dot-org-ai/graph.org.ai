# Coal Mining

Coal mining (NAICS 2121) encompasses the extraction of coal through surface and underground methods for use in electric power generation (steam coal) and steel production (metallurgical coal). Once the dominant energy source in the United States, coal production has declined significantly since 2008 due to competition from natural gas, renewable energy, and environmental regulations. However, coal mining remains a significant employer in Appalachia and the Western United States, with sophisticated operations employing advanced technology and stringent safety practices.

## Overview

The U.S. coal industry has undergone dramatic transformation over the past two decades:

- **Peak Production**: 1.2 billion short tons (2008)
- **Current Production**: ~600 million short tons/year (2024)
- **Employment**: ~40,000 coal miners (down from 90,000 in 2008)
- **Major Producing States**: Wyoming, West Virginia, Pennsylvania, Illinois, Montana
- **Exports**: 50-100 million tons/year (primarily metallurgical coal)
- **Domestic Consumption**: ~90% for electric power generation

### Coal Geology and Types

**Coal Formation**: Accumulated plant material buried and transformed over millions of years through increasing pressure and temperature.

**Coal Rank** (increasing carbon content and energy):

1. **Peat**: Precursor, not technically coal (50% carbon)
2. **Lignite**: Lowest rank, brownish, high moisture (25-35% carbon, 9,000-11,000 Btu/lb)
3. **Sub-bituminous**: Black, moderate energy (35-45% carbon, 8,300-11,500 Btu/lb)
4. **Bituminous**: Black, higher energy, most abundant (45-86% carbon, 10,500-15,500 Btu/lb)
5. **Anthracite**: Highest rank, hard, shiny (>86% carbon, 13,000-15,000 Btu/lb)

**Coal Seam Characteristics**:
- Thickness: 2 inches to 100+ feet (Western U.S.)
- Depth: Surface to 2,000+ feet underground
- Continuity: Miles of lateral extent in major basins
- Overburden: Overlying rock requiring removal (surface mining)

## Major U.S. Coal Producing Regions

### Powder River Basin (Wyoming/Montana)

**Geology**:
- Sub-bituminous coal in thick seams (50-100+ feet)
- Low sulfur content (<0.5%)
- Moderate energy content (8,500 Btu/lb)
- Multiple seams (Wyodak-Anderson, Big George, School)

**Production**: ~300 million tons/year (50% of U.S. total)

**Mining Method**: Large-scale surface mining (area mining)

**Major Mines**:
- North Antelope Rochelle (Peabody): Largest U.S. coal mine, 100+ million tons/year
- Black Thunder (Arch Resources)
- Belle Ayr, Eagle Butte, Cordero Rojo

**Transportation**: Unit trains (100-120 railcars) to power plants nationwide

**Economics**: Lowest cost production ($10-15/ton), high productivity, economies of scale

### Appalachia (West Virginia, Pennsylvania, Kentucky, Virginia)

**Geology**:
- Bituminous and anthracite coal
- High energy content (12,000-15,000 Btu/lb)
- Variable sulfur (low-sulfur and high-sulfur seams)
- Seam thickness: 3-12 feet typically
- Mountainous terrain

**Production**: ~200 million tons/year (declining)

**Mining Methods**: Both surface (mountaintop removal, contour mining) and underground (room-and-pillar, longwall)

**Coal Types**:
- **Steam Coal**: Electric power generation
- **Metallurgical Coal**: Coking coal for steel industry (premium pricing)

**Major Producers**: Consol Energy, Murray Energy, Blackjewel (bankrupt 2019)

**Challenges**: Seam thickness, terrain, environmental opposition, declining demand

### Illinois Basin (Illinois, Indiana, Western Kentucky)

**Geology**:
- Bituminous coal
- High sulfur content (2-5% sulfur)
- Moderate energy (11,000-12,500 Btu/lb)
- Flat-lying seams, 5-10 feet thick

**Production**: ~100 million tons/year

**Mining Methods**: Underground (room-and-pillar, longwall) and surface

**Markets**: Power plants with scrubbers (sulfur removal)

**Major Seams**: Herrin (No. 6), Springfield (No. 5)

### Other Regions

**Uinta Basin (Utah)**: Bituminous coal, underground and surface

**San Juan Basin (New Mexico/Colorado)**: Sub-bituminous, surface and underground

**Northern Appalachia (Pennsylvania)**: Anthracite (historic, limited current production), bituminous

## Surface Coal Mining (NAICS 212114)

Surface mining accounts for ~65% of U.S. coal production. Methods vary by terrain and geology.

### Area Mining (Powder River Basin)

**Principle**: Removing overburden in parallel strips to access flat or gently rolling coal seams.

**Process**:

1. **Overburden Removal**:
   - Drill blast-hole pattern (12-15 inch diameter, 50-100 ft depth)
   - Blast with ANFO or emulsion explosives
   - Dragline or shovel/truck excavation
   - Overburden placement in previously mined pit (backfilling)

2. **Coal Extraction**:
   - Front-end loader or hydraulic excavator
   - Haul trucks to processing plant
   - Selective mining of multiple seams

3. **Reclamation**:
   - Grading to approximate original contour
   - Topsoil replacement
   - Revegetation

**Equipment**:
- **Draglines**: Largest mobile land machines
  - Bucket capacity: 85-220 cubic yards
  - Boom length: 300-400 feet
  - Weight: 8,000-13,000 tons
  - Examples: "Big Muskie" (historic), Marion 8750
- **Electric Rope Shovels**: 100+ cubic yard capacity
- **Haul Trucks**: CAT 797F (400 tons), Komatsu 980E (400 tons)
- **Dozers**: CAT D11, Komatsu D575A

**Productivity**: 10-20 tons coal/employee-hour

**Stripping Ratio**: 3:1 to 10:1 (cubic yards overburden : tons coal)

### Contour Mining (Appalachia)

**Principle**: Following coal seam outcrop along hillside contour.

**Process**:
- Drill and blast overburden
- Excavate coal with front-end loaders
- Overburden cast to downslope
- Terraced highwall remains
- Reclamation challenges on steep slopes

**Highwall Mining**: Remote-controlled continuous miner deployed into highwall, extracting coal without human entry. Increases recovery from contour mines.

### Mountaintop Removal Mining (MTR)

**Principle**: Removing mountaintop to access multiple coal seams beneath.

**Process**:
- Drill and blast overburden (explosive used: 1,000+ tons ANFO per blast)
- Remove overburden with draglines, shovels, trucks
- Expose and mine coal seams (multiple seams, 100-500 ft vertical extent)
- Excess overburden placed in adjacent valley fills

**Scale**: Removal of 200-800 feet of mountaintop elevation

**Production**: High-volume operations, 5-20 million tons/year coal

**Reclamation**: Approximate original contour or variance for post-mining land use (commercial, pasture)

**Controversy**:
- Stream burial in valley fills
- Water quality impacts (selenium, sulfate, conductivity)
- Habitat destruction
- Community impacts
- Legal challenges and regulatory restrictions (Clean Water Act Section 404, Stream Protection Rule)

**Current Status**: Declining due to economics (low gas prices), environmental opposition, litigation

## Underground Coal Mining (NAICS 212115)

Underground mining accounts for ~35% of U.S. coal production, primarily in Appalachia and Illinois Basin. Higher cost than surface but accesses deeper reserves.

### Room-and-Pillar Mining

**Principle**: Excavating rooms with pillars of coal left for roof support.

**Layout**:
- Entries: Main tunnels (20-25 ft wide, 6-8 ft high)
- Crosscuts: Connecting entries
- Rooms: Excavated spaces between pillars (40-60 ft wide)
- Pillars: Coal left for support (40-80 ft square)
- Recovery: 50-60% of coal (conventional), up to 75% with retreat mining

**Equipment**:

1. **Continuous Miner**:
   - Rotating drum with carbide cutting bits
   - Cutting rate: 5-12 tons/min
   - Loading onto shuttle car or conveyor
   - Examples: Joy 12CM27, CAT EL3000

2. **Shuttle Car**:
   - Battery-powered haulage
   - Capacity: 10-20 tons
   - Transport coal to feeder breaker

3. **Roof Bolter**:
   - Installing roof support bolts
   - Mechanical or resin-anchored bolts
   - 4-6 ft length, 4-6 ft spacing
   - Dual-boom or triple-boom bolters

4. **Feeder Breaker**:
   - Breaking oversize coal
   - Feeding belt conveyor system

5. **Belt Conveyors**:
   - Transporting coal to surface
   - 36-60 inch width
   - 400-800 ft/min speed

**Retreat Mining**:
- After development, miners retreat while extracting pillars
- Controlled roof collapse behind
- 70-80% total recovery
- Subsidence at surface
- Careful planning to prevent roof collapse on active sections

### Longwall Mining

**Principle**: Shearer cuts coal from long face (800-1,500 ft) while hydraulic shields support roof. Controlled roof collapse behind shields.

**Layout**:
- Longwall panel: 600-1,200 ft wide, 1-3 miles long
- Gate roads: Entries on each side of panel for ventilation, haulage
- Face: Continuous coal extraction along panel width

**Equipment**:

1. **Shearer**:
   - Double-drum cutter on armored face conveyor (AFC)
   - Cutting drums: 6-10 ft diameter
   - Cutting rate: 15-25 tons/min
   - Bi-directional cutting

2. **Armored Face Conveyor (AFC)**:
   - Scraper chain conveyor along coal face
   - 600-1,000 ton/hour capacity

3. **Hydraulic Roof Supports (Shields)**:
   - Self-advancing powered supports
   - 800-1,500 ton capacity per shield
   - 150-200 shields per longwall
   - Advance with shearer cutting cycle

4. **Stage Loader**:
   - Transferring coal from AFC to belt conveyor

5. **Crusher**:
   - Reducing coal size for belt conveyor

6. **Belt Conveyor System**:
   - Transporting coal to surface

**Productivity**: 20-40 tons coal/employee-hour (highest of any method)

**Recovery**: 75-90% of panel coal

**Subsidence**: Inevitable, 50-90% of seam thickness, managed through planning

**Capital Cost**: $50-150 million for longwall system

**Applications**: Thick seams (>5 ft), extensive reserves, flat-lying or gently dipping

**U.S. Longwall Mines**: ~30 active longwalls, primarily Appalachia and Illinois Basin

### Underground Mine Development

**Shaft Mines**: Vertical shaft for access (elevators, hoisting)

**Slope Mines**: Inclined tunnel following seam or overburden

**Drift Mines**: Horizontal entry from hillside (Appalachia)

**Development Timeline**: 2-5 years before production

**Capital Cost**: $100-500 million for major underground mine

## Coal Preparation (Processing)

**Purpose**: Removing impurities (rock, sulfur, ash) to meet specifications for customers (power plants, steel mills).

**Process Steps**:

1. **Sizing**:
   - Crushing to 6-inch top size
   - Screening into size fractions (coarse, intermediate, fine)

2. **Density Separation**:
   - **Jigs**: Pulsating water, dense refuse settles
   - **Dense Medium Cyclones**: Heavy media (magnetite slurry), specific gravity separation
   - **Spirals**: Gravity separation for fine coal (-1 mm)

3. **Dewatering**:
   - **Screens**: Coarse coal
   - **Centrifuges**: Fine coal
   - **Thickeners**: Slurry dewatering
   - **Thermal Dryers**: Low-moisture product (export met coal)

4. **Refuse Disposal**:
   - Coarse refuse: Valley fills, impoundments
   - Fine refuse slurry: Impoundments (tailings dams)

**Product Specifications**:
- **Steam Coal**: 10,000-14,000 Btu/lb, ash <10%, sulfur <3% (varies by market)
- **Metallurgical Coal**: Low ash (<8%), low sulfur (<1%), coking properties

**Prep Plant Yield**: 70-85% clean coal (varies by raw coal quality)

## Coal Transportation

**Unit Trains**:
- 100-120 railcars, 10,000-15,000 tons coal
- Origin-to-destination service
- Powder River Basin to power plants nationwide (1,000+ miles)
- Transportation cost: $0.01-0.03/ton-mile

**Barge Transport**:
- Illinois Basin and Appalachia via river systems (Ohio, Mississippi)
- 1,500 ton barges in 15-barge tows
- Lower cost than rail for accessible locations

**Truck Transport**:
- Short haul to rail loadout or power plant (<100 miles)
- 20-25 tons per truck

**Coal Slurry Pipeline**:
- Historic (Black Mesa pipeline, closed 2005)
- Limited current use due to water requirements, economics

**Export Terminals**:
- **East Coast**: Baltimore (Curtis Bay, CNX), Norfolk (Lambert's Point, Dominion)
- **Gulf Coast**: New Orleans, Mobile
- Vessel loading: 50,000-180,000 ton capacity (Capesize, Panamax)

## Mine Safety and Health

### Major Hazards

**Underground Mining**:

1. **Methane Explosions**:
   - Coal seams release methane (natural gas)
   - Lower explosive limit: 5% methane in air
   - Ignition sources: Sparks, flame, hot surfaces
   - Disasters: Upper Big Branch (2010, 29 deaths), Sago Mine (2006, 12 deaths)

2. **Coal Dust Explosions**:
   - Airborne coal dust ignites, propagates explosion
   - Rock dusting: Applying limestone dust to inert coal dust
   - Water sprays on continuous miners

3. **Roof Falls**:
   - Leading cause of injuries and fatalities
   - Roof bolting, pillar design, monitoring (roof-to-floor convergence)

4. **Respirable Coal Dust (Black Lung)**:
   - Pneumoconiosis from chronic coal dust exposure
   - Progressive, irreversible lung disease
   - 10,000+ current cases, resurgence in Appalachia
   - Dust controls: Water sprays, ventilation, continuous personal dust monitors

**Surface Mining**:
- Equipment accidents (haul trucks, dozers)
- Highwall failures
- Blasting accidents
- Electrical hazards

### Mine Safety and Health Administration (MSHA)

**Regulatory Authority**: U.S. Department of Labor

**Key Regulations** (30 CFR):
- Part 48: Training and retraining of miners
- Part 75: Underground coal mines (safety and health standards)
- Part 77: Surface coal mines

**Inspections**:
- Underground: Quarterly (4 times/year)
- Surface: Semiannual (2 times/year)
- Additional spot inspections

**Enforcement**:
- Citations for violations
- Orders to withdraw miners for imminent danger
- Civil penalties
- Pattern of Violations (POV): Enhanced oversight

**Training Requirements**:
- New miner: 40 hours (24 hours classroom + 16 hours on-the-job)
- Experienced miner (new task): 8 hours
- Annual refresher: 8 hours
- Hazard training for task changes

### Ventilation (Underground Mines)

**Purpose**: Diluting and removing methane, coal dust, diesel exhaust

**Airflow Requirements**: 3,000-9,000 cubic feet per minute (cfm) per working section

**Main Fans**:
- Centrifugal or axial fans
- Capacity: 100,000-1,000,000 cfm
- Backup power for emergency

**Auxiliary Fans**:
- Ventilating dead-end entries and working sections
- Forcing or exhausting air

**Methane Monitoring**:
- Continuous monitors on mining equipment
- Handheld methanometers
- Tube bundle systems for longwall faces
- De-energize equipment at 1% methane, evacuate at 2%

**Explosion-Proof Equipment**:
- Electrical equipment rated for methane atmospheres

## Environmental Impacts and Regulation

### Surface Mining Control and Reclamation Act (SMCRA)

**Enacted**: 1977

**Regulatory Agency**: Office of Surface Mining Reclamation and Enforcement (OSMRE), U.S. Department of Interior

**Key Requirements**:

1. **Permit Application**:
   - Mine plan and operations
   - Environmental baseline (soils, vegetation, hydrology, wildlife)
   - Reclamation plan
   - Bonding for reclamation costs

2. **Mining Operations**:
   - Minimize disturbance
   - Prevent water pollution (sediment control, diversions)
   - Protect hydrologic balance
   - Topsoil segregation and stockpiling

3. **Reclamation**:
   - Backfilling and grading to approximate original contour (AOC)
   - Topsoil replacement (minimum 4 ft depth)
   - Revegetation with native or approved species
   - Success standards (ground cover, productivity)

4. **Bond Release**:
   - Phase I: Backfilling and grading complete
   - Phase II: Revegetation established (5 years)
   - Phase III: Vegetation success (10 years total)

**Variances**: Alternative post-mining land use (industrial, commercial, pasture)

**Enforcement**: OSMRE oversight, state programs (primacy), inspections, penalties

### Water Quality Impacts

**Acid Mine Drainage (AMD)**:
- Sulfide mineral (pyrite FeS2) oxidation
- Sulfuric acid formation, low pH (2-4)
- Metal dissolution (iron, aluminum, manganese)
- Stream impacts: Fish kills, ecosystem damage

**Treatment**:
- Limestone neutralization (raising pH)
- Passive treatment systems (wetlands, anoxic limestone drains)
- Active treatment plants (chemical dosing, sedimentation)

**Selenium**:
- Mobilized from overburden, coal
- Toxic to aquatic life at low concentrations (µg/L)
- Appalachia valley fill discharge
- Biological treatment (algae, bacteria)

**Sedimentation**:
- Erosion from disturbed areas
- Sediment ponds, silt fences, revegetation

**Subsidence Impacts (Underground)**:
- Surface cracking, structure damage
- Hydrologic changes (stream flow, wells)
- Longwall mining: Predictable, managed
- Pillar collapse: Sudden, hazardous

### Clean Water Act Compliance

**NPDES Permits**: Point source discharges (sediment ponds, treatment plants)

**Section 404 Permits**: Dredge and fill in waters of the U.S. (valley fills)

**Effluent Limitations**: Iron, manganese, pH, total suspended solids, selenium

**Best Management Practices (BMPs)**: Erosion control, sediment trapping

### Air Quality

**Fugitive Dust**:
- Haul roads, blasting, crushing
- Water sprays, chemical dust suppressants

**Methane Emissions**:
- Degasification wells in gassy seams
- Venting or utilization (power generation)

**Coal Processing Emissions**:
- Thermal dryers: Particulates, VOCs

## Coal Markets and Economics

### Steam Coal (Electric Power)

**U.S. Consumption**: ~500 million tons/year for electricity (declining)

**Market Drivers**:
- Natural gas prices (low gas displacing coal)
- Renewable energy growth (wind, solar)
- Coal plant retirements (200+ GW retired since 2010)
- Environmental regulations (mercury, NOx, SO2, CO2)

**Regional Markets**:
- Powder River Basin coal: Low sulfur, low cost, Midwest/South
- Appalachia coal: Higher Btu, East Coast and export
- Illinois Basin: High sulfur, scrubber-equipped plants

**Pricing**: $30-60/ton (varies by quality, transportation)

### Metallurgical Coal (Steel Industry)

**U.S. Production**: 50-70 million tons/year

**Types**:
- **Hard Coking Coal**: Premium quality for blast furnaces, low ash/sulfur
- **Semi-Soft Coking Coal**: Blending coal
- **PCI Coal**: Pulverized coal injection in blast furnace

**Markets**:
- Domestic steel mills (declining)
- Export to Asia (China, India, Japan, South Korea), Europe, Brazil

**Pricing**: $100-300/ton (volatile, premium to steam coal)

**Major Exporters**: Appalachia (West Virginia, Virginia), Alabama

### Coal Industry Economics

**Operating Costs**:
- Surface mining (Powder River Basin): $10-15/ton
- Surface mining (Appalachia): $30-50/ton
- Underground (room-and-pillar): $40-70/ton
- Underground (longwall): $30-50/ton

**Capital Costs**:
- Surface mine: $50-200 million
- Underground mine: $100-500 million
- Prep plant: $50-150 million

**Employment Productivity**: 10-20 tons coal/employee-hour (varies by method, region)

**Mine Life**: 10-40 years (depending on reserves, method)

## Coal Industry Transition

### Decline and Restructuring

**Bankruptcies** (2015-2020):
- Peabody Energy, Arch Coal, Alpha Natural Resources, Blackjewel, Murray Energy

**Restructuring**: Debt reduction, asset sales, closures

**Employment**: Decline from 90,000 (2008) to 40,000 (2024)

### Environmental and Social Challenges

**Climate Change**: Coal combustion major source of CO2 emissions

**Community Impacts**: Economic dislocation in coal-dependent regions

**Just Transition**: Programs for retraining, economic diversification
