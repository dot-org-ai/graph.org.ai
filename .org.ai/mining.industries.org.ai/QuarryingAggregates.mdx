# Quarrying and Aggregate Mining

Quarrying and aggregate mining (NAICS 2123) encompasses the extraction and processing of nonmetallic mineral resources used primarily in construction, including crushed stone, gravel, sand, dimension stone, and specialty minerals. This sector supplies essential raw materials for concrete, asphalt, fill, riprap, and structural applications in buildings, transportation infrastructure, and water systems.

## Overview

The U.S. aggregate and quarrying industry is characterized by:

- **Production Volume**: ~2 billion tons annually (aggregate + stone + sand-gravel)
- **Economic Value**: ~$20-25 billion annual revenue
- **Employment**: ~70,000-80,000 workers in quarrying and mining
- **Geographic Distribution**: Operations in all 50 states, driven by proximity to demand
- **Diverse Products**: Crushed stone, sand, gravel, dimension stone, specialty minerals
- **Capital Intensity**: Moderate compared to metal mining, simple open-pit methods dominate
- **Environmental Sensitivity**: Water management, dust control, habitat concerns

### Industry Characteristics

**Local Market Focus**: Transportation cost (bulk, low value) limits economic hauling distance to 50-150 miles, creating local supply-demand dynamics

**Vertical Integration**: Many aggregates producers backward-integrated with asphalt plants, ready-mix concrete plants to control supply chain

**Regulatory Environment**: State and local permitting, reclamation requirements vary significantly; federal permitting limited to specific contexts

**Technology Simplicity**: Well-established equipment and processes, incremental innovation in efficiency and environmental management

## Stone Quarrying and Crushing (NAICS 212310)

### Dimension Stone Quarrying

**Granite Quarrying**:
- Extracted for building facades, monuments, countertops, decorative applications
- Selective mining of high-quality blocks
- Methods: Wire saw cutting, diamond blade sawing
- Production: 300,000-400,000 tons annually in U.S.

**Marble and Limestone Quarrying**:
- Marble: Metamorphic limestone, valued for sculpture and architectural stone
- Limestone: Sedimentary rock, lighter weight, easier carving
- Applications: Interior finishes, decorative elements, specialty architectural features

**Slate Quarrying**:
- Naturally fissile, splits along planes into roofing, flooring, cladding
- Concentrated in Vermont, Pennsylvania, New York
- Premium product, skilled splitting and processing required
- Production: ~100,000 tons annually

**Extraction Methods**:
- Quarry pit: Open excavation, typically shallow (20-50 feet)
- Horizontal layers: Quarrying progresses downward, leaving terraces
- Block extraction: Splitting with wedges, chains, or diamond wire saws
- Finishing: Sawing, polishing, edge profiling

**Economics**:
- Capital cost: $5-20 million for quarry development
- Operating cost: $30-100/ton depending on stone type and finish
- Market: Premium pricing for decorative stone ($100-500/ton) vs. building stone ($30-80/ton)
- Mine life: 20-50 years typical for commercial operation

### Crushed and Broken Stone (NAICS 212311)

**Granite Crushing**:
- Low-cost extraction from lower-quality blocks unsuitable for dimension stone
- Basic crushing: Jaw crusher, cone crusher producing coarse aggregate
- Size gradation: #57 stone (1-1.5 inches), #411 (0.5-1 inches), fine chips

**Limestone Crushing**:
- Most abundant crushed stone material in U.S.
- Production: ~700 million tons annually
- Applications: Concrete aggregate, railroad ballast, agricultural lime, chemical feedstock

**Sandstone and Silica Stone Crushing**:
- Used for nonslip applications, specialty concrete
- Glass-quality silica: Processed to very high purity

**Crushing Process**:

1. **Primary Crushing**:
   - Jaw crusher: Fixed and moving jaws, feed 12-24 inches, product 2-4 inches
   - Gyratory crusher: Cone rotating eccentrically, higher capacity
   - Impact crushers: Belt-fed material, high-speed impact breaking

2. **Secondary and Tertiary Crushing**:
   - Cone crushers: Progressively finer sizing (1-2 inches, then <1 inch)
   - Impact crushers: Reducing fines generation (desirable for concrete)
   - Horizontal shaft impactors (HSI): Alternative secondary crushing

3. **Screening and Sizing**:
   - Vibrating screens: 2-4 deck (multiple sizes), separate crushed stone by size
   - Scalping: Removing oversize before crushing reduces energy
   - Classifiers: Removing fine dust (-200 mesh) improves concrete quality

4. **Dewatering**:
   - Wet production: Water-assisted crushing reduces dust
   - Dewatering screens: Removing moisture before stockpiling
   - Water recycling: Returning process water to reduce freshwater demand

**Equipment Specifications**:
- Primary jaw crusher: 400+ ton/hour capacity
- Secondary cone crusher: 200-400 ton/hour
- Screens: 300-800 ton/hour
- Total system: 200-400 ton/hour typical

**Product Specifications**:
- Gradation: ASTM C33 defines standard sizes for concrete aggregate
- Durability: LA abrasion test (percent loss) should be <40% for concrete
- Soundness: Freeze-thaw resistance tested with sodium sulfate
- Cleanness: Dust content (passing #200 sieve) limits, typically <3% for concrete
- Elongation: Length-to-thickness ratio for angularity

**Production Economics**:
- Capital cost: $10-50 million for crushing plant
- Operating cost: $3-8/ton crushing and screening
- Mine cost: $0.5-2/ton for material (low value)
- Total delivered cost: $8-15/ton depending on haul distance
- Production: 500,000-2 million tons/year for large operation

## Sand and Gravel Mining (NAICS 212320)

### Silica Sand

**Applications**:
- Glass-quality (99%+ SiO2): Glass container, flat glass, specialty optical
- Foundry sand (95-99% SiO2): Metal casting molds
- Industrial sand (90-95% SiO2): Abrasives, filtration, frac sand
- Construction sand (<90%): Concrete, mortar, fill

**Major Deposits**:
- Wisconsin: World's largest silica sand deposits
- Minnesota, Michigan: Significant deposits
- Illinois, Oklahoma, Missouri: Foundry and industrial sand

**Mining Methods**:
- Open-pit: Shallow excavation, 20-50 feet typical
- Wet mining: Dredging from river beds, submerged deposits
- Underground: Limited (Ottawa sand, Michigan historical)

**Processing**:

1. **Extraction and Initial Handling**:
   - Front-end loader or hydraulic excavator
   - Truck or conveyor to processing plant
   - Removing larger particles (debris)

2. **Screening and Classification**:
   - Vibrating screens or spiral classifiers
   - Separating into size fractions: 70-200 mesh typical
   - Removal of fine slimes (<200 mesh)

3. **Washing** (for higher-grade products):
   - Washing plant with agitator or screw classifier
   - Water removes clay and fine particles
   - Dewatering: Screens, hydrocyclones, or thermal dryers

4. **Drying** (glass-quality and foundry sand):
   - Rotary dryers at 300-400°F
   - Moisture reduced to <0.5%
   - Energy cost: $3-10/ton depending on moisture content

5. **Classification by Size**:
   - Air separators: Removing fines and large particles
   - Vibratory screens: Fine fractionation (100-200-325 mesh)

**Product Specifications**:
- Glass sand: 99.5%+ SiO2, <0.1% iron oxide, <0.05% clay, white color
- Foundry sand: 98-99% SiO2, medium size (100-170 mesh), low fines
- Frac sand: 98%+ SiO2, round to subround shape, 20-40 mesh (Permian sand: 100 mesh)
- Construction sand: 95%+ SiO2, includes fines for mortar, 0.1-0.6 mm typical

**Production Economics**:
- Capital cost (wet): $5-20 million for sand operation
- Capital cost (dry with drying): $15-50 million
- Operating cost: $3-10/ton depending on processing intensity
- Production: 500,000-3 million tons/year typical
- Pricing: Construction sand $5-15/ton, foundry $20-40/ton, glass sand $50-150/ton

### Gravel and Aggregate

**Natural Gravel Deposits**:
- Glacial deposits (northern U.S., Great Lakes)
- Alluvial deposits (river beds, floodplains)
- Residual deposits (weathered rock)

**Applications**:
- Construction aggregate (concrete, asphalt, base course)
- Road base and sub-base
- Fill material
- Railroad ballast
- Riprap and erosion control

**Mining Methods**:
- Wet pit (above or below water table):
  - Excavator and haul trucks
  - Dredging (bucket dredge, cutter dredge) from submerged pits
  - Processing on shore

- Dry pit:
  - Excavation and truck haulage
  - Simpler processing (minimal water management)

**Processing**:

1. **Primary Crushing** (optional, for bedrock gravel):
   - Jaw or cone crusher for reducing oversize

2. **Screening and Sizing**:
   - Vibrating screens separate natural grades
   - 3/4", 1/2", #4 coarse sizes typical
   - Removal of <#4 (sand)

3. **Washing** (for quality concerns):
   - Increasing durability and reducing clay
   - Water reduces mud content
   - Dewatering for stockpiling

4. **Crushing** (if producing manufactured aggregate):
   - Converting to angular shape, specific sizing
   - Secondary/tertiary crushing identical to stone crushing

**Product Gradation**:
- Natural gravel: Mixed sizes 2 inches down
- #57 stone: 1-1.5 inches (crushed)
- #411 aggregate: Well-graded mix 0.5-1 inches
- Base course: Large top size with sand fines for compaction

**Production Economics**:
- Capital cost: $3-15 million for excavation and simple processing
- Operating cost: $0.5-3/ton mining, $1-3/ton processing
- Total cost: $2-6/ton delivered
- Production: 1-5 million tons/year typical

### Bank Run and Crushed Gravel

**Bank Run Material**:
- Direct mining of natural deposit, minimal processing
- Mix of gravel, sand, clay as-received
- Lowest cost, acceptable for base course, fill
- Cost: $2-5/ton

**Crushed Gravel**:
- Processed to remove fines, crush oversized material
- Improved quality over bank run
- Cost: $5-10/ton

## Other Nonmetallic Minerals

### Clay and Ceramic Minerals

**Applications**:
- Ceramics: Pottery, tableware, decorative items
- Brick and tile: Building materials
- Pottery: Fine art and functional pieces
- Bentonite: Drilling mud additive, industrial absorbent, cat litter
- Fuller's earth: Bleaching earth, absorbent

**Production**: 30-40 million tons annually in U.S.

**Mining**: Simple open-pit excavation, surface deposits

**Processing**: Crushing, screening, beneficiation for specific applications

### Gypsum

**Applications**:
- Wallboard (drywall): 70% of U.S. consumption
- Plaster: Finish coat, molding
- Cement additive: Controlling set time
- Soil amendment: Replacing sulfur, improving structure

**Major Deposits**: Texas, Oklahoma, California, Iowa

**Production**: 20+ million tons annually

**Mining**: Open-pit, underground mines in some cases

**Processing**: Crushing, grinding, calcination (heating to produce plaster)

### Industrial Minerals

**Talc**:
- Applications: Cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, plastics, coatings
- Production: ~600,000 tons annually

**Feldspar**:
- Applications: Glass, ceramics, fillers
- Production: ~600,000 tons annually

**Mica**:
- Applications: Electrical insulation, roofing, cosmetics
- Production: Sheet mica (specialty) and scrap mica

**Soda Ash (Trona)**:
- Wyoming Green River Formation major source
- Applications: Glass (60%), chemicals, detergents
- Production: ~10 million tons annually

## Environmental Management

### Dust Control

**Sources**:
- Blasting (bedrock quarries)
- Material handling (loading, screening, conveying)
- Truck traffic on haul roads
- Product stockpiles

**Controls**:
- Water spraying: Blasting area, haul roads, stockpiles
- Chemical dust suppressants: Polymer or tackier compounds
- Wheel wash stations: Reducing track-out to public roads
- Pavement: Stabilized roads or asphalt reduce erosion
- Enclosures: Primary crushing, conveyor tunnels for sensitive areas

**Particulate Monitoring**:
- Ambient air sampling: Measuring PM2.5, PM10
- Compliance with state air quality standards
- Fugitive dust permits in some states/regions

### Water Management

**Pit Dewatering**:
- Pumping groundwater from below water table
- Discharging to surface water or disposal well
- NPDES permits required for surface discharge
- Water quality monitoring (sediment, chemistry)

**Process Water**:
- Sand-gravel washing: Recycling water to reduce freshwater demand
- Thickeners or settling tanks: Recovering water, settling solids
- Closed-loop systems: Minimizing external discharge

**Storm Water**:
- Erosion and sediment control: Silt fences, sediment ponds, slope protection
- Runoff management: Directing to sediment ponds before release
- Reclamation: Restored topography channels water appropriately

### Habitat and Ecosystem Impact

**Pre-Mining Assessment**:
- Wildlife habitat mapping: Identifying critical areas
- Wetland delineation: Areas requiring CWA Section 404 permits
- Species surveys: Endangered species presence/absence
- Groundwater dependence: Identifying sensitive ecosystems

**Mitigation**:
- Avoidance: Buffers around sensitive habitat
- Timing restrictions: Avoiding breeding/migration seasons
- Habitat creation: Restoring alternative habitat
- Wetland mitigation banks: Purchasing credits for unavoidable impacts

### Noise

**Sources**:
- Blasting: Highest noise levels
- Crushing equipment: Continuous operation
- Truck traffic: Haul road noise

**Controls**:
- Blast delay detonators: Reducing peak noise pressure
- Equipment silencing: Mufflers, enclosures
- Operational restrictions: Limited hours, setback distances
- Noise barriers: Berms or walls for sensitive receptors

## Reclamation and Afteruse

### Reclamation Obligations

**Bonding Requirements**:
- Most states require financial assurance for reclamation
- Performance bond: Guaranteeing completion of reclamation plan
- Trust funds or letters of credit held by regulatory agency

**Reclamation Plan Components**:
- Final grading: Slope angles, drainage
- Topsoil replacement: Soil depth appropriate for reuse
- Revegetation: Native or approved species, success criteria
- Afteruse: Intended post-mining land use (wildlife habitat, agriculture, recreation)

### Common Afteruses

**Water Features**:
- Lakes (recreational, habitat, stormwater retention)
- Wetlands (created habitat, water quality treatment)
- Ponds (golf course water hazards, stormwater management)

**Wildlife Habitat**:
- Created grassland, woodland, wetland habitat
- Management for specific species (waterfowl, prairie birds, deer)
- Long-term stewardship agreements

**Recreation**:
- Lakes for boating, fishing, swimming
- Parks with hiking, picnicking facilities
- Golf courses
- Sporting facilities

**Agriculture**:
- Restored farmland (prime agricultural land)
- Pasture (grazing land)
- Restored topography, soil replacement critical

**Retail and Commercial**:
- Retail shopping centers, office parks
- Warehouses, distribution centers
- Parking areas, industrial uses

**Community Facilities**:
- Landfill sites
- Wastewater treatment facilities
- Fire/emergency training grounds

## Market Economics

### Pricing Factors

**Material Type**:
- Crushed stone: $8-15/ton (bulk)
- Sand: $10-20/ton
- Gravel: $5-12/ton
- Dimension stone: $100-500+/ton depending on type/finish

**Transportation Distance**:
- Typical economic radius: 50-150 miles
- Transportation cost: $0.50-2/ton-mile depending on load, distance
- High-value specialty aggregate: Longer hauls economically justified

**Local Supply-Demand**:
- Regional market dynamics (construction activity, population growth)
- Seasonal variation (winter construction slowdown in cold climates)
- Infrastructure projects (highways, bridges) drive temporary demand

**Quality Premiums**:
- Glass-quality silica: 5-10x cost of construction sand
- Specialty minerals: Significant premiums for purity, particle shape
- Reclaimed aggregates: Lower cost but limited availability

### Industry Consolidation

**Large Operators**:
- Vulcan Materials: Largest aggregate producer in U.S., ~150 operations
- Martin Marietta Materials: Second largest, ~100+ operations
- Oldcastle Materials (Mitsubishi): Regional operator
- Independent operators: Still majority of operations, local markets

**Vertical Integration**:
- Aggregates + ready-mix concrete plants: Controlling supply
- Aggregates + hot-mix asphalt plants: Captive market
- Aggregates + construction: Building infrastructure internally

## Regulatory Framework

### Federal (Limited)

**Clean Water Act**:
- Section 404 permits: Dredging, fill in waters of U.S. (wetlands, streams)
- Mitigation requirements for impacts
- No net loss of wetlands policy

**Endangered Species Act**:
- Section 7 consultation: If federal permitting involved
- Habitat protection for listed species

### State Level

**State Aggregate Regulations**:
- Varying permit processes and requirements by state
- Environmental review (sometimes NEPA equivalent)
- Reclamation and bonding requirements
- Financial assurance mechanisms

**Water Law**:
- Groundwater and surface water rights
- Discharge standards and permits
- Wetland protection (state-level, varies widely)

### Local/County/Municipal

**Zoning**:
- Conditional use permits for mining operations
- Setback requirements from homes, schools, water supplies
- Hours of operation restrictions
- Truck traffic routes

**Noise and Air**:
- Local noise ordinances
- Air quality compliance (state standards)
