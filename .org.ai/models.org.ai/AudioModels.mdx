# Audio Models

Audio models enable machines to understand and generate speech and sound, powering applications from voice assistants to transcription services, music generation, and acoustic analytics. Modern audio models leverage transformer architectures and attention mechanisms to achieve unprecedented accuracy in speech understanding and synthesis.

## Overview

Audio processing involves converting continuous acoustic signals into meaningful representations. Audio models work with various modalities including speech, music, environmental sounds, and speaker characteristics, enabling both understanding and generation tasks.

## Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)

### End-to-End Speech Recognition

**Wav2Vec 2.0 (Meta/Facebook)**
- Self-supervised speech representation learning
- Contrastive learning on unlabeled audio
- State-of-the-art accuracy with fine-tuning
- Multilingual variant (XLSR-53)
- Foundation for many downstream tasks

**HuBERT (Hidden Unit BERT)**
- Self-supervised learning from hidden units
- Iterative refinement approach
- Strong multilingual capability
- Efficient fine-tuning
- Robust acoustic modeling

**Whisper (OpenAI)**
- Robust speech recognition
- Trained on 680K hours of multilingual audio
- Multilingual and code-switching capability
- Automatic punctuation and capitalization
- No need for language identification

**Conformer Architecture**
- Combines convolution and transformer
- Better time-frequency modeling
- Competitive with hybrid systems
- Efficient and practical
- Industry-standard architecture

### Traditional ASR Systems

**HMM-GMM (Hidden Markov Models)**
- Classic speech recognition approach
- Acoustic and language model separation
- Computationally light
- Still used in embedded systems
- Well-understood and interpretable

**Hybrid DNN-HMM Systems**
- Deep neural networks for acoustics
- HMM for temporal modeling
- Practical production systems
- Good balance of accuracy and efficiency
- Kaldi toolkit standard

**Attention-Based Models**
- Sequence-to-sequence with attention
- Eliminated hard alignments
- End-to-end training
- Listen, Attend and Tell approach
- Transformer variants

### Streaming and Real-Time ASR

**Transducer Architecture**
- Joint optimization of prediction and alignment
- Online decoding capability
- Low latency speech recognition
- RNN-T (Recurrent Neural Network Transducer)
- Streaming transformer variants

**Squeezeformer and Conformer Variants**
- Efficient streaming models
- Minimal context requirements
- Low latency inference
- Mobile-friendly designs
- Real-time capability

### Multilingual and Code-Switching ASR

**Multilingual Models**
- Shared representations across languages
- Cross-lingual transfer learning

**Code-Switching Recognition**

## Acoustic Features and Representation

**Mel-Spectrogram**
- Time-frequency representation
- Human hearing aligned
- Loss of phase information
- Standard DL input
- Visualization friendly

**MFCC (Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients)**
- Classic speech feature
- Robustness to background noise
- Reduced dimensionality
- Legacy system standard
- Still used in traditional ASR

**CQT (Constant-Q Transform)**
- Logarithmic frequency spacing
- Music analysis advantage
- Phase information retention
- Energy preservation
- Musical representation
