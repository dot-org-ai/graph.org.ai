# Contract Research

Third-party research, development, and testing services provided to pharmaceutical, biotechnology, medical device, chemical, and other technology companies on a contract or fee-for-service basis.

## Overview

Contract research organizations (CROs) and testing laboratories provide specialized R&D capabilities to companies that outsource these functions rather than conducting them in-house. This industry has grown dramatically as companies focus on core competencies and leverage external expertise, facilities, and capacity.

### Key Characteristics

- **Service-Based Business Model**: Fee-for-service or project-based contracts
- **Specialized Expertise**: Deep capabilities in specific research domains
- **Scalable Capacity**: Ability to handle varying workloads
- **Regulatory Compliance**: GLP, GMP, GCP certifications
- **Asset-Light for Clients**: Avoid capital investment in facilities
- **Risk Sharing**: Fixed-price or risk-sharing arrangements

## Business Models

### Pricing Structures

**Fee-for-Service**
- Hourly rates: $100-$500 per hour depending on expertise
- Fixed-price projects: Based on scope and deliverables
- Milestone-based payments
- Unit pricing (per sample, per assay)

**Full-Time Equivalent (FTE)**
- Dedicated staff allocated to client
- Monthly or annual fees
- Typical cost: $200K-$400K per FTE annually
- Common for long-term partnerships

**Risk-Sharing Models**
- Equity stakes in client companies
- Success-based fees
- Royalties on commercialized products
- Hybrid fee + equity

**Functional Service Provider (FSP)**
- Outsourced team embedded with client
- Dedicated capacity
- More integrated than traditional outsourcing

### Contract Types

**Time and Materials**
- Scope flexibility
- Pay as you go
- Suitable for exploratory work

**Fixed-Price Contracts**
- Well-defined scope
- Budget certainty for client
- CRO bears execution risk
- Typical for routine testing

**Retainer Agreements**
- Ongoing relationship
- Guaranteed capacity
- Predictable revenue for CRO

## Regulatory Compliance & Certifications

### Good Laboratory Practice (GLP)

**FDA 21 CFR Part 58**
- U.S. regulations for nonclinical studies
- Study protocols and SOPs
- Quality assurance unit
- Study directors and responsibilities
- Equipment calibration and maintenance
- Animal care standards

**OECD GLP**
- International harmonization
- Mutual acceptance of data
- Reduces duplicative testing

### Good Clinical Practice (GCP)

**ICH-GCP (E6)**
- International standard for clinical trials
- Protecting rights, safety, and well-being of subjects
- Ensuring data quality and integrity
- Investigator qualifications
- Informed consent
- Ethics committee review

**FDA Inspections**
- For-cause and routine inspections
- Form 483 observations
- Warning letters for serious violations
- Impact on CRO reputation

### Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP)

**21 CFR Parts 210 & 211**
- For clinical and commercial manufacturing
- Quality systems
- Process validation
- Contamination control
- Documentation and records

### ISO Certifications

**ISO/IEC 17025**: Testing and calibration laboratories
**ISO 9001**: Quality management systems
**ISO 13485**: Medical device quality management
**ISO 14001**: Environmental management

### Accreditations

- **CAP** (College of American Pathologists): Clinical labs
- **CLIA** (Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments)
- **AAALAC** (Association for Assessment and Accreditation of Laboratory Animal Care)
