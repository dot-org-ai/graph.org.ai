# Applied Research

Original investigation undertaken to acquire new knowledge with specific practical applications and commercial objectives in mind, bridging the gap between basic scientific discovery and industrial development.

## Overview

Applied research is systematic study to gain knowledge or understanding necessary to determine the means by which a recognized and specific need may be met. It translates scientific discoveries into practical applications, creating prototypes, pilot plants, and proof-of-concept demonstrations that lead to new products, processes, and services.

### Key Characteristics

- **Goal-Oriented**: Directed toward specific practical objectives
- **Shorter Timeline**: 2-10 year horizon to application
- **Interdisciplinary**: Combines multiple scientific disciplines
- **Industry Collaboration**: Partnership between academia and industry
- **IP Generation**: Patents and proprietary knowledge creation
- **Technology Readiness**: Moving from TRL 2-3 to TRL 6-7

## Technology Readiness Levels (TRL)

Applied research typically operates in the middle TRL range:

- **TRL 1-2**: Basic Research (transition point)
- **TRL 3**: Proof of concept demonstrated
- **TRL 4**: Component validation in laboratory
- **TRL 5**: Component validation in relevant environment
- **TRL 6**: System/subsystem model demonstrated
- **TRL 7**: System prototype in operational environment
- **TRL 8-9**: Development & Commercialization (next phase)

## Research Methodologies

### Translational Research Pipeline

1. **Target Identification**: Select promising basic research findings
2. **Feasibility Studies**: Assess technical and commercial viability
3. **Proof of Concept**: Demonstrate core functionality
4. **Prototype Development**: Create working prototypes
5. **Validation Testing**: Verify performance in relevant environments
6. **Scale-Up**: Develop manufacturing and deployment strategies
7. **Pilot Programs**: Limited deployment and real-world testing

### Industry-Academia Partnership Models

**Sponsored Research Agreements**
- Company funds university research
- Negotiated IP ownership
- Typical: $100K-$5M per project
- 1-3 year timelines

**Cooperative R&D Agreements (CRADAs)**
- Government labs partner with industry
- Shared resources and expertise
- IP shared or licensed
- Risk and cost sharing

**Research Centers & Institutes**
- NSF Industry-University Cooperative Research Centers
- DOE Energy Innovation Hubs
- NIH-industry partnerships
- State-funded research consortia

**Public-Private Partnerships**
- DARPA programs with industry
- Advanced Research Projects Agency-Energy (ARPA-E)
- National Network for Manufacturing Innovation
- Pandemic preparedness partnerships

## Intellectual Property & Commercialization

### Patent Strategy

**Types of IP Protection**
- Utility patents: Novel processes and products
- Design patents: Ornamental designs
- Trade secrets: Proprietary methods
- Copyright: Software and documentation

**Patent Metrics**
- Time to patent: 2-3 years average
- Patent cost: $10K-$30K including prosecution
- Maintenance fees: $10K+ over 20-year life
- 350,000+ U.S. patents granted annually

### Technology Transfer

**University Technology Transfer Offices (TTOs)**
- Invention disclosures: 25,000+ annually (U.S.)
- Patents filed: 20,000+ per year
- Licenses executed: 8,000+ per year
- Licensing revenue: $3+ billion
- Startup companies: 1,000+ per year

**Licensing Models**
- Exclusive license: Single licensee, higher royalties (3-7%)
- Non-exclusive: Multiple licensees, lower royalties (1-3%)
- Field-of-use: Exclusive by application area
- Sublicensing rights: Allow further licensing

**Typical License Terms**
- Upfront fees: $10K-$500K
- Milestone payments: $100K-$5M over development
- Royalties: 1-7% of net sales
- Minimum annual royalties: $25K-$100K
- Patent cost reimbursement

### Startup Formation

**University Spinoffs**
- 1,000+ formed annually in U.S.
- Faculty founders or licensed IP
- Incubator support common
- Accelerator programs
- University investment funds

**Venture Capital**
- Seed stage: $500K-$2M
- Series A: $2M-$15M
- Series B: $10M-$50M
- Deep tech requires more capital and time
- 10-year+ exit horizon common

- **Publications**: Applied research papers in field-specific journals

- **Prototypes**: Physical demonstrations of technology
- **Software/Algorithms**: Open source or commercial releases

### Technology Transfer Success

- **License Revenue**: $3+ billion annually (U.S. universities)
- **Equity Returns**: University stakes in spinoffs
- **Economic Impact**: Jobs created, regional development
- **Follow-on Funding**: Industry investment attracted
