# Hospitals

Hospitals are licensed healthcare facilities that provide inpatient medical and surgical care with round-the-clock availability of physicians, nurses, and specialized staff. As the cornerstone of the healthcare system, hospitals treat patients with serious illnesses and injuries, perform complex surgeries, manage emergencies, and coordinate comprehensive care. With over 6,000 hospitals employing more than 6.5 million people and generating $1.2+ trillion in annual revenue in the U.S., hospitals represent the largest component of healthcare spending and the most complex healthcare delivery organizations.

Hospitals are distinguished by their ability to admit patients for overnight stays, maintain 24/7 emergency capabilities, perform major surgery, provide intensive care, and deliver a wide array of diagnostic and therapeutic services under one roof. Modern hospitals are evolving from volume-based acute care facilities toward value-based care hubs that emphasize quality outcomes, care coordination, population health, and integration with outpatient and post-acute providers.

## Overview

Hospitals operate as highly complex organizations integrating clinical care, medical education, research, and community health services:

### Key Characteristics

**Clinical Capabilities**
- **Inpatient Services** - Medical, surgical, intensive care, obstetrics, pediatrics
- **Emergency Services** - 24/7 emergency departments with trauma capabilities
- **Surgical Services** - Operating rooms for inpatient and outpatient procedures
- **Diagnostic Services** - Laboratory, imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound), cardiac catheterization
- **Specialty Services** - Cardiology, oncology, neurology, orthopedics, and more
- **Critical Care** - ICU, CCU, NICU, burn units
- **Support Services** - Pharmacy, physical therapy, respiratory therapy, nutrition

**Organizational Structure**
- **Medical Staff** - Physicians with admitting privileges (employed or independent)
- **Nursing** - RNs, LPNs, nurse practitioners, nursing assistants
- **Allied Health** - Therapists, technologists, pharmacists, social workers
- **Administration** - CEO, COO, CFO, CNO, CMO, department directors
- **Support** - Environmental services, food services, facilities, security

**Financial Model**
- **Revenue Mix** - Medicare (35-45%), Medicaid (15-25%), commercial insurance (30-40%), self-pay/charity (5-10%)
- **Payment Systems** - DRG-based payments for Medicare, negotiated rates with commercial payers
- **Margins** - Operating margins typically 1-4%, supplemented by investment income
- **Capital Intensity** - Major investments in facilities, equipment, and technology

**Regulatory Environment**
- **Federal** - Medicare/Medicaid Conditions of Participation, EMTALA, HIPAA
- **State** - Licensure, certificate of need (in some states), health department oversight
- **Accreditation** - Joint Commission, DNV, or state-approved accreditors
- **Quality Reporting** - CMS quality measures, Leapfrog, state-specific programs

## Types of Hospitals (NAICS 622)

### 6221 - General Medical and Surgical Hospitals

Acute care hospitals providing a wide range of medical and surgical services.

#### Short-Term Acute Care Hospitals

**Community Hospitals** - The backbone of the hospital system:
- **Size Range** - 25 to 500+ beds
- **Services** - Emergency, medical-surgical, ICU, obstetrics, basic surgery
- **Staffing** - Hospitalists, emergency physicians, surgical specialists, primary care
- **Market** - Serve local communities, often sole provider in rural areas
- **Ownership** - Non-profit (60%), for-profit (20%), government (20%)
- **Challenges** - Thin margins, recruitment in rural areas, competition from larger systems

**Academic Medical Centers (AMCs)** - Teaching hospitals affiliated with medical schools:
- **Mission** - Patient care, medical education, research (triple mission)
- **Capabilities** - Tertiary and quaternary care, complex cases, rare conditions
- **Programs** - Residency and fellowship training programs across specialties
- **Research** - Clinical trials, translational research, NIH funding
- **Size** - Typically 500-1,500+ beds
- **Examples** - Massachusetts General, Johns Hopkins, Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic

**Children's Hospitals** - Pediatric specialty care:
- **Focus** - Neonatal through adolescent care
- **Services** - Pediatric surgery, oncology, cardiology, NICU, PICU
- **Family-Centered** - Accommodations for families, child life specialists
- **Research** - Pediatric research, clinical trials
- **Funding** - Significant philanthropic support, children's charities
- **Network** - Children's Hospital Association member hospitals

**Critical Access Hospitals (CAHs)** - Rural hospitals with special designation:
- **Criteria** - 25 or fewer beds, 35+ miles from another hospital, avg length of stay ≤96 hours
- **Reimbursement** - Cost-based Medicare reimbursement (not DRG-based)
- **Mission** - Maintain access to emergency and inpatient care in rural areas
- **Challenges** - Recruiting providers, limited services, financial sustainability
- **Count** - ~1,300 CAHs across the U.S.

#### Regional and Tertiary Care Centers

**Level I Trauma Centers**:
- **Capabilities** - 24/7 surgical coverage, neurosurgery, orthopedic surgery, ICU
- **Requirements** - Trauma surgeon in-house 24/7, research and education
- **Catchment** - Regional coverage for most severe injuries
- **Verification** - American College of Surgeons verification

**Comprehensive Cancer Centers**:
- **Services** - Medical oncology, radiation oncology, surgical oncology
- **Capabilities** - Bone marrow transplant, clinical trials, genetic counseling
- **NCI Designation** - National Cancer Institute comprehensive cancer center designation
- **Multidisciplinary** - Tumor boards, coordinated care across specialties

**Cardiovascular Centers**:
- **Capabilities** - Open-heart surgery, interventional cardiology, electrophysiology
- **Volume** - High-volume CABG, valve replacement, complex interventions
- **Cardiac ICU** - Specialized CCU, ECMO, ventricular assist devices
- **Accreditation** - Chest pain centers, STEMI receiving centers

### 6222 - Psychiatric and Substance Abuse Hospitals

Specialized facilities for behavioral health inpatient treatment.

#### Psychiatric Hospitals

**Acute Psychiatric Hospitals**:
- **Services** - Crisis stabilization, medication management, therapy
- **Length of Stay** - Typically 7-14 days for acute episodes
- **Patients** - Severe depression, psychosis, suicidal ideation, acute mania
- **Security** - Locked units, staff training in de-escalation, safety protocols
- **Treatment** - Psychiatry, psychology, nursing, social work, occupational therapy
- **Challenges** - Workforce shortages, insurance limitations, community resources

**State Psychiatric Hospitals**:
- **Mission** - Long-term care for severe and persistent mental illness
- **Patients** - Court-committed, forensic psychiatry, long-term stabilization
- **Size** - Often 200+ beds, historically much larger
- **Funding** - State-funded, Medicaid
- **Deinstitutionalization** - Shift toward community-based care reduced census

**Geriatric Psychiatric Facilities**:
- **Focus** - Mental health needs of older adults
- **Conditions** - Depression, dementia-related behaviors, late-life psychosis
- **Integration** - Medical and psychiatric care for complex patients
- **Environment** - Adapted for cognitive and physical limitations

#### Substance Abuse Treatment Centers

**Inpatient Detoxification**:
- **Purpose** - Medically supervised withdrawal from alcohol or drugs
- **Duration** - 3-7 days typically
- **Medical Monitoring** - Vital signs, medication protocols, safety
- **Transition** - Bridge to residential or outpatient treatment

**Residential Treatment Programs**:
- **Length** - 30, 60, or 90 days common
- **Structure** - Intensive therapy, group counseling, 12-step programs
- **Services** - Individual and family therapy, life skills, relapse prevention
- **Continuum** - Step-down to partial hospitalization, intensive outpatient

**Dual Diagnosis Programs**:
- **Population** - Co-occurring mental health and substance use disorders
- **Integration** - Simultaneous treatment of both conditions
- **Complexity** - Requires specialized expertise in both areas

### 6223 - Specialty Hospitals (except Psychiatric and Substance Abuse)

Hospitals focused on specific conditions or populations.

#### Long-Term Acute Care Hospitals (LTACHs)

**Mission** - Extended acute care for medically complex patients:
- **Criteria** - Average length of stay >25 days
- **Patients** - Ventilator-dependent, complex wounds, multi-organ failure, sepsis
- **Services** - Intensive nursing, respiratory therapy, wound care, rehabilitation
- **Outcomes** - Weaning from ventilators, wound healing, return to community or skilled nursing
- **Reimbursement** - Medicare LTACH PPS (prospective payment system)
- **Major Operators** - Kindred, Select Medical, Ernest Health

#### Rehabilitation Hospitals

**Inpatient Rehabilitation Facilities (IRFs)**:
- **Focus** - Intensive therapy for stroke, brain injury, spinal cord injury, amputations, orthopedic conditions
- **Requirements** - 3 hours of therapy per day, 5-6 days per week
- **Interdisciplinary** - Physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy, nursing, physicians
- **Outcomes** - Functional gains measured with FIM (Functional Independence Measure)
- **Length of Stay** - 10-20 days average
- **Accreditation** - CARF (Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities)

**Major Rehab Systems**:
- Encompass Health (formerly HealthSouth)
- Kindred Rehabilitation Hospitals
- Select Medical Rehabilitation Hospitals
- University-affiliated rehab hospitals

#### Specialty Surgical Hospitals

**Orthopedic Hospitals**:
- **Focus** - Joint replacement, spine surgery, sports medicine
- **Efficiency** - High-volume, standardized protocols, rapid recovery
- **Outcomes** - Lower infection rates, improved functional outcomes
- **Physician Ownership** - Often physician-owned or joint ventures

**Cardiac Hospitals**:
- **Services** - Cardiovascular surgery, interventional cardiology
- **Integration** - Often part of larger hospital system

**Women's Hospitals**:
- **Services** - Obstetrics, gynecology, high-risk pregnancy, neonatal intensive care
- **Family-Centered** - Private labor-delivery-recovery-postpartum (LDRP) rooms

**Cancer Hospitals**:
- **Focus** - Specialized oncology care
- **Examples** - MD Anderson, Memorial Sloan Kettering, Dana-Farber

## Hospital Operations

### Clinical Operations

#### Emergency Department (ED)

**Structure and Staffing**:
- **Emergency Physicians** - Board-certified in emergency medicine, 24/7 coverage
- **ED Nurses** - Specialized training in emergency care, trauma, and triage
- **Triage** - ESI (Emergency Severity Index) to prioritize patients
- **Fast Track** - Separate area for minor injuries and illnesses
- **Observation** - Short-stay unit for <24-hour monitoring
- **Trauma Activation** - Protocols for trauma team activation

**Operations**:
- **Volume** - Average ED sees 50,000-100,000 visits/year
- **Acuity Mix** - ESI Level 1-2 (emergent), Level 3 (urgent), Level 4-5 (non-urgent)
- **Door-to-Doctor Time** - Key metric, target <30 minutes
- **Length of Stay** - National average ~2-3 hours
- **Boarding** - ED holds for admitted patients waiting for beds (major problem)
- **Left Without Being Seen (LWBS)** - Patients who leave before treatment

**EMTALA Compliance**:
- **Medical Screening Exam** - Required for all patients regardless of ability to pay
- **Stabilization** - Must stabilize emergency medical conditions
- **Transfers** - Appropriate transfers only after stabilization
- **On-Call Specialists** - Must maintain on-call panel for emergencies

#### Inpatient Medical-Surgical Units

**Unit Organization**:
- **Medical Units** - General medicine, cardiology, pulmonary, neurology
- **Surgical Units** - General surgery, orthopedics, neurosurgery
- **Nurse Staffing Ratios** - Typically 1:4-6 for medical-surgical units
- **Multidisciplinary Rounds** - Daily interdisciplinary team meetings
- **Discharge Planning** - Social work, case management starting at admission

**Patient Flow**:
- **Admissions** - From ED, direct admits, transfers from other facilities
- **Length of Stay (LOS)** - Average 4-5 days for acute care
- **Discharge** - Home, home health, skilled nursing, rehab, LTAC
- **Readmissions** - 30-day readmission rate key quality and payment metric

#### Intensive Care Units (ICU)

**Types of ICUs**:
- **Medical ICU (MICU)** - Critical medical conditions, sepsis, respiratory failure
- **Cardiac Care Unit (CCU)** - Post-cardiac surgery, heart failure, MI
- **Surgical ICU (SICU)** - Post-operative critical care, trauma
- **Neurological ICU** - Stroke, brain injury, neurosurgery
- **Neonatal ICU (NICU)** - Premature and critically ill newborns
- **Pediatric ICU (PICU)** - Critically ill children

**ICU Operations**:
- **Staffing** - 1:1 or 1:2 nurse-to-patient ratios
- **Intensivists** - Critical care physicians, 24/7 coverage
- **Technology** - Continuous monitoring, ventilators, dialysis, ECMO
- **Sedation** - Daily interruption, spontaneous awakening trials
- **Mobility** - Early mobilization to prevent ICU-acquired weakness
- **Family** - Expanded visitation, family-centered rounds

#### Surgical Services

**Operating Rooms (ORs)**:
- **Scheduling** - Block time for surgeons, open time, add-ons
- **First Case Start** - On-time starts critical for throughput
- **Turnover Time** - Between-case cleaning and setup
- **Utilization** - Target 70-80% OR utilization during core hours
- **Staffing** - Surgeons, anesthesiologists, OR nurses, surgical techs
- **Safety** - Universal protocol, time-outs, surgical site infection prevention

**Types of Surgery**:
- **Inpatient** - Major surgery requiring hospitalization
- **Ambulatory** - Same-day surgery, outpatient procedures
- **Emergency** - Trauma, acute abdomen, vascular emergencies
- **Robotic** - da Vinci systems for minimally invasive surgery

**Post-Anesthesia Care Unit (PACU)**:
- **Recovery** - Immediate post-operative monitoring
- **Staffing** - PACU nurses trained in post-anesthesia care
- **Discharge Criteria** - Aldrete score for readiness to transfer
- **Phase II** - Step-down before discharge home for outpatients

#### Diagnostic and Therapeutic Services

**Laboratory Services**:
- **Core Lab** - Chemistry, hematology, coagulation, urinalysis
- **Microbiology** - Cultures, sensitivities, rapid diagnostics
- **Blood Bank** - Blood typing, crossmatch, component preparation
- **Point-of-Care Testing** - Bedside glucose, blood gas, rapid strep
- **Send-Out Tests** - Reference lab for specialized testing
- **Automation** - Track systems, auto-verification, middleware

**Radiology and Imaging**:
- **Modalities** - X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, nuclear medicine, PET
- **Interventional Radiology** - Minimally invasive procedures, biopsies, drains
- **PACS** - Picture archiving and communication system
- **Radiologists** - Subspecialty reads (neuroradiology, body imaging, etc.)
- **Turnaround Time** - Stat reads for ED and ICU, routine within 24 hours

**Pharmacy Services**:
- **Medication Dispensing** - Unit dose, automated dispensing cabinets (ADCs)
- **IV Admixture** - Sterile compounding, TPN, chemotherapy
- **Clinical Pharmacy** - Pharmacist rounding, medication reconciliation
- **Antimicrobial Stewardship** - Optimize antibiotic use, reduce resistance
- **Adverse Drug Event Prevention** - Order checking, allergy alerts

### Quality and Patient Safety

#### Hospital Quality Programs

**Regulatory Quality Measures**:
- **CMS Hospital Compare** - Public reporting of quality measures
- **Hospital-Acquired Conditions (HACs)** - Payment reductions for preventable conditions
- **Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP)** - Penalties for excess readmissions
- **Hospital Value-Based Purchasing (VBP)** - Pay-for-performance program

**Quality Domains**:
- **Clinical Outcomes** - Mortality, complications, readmissions
- **Patient Safety** - Infections, falls, pressure ulcers, medication errors
- **Patient Experience** - HCAHPS scores
- **Process Measures** - Evidence-based care delivery
- **Efficiency** - Cost per case, length of stay

#### Patient Safety Initiatives

**National Patient Safety Goals (Joint Commission)**:
- **Patient Identification** - Two identifiers before any procedure
- **Communication** - Handoff protocols, read-back of orders
- **Medication Safety** - High-alert medications, anticoagulation safety
- **Infection Prevention** - Hand hygiene, catheter-associated infections
- **Surgery Safety** - Universal protocol, wrong-site surgery prevention
- **Fall Prevention** - Risk assessment, interventions, post-fall management

**Safety Culture**:
- **Event Reporting** - Non-punitive reporting systems
- **Root Cause Analysis** - Systematic investigation of serious events
- **Failure Mode Effects Analysis (FMEA)** - Proactive risk assessment
- **Safety Huddles** - Daily briefings on safety concerns
- **TeamSTEPPS** - Team training for improved communication

**Infection Prevention and Control**:
- **Hospital Epidemiologist** - Physician oversight of infection prevention
- **Infection Preventionists** - Nurses specializing in infection control
- **Surveillance** - HAI monitoring (CLABSI, CAUTI, SSI, VAP, C. diff, MRSA)
- **Bundles** - Evidence-based infection prevention protocols
- **Antibiotic Stewardship** - Optimize antibiotic use, reduce resistance
- **Outbreak Management** - Protocols for infectious disease outbreaks

### Revenue Cycle Management

#### Patient Access

**Registration**:
- **Demographics** - Patient information collection
- **Insurance Verification** - Eligibility and benefits checking
- **Prior Authorization** - Obtaining payer approvals for procedures
- **Deposits and Co-pays** - Upfront collection of patient responsibility
- **Financial Counseling** - Assistance with insurance, charity care, payment plans

**Medical Necessity**:
- **Admission Status** - Inpatient vs. observation decision
- **Two-Midnight Rule** - Medicare inpatient admission criteria
- **Physician Certification** - Documentation of need for admission
- **Observation Services** - Outpatient status, affects Medicare coverage

#### Charge Capture and Coding

**Charge Master**:
- **Line Items** - Tens of thousands of individual charges
- **Pricing** - Hospital-set prices (not actual payment amounts)
- **Updates** - Regular review and updating
- **Transparency** - Price transparency requirements (since 2021)

**Medical Coding**:
- **ICD-10-CM** - Diagnosis codes (75,000+ codes)
- **ICD-10-PCS** - Procedure codes for inpatients
- **CPT** - Procedure codes for outpatients
- **DRG Assignment** - Grouper assigns Medicare Severity DRG
- **Coding Staff** - Certified coders (RHIA, RHIT, CCS, CPC)

**Clinical Documentation Improvement (CDI)**:
- **CDI Specialists** - Nurses reviewing documentation for specificity
- **Physician Queries** - Clarifying diagnoses and procedures
- **Severity Capture** - Ensuring complications and comorbidities documented
- **Impact** - Appropriate DRG assignment, case mix index

#### Billing and Collections

**Claims Submission**:
- **Claim Scrubbing** - Edit checking before submission
- **Electronic Submission** - 837 institutional claims
- **Timely Filing** - Payer-specific deadlines (typically 90-365 days)

**Payment Posting**:
- **Remittance Advice** - 835 electronic remittance
- **Contractual Adjustments** - Write-off of charges above contracted rates
- **Denials** - Payer denials requiring follow-up and appeals

**Patient Billing**:
- **Patient Balance** - Deductibles, co-insurance, non-covered services
- **Statements** - Monthly patient bills
- **Payment Plans** - Flexible payment arrangements
- **Collections** - Third-party collections after internal efforts

**Key Metrics**:
- **Days in A/R** - Accounts receivable days (target <50)
- **Clean Claim Rate** - Claims paid on first submission (target >95%)
- **Denial Rate** - Percentage of claims denied (target <5%)
- **Cash Collections** - Cash as percentage of net revenue
- **Point-of-Service Collections** - Deposits and co-pays collected upfront

### Technology and Information Systems

#### Hospital Information Systems

**Electronic Health Record (EHR)**:
- **Core Functions** - Orders, documentation, results, medication administration
- **Meaningful Use / Promoting Interoperability** - CMS requirements for certified EHRs
- **Clinical Decision Support** - Order sets, alerts, evidence-based guidelines
- **Interoperability** - Health information exchange with external providers

**Department Systems**:
- **Radiology (RIS/PACS)** - Imaging orders, reading, storage
- **Laboratory (LIS)** - Lab orders, auto-verification, result reporting
- **Pharmacy** - Order management, clinical checking, dispensing
- **Surgery** - OR scheduling, preference cards, supply tracking
- **Emergency** - ED tracking, triage, fast track management

**Administrative Systems**:
- **Registration** - Patient demographics, insurance
- **Scheduling** - Appointments, OR scheduling, resource management
- **Billing** - Charge capture, claims, collections
- **Financial** - General ledger, payroll, accounts payable

**Analytics and Reporting**:
- **Data Warehouse** - Centralized repository of clinical and financial data
- **Business Intelligence** - Dashboards, scorecards, reports
- **Predictive Analytics** - Readmission risk, sepsis prediction, capacity planning
- **Population Health** - Risk stratification, care gap identification

#### Cybersecurity

**Threats**:
- **Ransomware** - Encryption of hospital systems with ransom demands
- **Phishing** - Social engineering attacks on staff
- **Medical Device Vulnerabilities** - Legacy devices with security flaws
- **Insider Threats** - Unauthorized access by staff

**Protections**:
- **HIPAA Security Rule** - Administrative, physical, and technical safeguards
- **Risk Assessment** - Regular evaluation of vulnerabilities
- **Access Controls** - Role-based access, multi-factor authentication
- **Encryption** - Data at rest and in transit
- **Incident Response** - Plans for breach detection and response
- **Training** - Staff security awareness training

## Hospital Financial Management

### Revenue Sources

**Medicare** (35-45% of revenue):
- **Medicare Inpatient Prospective Payment System (IPPS)** - DRG-based payments
- **Outliers** - Additional payment for extraordinarily high costs
- **Disproportionate Share (DSH)** - Supplemental payments for serving low-income patients
- **Graduate Medical Education (GME)** - Teaching hospital payments
- **Medicare Advantage** - Managed care plans, negotiated rates

**Medicaid** (15-25% of revenue):
- **State-Specific** - Payments vary widely by state
- **Managed Care** - Increasingly through managed care plans
- **Disproportionate Share** - DSH payments
- **Generally Lower** - Below cost of care in most states

**Commercial Insurance** (30-40% of revenue):
- **Negotiated Rates** - Contracts with insurers
- **Higher Payment** - Generally 150-250% of Medicare rates
- **Value-Based** - Increasing use of quality incentives and bundled payments
- **Narrow Networks** - Insurers creating limited provider networks

**Self-Pay and Charity Care** (5-10%):
- **Uninsured Patients** - No insurance coverage
- **Underinsured** - High deductibles and cost-sharing
- **Charity Care** - Free care for patients meeting income criteria
- **Bad Debt** - Uncollectible patient balances

### Cost Structure

**Labor Costs** (50-60% of expenses):
- **Salaries and Wages** - Physicians, nurses, allied health, support staff
- **Benefits** - Health insurance, retirement, paid time off
- **Contract Labor** - Temporary nurses, locum tenens physicians
- **Workforce Challenges** - Shortages, turnover, wage pressure, burnout

**Supplies and Pharmaceuticals** (20-25%):
- **Medical-Surgical Supplies** - Disposables, implants, surgical supplies
- **Pharmaceuticals** - Inpatient medications, chemotherapy, specialty drugs
- **Purchased Services** - Lab send-outs, physician services, IT support
- **Group Purchasing** - GPO contracts for supply discounts

**Facilities and Equipment** (10-15%):
- **Depreciation** - Buildings and equipment depreciation
- **Utilities** - Electric, gas, water
- **Maintenance** - Facilities and equipment maintenance
- **Capital Expenditures** - CT scanners, MRI machines, surgical robots, building renovations

**Other Operating Expenses** (5-10%):
- **Professional Liability Insurance** - Malpractice coverage
- **Information Technology** - EHR, infrastructure, support
- **Marketing and Business Development**
- **Administrative** - Finance, HR, legal, compliance

### Financial Performance

**Operating Margins**:
- **National Average** - 1-4% operating margin
- **Variability** - Wide variation by hospital type, location, payer mix
- **Challenges** - Labor costs, drug costs, low reimbursement, bad debt
- **Investment Income** - Non-operating income supplements operating margins

**Financial Benchmarks**:
- **Days Cash on Hand** - 150-200 days typical for stable hospitals
- **Debt Service Coverage** - >3.0 for strong credit
- **Operating Cash Flow** - Positive cash flow from operations
- **Capital Spending** - 6-8% of revenue typical

## Hospital Consolidation and Integration

### Health System Affiliation

**Multi-Hospital Systems**:
- **National For-Profit** - HCA Healthcare, Tenet, Community Health Systems
- **Large Non-Profit** - Ascension, Providence, CommonSpirit, Trinity Health
- **Regional Systems** - Academic medical centers with community hospitals
- **Benefits** - Economies of scale, shared services, access to capital, clinical integration

**Integration Strategies**:
- **Horizontal** - Acquisition of other hospitals
- **Vertical** - Integration with physician practices, post-acute, insurance
- **Clinical Integration** - Standardized care protocols, shared EHR
- **Operational Integration** - Centralized services (HR, finance, IT, supply chain)

### Physician-Hospital Alignment

**Employment**:
- **Trend** - Majority of physicians now employed (was minority 20 years ago)
- **Specialties** - Hospitalists, emergency physicians, intensivists commonly employed
- **Primary Care** - Employment as loss leader to drive referrals
- **Compensation** - Salary with productivity and quality incentives

**Joint Ventures**:
- **Ambulatory Surgery Centers** - Hospital-physician ownership
- **Imaging Centers** - Shared ownership and revenue
- **Specialty Hospitals** - Physician-owned or joint ventures

**Co-Management**:
- **Service Line Management** - Physicians managing clinical programs
- **Shared Governance** - Joint decision-making on operations and strategy
