# Drinking Places - Alcoholic Beverages (NAICS 7224)

Drinking establishments are venues primarily focused on serving alcoholic beverages for on-premises consumption, creating social spaces for entertainment, relaxation, and community gathering. This sector includes traditional bars, upscale cocktail lounges, nightclubs, brewpubs, and sports bars.

## Overview

The bar and drinking establishment industry is characterized by evening and late-night operations, strong beverage margins (70-80% gross margin), music and entertainment offerings, and strict regulatory compliance requirements. Success depends on atmosphere, location, bartending skill, and the ability to create memorable social experiences.

## Types of Drinking Establishments

### Traditional Bars and Taverns (NAICS 722410)

#### Neighborhood Bar / Local Pub
Community gathering places with familiar atmosphere:
- **Characteristics**: Regular clientele, casual vibe, local focus
- **Beverage Focus**: Beer (domestic and craft), basic spirits, simple cocktails
- **Food**: Bar snacks, wings, burgers, pizza (often limited menu)
- **Entertainment**: TVs for sports, darts, pool tables, trivia nights
- **Examples**: Independent local bars, Irish pubs, corner taverns
- **Target Market**: Local residents, after-work crowd, regulars

#### Sports Bar
Sports-focused viewing venues:
- **Characteristics**: Multiple large-screen TVs, sports memorabilia, energetic atmosphere
- **Beverage**: Wide beer selection, domestic focus, pitcher specials
- **Food**: Wings, nachos, burgers, shareable appetizers (significant revenue)
- **Technology**: Satellite TV packages (NFL Sunday Ticket, etc.)
- **Examples**: Buffalo Wild Wings, Hooters, Twin Peaks, local sports bars
- **Target Market**: Sports fans, groups, game day gatherings

#### Dive Bar
No-frills establishments with character and authenticity:
- **Characteristics**: Minimal décor, affordable drinks, eclectic regulars, cash-only often
- **Beverage**: Basic beer and spirits, no craft cocktails
- **Atmosphere**: Unpretentious, lived-in, loyal following
- **Examples**: Local independent bars, historic taverns
- **Target Market**: Locals, budget-conscious drinkers, authenticity seekers

### Upscale Establishments

#### Cocktail Bar / Craft Cocktail Lounge
Mixology-focused venues with artisanal drinks:
- **Characteristics**: Skilled bartenders, house-made ingredients, curated spirits selection
- **Beverage**: Craft cocktails ($14-20+), premium spirits, seasonal menus
- **Atmosphere**: Sophisticated, intimate lighting, quality design
- **Service**: Knowledgeable bartenders, attention to detail
- **Examples**: Employees Only (NYC), The Violet Hour (Chicago), Dead Rabbit (NYC)
- **Target Market**: cocktail enthusiasts, date nights, special occasions

#### Wine Bar
Wine-focused venues with food pairings:
- **Characteristics**: Extensive wine list by the glass and bottle, wine education
- **Beverage**: 30-100+ wines by the glass, rare/premium bottles
- **Food**: Cheese and charcuterie, small plates, wine-friendly cuisine
- **Service**: Sommelier or wine-knowledgeable staff
- **Examples**: City Winery, local wine bars, enotecas
- **Target Market**: Wine enthusiasts, professionals, couples

#### Speakeasy / Hidden Bar
Exclusive, reservation-focused venues with hidden entrances:
- **Characteristics**: Secret entrance, reservation required, intimate atmosphere
- **Beverage**: Craft cocktails, curated spirits, custom creations
- **Experience**: Discovery, exclusivity, theatricality
- **Examples**: Please Don't Tell (NYC), The Varnish (LA), Milk Room (Chicago)
- **Target Market**: Experience seekers, cocktail aficionados, special occasions

#### Rooftop Bar
Elevated venues with views and ambiance:
- **Characteristics**: Outdoor/semi-outdoor space, skyline views, seasonal operation
- **Beverage**: Cocktails, wine, beer, often premium pricing
- **Atmosphere**: Social, Instagram-worthy, destination venue
- **Challenges**: Weather-dependent, seasonal, permitting complexity
- **Examples**: 230 Fifth (NYC), Spire 73 (LA), Azul (Miami)
- **Target Market**: Tourists, special occasions, social gatherings

### Nightlife Venues

#### Nightclub
Dance-focused venues with DJ entertainment:
- **Characteristics**: Large dance floor, DJ booth, sound system, lighting effects
- **Beverage**: Bottle service, premium spirits, cocktails, champagne
- **Revenue Model**: Cover charge + high-margin bottle service
- **Hours**: Late night (10 PM - 4 AM)
- **Examples**: XS (Las Vegas), Marquee (NYC), LIV (Miami)
- **Target Market**: 21-35 demographic, nightlife enthusiasts, bachelorette/bachelor parties

#### Dance Club / Disco
Music and dancing in various genres:
- **Characteristics**: Theme nights (80s, Latin, EDM), resident DJs
- **Beverage**: High-volume cocktails, shots, beer
- **Cover Charge**: $10-50+ depending on night and talent
- **Capacity**: 200-2,000+ depending on venue size
- **Target Market**: Dance enthusiasts, specific music genre fans

#### Lounge / Ultra Lounge
Upscale social venues with VIP service:
- **Characteristics**: VIP tables, bottle service, upscale décor, velvet ropes
- **Beverage**: Premium bottles ($300-10,000+), craft cocktails
- **Service**: Table hosts, cocktail servers, VIP coordinators
- **Atmosphere**: Sophisticated, exclusive, see-and-be-seen
- **Examples**: Hyde Lounge, 1Oak, Avenue
- **Target Market**: Affluent clientele, celebrities, special occasions

#### Live Music Venue / Concert Bar
Performance-focused drinking establishments:
- **Characteristics**: Stage, sound system, artist bookings, standing room or seating
- **Beverage**: Beer-focused, cocktails, wine
- **Revenue**: Cover charge + drinks, sometimes tickets for larger acts
- **Genres**: Rock, jazz, blues, indie, acoustic
- **Examples**: Bowery Ballroom (NYC), Troubadour (LA), local music venues
- **Target Market**: Music fans, local music scene participants

### Specialty Concepts

#### Brewpub / Taproom (NAICS 722410)
Brewery with on-premises consumption:
- **Characteristics**: On-site beer production, brewery tours, tasting flights
- **Beverage**: House-brewed beers, limited guest taps, non-alcoholic options
- **Food**: Brewpub fare (burgers, pretzels, beer-friendly food)
- **Experience**: Education about brewing process, seasonal releases, beer club
- **Regulations**: Often requires both manufacturing and retail licenses
- **Examples**: Local breweries with taprooms, Goose Island, Brooklyn Brewery
- **Target Market**: Craft beer enthusiasts, locals, beer tourists

#### Distillery Tasting Room
Spirit producer with cocktail bar:
- **Characteristics**: On-site distillation, cocktails using house spirits, tours
- **Beverage**: House-distilled spirits, craft cocktails, limited beer/wine
- **Experience**: Educational, behind-the-scenes access, bottle sales
- **Regulations**: Complex federal and state licensing
- **Examples**: Tito's, local craft distilleries, Westland Distillery
- **Target Market**: Spirit enthusiasts, tourists, cocktail aficionados

#### Karaoke Bar
Singing-focused entertainment venue:
- **Characteristics**: Private rooms or open mic format, song catalogs, sound systems
- **Beverage**: Beer, cocktails, sake (Asian-influenced venues)
- **Revenue**: Room rental + beverage minimums (private room model)
- **Format**: Japanese-style private rooms or Western-style open stage
- **Examples**: Karaoke Duet (chain), local karaoke bars
- **Target Market**: Groups, birthday parties, social gatherings

#### Comedy Club with Bar
Stand-up comedy with beverage service:
- **Characteristics**: Stage, seating with table service, ticketed shows
- **Beverage**: Two-drink minimum common practice, full bar
- **Revenue**: Ticket sales + beverage sales
- **Shows**: Headliners and open mic nights
- **Examples**: The Comedy Store (LA), Caroline's (NYC), Laugh Factory
- **Target Market**: Comedy fans, date nights, groups

#### Tiki Bar
Tropical-themed bars with rum-based cocktails:
- **Characteristics**: Polynesian décor, tiki mugs, tropical drinks, escapist atmosphere
- **Beverage**: Rum-forward cocktails, Mai Tais, Zombies, elaborate garnishes
- **Food**: Polynesian-inspired appetizers, shareable plates
- **Atmosphere**: Kitschy, fun, immersive theming
- **Examples**: Smuggler's Cove (SF), Three Dots and a Dash (Chicago)
- **Target Market**: Cocktail enthusiasts, tropical drink lovers, nostalgia seekers

## Bar Operations

### Beverage Management

#### Liquor Inventory
Organizing spirits by category and price tier:
- **Well/House**: Basic spirits for mixed drinks ($15-25/bottle cost)
- **Call**: Mid-tier brands requested by name ($25-40/bottle)
- **Premium**: Top-shelf spirits ($40-80/bottle)
- **Super-Premium**: Luxury and rare spirits ($80-500+/bottle)

#### Beer Program
- **Draft Beer**: 8-40+ tap lines, requires keg management and line cleaning
- **Bottled/Canned**: Domestic, craft, imports, variety for different tastes
- **Pricing**: $5-10 for domestic, $7-12 for craft, $8-15 for premium/imports

#### Wine by the Glass
- **Selection**: 8-20 wines by the glass, variety across styles and price points
- **Preservation**: Argon/nitrogen systems for open bottles (Coravin, WineKeeper)
- **Markup**: 3-4x bottle cost typical

#### Cocktail Program
- **Classic Cocktails**: Martini, Old Fashioned, Manhattan, Margarita, etc.
- **Signature Cocktails**: House creations, seasonal offerings
- **Ingredients**: Fresh juices, house-made syrups, bitters, garnishes
- **Glassware**: Appropriate glass for each cocktail type

### Bartending Operations

#### Bartender Skills
Essential competencies:
- **Drink Knowledge**: Classic and contemporary cocktail recipes
- **Speed and Efficiency**: Fast service during peak times
- **Customer Service**: Friendly, attentive, conversational
- **Multitasking**: Managing multiple orders, keeping bar clean
- **Responsible Service**: Checking IDs, recognizing intoxication, cutting off guests
- **Upselling**: Suggesting premium spirits, cocktails, food items

#### Bar Setup and Prep
- **Mise en Place**: Cut fruit, juice prep, simple syrups, garnishes
- **Ice**: Multiple types (cubed, crushed, large format for rocks drinks)
- **Glassware**: Polished and organized by type
- **Speed Rail**: Well spirits within easy reach
- **Beer Taps**: Line cleaning, proper pouring technique
- **POS Setup**: Register login, menu knowledge

#### Service Flow
1. **Greet**: Welcome guests, make eye contact, friendly greeting
2. **Take Order**: Listen carefully, suggest additions or upgrades
3. **Prepare Drinks**: Efficient, proper technique, consistent recipes
4. **Serve**: Present drinks professionally, explain if needed
5. **Check In**: Monitor drink levels, offer another round
6. **Close Out**: Process payment, thank guest, invite return

### Bar Layout and Design

#### Essential Bar Areas

**Front Bar**
- Guest-facing bar top (36-42" height)
- Bar stools (18-24" seat height, 24-30" apart)
- Glass rail/drip tray
- Condiment station (straws, napkins, stirrers)

**Back Bar**
- Spirit display and storage (stepped shelving for visibility)
- Speed rail for well spirits
- Glass storage (hanging racks, shelves)
- Décor and mirrors

**Underbar**
- Ice wells and speed wells
- Blender station
- Sinks (three-compartment for washing, separate hand sink)
- Glass washer or dishwasher
- Refrigeration for beer, garnishes
- Storage for tools, smallwares

**Service Bar** (for cocktail servers)
- Separate area for server drink orders
- Expediter to call out finished drinks
- Limited guest access

### Beverage Cost Control

#### Pour Cost Management
Target beverage cost: 18-24% of beverage sales

**Standard Pour Sizes**:
- Beer: 12-16 oz
- Wine: 5-6 oz (standard), 9 oz (large)
- Spirits: 1.5 oz (standard shot), 2 oz (cocktail pour)
- Cocktails: Varies by recipe

**Cost Control Methods**:
- Measured pouring (jiggers, measured pourers)
- POS integration tracking every pour
- Regular inventory counts (weekly or bi-weekly)
- Par level management
- Standardized recipes and training

#### Common Shrinkage Causes
- Over-pouring by bartenders
- Giving away free drinks (friends, regulars)
- Spillage and waste
- Theft by staff
- Breakage (bottles, glassware)

**Prevention Strategies**:
- Bottle tracking and audits
- Mystery shoppers and observation
- Clear policies on comps and staff drinks
- Responsible pour training
- Surveillance cameras

### Pricing Strategy

#### Standard Markup
- **Beer**: 200-400% markup (draft: $0.50-2 cost → $5-10 retail)
- **Wine**: 300-400% markup by the glass, 200-300% by the bottle
- **Well Cocktails**: 400-500% markup ($2 cost → $10-12 retail)
- **Call/Premium Cocktails**: 300-400% markup ($4 cost → $14-18 retail)

#### Happy Hour Pricing
Discounted drinks to drive off-peak traffic:
- $2-5 off cocktails
- $1-3 off beer
- $5-8 glasses of wine
- Typical hours: 3-7 PM weekdays
- Food specials to boost revenue

#### Bottle Service Pricing
Premium markup for nightclub/lounge table service:
- 300-500% markup over retail ($40 bottle → $200-400 in club)
- Includes mixers, ice, glassware, dedicated server
- VIP experience and table reservation
- Champagne, premium vodka (Grey Goose, Belvedere), cognac

## Regulatory Compliance

### Liquor Licensing

#### License Types (vary by state)
- **On-Premises License**: Serve alcohol for consumption on-site
- **Beer and Wine**: Limited to beer and wine only
- **Full Liquor**: All alcoholic beverages
- **Brewpub/Distillery**: Manufacturing with on-site service
- **Temporary/Event License**: One-time events

#### License Restrictions
- **Hours of Operation**: Mandated closing times (often 2-4 AM)
- **Sunday Sales**: Restricted in some states/counties
- **Location Requirements**: Distance from schools, churches
- **Capacity Limits**: Maximum occupancy
- **Food Service**: Some licenses require food availability

#### License Costs
Vary dramatically by state and local jurisdiction:
- **Low**: $300-3,000 annually (many states)
- **High**: $50,000-400,000+ for transferable licenses (NYC, San Francisco, etc.)
- **Quota Systems**: Limited number of licenses available (requires purchasing existing)

### Responsible Service

#### Checking Identification
- Verify all guests who appear under 30-40
- Acceptable IDs: Driver's license, passport, state ID, military ID
- Check for expiration, photo match, tampering
- ID scanning technology for high-volume venues

#### Signs of Intoxication
- Slurred speech
- Impaired coordination
- Bloodshot eyes
- Aggressive or inappropriate behavior
- Inability to focus or pay attention

#### Refusing Service / Cutting Off
- Politely explain concern for their safety
- Offer water, food, or coffee (doesn't sober, but slows drinking)
- Arrange transportation (taxi, rideshare, friend)
- Do not argue or escalate
- Document incident for legal protection

#### Dram Shop Liability
Legal responsibility for over-serving:
- Bars can be held liable for accidents caused by intoxicated patrons
- Third-party injury lawsuits
- Significant financial exposure
- Insurance requirements and costs
- Staff training critical (TIPS, ServSafe Alcohol)

### Training and Certification

#### TIPS (Training for Intervention ProcedureS)
- Industry-standard responsible service training
- Recognizing intoxication
- Checking IDs effectively
- Intervention techniques
- Legal responsibilities

#### ServSafe Alcohol
- National Restaurant Association program
- Alcohol service fundamentals
- Responsible service practices
- Legal issues and liability
- Certification recognized nationwide

#### Local Certifications
Many jurisdictions require:
- Alcohol server permits/cards
- Food handler cards (if serving food)
- Renewal every 2-5 years

## Financial Performance

### Revenue Mix
Typical bar revenue breakdown:
- **Beverage**: 70-90% (spirits, beer, wine)
- **Food**: 10-30% (if offered)
- **Cover Charges**: 0-20% (nightclubs, live music venues)
- **Other**: Merchandise, bottle sales, private events

### Cost Structure

#### Beverage Cost: 18-24% of beverage sales
- Spirits: 15-20% (highest margin)
- Beer: 20-30%
- Wine: 25-35%

#### Labor Cost: 25-35% of sales
- Bartenders (typically $5-15/hr + tips)
- Barbacks (support staff)
- Security/Bouncers
- Cocktail servers (if applicable)
- Managers

#### Occupancy: 8-15% of sales
Often higher in prime locations (downtown, tourist areas)

#### Operating Expenses: 20-30%
- Utilities
- Marketing and entertainment
- Supplies (glassware, napkins, straws)
- Credit card processing
- Insurance (liability, liquor liability)
- Licenses and permits
- Repairs and maintenance

### Profit Margins
- **Neighborhood Bar**: 8-12% net profit
- **Nightclub**: 10-15% net profit (higher beverage margins, bottle service)
- **Cocktail Bar**: 6-10% net profit (higher labor, ingredient costs)
- **Sports Bar**: 5-8% net profit (higher food costs, lower margins)

### Key Metrics

#### Sales per Square Foot
```
Annual Sales / Total Square Footage
```
Benchmark: $400-800+ depending on concept

#### Sales per Labor Hour
```
Total Sales / Total Labor Hours Worked
```
Target: $80-150+

#### Average Transaction
```
Total Sales / Number of Transactions
```
Varies widely: $15-30 for casual bars, $40-100+ for upscale/nightclubs

#### Check per Person
```
Total Sales / Guest Count
```

#### Liquor Cost Percentage
```
Liquor COGS / Liquor Sales
```
Target: 18-24%
