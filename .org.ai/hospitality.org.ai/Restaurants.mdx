# Restaurants and Other Eating Places (NAICS 7225)

The restaurant industry represents establishments that prepare and serve meals, snacks, and beverages for immediate consumption on-premises or for takeout. This sector encompasses diverse formats from fine dining to quick-service restaurants, serving as essential community gathering places and major employment generators.

## Overview

Restaurants are highly competitive, customer-focused businesses characterized by thin profit margins, labor-intensive operations, and strong dependence on location, service quality, and culinary execution. Success requires operational excellence, marketing savvy, and the ability to adapt to changing consumer preferences.

## Restaurant Formats

### Full-Service Restaurants (NAICS 722511)

#### Fine Dining
High-end establishments with exceptional cuisine and formal service:
- **Service Style**: Multi-course meals, sommelier service, tableside preparation
- **Average Check**: $75-200+ per person
- **Staffing**: High staff-to-guest ratio, specialized positions (captain, sommelier)
- **Menu**: Seasonal, chef-driven, premium ingredients, extensive wine list
- **Examples**: The French Laundry, Eleven Madison Park, Alinea, Per Se
- **Target Market**: Special occasions, affluent diners, culinary enthusiasts

#### Upscale Casual
Premium-casual dining with quality food in a relaxed atmosphere:
- **Service Style**: Full table service, attentive but not formal
- **Average Check**: $35-75 per person
- **Menu**: Diverse offerings, seasonal specials, craft cocktails
- **Décor**: Sophisticated but comfortable, mood lighting
- **Examples**: The Cheesecake Factory, Capital Grille, Fleming's, Eddie V's
- **Target Market**: Date nights, business dining, celebrations

#### Casual Dining
Family-friendly restaurants with moderate prices:
- **Service Style**: Table service, casual atmosphere
- **Average Check**: $15-35 per person
- **Menu**: Broad appeal, familiar dishes, kids' menu
- **Examples**: Applebee's, Chili's, Olive Garden, Red Lobster, Outback Steakhouse
- **Target Market**: Families, groups, everyday dining

#### Family Style
Shared platters served to the table:
- **Service Style**: Large portions for sharing, communal dining
- **Average Check**: $20-40 per person
- **Menu**: Traditional comfort foods, hearty portions
- **Examples**: Maggiano's, Carrabba's, local family restaurants
- **Target Market**: Large groups, families, celebrations

### Limited-Service Restaurants (NAICS 722513)

#### Fast Casual
Quick service with higher quality and fresh ingredients:
- **Service Style**: Order at counter, food brought to table or called when ready
- **Average Check**: $10-18 per person
- **Menu**: Customizable bowls/burritos, fresh ingredients, healthy options
- **Décor**: Modern, clean, often urban aesthetic
- **Examples**: Chipotle, Panera Bread, Sweetgreen, Shake Shack, Cava
- **Target Market**: Health-conscious consumers, millennials, lunch crowd

#### Quick Service / Fast Food (QSR)
Standardized menu with speed and efficiency:
- **Service Style**: Counter service, drive-thru, mobile ordering
- **Average Check**: $7-12 per person
- **Menu**: Limited selection, consistent nationwide, value focus
- **Speed**: Food served within 3-5 minutes
- **Examples**: McDonald's, Burger King, Taco Bell, Subway, KFC, Wendy's
- **Target Market**: Value seekers, families, convenience-focused consumers

#### Fast Fine / Premium Fast Casual
Upscale quick service with quality ingredients:
- **Service Style**: Counter ordering with elevated presentation
- **Average Check**: $15-25 per person
- **Menu**: Chef-crafted dishes, premium proteins, sophisticated flavors
- **Examples**: Dig Inn, Mendocino Farms, &pizza, Flower Child
- **Target Market**: Urban professionals, quality-seeking diners in a hurry

#### Coffee Shops and Cafés
Beverage-focused with light food options:
- **Service Style**: Counter service, sometimes table service
- **Primary Revenue**: Coffee, espresso drinks, teas
- **Food**: Pastries, sandwiches, breakfast items
- **Examples**: Starbucks, Peet's Coffee, local cafés, Caribou Coffee
- **Target Market**: Morning commuters, remote workers, social gatherings

### Specialty Restaurants

#### Buffet Restaurants
All-you-can-eat format with self-service:
- **Service Style**: Customers serve themselves from buffet line
- **Pricing**: Fixed price per person
- **Menu**: Wide variety to appeal to diverse tastes
- **Examples**: Golden Corral, Old Country Buffet, Souplantation (closed)
- **Challenges**: Food waste, cost control, health code compliance

#### Cafeterias
Self-service with à la carte pricing:
- **Service Style**: Customers select items from serving line
- **Pricing**: Individual item pricing
- **Location**: Often in offices, hospitals, schools, government buildings
- **Examples**: Luby's, institutional cafeterias

#### Snack and Beverage Bars
Quick refreshments and light snacks:
- **Offerings**: Coffee, smoothies, juices, pastries, snacks
- **Service**: Quick counter service
- **Examples**: Jamba Juice, Smoothie King, Juice Press, Auntie Anne's

### Emerging Formats

#### Ghost Kitchens / Virtual Restaurants
Delivery-only operations with no dining room:
- **Service Style**: Online ordering, third-party delivery only
- **Real Estate**: Commissary kitchens, minimal front-of-house
- **Menu**: Optimized for delivery (travels well, maintains quality)
- **Efficiency**: Multiple brands from one kitchen
- **Examples**: CloudKitchens, Kitchen United, virtual brands on DoorDash

#### Food Halls
Curated collection of food vendors with shared seating:
- **Format**: Multiple independent vendors in one space
- **Service**: Order from different vendors, shared dining area
- **Atmosphere**: Social, vibrant, urban
- **Examples**: Eataly, Chelsea Market, Time Out Market, local food halls
- **Target Market**: Variety seekers, social diners, tourists

#### Pop-Ups and Supper Clubs
Temporary or exclusive dining experiences:
- **Format**: Limited-time restaurants or invitation-only dinners
- **Service**: Often tasting menus, experimental concepts
- **Purpose**: Test concepts, create buzz, chef showcases
- **Marketing**: Social media, word-of-mouth, limited availability

## Restaurant Operations

### Front of House (FOH)

#### Host/Hostess
Guest arrival and seating management:
- Table management and waitlist
- Reservation systems (OpenTable, Resy, Yelp)
- Guest greeting and farewell
- Managing wait times and guest expectations
- Coordinating with servers on table status

#### Servers
Primary guest service and order taking:
- Menu knowledge and recommendations
- Order taking and entry into POS
- Food and beverage delivery
- Guest check-ins and service recovery
- Upselling appetizers, desserts, drinks
- Payment processing and tips

#### Bartenders
Beverage preparation and bar service:
- Cocktail, beer, and wine service
- Recipe knowledge and consistency
- Inventory management and ordering
- Bar guest service and conversation
- Cash handling and tab management
- Responsible alcohol service (checking IDs, cutting off intoxicated guests)

#### Bussers
Table clearing and dining room maintenance:
- Clearing and resetting tables
- Refilling water and bread
- Assisting servers with service
- Maintaining dining room cleanliness
- Restocking server stations

#### Runners/Expeditors
Food delivery and quality control:
- Delivering food from kitchen to tables
- Verifying order accuracy
- Food presentation inspection
- Coordinating timing between courses
- Communication between FOH and BOH

### Back of House (BOH)

#### Executive Chef / Chef de Cuisine
Kitchen leadership and culinary direction:
- Menu development and pricing
- Recipe creation and standardization
- Kitchen staff management
- Quality control and plating standards
- Vendor relationships and purchasing
- Food cost management
- Compliance with health codes

#### Sous Chef
Second-in-command kitchen management:
- Day-to-day kitchen operations
- Staff scheduling and training
- Inventory and ordering
- Line supervision and quality control
- Covering chef's days off

#### Line Cooks
Station-specific food preparation:
- **Grill**: Grilled meats, fish, vegetables
- **Sauté**: Pan-cooked dishes, sauces
- **Fry**: Fried items, fryers management
- **Pantry/Garde Manger**: Cold appetizers, salads, dessert plating
- **Expo**: Final plate assembly and quality check

#### Prep Cooks
Ingredient preparation and mise en place:
- Chopping vegetables
- Preparing sauces and stocks
- Portioning proteins
- Prepping ingredients for service
- Following prep lists and recipes

#### Dishwashers
Sanitation and dish management:
- Washing dishes, glassware, and utensils
- Maintaining clean dish area
- Organizing clean dishes for service
- Kitchen cleaning support
- Proper chemical handling

### Management

#### General Manager
Overall restaurant operations:
- P&L responsibility and financial performance
- Staff hiring, training, and management
- Guest experience and service standards
- Marketing and community relations
- Compliance with regulations
- Vendor relationships

#### Kitchen Manager
Back of house operations:
- Food cost management (target: 28-35%)
- Inventory control and ordering
- Kitchen staff scheduling
- Equipment maintenance
- Health and safety compliance
- Waste reduction

#### Front of House Manager
Dining room operations:
- Service standards and training
- Staff scheduling and labor cost control
- Guest satisfaction and complaints
- Reservation management
- Floor supervision during service

## Financial Management

### Revenue Drivers

#### Average Check
Total sales divided by guest count:
- Menu pricing strategy
- Upselling training
- Menu engineering (promoting high-margin items)
- Beverage sales (typically higher margin)
- Add-ons and premium options

#### Table Turns
Number of seatings per table per service period:
- Service efficiency
- Reservation spacing
- Table size mix
- Menu complexity (affects kitchen timing)
- Target: 1.5-3 turns for dinner, 2-4 for lunch

#### Seat Utilization
Percentage of seats occupied during operating hours:
- Day-part analysis (breakfast, lunch, dinner)
- Weekday vs. weekend patterns
- Reservation vs. walk-in mix
- Bar seating utilization

### Cost Structure

#### Food Cost (COGS)
Target: 28-35% of food sales
- Recipe costing and menu pricing
- Portion control and consistency
- Vendor negotiation
- Waste reduction
- Inventory management (FIFO)
- Theft prevention

#### Beverage Cost
Target: 20-30% of beverage sales
- Pour cost management
- Proper measuring and pouring
- Inventory tracking and par levels
- Spillage and waste control
- Theft prevention (employee drinking)

#### Labor Cost
Target: 25-35% of total sales (varies by format)
- Staff scheduling to sales forecasts
- Productivity monitoring
- Cross-training and efficiency
- Overtime minimization
- Benefits and payroll taxes
- Turnover costs

#### Prime Cost
Food + Beverage + Labor costs
- Target: 55-65% of sales
- Primary controllable expenses
- Key indicator of operational health

#### Occupancy Cost
Target: 6-10% of sales
- Rent (often percentage rent in malls/airports)
- Common area maintenance (CAM)
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Location risk (too high impacts profitability)

#### Operating Expenses
Remaining costs to run the business:
- Utilities (gas, electric, water)
- Supplies (paper goods, cleaning supplies, smallwares)
- Marketing and advertising
- Repairs and maintenance
- Credit card processing fees (2-3%)
- Technology and software subscriptions
- Professional services (accounting, legal)

### Profit Margins

#### Full-Service Casual Dining
- Gross margin: 60-65%
- Net profit: 3-7%

#### Fast Casual
- Gross margin: 65-70%
- Net profit: 6-9%

#### Quick Service (QSR)
- Gross margin: 65-70%
- Net profit: 6-10%

#### Fine Dining
- Gross margin: 58-62%
- Net profit: 3-5% (lower due to higher labor costs)

### Key Performance Indicators

#### Sales per Square Foot
```
Annual Sales / Total Square Footage
```
Benchmark: $300-500+ (varies by concept)

#### Sales per Labor Hour
```
Total Sales / Total Labor Hours
```
Measures labor productivity

#### Revenue per Available Seat Hour (RevPASH)
```
Revenue / (Seats × Hours Open)
```
Optimizes seating capacity and operating hours

#### Guest Count Growth
Year-over-year or comp period growth in number of guests

#### Same-Store Sales Growth
Revenue growth at locations open 12+ months (excludes new openings)

## Menu Engineering

### Menu Design Principles

#### Menu Psychology
- Eye movement patterns (top-right "sweet spot")
- Pricing presentation (no $ signs, prices in text)
- Strategic placement of high-margin items
- Use of boxes, icons, or callouts for featured items
- Descriptions that appeal to senses and emotions

#### Menu Mix Analysis
Classifying items by profitability and popularity:
- **Stars**: High profit, high popularity (promote heavily)
- **Plowhorses**: Low profit, high popularity (consider repricing)
- **Puzzles**: High profit, low popularity (improve visibility or remove)
- **Dogs**: Low profit, low popularity (remove from menu)

#### Menu Size
- **Fine Dining**: 6-12 entrées, focused menu
- **Casual Dining**: 40-80+ items, broad appeal
- **Fast Casual**: 10-20 core items, simple customization
- **QSR**: 10-30 items, value meal bundles

### Pricing Strategy

#### Cost-Plus Pricing
```
Menu Price = (Food Cost / Target Food Cost %)
```
Example: $8 food cost / 32% = $25 menu price

#### Competitive Pricing
Setting prices based on local market and competition

#### Value Perception
Balancing price with perceived value (portion, quality, service, ambiance)

#### Price Psychology
- $19.95 vs. $20 (charm pricing)
- Price anchoring (expensive item makes others seem reasonable)
- Decoy pricing (medium option to make large seem like better value)

### Menu Optimization

#### Seasonal Menus
- Fresh, peak-season ingredients
- Limited-time offers (LTOs) to drive traffic
- Regional specialization
- Reduced food waste

#### Dietary Accommodations
- Vegetarian and vegan options
- Gluten-free items
- Allergen awareness and labeling
- Healthy/low-calorie options

#### Menu Item Performance
- Track sales mix by item
- Analyze profit contribution
- Monitor food cost variance
- Customer feedback and reviews

## Challenges and Risks

### Labor Challenges
- Staffing shortages (cooks, servers, dishwashers)
- Minimum wage increases
- Benefits expectations (health insurance, paid time off)
- High turnover (70-100% annually)
- Immigration policy impacts

### Rising Costs
- Food inflation (proteins, produce)
- Occupancy costs (rent increases)
- Utilities and energy
- Insurance (liability, workers comp)
- Delivery platform commissions

### Competition
- Market saturation in many areas
- New openings and concepts
- Chain expansion
- Delivery app discovery favors new restaurants
- Consumer trend shifts

### Regulatory Compliance
- Health department inspections
- Liquor licensing and compliance
- Labor law compliance (overtime, breaks)
- ADA accessibility requirements
- COVID-19 and future health protocols

### Economic Sensitivity
- Restaurants among first spending cuts in downturns
- Discretionary spending category
- Regional economic impacts
- Seasonality and tourism dependence

### Failure Rate
- ~60% of restaurants fail within first year
- ~80% fail within five years
- Primary causes: undercapitalization, poor location, weak concept, operational execution
