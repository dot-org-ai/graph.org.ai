# Electric Power Generation, Transmission and Distribution

The electric power industry (NAICS 2211) comprises the generation of electrical energy from various sources, transmission over high-voltage networks, and distribution to end-use customers. This critical infrastructure sector operates as a highly regulated, capital-intensive industry requiring continuous real-time balancing of supply and demand.

## Overview

The electric utility value chain consists of three main segments:

- **Generation (APQC 10.0)**: Producing electricity from fossil fuels, nuclear, renewable, and other sources
- **Transmission (APQC 11.0)**: Moving power over long distances via high-voltage networks (69 kV to 765 kV)
- **Distribution (APQC 11.0)**: Delivering power to customers via local networks (typically <69 kV)

### Industry Scale

- **U.S. Generation Capacity**: ~1,200 GW (2024)
- **Annual Generation**: ~4,200 TWh
- **Transmission Lines**: 700,000+ circuit miles
- **Distribution Lines**: 5.5 million+ miles
- **Customers**: 150+ million residential, commercial, industrial
- **Grid Reliability**: 99.9%+ uptime (SAIDI/SAIFI metrics)

## Power Generation (APQC 10.0)

### Generation Technologies

#### Fossil Fuel Generation

**Natural Gas Combined Cycle (NGCC) - NAICS 221112**
- Gas turbine + steam turbine (combined cycle)
- Efficiency: 50-60% (vs. 33-38% for simple cycle)
- Fast ramping capability (30-60 minutes to full power)
- Lower emissions than coal (50% less CO2 per kWh)
- Capacity factor: 50-60%
- Capital cost: $1,000-1,200/kW

**Coal-Fired Steam Plants - NAICS 221112**
- Pulverized coal boilers, steam turbines
- Efficiency: 33-40% (subcritical), 40-45% (supercritical)
- Baseload operation, slow ramping
- Emissions controls: SCR (NOx), FGD (SO2), ESP (particulates)
- Capacity factor: 40-50% (declining due to retirements)
- Coal ash handling and disposal

**Peaking/Simple Cycle Gas Turbines**
- Rapid start capability (10-20 minutes)
- Lower efficiency (25-35%) but low capital cost
- Operate during peak demand periods
- Capacity factor: 5-15%
- Capital cost: $700-900/kW

#### Nuclear Power - NAICS 221113

**Light Water Reactors (LWRs)**
- Pressurized Water Reactors (PWR): 2-loop, 3-loop, 4-loop designs
- Boiling Water Reactors (BWR)
- Typical capacity: 1,000-1,400 MW per unit
- Capacity factor: 90%+ (high reliability)
- Fuel: Enriched uranium (3-5% U-235)
- Operating license: 40 years, many extended to 60-80 years

**Nuclear Operations**
- 18-24 month refueling cycles
- Strict Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) oversight
- Emergency core cooling systems (ECCS)
- Spent fuel storage: on-site pools and dry casks
- Security: armed guards, protected area, vital areas

**Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)**
- Capacity: 50-300 MW per module
- Factory fabrication, modular construction
- Passive safety systems
- NuScale (first U.S. design certification 2020)

#### Renewable Energy

**Hydroelectric Power - NAICS 221111**
- Conventional dams: Impoundment reservoirs
- Run-of-river: Minimal storage
- Pumped storage: Energy storage (charge/discharge)
- Capacity factor: 30-50% (varies by hydrology)
- Licensing: FERC, environmental (fish passage, flows)
- U.S. capacity: ~80 GW conventional, ~23 GW pumped storage

**Wind Power - NAICS 221115**
- Onshore turbines: 2-5 MW, 80-120 m hub height
- Offshore turbines: 8-15 MW, larger rotor diameters
- Capacity factor: 25-35% onshore, 40-50% offshore
- Curtailment during low demand or transmission constraints
- Production Tax Credit (PTC) support
- U.S. capacity: ~140 GW

**Solar Photovoltaic (PV) - NAICS 221114**
- Utility-scale: >5 MW, fixed tilt or single-axis tracking
- Distributed: Rooftop, community solar
- Technology: Crystalline silicon (mono, poly), thin film
- Capacity factor: 15-25% (varies by location, tracking)
- Inverters: String, central, microinverters
- U.S. capacity: ~100 GW utility-scale, ~40 GW distributed

**Concentrated Solar Power (CSP) - NAICS 221114**
- Parabolic troughs, power towers, linear Fresnel
- Thermal storage (molten salt) enables dispatchability
- Capacity factor: 25-35% (higher with storage)
- Primarily in southwestern U.S.

**Geothermal - NAICS 221116**
- Flash steam, binary cycle plants
- Baseload operation (90%+ capacity factor)
- Resources in western U.S. (California, Nevada)
- U.S. capacity: ~3.7 GW

**Biomass - NAICS 221117**
- Wood waste, agricultural residues, dedicated energy crops
- Combustion, gasification, anaerobic digestion
- Carbon considerations: Renewable but emits CO2
- U.S. capacity: ~14 GW

### Generation Dispatch and Optimization (APQC 10.2)

**Merit Order Dispatch**
- Economic dispatch based on marginal cost
- Lowest variable cost units dispatched first
- Typical order: Nuclear → hydro → coal → NGCC → peakers
- Renewable energy: Zero marginal cost, priority dispatch

**Unit Commitment**
- Day-ahead scheduling of generation units
- Start-up costs, minimum run times, ramp rates
- Reserve requirements (spinning, non-spinning, regulation)
- Security-constrained unit commitment (SCUC)

**Automatic Generation Control (AGC)**
- Real-time frequency regulation (maintain 60.00 Hz)
- Area Control Error (ACE) monitoring
- Economic dispatch signal every 4-10 seconds
- Ancillary services: regulation up/down

**Renewable Integration Challenges**
- Variability: Wind and solar output changes with weather
- Forecasting: Day-ahead and hour-ahead predictions
- Flexibility: Fast-ramping resources needed (gas, hydro, batteries)
- Curtailment: Reducing renewable output during oversupply

### Operations and Maintenance (APQC 10.3)

**Preventive Maintenance**
- Scheduled outages for inspections, overhauls
- Turbine/generator maintenance
- Boiler/heat exchanger cleaning
- Transformer oil analysis

**Predictive Maintenance**
- Vibration monitoring (rotating equipment)
- Thermography (electrical connections)
- Oil/coolant analysis
- Acoustic monitoring (steam leaks)

**Reliability and Availability**
- Equivalent Availability Factor (EAF)
- Forced Outage Rate (FOR)
- Heat rate monitoring (efficiency)
- Emissions monitoring (CEMS - Continuous Emissions Monitoring)

## Transmission (APQC 11.1)

### Transmission Infrastructure

**High-Voltage Networks**
- Extra-High Voltage (EHV): 230 kV, 345 kV, 500 kV, 765 kV
- Long-distance bulk power transfer
- Interconnection between utilities
- U.S.: Three interconnections (Eastern, Western, Texas)

**Substations**
- Step-up substations: Generation to transmission voltage
- Switching substations: Network reconfiguration
- Step-down substations: Transmission to distribution voltage
- Equipment: Transformers, circuit breakers, disconnect switches, bus

**HVDC (High-Voltage Direct Current)**
- Long-distance transmission with lower losses
- Asynchronous interconnection (different frequencies)
- Submarine cables
- Converter stations (AC/DC, DC/AC)

### Grid Operations (APQC 11.4)

**Balancing Authorities**
- Maintain load-resource balance in real-time
- North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC) regions
- Energy Imbalance Markets (EIM): Western EIM, SPP Markets+

**Transmission System Operators (TSO/ISO)**
- Independent System Operators (ISO): CAISO, ERCOT, ISO-NE, NYISO, PJM, SPP, MISO
- Day-ahead and real-time energy markets
- Locational Marginal Pricing (LMP)
- Congestion management and transmission rights

**Supervisory Control and Data Analysis (SCADA)**
- Real-time monitoring and control
- Remote terminal units (RTUs) at substations
- Energy Management System (EMS)
- State estimation and contingency analysis

**Reliability Coordination**
- Transmission Reliability Margin (TRM)
- Capacity Benefit Margin (CBM)
- Transfer capability calculations (ATC, TTC)
- Outage coordination

### Grid Reliability and Planning

**NERC Reliability Standards**
- Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP): Cybersecurity
- Transmission Planning (TPL): N-1, N-1-1 contingency criteria
- Operations and Planning (TOP, IRO): Real-time operations
- Compliance monitoring and enforcement

**Transmission Planning**
- Load forecasting (peak demand, energy)
- Generation interconnection studies
- Transmission expansion planning
- Cost allocation (beneficiary pays, socialized)

**Interconnection Queue**
- Generator interconnection requests
- System impact studies, facilities studies
- Network upgrades required
- Queue reform (addressing backlogs)

## Distribution (APQC 11.2)

### Distribution Infrastructure

**Distribution Networks**
- Primary distribution: 4 kV to 35 kV (typical 12.47 kV or 13.8 kV)
- Secondary distribution: 120/240 V single-phase, 208/120 V or 480/277 V three-phase
- Overhead lines: Wood poles, crossarms, insulators
- Underground cables: Direct buried, conduit, pad-mount transformers

**Distribution Substations**
- Transmission to distribution voltage transformation
- Circuit breakers and reclosers
- Capacitor banks (voltage support, power factor correction)
- SCADA monitoring and control

**Sectionalizing and Protection**
- Reclosers: Automatic circuit interruption and restoration
- Sectionalizers: Isolate faulted sections
- Fuses: Overcurrent protection for laterals
- Coordination: Selective fault clearing

### Distribution Operations (APQC 11.2)

**Voltage Regulation**
- Load Tap Changers (LTCs) on substation transformers
- Line voltage regulators
- Capacitor banks (fixed, switched)
- Distributed generation impacts on voltage

**Outage Management**
- Outage Management System (OMS)
- Trouble call analysis
- Crew dispatch and routing
- Customer notifications

**Reliability Metrics**
- SAIDI (System Average Interruption Duration Index): Minutes/year
- SAIFI (System Average Interruption Frequency Index): Interruptions/year
- CAIDI (Customer Average Interruption Duration Index): Minutes/interruption
- MAIFI (Momentary Average Interruption Frequency Index)

**Vegetation Management**
- Tree trimming programs (critical for reliability)
- Cycle: 3-7 years depending on growth rates
- Line clearance standards
- Cost: 10-15% of distribution O&M

### Advanced Distribution Management

**Distribution Management System (DMS)**
- Real-time monitoring and control
- Fault location, isolation, and service restoration (FLISR)
- Volt/VAR optimization (VVO)
- Topology processing

**Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI)**
- Smart meters: Interval data (15-min, hourly)
- Two-way communication
- Remote connect/disconnect
- Meter Data Management System (MDMS)

**Distributed Energy Resources (DER)**
- Rooftop solar, battery storage, electric vehicles
- IEEE 1547 interconnection standard
- Hosting capacity analysis
- DER management systems (DERMS)

## Customer Service (APQC 12.0)

### Metering and Billing (APQC 12.1)

**Rate Structures**
- Residential: Flat rate, tiered, time-of-use (TOU)
- Commercial: Demand charges ($/kW), energy charges ($/kWh)
- Industrial: Large power rates, interruptible service
- Rate cases and public utility commission (PUC) approval

**Net Metering**
- Crediting customers for excess DER generation
- One-for-one credit vs. avoided cost
- Caps on program size or participation
- Successor tariffs (NEM 3.0 in California)

**Customer Information System (CIS)**
- Billing and payment processing
- Customer accounts and service history
- Integration with AMI, OMS, GIS

### Demand Response and Efficiency (APQC 12.4)

**Demand Response Programs**
- Direct load control: Cycling of air conditioners, water heaters
- Interruptible/curtailable service: Industrial customers
- Time-based rates: Peak pricing, critical peak pricing
- Aggregator platforms: Residential and C&I participation

**Energy Efficiency Programs**
- Lighting (LEDs), HVAC upgrades, building insulation
- Utility incentives and rebates
- Energy audits
- Regulatory mandates: Energy Efficiency Resource Standards (EERS)

### Customer Connections (APQC 12.3)

**New Service Connections**
- Service application and engineering review
- Trenching, transformer installation, meter set
- Temporary service (construction)

**Disconnections and Collections**
- Non-payment disconnection policies
- Reconnection fees
- Payment plans and assistance programs

## Electricity Markets (APQC 13.0)

### Wholesale Markets

**Day-Ahead Market**
- Generator offers, load bids
- Security-constrained economic dispatch (SCED)
- Locational Marginal Pricing (LMP): Energy, congestion, losses
- Financial transmission rights (FTRs), congestion revenue rights (CRRs)

**Real-Time Market**
- 5-minute dispatch
- Imbalance settlement
- Real-time LMPs

**Ancillary Services Markets**
- Regulation: Frequency control (AGC)
- Spinning reserves: Immediate response to contingencies
- Non-spinning reserves: 10-30 minute response
- Replacement reserves

**Capacity Markets**
- Ensuring resource adequacy (sufficient generation for peak demand)
- Forward capacity auctions (e.g., PJM: 3 years ahead)
- ICAP (Installed Capacity), UCAP (Unforced Capacity)
- Demand curve pricing

### Bilateral Contracts

**Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs)**
- Long-term contracts (10-25 years) for renewable energy
- Fixed price or indexed pricing
- Physical or financial settlement
- Corporate PPAs (large customers)

**Tolling Agreements**
- Generator operates for buyer's account
- Buyer provides fuel, receives output
- Common for merchant gas plants

## Environmental and Sustainability

### Air Quality Regulations

**Clean Air Act**
- National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS)
- New Source Performance Standards (NSPS)
- Maximum Achievable Control Technology (MACT)
- Regional Haze Rule

**Emissions Controls**
- Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR): NOx removal
- Flue Gas Desulfurization (FGD): SO2 scrubbing (wet, dry)
- Electrostatic Precipitators (ESP), Baghouses: Particulate removal
- Activated carbon injection: Mercury control

### Carbon and Climate

**Greenhouse Gas Emissions**
- Coal: ~1,000 kg CO2/MWh
- Natural gas: ~400-500 kg CO2/MWh (NGCC)
- Nuclear, renewables: Near-zero direct emissions

**Carbon Regulations and Pricing**
- EPA Clean Power Plan (vacated), Affordable Clean Energy (ACE) rule
- State programs: RGGI (Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative), California cap-and-trade
- Carbon taxes (not in U.S., but in other countries)

**Decarbonization Pathways**
- Renewable energy expansion (wind, solar)
- Energy storage deployment
- Coal plant retirements
- Electrification of transportation, heating
- Carbon capture and storage (CCS) on gas plants
- Nuclear new builds and SMRs
- Green hydrogen for industrial processes

### Water Use

**Cooling Systems**
- Once-through cooling: Large water withdrawals, thermal discharge
- Recirculating cooling towers: Lower withdrawals, evaporation
- Dry cooling: No water use, efficiency penalty
- Hybrid (wet-dry) cooling

**Clean Water Act**
- Effluent Limitation Guidelines (ELG)
- Thermal discharge limits (NPDES permits)
- 316(b): Cooling water intake structures (fish protection)

## Grid Modernization and Future Trends

### Smart Grid Technologies

**Grid-Edge Intelligence**
- Smart inverters (IEEE 1547-2018): Voltage/frequency support
- Battery energy storage systems (BESS): Grid services, peak shaving
- Electric vehicle charging management: Load flexibility
- Building energy management systems (BEMS)

**Grid Analytics**
- High-resolution load forecasting
- Predictive asset maintenance
- Outage prediction (weather-driven)
- Synthetic PMU data for state estimation

### Microgrids

**Characteristics**
- Ability to island from main grid
- Local generation and storage
- Resiliency during grid outages
- Campus microgrids (universities, military bases, hospitals)

**Control Systems**
- Microgrid controller
- Energy management system (EMS)
- Grid synchronization and islanding logic

### Electrification

**Transportation**
- Electric vehicles (EVs): Light-duty, medium/heavy-duty trucks, buses
- Charging infrastructure: Level 1 (120V), Level 2 (240V), DC fast charging
- Grid impacts: Peak demand, distribution upgrades
- Managed charging: Time-of-use rates, demand response

**Building Heating**
- Heat pumps replacing gas furnaces, boilers
- Induction cooktops replacing gas stoves
- Building electrification policies (Berkeley, California)

**Industrial Processes**
- Electric arc furnaces (steel)
- Heat pumps for industrial heat
- Green hydrogen for ammonia, chemicals

### Transmission Expansion

**Challenges**
- Siting and permitting (10+ years)
- Cost allocation disputes
- NIMBY (Not In My Backyard) opposition

**Solutions**
- Regional transmission planning
- Federal siting authority (limited)
- Advanced conductors (higher capacity on existing rights-of-way)
- Dynamic line rating (DLR)

## Safety and Reliability

### Worker Safety

**Electrical Hazards**
- Arc flash: Protective equipment, arc-rated clothing
- Electrocution: Lockout/tagout, grounding procedures
- Working at heights: Fall protection, pole climbing

**OSHA Regulations**
- 1910.269: Electric power generation, transmission, and distribution
- 1910.147: Lockout/tagout (LOTO)
- 1926 Subpart V: Power transmission and distribution (construction)

### Public Safety

**Vegetation Management**
- Preventing tree contact with lines
- Wildfire risk (California: PSPS - Public Safety Power Shutoffs)

**Underground Damage Prevention**
- Call before you dig (811)
- One-call notification systems

**Stray Voltage**
- Neutral-to-earth voltage
- Livestock and human safety concerns

### Cybersecurity

**NERC CIP Standards**
- CIP-002: Critical asset identification
- CIP-003 to CIP-009: Security management controls
- CIP-010 to CIP-014: Configuration, vulnerability, physical security

**Threats**
- Nation-state actors (Russia, China)
- Ransomware attacks
- Insider threats
