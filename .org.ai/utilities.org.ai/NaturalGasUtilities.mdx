# Natural Gas Distribution and Supply

The natural gas industry (NAICS 2212) encompasses the distribution of natural gas to residential, commercial, and industrial customers through extensive pipeline networks and local distribution companies. This sector represents a critical component of the energy infrastructure, providing heating, cooking, and power generation services to millions of customers.

## Overview

Natural gas distribution involves the collection, transmission, storage, and delivery of natural gas from production sources to end consumers. The value chain includes:

- **Production/Sourcing (APQC 13.0)**: Acquiring natural gas from upstream E&P operators
- **Transmission (APQC 11.0)**: Long-distance pipeline transport via high-pressure networks
- **Storage (APQC 13.0)**: Underground and LNG storage for supply management
- **Distribution (APQC 11.0-11.4)**: Local delivery via medium and low-pressure networks
- **Customer Service (APQC 12.0)**: Metering, billing, safety, and customer support

### Industry Scale (U.S., 2024)

- **Annual Consumption**: 30-32 trillion cubic feet (Tcf)
- **Transmission Pipelines**: 300,000+ miles
- **Distribution Pipelines**: 2+ million miles
- **Distribution Companies (LDCs)**: 500+ local distribution companies
- **Customers Served**: 70+ million (residential, commercial, industrial)
- **Residential Customers**: ~50 million
- **Commercial Customers**: ~5 million
- **Industrial Customers**: <1 million (but high consumption)
- **Market Value**: $150+ billion (distribution)
- **Employees**: 100,000+ (distribution, operations, service)

### Market Participants

**Integrated Utilities**
- Own production, transmission, and distribution assets
- Vertically integrated from wellhead to customer meter
- Examples: Dominion Energy, Duke Energy, American Electric Power

**Pipeline Companies**
- Own and operate transmission pipelines
- Regulated as natural monopolies (FERC jurisdiction)
- Open access: Third-party gas shippers use pipeline capacity
- Examples: Williams Company, Energy Transfer, Kinder Morgan

**Local Distribution Companies (LDCs)**
- Own and operate distribution networks
- Serve specific geographic areas
- Primarily investor-owned utilities (IOUs)
- Also public utilities and cooperatives
- Examples: Con Edison, Peoples Natural Gas, Southwest Gas

**Producers and Marketers**
- Extract or procure natural gas
- Sell to utilities, pipeline companies, and large industrial users
- Wholesale market: Henry Hub (Louisiana), trading hubs

**Storage and LNG Operators**
- Operate underground storage and LNG facilities
- Manage supply/demand mismatch across seasons
- Provide capacity lease services

## Supply Sources and Economics

### Natural Gas Supply Chain

**Production Sources**
- Conventional onshore: Texas, Oklahoma, Wyoming, Kansas
- Unconventional: Shale gas (Permian, Marcellus, Haynesville, Bakken)
  - Hydraulic fracturing (fracking): Extraction technology
  - Horizontal drilling: Multiple wells per pad
- Offshore Gulf of Mexico: Deepwater and shallow water platforms
- Canadian imports: Alberta, British Columbia, Prairie provinces
- LNG imports: Minimal (U.S. is now exporter)

**Production Volumes (U.S., 2024)**
- Total production: ~30+ Tcf/year
- Shale gas: ~65% of production (major change from 2010)
- Conventional: ~25%
- Associated gas (oil production byproduct): ~10%

**Price Formation (APQC 13.0)**
- Spot markets: Henry Hub (Louisiana) reference price
- Futures: NYMEX natural gas contracts
- Regional hubs: Basis differentials from Henry Hub
- Seasonal variation: Summer lows, winter peaks (heating demand)
- Winter spike: December-February demand 2-3x summer
- Price range: $2-4/MMBtu typical (historically $0.5-14+/MMBtu)

### Sourcing and Procurement (APQC 13.1-13.2)

**Supply Portfolios**
- Base load: Long-term supply contracts (5-10 years)
- Spot purchases: Daily or monthly market purchases
- Portfolio optimization: Minimize costs, manage exposure
- Hedging: Futures contracts, options, swaps
- Diversification: Multiple suppliers, transportation routes

**Pipeline Capacity**
- Firm transportation: Guaranteed capacity (highest cost)
- Interruptible service: Non-guaranteed but lower cost
- Capacity auctions: Secondary market for unused capacity
- Expansion: Regulatory approval and infrastructure investment
- Congestion: Peak periods, limited available capacity

## Transmission Infrastructure (APQC 11.0-11.4)

### Pipeline Systems

**High-Pressure Transmission Pipelines**
- Diameter: 8-48 inches typical
- Operating pressure: 300-1,500 psi (pounds per square inch)
- Construction: Steel, coated and buried for corrosion protection
- Depth: 4-6 feet typical (varies by terrain)
- Right-of-way: Easements across private property
- Maintenance: In-line inspection (ILI), cathodic protection, coating repair

**Compressor Stations (APQC 11.1)**
- Function: Overcome pressure drops, maintain flow
- Spacing: Every 40-100 miles typical
- Horsepower: 1,000-20,000+ HP per station
- Power: Electric (preferred) or gas turbine (portable)
- Automation: Remote control, monitoring systems
- Environmental: Methane emissions from venting (air emissions)

**Regulation Stations**
- Reduce pressure for distribution systems
- Function: Flow metering and measurement
- Odorization: Adding mercaptan (odorant) for leak detection (natural gas is odorless)

**Storage Facilities (APQC 13.2)**

**Underground Storage**
- Depleted reservoirs: Former oil and gas fields
  - Most common type (70% of storage)
  - Existing infrastructure and geology understood
- Aquifers: Saltwater-bearing formations
  - Lower cost but less efficient (cycling losses)
  - Limited by geological suitability
- Salt caverns: Solution-mined cavities
  - High deliverability, good for peaking
  - More expensive to develop
- Functionality: Base load storage (summer injection, winter withdrawal)
- Capacity: Peak withdrawal rate: 10-15 Tcf/day
- Cycling: Single annual cycle (summer → winter) typical

**LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas) Storage**
- Cryogenic liquefaction: -260°F (-160°C)
- Volume reduction: 600:1 (gas to liquid)
- Applications: Peak shaving, import/export, transportation
- Cost: Higher than underground storage
- Baseload supply: Regasification for continuous supply
- Emerging: Small-scale LNG for remote areas

**Capacity and Supply Balance**
- Underground storage: ~3 Tcf working capacity (U.S.)
- LNG terminals: 20+ receiving facilities (capacity: 10+ Bcf/d regasification)
- Seasonal pattern: Winter demand 30-40% higher than summer
- Supply reliability: Storage ensures year-round availability

## Distribution Infrastructure (APQC 11.2-11.4)

### Distribution Networks

**Main Distribution Lines**
- Diameter: 1-12 inches typical
- Operating pressure: 60-150 psi (higher pressure)
- Backbone of distribution system
- Connects transmission to branch lines and customer connections

**Branch Distribution Lines**
- Diameter: 0.75-3 inches
- Operating pressure: 20-60 psi (lower pressure)
- Serves multiple customer areas
- Local regulation and pressure management

**Service Lines**
- Diameter: 0.5-1.5 inches
- Operating pressure: 5-20 psi (low pressure)
- Individual customer connections
- Meter installation point
- Length: 100-500 feet typical

**District Regulator Stations**
- Reduce pressure from high-pressure to lower-pressure distribution
- Flow regulation: Maintain constant flow despite demand variations
- Temperature compensation: Cold weather operation challenges
- Safety: Pressure relief, shut-off capability

### Distribution Asset Management (APQC 11.3-11.4)

**Infrastructure Maintenance**
- Inspection: In-line inspection (ILI), hydrostatic testing, above-ground inspection
- Material: Steel, polyethylene, PVC (varying by age and design)
- Replacement: High-pressure lines: 50-70 year life; low-pressure: 40-60 years
- Reinforcement: Cathodic protection against corrosion
- Vegetation management: Pipe marking to prevent damage during construction

**Safety and Leak Detection (APQC 14.2-14.4)**
- Leak surveys: Annual pressure testing, odor detection, acoustic survey
- Leak repair: Pressure drop investigations, gas detector surveys
- Odorization: Mercaptan added to detect leaks by smell
- Emergency response: 24/7 leak response teams
- One-Call programs: Damage prevention system for excavation notification
- Distribution Integrity Management Plan (DIMP): Risk assessment, mitigation

**Methane Emissions (APQC 14.4)**
- Fugitive emissions: Leaks from joints, connections, equipment
- Venting: Necessary for equipment maintenance and operations
- Methane (CH4): 25-28x more potent than CO2 (100-year GWP)
- Baseline: Distribution system leakage rate: 1-3% of volumes
- Reduction targets: Utility programs, regulatory pressure
- Monitoring: Aerial surveys, drone technology, continuous sensors

## Customer Operations (APQC 12.0)

### Meter Reading and Billing (APQC 12.1)

**Metering Systems**
- Diaphragm meters: Standard residential and small commercial meters
  - Accuracy: ±3% (within acceptable range)
  - Size: 1-2 inch connections
  - Longevity: 20+ years
- Rotary/turbine meters: Larger commercial and industrial
  - Accuracy: ±1-2%
  - High-volume capability
- Orifice plate measurement: Large industrial customers
  - Indirect measurement via pressure differential
- Smart meters: Remote reading enabled, interval consumption data

**Billing Systems (APQC 12.1)**
- Meter reading: Monthly traditional, 15-minute intervals (smart meter)
- Usage calculation: Therms (100,000 BTU) as unit of sale
- Rate structures:
  - Base/fixed charges: Monthly service charge
  - Usage charges: Tiered or declining block rates
  - Demand charges: Peak daily usage charge (large commercial/industrial)
  - Seasonal rates: Winter vs. summer pricing differences
  - Adjustment clauses: Fuel cost pass-through, weather normalization

**Billing periods and collection**
- Monthly billing cycles
- Payment methods: Online, automatic bank draft, in-person
- Low-income assistance: Bill assistance programs, budget billing
- Aged receivables: Disconnection risk for non-payment (winter protection laws)

### Customer Service (APQC 12.2)

**Service Centers**
- Multi-channel: Phone, online portal, mobile app, walk-in centers
- Operating hours: 24/7 for emergency services
- Languages: Multilingual support based on service territory
- Accessibility: ADA compliance, alternative communication methods

**Outage Management (APQC 12.2, 12.3)**
- Reporting: IVR system, online portal, mobile app
- Response: Emergency (immediate), urgent (hours), scheduled (days)
- Restoration: System prioritizes by impact and safety
- Communication: Status updates to customers during extended outages

**Service Connections and Disconnections (APQC 12.3)**
- New service: Engineering review, main extension/service installation
- Upgrade: Meter upgrade, line reinforcement for increased demand
- Reconnection: Restoring service after disconnection
- Disconnection: Non-payment (with notice and opportunity to cure)
- Winter protection: Restrictions on winter disconnections (varies by state)

**Demand Response and Energy Management (APQC 12.4-12.5)**
- Budget billing: Equal monthly payments
- Energy efficiency: Audit services, insulation rebates, appliance efficiency
- Conservation programs: During peak demand periods
- Smart thermostat: Remote control, automated demand response

## Emerging Technologies and Trends

**Renewable Natural Gas (RNG)**
- Biogas from anaerobic digesters: Wastewater treatment, landfills, manure
- Landfill gas: Methane capture from decomposition
- Biosolids: Treated sewage sludge
- Carbon intensity: Lower than fossil natural gas
- RNG certificates: Environmental attributes trading
- Integration: Blended into existing distribution networks

**Power-to-Gas (P2G)**
- Hydrogen production: Electrolysis of water (low carbon if renewable electricity)
- Synthetic methane: Hydrogen + CO2 (sabatier reaction)
- Energy storage: Converting excess renewable electricity to gas
- Grid injection: Blending into existing natural gas networks
- Large-scale deployment: Pilot projects, cost reduction ongoing

**Hydrogen Blending**
- Hydrogen content: 5-20% blending (some pipelines and appliances)
- Low hydrogen: 0-5% (already in most systems due to blend points)
- Safety: Different burn characteristics, detection methods
- Material compatibility: Pipeline materials, customer equipment issues
- Pilot programs: Demonstrations in Europe, Canada, early U.S. tests

**Microgrid and District Energy**
- Community-scale heating: District heating from combined heat and power (CHP)
- Thermal storage: Hot water storage for time-shifting
- Waste heat recovery: From power generation, industrial processes
- Resilience: Backup systems, islanding capability
