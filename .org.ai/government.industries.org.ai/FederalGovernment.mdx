# Federal Government

## Overview

The Federal Government of the United States comprises three branches: Executive, Legislative, and Judicial. The Executive Branch, headed by the President, includes cabinet departments, independent agencies, and various federal offices. The Legislative Branch (Congress) consists of the Senate and House of Representatives. The Judicial Branch includes the Supreme Court and federal court system. This sector includes approximately 2.8 million civilian employees and operates with an annual budget exceeding $6 trillion.

## Structure & Organization

### Executive Branch

#### The Presidency
- **The President**: Chief executive officer of the United States
- **The Vice President**: Succeeds to presidency, breaks Senate ties
- **Executive Office of the President**: White House staff, policy coordination
  - White House Chief of Staff
  - National Security Advisor
  - Director of the Office of Management and Budget (OMB)
  - Press Secretary and Communications staff

#### Cabinet Departments (15 principal departments)
- **State Department**: Foreign affairs, diplomacy, international relations
- **Treasury Department**: Financial management, taxation, monetary policy
- **Defense Department**: Military operations, national defense
- **Interior Department**: Natural resources, public lands, Native American affairs
- **Agriculture Department**: Farm programs, food safety, rural development
- **Commerce Department**: Economic development, trade, census
- **Labor Department**: Employment, workplace safety, worker training
- **Health and Human Services**: Health programs, Medicare, Medicaid, welfare
- **Housing and Urban Development**: Housing, community development
- **Transportation Department**: Infrastructure, highways, aviation, transit
- **Energy Department**: Energy research, nuclear safety, electricity regulation
- **Education Department**: K-12 education, higher education, student aid
- **Veterans Affairs**: Services and benefits for military veterans
- **Homeland Security**: Border security, emergency management, cybersecurity
- **Justice Department**: Law enforcement, federal courts administration

#### Independent Agencies (selected examples)
- **Social Security Administration**: Social Security benefits and programs
- **Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)**: Environmental regulation and protection
- **National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)**: Space exploration and research
- **Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI)**: Federal law enforcement and intelligence
- **Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)**: Foreign intelligence and analysis
- **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)**: Financial market regulation
- **Federal Communications Commission (FCC)**: Telecommunications regulation
- **Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC)**: Nuclear facility oversight
- **General Services Administration (GSA)**: Federal facilities and procurement

### Legislative Branch

#### The Senate
- **100 Senators**: 2 from each state
- **Leadership**: President of the Senate (Vice President), Majority/Minority Leaders
- **Committees**: ~16 standing committees, numerous subcommittees
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Legislation, treaty ratification, presidential confirmations
  - Budget approval, appropriations
  - Impeachment trials for executive and judicial officials

#### The House of Representatives
- **435 Representatives**: Apportioned by state population
- **Leadership**: Speaker of the House, Majority/Minority Leaders
- **Committees**: ~22 standing committees, numerous subcommittees
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Legislation, revenue bills (origination requirement)
  - Impeachment proceedings
  - Presidential election (in event of Electoral College tie)

#### Congressional Support Agencies
- **Congressional Research Service (CRS)**: Policy research and analysis
- **Government Accountability Office (GAO)**: Audits and evaluations
- **Congressional Budget Office (CBO)**: Budget analysis and projections
- **Library of Congress**: Information and research services

### Judicial Branch

#### Supreme Court
- **1 Chief Justice and 8 Associate Justices**
- **Jurisdiction**: Final authority on constitutional interpretation
- **Types of cases**: Constitutional issues, federal law, disputes between states
- **Annual caseload**: ~7,000-9,000 filings, ~70-80 cases heard

#### Federal Appeals Courts
- **13 Circuit Courts of Appeals**
- **Jurisdiction**: Review federal district court decisions
- **Specialized courts**: Federal Circuit (patent cases), Court of International Trade
- **Caseload**: ~50,000+ cases annually

#### Federal District Courts
- **94 District Courts** across 50 states
- **Jurisdiction**: Original jurisdiction for federal cases (criminal, civil)
- **Caseload**: ~200,000+ cases annually
- **Judges**: ~650 active federal judges

## Federal Systems & Processes

### Legislative Process

#### Bill Introduction & Committee Review
1. Bill introduced in Senate or House
2. Assigned to relevant committee
3. Committee review, amendment, and markup
4. Committee vote and recommendation

#### Floor Consideration
1. Scheduled for full chamber debate
2. Floor amendments and voting
3. Passage or rejection by chamber

#### Conference Committee (if needed)
1. Reconciliation of differing Senate/House versions
2. Compromise language development
3. Final vote in both chambers

#### Presidential Action
1. Presidential signature (becomes law)
2. Presidential veto (returned to Congress, requires 2/3 override vote)
3. No action within 10 days (becomes law or pocket veto)

### Appropriations & Budget Process

#### Annual Cycle
1. **February**: President submits budget proposal
2. **Spring**: Congressional committee hearings and mark-up
3. **Summer**: Floor consideration and passage
4. **Fall**: Final appropriations bills to President
5. **October 1**: Fiscal year begins

#### Budget Components
- **Discretionary Spending**: Approved annually through appropriations (~32% of budget)
- **Mandatory Spending**: Determined by formula (Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid)
- **Interest on Debt**: Non-discretionary payments on federal borrowing

### Regulatory Process

#### Notice & Comment Rulemaking
1. **Proposed Rule**: Published in Federal Register
2. **Public Comment Period**: 30-60 days for stakeholder input
3. **Final Rule**: Agency analysis of comments, final rule
4. **Effective Date**: Typically 60+ days after final rule publication
5. **Judicial Review**: Available for challenging regulations

#### Environmental Review
- **National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA)**: Environmental assessments required
- **Environmental Impact Statements (EIS)**: For major federal actions
- **Public hearing and comment periods**: Stakeholder input
