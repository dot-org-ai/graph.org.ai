# Local Government

## Overview

Local governments represent the most direct connection between citizens and government, providing essential public services at the community level. The United States has approximately 89,000 local governments including counties, municipalities, townships, special districts, and school districts, with a combined workforce of ~13 million employees. Local governments manage budgets totaling over $2 trillion annually and are responsible for services including public safety, infrastructure, water/sanitation, zoning and land use, and local schools.

## NAICS Classification

**NAICS 923000: Local Government Administration**

Detailed subcategories:
- **923110**: Legislative offices (city councils, county commissions, town councils)
- **923120**: Judicial and administrative offices (local courts, town halls, county offices)
- **923130**: Executive offices (mayor's office, county executive)
- **923140**: Public finance activities (local taxation, treasury, auditing)
- **923150**: General government support (local administration, planning, zoning)
- **923190**: Other local government services

## Types of Local Governments

### Counties
- **Number**: 3,069 counties plus county equivalents in 50 states
- **Structure**: County commission/board of supervisors (typically 3-7 elected commissioners)
- **Authority**: Derived from state government ("creature of the state")
- **Geographic scope**: Geographically based, covering entire state area
- **Services**: Roads, courts, sheriff, assessor, recorder, planning

#### County Characteristics
- **Governance**: Commission/board-based (executive and legislative combined)
- **Leadership**: County Commission Chair or County Executive (where appointed)
- **Employees**: Average 100-1,000+ (varies by county size)
- **Budget**: Average $25-100+ million (varies significantly)

### Municipalities (Cities and Towns)
- **Number**: ~19,500 incorporated municipalities
- **Population range**: 10 to 8+ million (varies widely)
- **Charter types**: General law cities, home rule charter cities, special charter cities
- **Governance structures**: Mayor-council, city manager, commission, or hybrid

#### Mayor-Council Form
- **Weak Mayor**: Limited executive power, city council dominates
- **Strong Mayor**: Significant executive power, budget authority, appointment power
- **Responsibilities**: Executive function, legislative coordination, city representation

#### City Manager Form
- **Professional management**: Non-elected city manager hired by council
- **Mayor role**: Ceremonial, council liaison, representative function
- **City Manager**: Reports to city council, manages administration and staff
- **Advantages**: Professional management, reduced political interference

#### Municipal Services
- **Public Safety**: Police, fire, emergency services
- **Planning & Zoning**: Land use regulation, development review
- **Public Works**: Streets, sidewalks, storm water, sanitation
- **Recreation**: Parks, recreation programs, community centers
- **Building Services**: Code enforcement, permits, inspections
- **Finance**: Property tax assessment, license administration, utility billing

### Townships
- **Number**: ~16,600 townships (primarily in Midwest and Northeast)
- **Structure**: Town meetings (direct democracy) or elected town boards
- **Authority**: Limited authority over zoning, roads, planning
- **Population range**: Often 1,000-10,000 residents
- **Organization**: Treasurer, clerk, selectmen (in New England)

### Special Districts
- **Number**: ~50,000 special districts nationwide
- **Purpose**: Single-function government entities for specific services
- **Services**: Water supply, wastewater, stormwater, public transportation, fire protection

#### Special District Types
- **Water districts**: Supply, treatment, distribution, reclamation
- **Transit districts**: Bus systems, light rail, commuter rail
- **Fire districts**: Fire protection service in unincorporated areas
- **Park districts**: Parks, recreation facilities, programs
- **Hospital districts**: Hospital and healthcare facility operation
- **Cemetery districts**: Cemetery maintenance and operation
- **School districts**: Education service delivery (discussed separately)

### School Districts
- **Number**: ~13,500 public school districts
- **Structure**: Elected school board, superintendent (professional administrator)
- **Funding**: Property tax (~45%), state funds (~45%), federal (~10%)
- **Services**: K-12 education, special education, transportation, meals

#### School District Organization
- **School Board**: Elected 5-7 member governance body
- **Superintendent**: Chief executive officer, professional educator
- **District Staff**: Central office administration, curriculum, support services
- **Principals**: School-level building administrators
- **Teachers**: Instructional staff (~3.5 million in U.S.)

## Local Government Operations & Functions

### Local Workforce

#### Total Employment: ~13 million local government employees

Employment by sector:
- **K-12 Education**: ~3.7 million (schools, administration)
- **Local General Administration**: ~2.0 million
- **Public Safety**: ~2.0 million (police, fire, corrections)
- **Utilities**: ~1.5 million (water, sanitation, electric, gas)
- **Public Welfare/Social Services**: ~1.0 million
- **Transportation**: ~0.5 million
- **Other functions**: ~2.3 million

#### Compensation
- **Average salary**: $45,000-55,000 (varies by function and municipality)
- **Benefits**: Health insurance, defined benefit pensions (common), ICMA/IMRF programs
- **Union representation**: 35-40% of local employees unionized (varies by region)

### Local Government Finances

#### Annual Revenue (all local governments): ~$2+ trillion

Revenue sources:
- **Property taxes**: ~40% (primary local revenue source)
- **Sales taxes**: ~15-20% (where authorized by state)
- **Service charges/fees**: ~15-20% (utilities, permits, licenses)
- **State funds**: ~15-20% (state aid, education funding, grants)
- **Federal funds**: ~5-10% (grants, programs)
- **Other revenues**: ~5-10% (fines, forfeitures, investments)

#### Spending Categories

**General Fund Spending**:
- **Education**: 35-40% (school district portion)
- **Public Safety**: 15-20% (police, fire, courts)
- **Public Works**: 10-15% (roads, infrastructure)
- **Administration**: 8-12% (city hall, management)
- **Recreation**: 5-8% (parks, programs)
- **Other Services**: 15-20% (zoning, utilities, etc.)

**Capital Budgets**: Infrastructure investment (roads, utilities, buildings)

### Primary Local Government Functions

#### Public Safety
- **Police Services**: Law enforcement, community policing, traffic control
- **Fire Services**: Fire protection, emergency response, fire prevention
- **Emergency Services**: 911 dispatch, emergency management, disaster response

#### Land Use & Development
- **Planning**: Comprehensive plans, land use policy, growth management
- **Zoning**: Zoning ordinances, zoning permits, variance review
- **Code Enforcement**: Building code compliance, nuisance enforcement
- **Development Review**: Subdivision approval, environmental review

#### Public Infrastructure
- **Streets & Roads**: Maintenance, repair, snow removal, street lighting
- **Water Systems**: Water supply, treatment, distribution, emergency repairs
- **Sanitary Sewers**: Collection, treatment, discharge compliance
- **Stormwater**: Drainage, flood control, water quality management
- **Solid Waste**: Garbage collection, recycling, waste disposal

#### Recreation & Culture
- **Parks & Recreation**: Park maintenance, recreation programs, community events
- **Libraries**: Library systems, public collections, programming
- **Cultural Services**: Museums, cultural centers, historic preservation
- **Community Centers**: Facilities for classes, meetings, events

#### Administrative Functions
- **Finance**: Budget development, accounting, tax collection, treasury management
- **Human Resources**: Personnel, benefits administration, payroll, training
- **Procurement**: Purchasing, vendor management, contract administration
- **Information Technology**: Systems support, cybersecurity, digital services
- **Legal Services**: Legal counsel, contract review, litigation
- **Planning & Analysis**: Policy development, performance measurement

## Local Government Processes

### Budget Development & Adoption

#### Annual Cycle (varies by jurisdiction)
1. **January-February**: Manager prepares budget proposal
2. **February-March**: Budget reviewed by finance committee
3. **April-May**: Public hearings and council review
4. **May-June**: Amendments and final council debate
5. **June-July**: Final vote and adoption
6. **July-August**: Fiscal year begins, budget implementation

#### Budget Components
- **General Fund**: Tax and general revenues, day-to-day operations
- **Enterprise Funds**: Self-supporting utilities (water, sewer, electric)
- **Capital Budget**: Long-term infrastructure investment
- **Special Funds**: Restricted revenues (parks, libraries, etc.)

### Development Process & Permitting

#### Development Review Phases

1. **Pre-Application Meeting**
   - Applicant meets with staff
   - Preliminary review and guidance
   - Initial feedback on feasibility

2. **Application Submission**
   - Detailed plans and specifications
   - Environmental review (if required)
   - Professional fees and deposits
   - Completeness review

3. **Staff Review**
   - Engineering review
   - Environmental analysis
   - Traffic impact assessment
   - Utility availability verification

4. **Planning Commission Review**
   - Public hearing (typically required)
   - Review against comprehensive plan
   - Conditions and recommendations
   - Commission approval or denial

5. **Design Review** (where required)
   - Architectural compatibility
   - Streetscape and landscaping
   - Aesthetic compliance

6. **Final Approval**
   - City council approval (for major projects)
   - Conditional approval with requirements
   - Permits issued for construction
   - Final inspections

### Municipal Code & Ordinances

#### Primary Code Areas
- **Zoning Code**: Land use classification, permitted uses, development standards
- **Building Code**: Construction standards, safety requirements, inspections
- **Fire Code**: Fire safety, emergency egress, occupancy limits
- **Utilities Code**: Water, sewer, stormwater standards
- **Public Works Standards**: Street design, sidewalk requirements
- **Administrative Code**: Council procedures, procurement, personnel

#### Ordinance Process
1. Introduction by council member or manager
2. First reading (introduction)
3. Public notice and comment period
4. Second reading and discussion
5. Public hearing (if required)
6. Final vote
7. Implementation and enforcement
