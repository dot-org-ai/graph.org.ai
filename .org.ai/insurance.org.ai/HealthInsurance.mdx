# Health Insurance

Health insurance (NAICS 52412) finances healthcare services through risk pooling and managed care delivery systems. The industry encompasses commercial insurance, government programs (Medicare, Medicaid), and managed care organizations (HMOs, PPOs).

## Overview

The U.S. health insurance system is complex, multi-payer, and highly regulated:
- **Commercial insurance**: Employer-sponsored and individual coverage
- **Government programs**: Medicare (seniors/disabled), Medicaid (low-income), CHIP (children)
- **Managed care**: Networks, utilization management, care coordination
- **Fee-for-service vs. value-based**: Payment model evolution
- **Regulatory environment**: ACA, HIPAA, ERISA, state insurance departments

### Market Statistics
- Total U.S. healthcare spending: $4.5 trillion (17% of GDP)
- Health insurance premiums: $1.2 trillion
- People with health insurance: 300 million (92% of population)
- Employer-sponsored: 160 million (54%)
- Medicare: 65 million (18%)
- Medicaid/CHIP: 85 million (23%)
- Individual market (ACA exchanges): 21 million (7%)
- Uninsured: 28 million (8%)

## Types of Health Insurance Coverage

### Employer-Sponsored Insurance (ESI)

**Group health insurance provided as employee benefit**

#### Fully Insured Plans
- Insurance company assumes risk and administers benefits
- Employer pays premium to carrier
- State insurance regulation applies
- Guaranteed issue, no medical underwriting for groups
- Community rating or experience rating based on group size

#### Self-Insured Plans
- Employer assumes financial risk for claims
- Third-party administrator (TPA) handles claims processing
- **ERISA preemption**: Federal regulation, exempt from state mandates
- Stop-loss insurance protects against catastrophic claims
- Common for large employers (500+ employees)

#### Plan Design Elements
- **Premiums**: Monthly cost shared between employer and employee
- **Deductible**: Annual out-of-pocket before coverage begins ($1,500-5,000 individual)
- **Coinsurance**: Percentage of costs after deductible (typically 80/20 or 70/30)
- **Out-of-pocket maximum**: Annual cap on member cost-sharing ($8,000-18,000)
- **Copayments**: Fixed fees for specific services (e.g., $20 office visit, $10 generic drug)

**Market Statistics**
- Average annual premium: $8,400 individual, $23,900 family (2024)
- Employer contribution: 83% (individual), 73% (family)
- Employee contribution: $1,400 (individual), $6,400 (family)

### Individual & Family Plans

**Coverage purchased directly by consumers**

#### ACA Health Insurance Marketplace (Exchanges)
- **Guaranteed issue**: No medical underwriting, pre-existing conditions covered
- **Essential health benefits (EHB)**: Minimum coverage requirements
- **Metallic tiers**: Bronze (60% actuarial value), Silver (70%), Gold (80%), Platinum (90%)
- **Premium subsidies**: Advance Premium Tax Credits (APTC) for incomes 100-400% FPL
- **Cost-sharing reductions (CSR)**: Lower deductibles/copays for Silver plans (incomes 100-250% FPL)
- **Open enrollment**: Annual enrollment period (Nov 1 - Jan 15)
- **Special enrollment periods (SEP)**: Qualifying life events trigger mid-year enrollment

#### Off-Exchange Individual Plans
- Purchased directly from carriers or brokers
- Same ACA regulations (guaranteed issue, EHB, no lifetime limits)
- No subsidies available
- Often higher premiums than exchange plans

**Market Statistics**
- Average ACA exchange premium: $600/month (before subsidies)
- Average subsidy: $400/month
- Average net premium: $200/month
- Enrollment: 21 million (2024 open enrollment)

### Medicare

**Federal health insurance for seniors and disabled**

#### Eligibility
- **Age 65+**: Automatic enrollment if receiving Social Security
- **Disabled**: After 24 months of Social Security disability benefits
- **ESRD**: End-stage renal disease (kidney failure requiring dialysis)
- **ALS**: Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (Lou Gehrig's disease)

#### Medicare Parts

**Part A - Hospital Insurance**
- **Inpatient hospital care**: Deductible $1,632 per benefit period (2024)
- **Skilled nursing facility**: After 3-day hospital stay, days 21-100 require copay
- **Hospice care**: End-of-life care
- **Home health**: Part-time skilled nursing, therapy
- **Premium**: $0 for most (if 40+ quarters of Medicare taxes), $505/month if not

**Part B - Medical Insurance**
- **Physician services**: Office visits, outpatient care, preventive services
- **Outpatient hospital**: ER, observation, surgery
- **Durable medical equipment (DME)**: Wheelchairs, walkers, oxygen
- **Preventive care**: Annual wellness visit, screenings (no cost-sharing)
- **Premium**: $174.70/month (2024 standard, income-based surcharges apply)
- **Deductible**: $240/year
- **Coinsurance**: 20% of Medicare-approved amount (no out-of-pocket max)

**Part C - Medicare Advantage**
- **Private insurance alternative** to Original Medicare (Parts A & B)
- **Bundled coverage**: Medical, hospital, often includes Part D (drugs)
- **Managed care**: HMO, PPO, SNP (Special Needs Plans)
- **Additional benefits**: Vision, dental, hearing, fitness memberships, OTC allowances
- **Out-of-pocket maximum**: Annual cap on cost-sharing (required by CMS)
- **Premium**: Varies, many $0 premium plans available
- **Enrollment**: 32 million (50% of Medicare beneficiaries)

**Part D - Prescription Drug Coverage**
- **Stand-alone PDPs**: For Original Medicare beneficiaries
- **MA-PD**: Bundled with Medicare Advantage plans
- **Coverage stages** (2024):
  - **Deductible**: Up to $545
  - **Initial coverage**: 25% coinsurance until $5,030 total drug costs
  - **Coverage gap**: 25% coinsurance until $8,000 out-of-pocket
  - **Catastrophic coverage**: $0-$4 copays or 5% coinsurance
- **Formulary**: Tiered drug list, prior authorization, step therapy
- **Premium**: Average $55/month, varies by plan

#### Medigap (Medicare Supplement)

**Private insurance to fill Original Medicare gaps**
- Covers Part A and Part B deductibles, coinsurance, copays
- Standardized plans (A, B, C, D, F, G, K, L, M, N)
- **Plan G** most popular (covers everything except Part B deductible)
- **Plan F** no longer available to new enrollees (as of 2020)
- Guaranteed issue during 6-month enrollment window at age 65
- Medical underwriting required if enrolling later

**Market Statistics**
- Medicare enrollment: 65 million
- Medicare Advantage enrollment: 32 million (50% penetration)
- Part D enrollment: 51 million
- Medigap enrollment: 14 million
- Total Medicare spending: $900 billion (2024)

### Medicaid & CHIP

**Federal-state health coverage for low-income populations**

#### Medicaid Eligibility
- **Income**: Varies by state, expansion states cover up to 138% FPL
- **Categorical**: Pregnant women, children, parents, elderly, disabled
- **ACA expansion**: 40 states + DC expanded Medicaid to 138% FPL (as of 2024)
- **Non-expansion states**: Income limits as low as 17-50% FPL for parents

#### Covered Benefits
- **Mandatory benefits**: Inpatient/outpatient hospital, physician, lab/X-ray, EPSDT (children), nursing facility, home health
- **Optional benefits**: Prescription drugs, dental, vision, physical therapy
- **Managed care**: 72% of enrollees in Medicaid MCOs (HMOs, PPOs)

#### Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP)
- Children in families with incomes too high for Medicaid but cannot afford private insurance
- Income limits: 200-400% FPL (varies by state)
- Low or no premiums, minimal cost-sharing

**Market Statistics**
- Medicaid enrollment: 85 million (as of 2024)
- CHIP enrollment: 7 million
- Federal spending: $450 billion
- State spending: $200 billion
- Per-enrollee cost: $7,500 (varies widely by eligibility category)

## Managed Care Models

### Health Maintenance Organization (HMO)

**Coordinated care through primary care physician (PCP) gatekeeper**

#### Characteristics
- **PCP required**: All care coordinated through primary care physician
- **Referrals**: Specialist visits require PCP referral
- **Network restrictions**: No out-of-network coverage (except emergencies)
- **Lower premiums**: Tighter controls reduce costs
- **Preventive focus**: Emphasis on wellness and early intervention

#### HMO Models
- **Staff model**: HMO employs physicians directly (e.g., Kaiser Permanente)
- **Group model**: HMO contracts with physician groups
- **IPA model**: Independent Practice Association of contracted physicians
- **Network model**: Multiple physician groups contracted

### Preferred Provider Organization (PPO)

**Flexible network with in-network and out-of-network benefits**

#### Characteristics
- **No PCP required**: Direct access to specialists
- **No referrals**: Self-refer to any provider
- **In-network incentives**: Lower cost-sharing for network providers
- **Out-of-network coverage**: Higher deductibles and coinsurance, but available
- **Higher premiums**: Flexibility comes at higher cost

### Point of Service (POS)

**Hybrid model combining HMO and PPO features**
- PCP gatekeeper like HMO
- Out-of-network benefits like PPO (with referral)
- Lower cost than PPO, more flexibility than HMO

### Exclusive Provider Organization (EPO)

**Network-only coverage without PCP gatekeeper**
- No out-of-network benefits (except emergencies)
- No PCP or referrals required
- Lower premiums than PPO
- Common in ACA marketplace

### High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)

**High deductible paired with Health Savings Account (HSA)**

#### HDHP Requirements (2024)
- Minimum deductible: $1,600 individual, $3,200 family
- Maximum out-of-pocket: $8,050 individual, $16,100 family
- No coverage before deductible except preventive care

#### Health Savings Account (HSA)
- **Tax-advantaged savings**: Pre-tax contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals for qualified medical expenses
- **Contribution limits** (2024): $4,150 individual, $8,300 family
- **Catch-up**: Additional $1,000 for age 55+
- **Portability**: Account follows individual, employer or employee can fund
- **Triple tax advantage**: Deductible contributions, tax-free earnings, tax-free withdrawals

**Market Adoption**
- 30% of employer-sponsored plans are HDHPs
- Average deductible: $2,500 individual, $5,000 family

## Health Insurance Operations

### Underwriting

#### Group Underwriting
- **Small group** (2-50 employees): Community rating or adjusted community rating
- **Large group** (51+ employees): Experience rating based on claims history
- **Guaranteed issue**: ACA prohibits medical underwriting for groups
- **Rating factors**: Age, geography, tobacco use, family composition (individual market)

#### Risk Adjustment
- **ACA risk adjustment**: Transfers funds from low-risk to high-risk plans
- **Medicare Advantage**: Risk scores based on diagnoses (HCCs)
- **Medicaid managed care**: Acuity-based payments

### Medical Management

#### Utilization Management
- **Prior authorization**: Pre-approval required for certain services (imaging, surgeries, specialty drugs)
- **Concurrent review**: Hospital stay monitoring, discharge planning
- **Retrospective review**: Post-service claims review for medical necessity
- **Step therapy**: Require lower-cost treatments before approving expensive alternatives

#### Case Management
- **High-risk members**: Chronic conditions, complex care needs
- **Care coordination**: Integrate medical, behavioral, social services
- **Disease management**: Diabetes, asthma, CHF, COPD programs
- **Transitional care**: Post-hospitalization follow-up to prevent readmissions

#### Pharmacy Benefit Management (PBM)
- **Formulary management**: Tiered drug lists (generic, preferred brand, non-preferred, specialty)
- **Rebate negotiation**: Manufacturer rebates for formulary placement
- **Claims adjudication**: Real-time claims processing at pharmacy
- **Mail order**: 90-day supplies at lower cost
- **Specialty pharmacy**: High-cost biologics and specialty drugs

**Top PBMs**: CVS Caremark, Express Scripts (Cigna), OptumRx (UnitedHealth)

### Provider Networks

#### Network Development
- **Contracting**: Negotiate reimbursement rates with hospitals, physicians, ancillary providers
- **Credentialing**: Verify licenses, board certifications, malpractice coverage
- **Network adequacy**: Ensure sufficient access by specialty and geography
- **Centers of Excellence (COE)**: High-quality, cost-effective providers for complex procedures

#### Reimbursement Models
- **Fee-for-service (FFS)**: Pay per service rendered
- **Discounted FFS**: Percentage of charges or fee schedule
- **Per diem**: Daily rate for inpatient stays
- **DRG (Diagnosis-Related Group)**: Fixed payment per hospital admission
- **Capitation**: Fixed per-member-per-month (PMPM) payment
- **Bundled payments**: Single payment for episode of care (e.g., joint replacement)
- **Value-based contracts**: Pay-for-performance, shared savings, risk-sharing

### Claims Processing

**Covered in detail in** [ClaimsManagement.mdx](./ClaimsManagement.mdx)

#### Claims Adjudication
- **Auto-adjudication**: 90%+ of claims processed automatically
- **Electronic claims**: EDI 837 (institutional), 837P (professional)
- **Real-time adjudication**: Pharmacy claims, some medical claims
- **Coordination of benefits (COB)**: Primary, secondary payer determination

## Regulatory Environment

### Affordable Care Act (ACA)

**Comprehensive health reform law (2010)**

#### Market Reforms
- **Guaranteed issue**: No denial based on pre-existing conditions
- **Community rating**: Limited rating factors (age, geography, tobacco)
- **Essential health benefits**: 10 categories of minimum coverage
- **Preventive care**: No cost-sharing for preventive services
- **Lifetime/annual limits**: Prohibited
- **Dependent coverage**: Children covered to age 26

#### Individual Mandate (Repealed)
- Originally required coverage or pay penalty
- Penalty reduced to $0 (effective 2019)
- Some states enacted state-level mandates (CA, MA, NJ, VT, DC)

#### Medical Loss Ratio (MLR)
- **Large group**: 85% of premiums on medical care
- **Small group & individual**: 80% of premiums on medical care
- Rebates required if MLR not met

### HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)

**Privacy, security, and portability regulations (1996)**

#### Privacy Rule
- Protected Health Information (PHI) safeguards
- Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP)
- Authorization for uses/disclosures
- Individual rights (access, amendment, accounting)

#### Security Rule
- Administrative, physical, technical safeguards
- Risk assessments and management
- Encryption, access controls, audit logs

#### Breach Notification
- Notify individuals, HHS, media (if 500+ affected)
- Within 60 days of breach discovery

**Penalties**: $100-50,000 per violation, max $1.5M per year

### ERISA (Employee Retirement Income Security Act)

**Federal regulation of employer-sponsored benefits (1974)**

#### Fiduciary Requirements
- Act in best interest of participants
- Plan documents, summary plan description (SPD)
- Claims and appeals procedures

#### Preemption
- Self-insured plans exempt from state insurance laws
- Uniform federal regulation for large employers

### State Insurance Regulation

**State Departments of Insurance**
- Rate review and approval
- Market conduct examinations
- Solvency oversight
- Consumer protection
- Network adequacy standards
- Provider prompt payment laws

## Health Insurance Trends & Innovation

### Value-Based Care

**Shift from fee-for-service to value-based payments**

#### Accountable Care Organizations (ACOs)
- Provider groups share risk/savings for quality and cost
- Medicare Shared Savings Program (MSSP)
- Commercial ACO contracts

#### Bundled Payments
- Single payment for episode of care (90 days pre/post-procedure)
- Joint replacements, cardiac surgery, maternity care

#### Capitation & Risk-Sharing
- Global capitation: Full-risk contracts for all medical services
- Partial capitation: Primary care, specialists

### Telehealth & Virtual Care

**Remote healthcare delivery via technology**

#### Telemedicine
- Video visits with physicians, specialists
- Mental health counseling
- Chronic disease monitoring

#### Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM)
- Wearables, home monitoring devices
- Real-time data transmission to providers
- Chronic condition management (diabetes, hypertension, CHF)

**Market Growth**
- Pre-pandemic: 1-2% of visits via telehealth
- Pandemic peak: 40% of visits
- Post-pandemic: 15-20% of visits (stabilized)
- Reimbursement parity: Many states require equal payment to in-person

### Consumerism & Transparency

#### Price Transparency
- **Hospital price transparency**: Machine-readable files, consumer-friendly display (2021)
- **Insurer price transparency**: In-network rates, out-of-network allowed amounts (2022)
- **No Surprises Act**: Protection from surprise medical bills (2022)

#### Consumer Tools
- Cost estimators and provider directories
- Quality ratings (HEDIS, star ratings, patient satisfaction)
- Decision support tools for treatment options

### Health Equity & Social Determinants

**Addressing disparities and non-medical drivers of health**

#### Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)
- Housing, food security, transportation
- Education, employment, income
- Neighborhood safety, environmental quality

#### Health Equity Initiatives
- Culturally competent care
- Language services, health literacy programs
- Community health workers, navigators
- Medicaid coverage for SDOH services

## Key Health Plan Metrics

### Financial Metrics
- **Medical Loss Ratio (MLR)**: Medical costs / premium revenue
- **Administrative expense ratio**: Admin costs / premium revenue
- **Underwriting gain/loss**: Premium - medical costs - admin
- **Days claims payable (DCP)**: Medical payables / (medical costs / 365)

### Clinical Metrics (HEDIS)
- **Diabetes care**: HbA1c testing, eye exams, nephropathy screening
- **Hypertension control**: Blood pressure <140/90
- **Breast cancer screening**: Mammography rates
- **Colorectal cancer screening**: Colonoscopy, FIT, Cologuard
- **Childhood immunizations**: Combo 10 (vaccines by age 2)

### Star Ratings
- **Medicare Advantage**: 1-5 star quality ratings (bonus payments for 4+ stars)
- **ACA marketplace**: Quality rating system (QRS)
- **Medicaid**: State-specific quality metrics

### Member Experience
- **CAHPS (Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems)**: Standardized surveys
- **Net Promoter Score (NPS)**: Likelihood to recommend plan
- **Complaint ratios**: Grievances and appeals per 1,000 members
