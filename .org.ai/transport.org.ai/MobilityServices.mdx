# Mobility Services

Mobility Services represents the convergence of technology and transportation, creating seamless, multimodal travel experiences. From e-scooters to integrated journey planning, these services are reshaping how people move through cities and beyond.

## Overview

Modern mobility services, partially classified under **NAICS 4859 (Other Transit and Ground Passenger Transportation)**, encompass a diverse range of emerging and technology-enabled transportation solutions. This sector includes Mobility as a Service (MaaS) platforms, micromobility options, and integrated transportation services.

### Service Categories

**Mobility as a Service (MaaS):**
- Integrated journey planning across modes
- Unified payment and ticketing
- Subscription-based access
- Seamless multimodal trips
- Real-time information and booking

**Micromobility:**
- E-scooters (dockless and docked)
- Bike sharing (traditional and electric)
- E-bikes and cargo bikes
- Electric mopeds and motorcycles
- Last-mile solutions

**Integrated Platforms:**
- Transit apps and journey planners
- Parking and payment integration
- Demand-responsive transit
- Corporate mobility management
- Unified mobility accounts

## Mobility as a Service (MaaS)

### Concept and Framework

**Core Principles:**
- **Integration**: Multiple transport modes in one platform
- **Accessibility**: Easy access to diverse transportation options
- **Personalization**: Tailored recommendations and preferences
- **Simplicity**: One app, one account, one payment
- **Flexibility**: Choose optimal mode for each trip

**Service Levels (Sochor et al., 2018):**
- **Level 0**: No integration
- **Level 1**: Information integration (journey planning)
- **Level 2**: Booking and payment integration
- **Level 3**: Service offer integration (bundled packages)
- **Level 4**: Social integration (incentives, gamification)

### MaaS Platforms

**Whim (Finland - MaaS Global):**
- Founded: 2016
- First comprehensive MaaS platform
- Location: Helsinki, Antwerp, Vienna, others
- Subscription tiers:
  - Pay-as-you-go
  - Urban (transit + taxi credits)
  - Unlimited (all modes unlimited)
- Integration: Transit, taxi, bike share, car rental, scooters

**Features:**
- Single app for planning and booking
- Monthly subscription with mode bundles
- CO2 tracking and sustainability metrics
- Corporate mobility programs
- Data insights for city planning

**Transit (North America):**
- Founded: 2012 (as Transit App)
- Coverage: 200+ cities
- Free journey planning app
- Real-time transit information
- Integration: Transit, bike share, scooters, ride-hail
- Revenue: Partnerships and advertising
- Features: GO (combines transit + last-mile options)

**Moovit (Intel):**
- Founded: 2012 (acquired by Intel 2020)
- Coverage: 3,000+ cities in 100+ countries
- Crowdsourced transit data
- Journey planning and real-time info
- Integration: Public transit focus, expanding to other modes
- Corporate: Employee commute solutions

**Citymapper:**
- Founded: 2011
- Coverage: Major global cities
- Multimodal journey planning
- Real-time information
- Smart Ride: On-demand bus service (London pilot)
- Citymapper Pass: Subscription (London)

**Regional Platforms:**
- **Jelbi** (Berlin): BVG-operated MaaS platform
- **SMILE** (Vienna): Comprehensive urban mobility
- **UbiGo** (Sweden): Subscription-based mobility
- **Tuup** (Netherlands): Employer mobility platform

### MaaS Business Models

**Consumer Models:**
- **Pay-Per-Use**: Individual trip charges
- **Subscription**: Monthly packages with bundled services
- **Freemium**: Free app with premium features
- **Advertising**: Free to users, advertiser-supported

**B2B Models:**
- **Corporate Mobility**: Employee transportation benefits
- **White Label**: Platform for transit agencies/cities
- **Data Services**: Mobility insights and analytics
- **Partnership Fees**: Revenue from integrated providers

**Challenges:**
- **Provider Cooperation**: Getting competitors to collaborate
- **Data Sharing**: Privacy and proprietary concerns
- **Pricing Complexity**: Bundling diverse services
- **Regulatory**: Different rules for different modes
- **Profitability**: High customer acquisition costs

## Micromobility

### E-Scooter Sharing

**Major Operators:**

**Lime:**
- Founded: 2017
- Coverage: 200+ cities globally
- Vehicles: E-scooters, e-bikes, e-mopeds
- Model: Dockless, free-floating
- Funding: Uber investment, IPO expected
- Operational Model: Gen 4 scooters, longer lifespan

**Bird:**
- Founded: 2017
- Coverage: 300+ cities
- Focus: E-scooters
- Bankruptcy: 2023, restructured
- Features: Community pricing, helmet partnerships
- Vehicle Lifecycle: Sustainability focus, refurbishment

**Spin (Ford):**
- Founded: 2017 (acquired by Ford 2018, sold to T3 2022)
- Coverage: U.S. cities and campuses
- Model: Dockless scooters
- Safety: Helmet rewards, education

**Voi (Europe):**
- Founded: 2018 (Sweden)
- Coverage: 70+ European cities
- Focus: Sustainable operations
- Carbon neutral operations
- Parking technology (computer vision)

**Tier (Europe):**
- Founded: 2018 (Germany)
- Coverage: 80+ European cities
- Sustainability: Swappable batteries, solar charging
- Acquisition: Merged with Dott (2022)

**Operational Model:**
- Dockless parking (GPS-based)
- Per-minute pricing ($0.15-$0.40/min)
- Unlock fees ($1)
- Smartphone app for unlock/payment
- Battery swapping (gig economy workers or staff)
- Geofencing for parking zones and speed limits

**Challenges:**
- **Sidewalk clutter**: Improper parking
- **Safety**: Rider injuries, helmet usage
- **Vandalism**: Theft and damage
- **Regulatory**: Permitting, caps, fees
- **Lifespan**: Early scooters lasted months (now 1-2+ years)
- **Profitability**: Unit economics, high operational costs

### Bike Sharing

**Docked Systems:**

**Citi Bike (New York):**
- Launched: 2013
- Operator: Lyft
- Stations: 1,000+
- Bikes: 20,000+ (classic and electric)
- Rides: 30+ million annually
- Model: Station-based, membership and day passes

**Divvy (Chicago):**
- Launched: 2013
- Operator: Lyft
- Integration: Transit system coordination
- Expansion: South and west side neighborhoods

**Capital Bikeshare (Washington DC):**
- Launched: 2010
- Operator: Lyft
- Regional: DC, Maryland, Virginia
- Oldest large-scale U.S. system

**Dockless Bike Sharing:**

**Mobike (China - defunct internationally):**
- Founded: 2015
- Peak: 200+ cities, millions of bikes
- Acquisition: Meituan (2018)
- Exit: International markets, focus on China
- Technology: GPS tracking, IoT locks

**Ofo (China - defunct):**
- Founded: 2014
- Peak: Global expansion
- Collapse: 2018-2020 (over-expansion, unit economics)
- Lesson: Importance of sustainable business model

**Current Dockless Operators:**
- **Jump** (Uber, discontinued scooters/bikes 2020)
- **Lime Bikes**: Integrated with scooters
- **Spin**: E-bikes in some markets

**E-Bike Sharing:**
- Growing segment
- Higher revenue per ride
- Longer distances
- Appeal to broader demographics
- Pedal-assist technology

### Other Micromobility

  - Expansion: EV charging infrastructure

**Cargo Bikes:**

## Regulatory Landscape

### Municipal Regulations

**Permitting:**
- Fleet size caps
- Geographic service areas
- Right-of-way usage fees
- Performance requirements
- Data sharing mandates
- Equity requirements (underserved areas)

**Safety Requirements:**
- Age restrictions (typically 18+)
- Helmet requirements (varies by jurisdiction)
- Vehicle safety standards
- Speed limits (geo-fenced)
- Riding location rules (sidewalks vs. streets)
- Insurance requirements

**Parking Regulations:**
- Designated parking zones
- Prohibited parking areas
- Parking fees or fines
- Impound authority
- Clear path of travel (ADA compliance)
- Removal timelines for damaged vehicles

### Data Sharing

**Mobility Data Specification (MDS):**
- Open-source data standard (Open Mobility Foundation)
- Real-time vehicle location and status
- Trip data (anonymized)
- Privacy concerns and lawsuits
- Municipal planning and enforcement tool
- Industry resistance to granular tracking

**General Bikeshare Feed Specification (GBFS):**
- Open data standard for bike/scooter sharing
- Station and vehicle availability
- Real-time status updates
- Integration with journey planning apps
- Widely adopted standard

## Business Models and Economics

### Unit Economics

**Revenue per Ride:**
- E-scooters: $3-6 average ride
- Bike share: $2-5 average
- Pricing: Unlock fee + per-minute charge
- Subscriptions: Monthly unlimited plans
- Corporate programs: Subsidized access

**Cost per Ride:**
- Vehicle depreciation and financing
- Operations (charging, rebalancing, maintenance)
- Customer acquisition
- Payment processing
- Insurance and liability
- Regulatory compliance

**Key Metrics:**
- Rides per vehicle per day (target: 3-5+)
- Revenue per vehicle per day
- Vehicle lifetime (target: 18-36 months)
- Charge/rebalance cost
- Contribution margin per ride
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC)

### Funding and Investment

**Venture Capital:**
- 2017-2019: Peak investment in scooters
- $5B+ invested in micromobility startups
- Subsequent consolidation
- Focus on profitability over growth

**Public Funding:**
- Transit agency bike share systems
- Infrastructure grants (bike lanes, parking)
- Subsidies for underserved areas
- Pilot programs and demonstrations

## Demand-Responsive Transit

### Microtransit Services

**Concept:**
- Flexible routes based on demand
- Shared rides with dynamic routing
- Between fixed-route transit and ride-hail
- Smaller vehicles (vans, shuttles)
- App-based booking

**Operators:**

**Via:**
- Founded: 2012
- Model: B2B technology platform
- Clients: Cities, transit agencies, employers
- Services: On-demand transit, paratransit, school transport
- Coverage: 35+ countries

**TransLoc (Ford):**
- On-demand microtransit
- Transit agency partnerships
- Acquired by Ford 2018
- Technology platform for agencies

**Other Providers:**
- **Spare Labs** (Canada): Microtransit platform
- **Pantonium**: Dynamic transit technology
- **RideCo**: On-demand transit software

**Use Cases:**
- **First/Last Mile**: Connect to transit hubs
- **Suburban Coverage**: Low-density areas
- **Off-Peak Service**: Replace infrequent fixed routes
- **Paratransit**: ADA complementary service
- **Corporate Shuttles**: Employee transportation

### Challenges and Opportunities

**Challenges:**
- **Cost**: Often more expensive than fixed-route
- **Utilization**: Achieving sufficient ridership
- **Cannibalization**: Competing with existing transit
- **Reliability**: Variable travel times
- **Driver Availability**: Gig economy labor model

**Opportunities:**
- **Service Coverage**: Extend transit to underserved areas
- **Efficiency**: Right-size vehicles to demand
- **Accessibility**: Door-to-door or near-door service
- **Data**: Understand travel patterns
- **Integration**: Part of MaaS ecosystem

## Parking and Curb Management

### Smart Parking

**Parking Apps:**
- **ParkWhiz/SpotHero**: Reserve parking in advance
- **ParkMobile**: Mobile parking payment
- **Passport**: Meter payment and enforcement
- **PayByPhone**: Mobile parking payments

**Sensors and Guidance:**
- In-ground sensors detecting occupancy
- Camera-based detection
- Real-time availability displays
- Navigation to available spaces
- Dynamic pricing based on demand

### Curb Management

**Challenges:**
- Competing uses (parking, loading, transit, bike lanes, pick-up/drop-off)
- Ride-hail double parking
- Delivery vehicle congestion
- Limited curb space
- Enforcement complexity

**Solutions:**
- **Flex Zones**: Time-based usage (loading AM, parking PM)
- **Digital Permits**: App-based curb reservations
- **Pricing**: Curb space pricing by time/demand
- **Data**: Curb usage analytics
- **Color Coding**: Visual designation of curb zones

**Technology Platforms:**
- **Coord** (Sidewalk Labs, now Via): Curb management APIs
- **Passport**: Curb management and payments
- **Automotus**: Computer vision for curb enforcement
- **Remix** (Via): Planning tools for curb allocation

## Sustainability and Impact

### Environmental Benefits

**Emissions Reduction:**
- Replace short car trips
- Electric propulsion (zero tailpipe emissions)
- Reduced parking demand
- Transit connectivity (modal shift)
- Lifecycle emissions considerations

**Micromobility Lifecycle Analysis:**
- Manufacturing emissions
- Operational emissions (charging, rebalancing)
- End-of-life recycling
- Vehicle lifespan extension
- Renewable energy charging

**Carbon Footprint:**
- E-scooters: ~200g CO2e per trip (including operations)
