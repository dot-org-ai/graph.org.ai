# Public Transit

Public transit systems provide essential mass transportation services for urban and regional populations, offering sustainable alternatives to private vehicle use. These systems form the backbone of urban mobility in cities worldwide.

## Overview

Public transit encompasses various modes of transportation that serve the general public on fixed routes and schedules. As defined by **NAICS 485 (Transit and Ground Passenger Transportation)**, this sector includes:

- **Urban Transit Systems** (NAICS 4851): Subway, light rail, streetcar, and bus services
- **Interurban and Rural Bus** (NAICS 4852): Long-distance and rural bus services
- **Commuter Rail**: Regional rail services connecting suburbs to urban centers
- **Bus Rapid Transit (BRT)**: High-capacity bus systems with dedicated lanes
- **Paratransit**: Specialized services for people with disabilities

## System Types

### Heavy Rail (Subway/Metro)

Heavy rail systems are high-capacity, grade-separated rail transit systems serving major metropolitan areas:

**Characteristics:**
- Fully grade-separated (underground, elevated, or at-grade with barriers)
- High frequency and capacity (30,000+ passengers/hour/direction)
- Electric third-rail or overhead catenary power
- Automated or manual train operation
- Station spacing: 0.5-1.5 miles

**Major Systems:**
- New York City Subway (472 stations, largest in North America)
- London Underground (270 stations)
- Tokyo Metro (180+ stations)
- Paris Métro (300+ stations)
- Beijing Subway (400+ stations, longest network)

**Technology:**
- Communication-Based Train Control (CBTC)
- Automatic Train Operation (ATO)
- Platform screen doors
- Real-time passenger information systems

### Light Rail Transit (LRT)

Light rail systems offer medium-capacity rail service with lighter vehicles and infrastructure:

**Characteristics:**
- Mixed traffic or dedicated right-of-way
- Lower capacity than heavy rail (10,000-20,000 passengers/hour/direction)
- Overhead catenary power
- Single or multiple-unit vehicles
- Frequent stops (0.25-0.5 mile spacing)

**Notable Systems:**
- Portland MAX
- San Diego Trolley
- Denver RTD Light Rail
- Minneapolis Metro Transit
- Sacramento RT Light Rail

**Advantages:**
- Lower construction costs than heavy rail
- Flexibility in routing and integration
- Supports mixed development patterns
- Quieter operation than buses

### Bus Transit

Bus systems remain the most flexible and cost-effective public transit mode:

**Types:**
- **Local Bus**: Frequent stops, comprehensive coverage
- **Express Bus**: Limited stops, faster service
- **Bus Rapid Transit (BRT)**: Dedicated lanes, station-like stops
- **Articulated Buses**: High-capacity vehicles for busy routes
- **Electric Buses**: Zero-emission battery or trolleybus systems

**BRT Key Features:**
- Dedicated bus lanes or transitways
- Off-board fare collection
- Level boarding platforms
- Priority at traffic signals
- High-frequency service

**Leading BRT Systems:**
- TransMilenio (Bogotá, Colombia)
- Metrobús (Mexico City)
- RapidRide (Seattle)
- HealthLine (Cleveland)
- Orange Line (Los Angeles)

### Commuter Rail

Commuter rail provides regional connectivity between suburbs and urban centers:

**Characteristics:**
- Longer distances (20-100+ miles)
- Fewer stops than urban transit
- Peak-hour focused service
- Diesel or electric propulsion
- Shared or dedicated tracks

**Major North American Systems:**
- Metra (Chicago)
- Long Island Rail Road (New York)
- Caltrain (San Francisco Bay Area)
- MARC (Maryland)
- Metrolink (Los Angeles)

## Operations and Management

### Service Planning

**Network Design:**
- Route planning and optimization
- Service frequency determination
- Schedule development
- Coverage vs. ridership trade-offs
- Equity considerations

**Service Types:**
- Frequent network (10-15 minute headways)
- Peak service (rush hour focused)
- All-day service
- Night/owl service
- Weekend service patterns

### Fare Systems

**Fare Structures:**
- Flat fare vs. distance-based
- Time-based passes (daily, weekly, monthly)
- Reduced fares (seniors, students, disabled)
- Transfer policies
- Free transit zones

**Fare Collection Technology:**
- Contactless smart cards (e.g., London Oyster, Boston CharlieCard)
- Mobile ticketing apps
- Account-based ticketing
- Open-loop payment (credit/debit cards)
- Fare capping

### Fleet Management

**Vehicle Maintenance:**
- Preventive maintenance schedules
- Inspection protocols
- Overhaul cycles
- Parts inventory management
- Fleet replacement planning

**Technology Systems:**
- Automatic Vehicle Location (AVL)
- Computer-Aided Dispatch (CAD)
- Fleet Management Systems (FMS)
- Predictive maintenance analytics

### Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS)

- GPS-based vehicle tracking
- Arrival predictions
- Mobile apps and websites
- Digital signage at stops/stations
- SMS/text alerts

**Operations Systems:**
- Transit signal priority (TSP)

### Electrification

**Zero-Emission Transit:**
- Battery electric buses (BEB)
- Fuel cell electric buses (FCEB)
- Trolleybus systems

- Depot charging infrastructure

**Leading Deployments:**
- Shenzhen, China (100% electric bus fleet, 16,000+ buses)

## Funding and Finance

### Revenue Sources

**Farebox Revenue:**
- Typically 20-50% of operating costs
- Varies significantly by system and region
- Impacts service decisions and accessibility

**Public Funding:**
- Federal Transit Administration (FTA) grants
- State transportation funding
- Local sales or property taxes
- Dedicated transit taxes

**Funding Programs:**
- **FTA Section 5307**: Urbanized Area Formula Grants
- **FTA Section 5309**: Capital Investment Grants (New Starts)
- **FTA Section 5311**: Rural Area Formula Grants
- **FTA Section 5337**: State of Good Repair Grants

### Capital Projects

**Infrastructure Investment:**
- New rail line construction ($100M-$1B+ per mile)
- Station modernization
- Vehicle procurement
- Maintenance facility upgrades
- Technology systems
