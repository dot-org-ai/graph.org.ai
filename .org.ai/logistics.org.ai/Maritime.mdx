# Maritime Shipping and Ports

Ocean freight transportation services including containerized cargo, bulk carriers, tankers, and port terminal operations under NAICS 483, serving as the foundation of international trade and global supply chains.

## Overview

Maritime shipping moves over 90% of global trade by volume and 80% by value, transporting approximately 11 billion tons of cargo annually. The shipping industry connects producers and consumers across continents, enabling global commerce at scale and efficiency unmatched by any other transportation mode.

The maritime sector encompasses diverse vessel types, from ultra-large container ships carrying 24,000 TEUs to specialized chemical tankers, along with the port infrastructure and intermodal systems that facilitate cargo transfer between ocean and land transportation.

## Container Shipping

### Container Vessel Types

**Ultra Large Container Vessels (ULCV):**
- 18,000-24,000 TEU capacity
- 400m length, 60m beam (width)
- Economies of scale on major trade lanes
- Limited to deep-water ports with post-Panamax infrastructure
- Examples: MSC Irina (24,346 TEU), Ever Alot (24,004 TEU)

**New Panamax:**
- 13,000-14,500 TEU capacity
- Maximum size for expanded Panama Canal
- 366m length, 49m beam, 15.2m draft
- Flexibility across trade lanes

**Panamax:**
- 4,500-5,000 TEU capacity
- Original Panama Canal size limits
- 294m length, 32.3m beam
- Regional and feeder services

**Feeder Vessels:**
- 1,000-3,000 TEU capacity
- Hub-and-spoke distribution
- Port-to-port regional services
- Access to smaller ports with draft restrictions

### Container Types and Specifications

**Standard Containers:**
- **20-foot (TEU - Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit)**: 20' × 8' × 8'6", 33 cubic meters
- **40-foot (FEU - Forty-foot Equivalent Unit)**: 40' × 8' × 8'6", 67 cubic meters
- **40-foot High Cube**: 40' × 8' × 9'6", 76 cubic meters
- **45-foot High Cube**: 45' × 8' × 9'6", 86 cubic meters

**Specialized Containers:**
- **Refrigerated (Reefer)**: Temperature-controlled for perishables and pharma
- **Open Top**: For oversized cargo loaded from above
- **Flat Rack**: Collapsible sides for wide or heavy cargo
- **Tank Containers**: For liquid bulk (chemicals, food-grade liquids)
- **Garmentainers**: Hanging rails for clothing on hangers

**Container Ownership:**
- Carrier-Owned Containers (COC)
- Shipper-Owned Containers (SOC)
- Container leasing companies (Triton, Textainer, CAI)

### Container Shipping Operations

**Liner Services:**
- Fixed schedules and published timetables
- Regular port calls on defined rotations
- Service strings: weekly departures on major lanes
- Port rotation example: Shanghai → Ningbo → Hong Kong → Singapore → Suez → Rotterdam → Hamburg → Felixstowe → return

**Voyage Planning:**
- Route optimization for fuel efficiency
- Weather routing to avoid storms
- Slow steaming (16-18 knots vs. 22-24 knots) for fuel savings
- Port call optimization and omissions during slack demand

**Vessel Utilization:**
- Load factor (% of capacity utilized)
- Head-haul vs. backhaul imbalances
- Equipment repositioning for empty containers
- Slots sales to cargo owners and freight forwarders

### Container Freight Pricing

**Freight Rate Components:**
- **Base Ocean Freight**: Per container (20' or 40')
- **Bunker Adjustment Factor (BAF)**: Fuel surcharge
- **Currency Adjustment Factor (CAF)**: Exchange rate fluctuation
- **Peak Season Surcharge (PSS)**: Capacity premium during high demand
- **General Rate Increase (GRI)**: Carrier-initiated rate hikes
- **Port Congestion Surcharge**: Delays and extended port stays
- **War Risk Surcharge**: Insurance for conflict zones

**Contract vs. Spot Rates:**
- Annual service contracts negotiated with BCOs (beneficial cargo owners)
- Spot rates for immediate bookings, market-driven
- 2021-2022: spot rates exceeded $20,000 per FEU (Shanghai to LA)
- 2023-2024: normalization to $1,500-$3,000 per FEU
- Trans-Pacific, Trans-Atlantic, Asia-Europe rate variations

**Incoterms:**
- **FOB (Free on Board)**: Buyer pays ocean freight
- **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)**: Seller pays ocean freight and insurance
- **EXW (Ex Works)**: Buyer pays all transportation
- **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)**: Seller pays all costs to final destination

## Bulk and Breakbulk Shipping

### Dry Bulk Carriers

**Vessel Categories:**
- **Capesize**: 150,000-400,000 DWT, too large for Panama/Suez Canals
- **Panamax**: 60,000-80,000 DWT, maximum for Panama Canal
- **Handymax**: 40,000-60,000 DWT, flexible size for various ports
- **Handysize**: 15,000-40,000 DWT, access to smaller ports

**Dry Bulk Commodities:**
- **Major Bulk (80% of volume)**:
  - Iron ore: Australia, Brazil to China, Japan, Korea
  - Coal: thermal and metallurgical, Australia, Indonesia, Russia
  - Grain: wheat, corn, soybeans from US, Brazil, Argentina
- **Minor Bulk**:
  - Fertilizers, cement, sugar, salt, bauxite, alumina

**Baltic Dry Index (BDI):**
- Freight rate index for dry bulk shipping
- Weighted average of Capesize, Panamax, Supramax routes
- Leading economic indicator for global trade
- Highly volatile based on supply-demand dynamics

### Tankers

**Oil Tankers:**
- **VLCC (Very Large Crude Carrier)**: 200,000-320,000 DWT, 2 million barrels
- **Suezmax**: 120,000-200,000 DWT, Suez Canal maximum
- **Aframax**: 80,000-120,000 DWT, regional crude distribution
- **Panamax**: 50,000-80,000 DWT
- **Product Tankers**: Refined petroleum products (gasoline, diesel, jet fuel)

**Chemical Tankers:**
- Specialized coatings and tank configurations
- Segregated tanks for multiple chemical cargoes
- IMO Type 1 (high hazard), Type 2 (medium), Type 3 (low)
- Stainless steel tanks for corrosive chemicals

**LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas) Carriers:**
- Cryogenic tanks at -162°C
- Q-Flex (210,000-217,000 m³) and Q-Max (266,000 m³) vessels
- Membrane and moss tank designs
- Growing fleet for US LNG exports

**LPG (Liquefied Petroleum Gas) Carriers:**
- Propane and butane transportation
- VLGC (Very Large Gas Carrier): 80,000 m³
- Pressurized and refrigerated storage

### Breakbulk and Project Cargo

**Non-Containerized General Cargo:**
- Machinery, steel products, forest products
- Oversized and heavy-lift cargo
- Roll-on/Roll-off (Ro-Ro) vessels for vehicles
- Heavy-lift vessels with cranes up to 3,000 tons capacity

**Project Cargo:**
- Power plant components (turbines, generators, transformers)
- Oil and gas modules and FPSOs
- Wind turbine components
- Mining and construction equipment
- Specialized vessels: semi-submersible ships, dock ships

## Port and Terminal Operations

### Container Terminals

**Terminal Infrastructure:**
- Berth length: 300-400m per berth for ULCV vessels
- Water depth: 16-18m for deep-draft vessels
- Quay cranes (STS - Ship-to-Shore): 22-25 container rows wide
- Container yard: straddle carriers, rubber-tired gantry (RTG), rail-mounted gantry (RMG)
- Gate operations: truck and rail interchange

**Terminal Operators:**
- **Global Terminal Operators**:
  - APM Terminals (Maersk)
  - PSA International (Singapore)
  - DP World (Dubai)
  - China COSCO Shipping Ports
  - Hutchison Ports
  - Terminal Investment Limited (TiL - MSC)
- **Port Authorities**: Public ownership and operation (e.g., Port of Los Angeles, Rotterdam)

**Terminal Performance Metrics:**
- **Berth Productivity**: Container moves per hour (CMPH), target 120-150 for modern terminals
- **Crane Productivity**: Gross moves per hour (GMPH), 25-35 moves/hour
- **Vessel Turnaround Time**: Hours from arrival to departure, target 24-48 hours
- **Dwell Time**: Days containers remain in terminal, target <5 days
- **Truck Turn Time**: Gate in to gate out, target <60 minutes

**Automation:**
- Automated Stacking Cranes (ASC)
- Automated Guided Vehicles (AGV) for container transport
- Automated Gate Systems with OCR and RFID
- Remote-controlled STS cranes
- Examples: Rotterdam Maasvlakte II, Los Angeles TraPac, Singapore Tuas Port

### Inland Ports and Intermodal

**Intermodal Transportation:**
- On-dock rail for direct vessel-to-train transfer
- Near-dock rail within 5-10 miles of port
- Inland container depots (ICD) and dry ports
- Truck drayage between port and rail/warehouse

**US Intermodal Corridors:**
- **Alameda Corridor**: LA/Long Beach to downtown LA rail yards
- **Southern California to Midwest**: Chicago, Memphis, Dallas rail hubs
- **East Coast**: Savannah, Norfolk, Charleston to Southeast and Midwest
- **Panama Canal All-Water Route**: Asia to East Coast via expanded canal

**Rail Intermodal:**
- Double-stack container trains (2 containers high)
- Unit trains with 100+ containers
- Dedicated intermodal terminals and lift equipment
- Rail ramp operations: lift-on/lift-off (LOLO)

## Maritime Regulations and Compliance

### International Maritime Organization (IMO)

**SOLAS (Safety of Life at Sea):**
- Construction standards for vessels
- Life-saving equipment requirements
- Fire protection and detection systems
- Navigation safety and communication
- Container weight verification (VGM - Verified Gross Mass)

**MARPOL (Marine Pollution Prevention):**
- Annex I: Oil pollution prevention
- Annex II: Noxious liquid substances in bulk
- Annex III: Harmful substances in packaged form
- Annex IV: Sewage pollution prevention
- Annex V: Garbage pollution prevention
- Annex VI: Air pollution and emissions

**IMO 2020 Sulfur Cap:**
- Reduction of sulfur in marine fuel from 3.5% to 0.5%
- Low-sulfur fuel oil (LSFO) or marine gas oil (MGO)
- Scrubber technology to clean exhaust emissions
- Significant fuel cost increase (30-50%)

### Port State Control and Flag State

**Flag State Responsibilities:**
- Ship registration and certification
- Safety and environmental compliance oversight
- Crewing standards and manning requirements
- Open registries (flags of convenience): Panama, Liberia, Marshall Islands

**Port State Control (PSC):**
- Inspection of foreign vessels in port
- Detention for serious deficiencies
- Regional agreements: Paris MOU (Europe), Tokyo MOU (Asia-Pacific), USCG (US)

### Customs and Border Protection

**Import/Export Documentation:**
- **Bill of Lading (B/L)**: Contract of carriage and title document
- **Sea Waybill**: Non-negotiable transport document
- **Commercial Invoice**: Value and description of goods
- **Packing List**: Detailed cargo manifest
- **Certificate of Origin**: Country of manufacture
- **Importer Security Filing (ISF/10+2)**: Advance cargo data (US)
- **Automated Manifest System (AMS)**: Pre-arrival cargo declaration

**Customs Clearance:**
- Entry summary and duty payment
- Cargo release and delivery orders
- Inspection and examination procedures
- Continuous bond for regular importers

### Maritime Security

**ISPS Code (International Ship and Port Facility Security):**
- Post-9/11 maritime security framework
- Ship Security Plans and Port Facility Security Plans
- Security levels 1-3 based on threat assessment
- Continuous Synopsis Record (CSR) for vessel history

**Container Security:**
- **CSI (Container Security Initiative)**: Pre-screening at foreign ports
- **C-TPAT (Customs-Trade Partnership Against Terrorism)**: Supply chain security
- **ACI (Advance Commercial Information)**: 24-hour rule for cargo manifest
- **SAFE Framework**: WCO (World Customs Organization) standards
- High-security seals and tamper-evident devices

## Industry Trends

### Decarbonization and Sustainability

**IMO 2030 and 2050 Targets:**
- 40% reduction in carbon intensity by 2030 (vs. 2008 baseline)
- 70% reduction in carbon intensity by 2050
- 50% reduction in total GHG emissions by 2050
- Net-zero by 2050 under discussion

**Alternative Fuels:**
- **LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas)**: 20% CO2 reduction, methane slip concerns
- **Methanol**: Carbon-neutral with green methanol, Maersk ordering 12+ methanol vessels
- **Ammonia**: Zero-carbon fuel, toxic handling challenges, 2025+ adoption
- **Hydrogen**: Long-term potential, storage and production infrastructure needed
- **Biofuels**: Drop-in replacement, limited scalability and cost

**Operational Efficiency:**
- Slow steaming for fuel reduction (12-15 knots vs. 18-22 knots)
- Hull coatings and propeller optimization
- Air lubrication systems reducing friction
- Wind-assisted propulsion: rotor sails, kites

### Supply Chain Disruptions

**COVID-19 Pandemic Impacts:**
- Port congestion: 100+ container ships anchored off LA/Long Beach (2021-2022)
- Equipment shortages: container scarcity in Asia
- Blank sailings and schedule unreliability
- Freight rate spikes: $20,000+ per FEU Trans-Pacific
- Labor shortages at ports and warehouses

**Suez Canal Blockage (Ever Given, March 2021):**
- 6-day closure disrupted $9 billion/day in trade
- 400+ vessels delayed
- Highlighted single-point-of-failure risk
- Alternative routing around Cape of Good Hope adds 10-14 days

**Resiliency Strategies:**
- Diversification of suppliers and routes
- Safety stock and inventory buffers
- Nearshoring and reshoring manufacturing
- Digitalization for visibility and predictability

### Digitalization and Technology

**Smart Shipping:**
- IoT sensors for cargo monitoring (temperature, humidity, shock)
- Predictive maintenance for vessel systems
- Autonomous vessels: Yara Birkeland, Mayflower Autonomous Ship
- AI-powered route optimization and fuel efficiency

**Blockchain and Digital Platforms:**
- TradeLens (Maersk/IBM): blockchain for supply chain transparency
- GSBN (Global Shipping Business Network): digital shipping ecosystem
- E-Bill of Lading: paperless documentation (Bolero, essDOCS, CargoX)
- Digital freight platforms: Freightos, Flexport, Shippo

**Port Digitalization:**
- Port Community Systems for data sharing
- Automated gate systems and truck appointment scheduling
- Real-time berth and crane allocation
- AI-powered predictive analytics for throughput optimization

### Consolidation and Mega-Vessels

**Shipping Line Consolidation:**
- MSC acquisition of Bollore Africa Logistics
- CMA CGM acquisition of Ceva Logistics and Gefco
- Vertical integration: shipping lines acquiring logistics and terminals
- Market concentration: Top 10 carriers control 85% of global capacity

**Vessel Upsizing:**
- ULCV deployment on Asia-Europe and Trans-Pacific
- Port infrastructure investments for deeper berths
- Economies of scale vs. port rotation flexibility
- Slot cost reduction but higher capital investment
