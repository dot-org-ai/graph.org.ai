# Trucking and Motor Freight

Road-based freight transportation services including full truckload (TL), less-than-truckload (LTL), and specialized hauling operations under NAICS 484.

## Overview

The trucking industry is the backbone of freight transportation in North America, moving approximately 72% of all freight tonnage. With over 3.5 million truck drivers and 500,000+ motor carriers, trucking represents a $875 billion industry that connects manufacturers, distributors, retailers, and consumers.

### Industry Segments

The trucking industry divides into several key segments:

- **Truckload (TL)**: Full-trailer capacity shipments, typically 15+ pallets or 20,000+ lbs
- **Less-Than-Truckload (LTL)**: Consolidated shipments from multiple shippers sharing trailer space
- **Specialized Freight**: Dedicated equipment for refrigerated, flatbed, tanker, and oversized loads
- **Parcel and Package**: Small shipments under 150 lbs delivered via ground transportation
- **Drayage**: Short-haul container movements between ports, rail terminals, and warehouses

## Trucking Operations

### Truckload (TL) Operations

Truckload carriers provide dedicated trailer capacity for single-shipper freight:

**Operational Characteristics:**
- Point-to-point service without intermediate stops
- Typical load size: 24-26 pallets or 40,000+ lbs
- Average length of haul: 500-800 miles
- Driver per-mile compensation model
- Asset-based carriers own/lease tractors and trailers
- Owner-operators lease to carriers

**Business Models:**
- **Dry Van**: General merchandise in 53' enclosed trailers
- **Refrigerated (Reefer)**: Temperature-controlled transportation for food and pharmaceuticals
- **Flatbed**: Open trailers for construction materials, steel, and machinery
- **Specialized**: Oversized, heavy-haul, and unique equipment requirements

**Key Metrics:**
- Revenue per loaded mile
- Empty mile percentage (deadhead)
- Tractor utilization (%)
- On-time delivery rate
- Freight damage claims ratio

### Less-Than-Truckload (LTL) Operations

LTL carriers consolidate freight from multiple shippers into full trailer loads:

**Network Structure:**
- **Pickup and Delivery (P&D)**: Local terminals serve metro areas
- **Linehaul**: Long-haul between terminals, typically at night
- **Break Bulk Terminals**: Regional hubs for freight sorting and consolidation
- **Cross-Dock Operations**: Direct transfer between inbound and outbound trailers

**Service Levels:**
- Standard LTL: 2-5 day transit for regional shipments
- Expedited LTL: Guaranteed delivery within 1-3 days
- Volume LTL: Partial truckload (6-12 pallets)
- LTL with liftgate, inside delivery, or appointment scheduling

**Freight Classification:**
- NMFC (National Motor Freight Classification) codes
- Density-based pricing (lbs per cubic foot)
- Freight class ranges from 50 (densest) to 500 (least dense)
- Accessorial charges for special handling

**Key Metrics:**
- Shipments per day
- Tonnage per terminal
- Dock productivity (shipments per dock hour)
- Linehaul load factor (% of trailer capacity)
- On-time delivery percentage
- OS&D (overage, shortage, and damage) rates

### Specialized Freight Operations

**Refrigerated Transportation:**
- Temperature ranges: frozen (-20°F to 0°F), chilled (32°F-40°F), fresh (50°F-65°F)
- Multi-temp trailers with compartments
- Continuous temperature monitoring and recording
- FSMA (Food Safety Modernization Act) compliance
- Sanitary transportation requirements

**Heavy-Haul and Oversized Loads:**
- Oversize/overweight permits by state
- Pilot car requirements
- Route surveys for clearance verification
- Specialized trailers: lowboys, RGN (removable gooseneck), multi-axle configurations
- Modular and project cargo transportation

**Bulk Transportation:**
- Liquid bulk: food grade (milk, juice, oils), chemicals, petroleum products
- Dry bulk: cement, aggregates, grain, plastic pellets
- Pneumatic offloading systems
- Tank cleaning and compartmentalization
- Hazmat endorsements and DOT compliance

## Regulations and Compliance

### Hours of Service (HOS)

Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) regulates driver working hours:

**Current HOS Rules:**
- **11-Hour Driving Limit**: After 10 consecutive hours off duty
- **14-Hour On-Duty Limit**: Cannot drive after 14th hour after coming on duty
- **30-Minute Break**: Required after 8 cumulative hours of driving
- **60/70 Hour Limit**: Cannot drive after 60/70 hours on duty in 7/8 consecutive days
- **34-Hour Restart**: Reset 60/70 hour clock with 34 consecutive hours off duty

**Electronic Logging Devices (ELD):**
- Automatic recording of driving time
- Synchronization with vehicle engine
- Required for most commercial vehicles over 10,000 lbs GVWR
- Reduces logbook violations and improves compliance

### Safety and Compliance

**DOT Number and Operating Authority:**
- USDOT Number for interstate commerce
- MC (Motor Carrier) Number for for-hire authority
- Unified Registration System (URS)
- BOC-3 process agent designation

**Safety Ratings:**
- FMCSA's Safety Measurement System (SMS)
- BASIC categories: Unsafe Driving, Hours of Service, Driver Fitness, Controlled Substances/Alcohol, Vehicle Maintenance, Hazardous Materials, Crash Indicator
- Compliance, Safety, Accountability (CSA) scores
- Safety rating: Satisfactory, Conditional, Unsatisfactory

**Insurance Requirements:**
- $750,000 minimum for general freight
- $1 million for hazmat
- $5 million for certain hazmat commodities
- Cargo insurance for shipper protection
- Workers' compensation and liability coverage

### Environmental Regulations

**Emissions Standards:**
- EPA SmartWay Program for fuel efficiency
- California CARB (California Air Resources Board) compliance
- Clean Truck Programs at major ports
- Transition to EPA 2027 engine standards

**Sustainability Initiatives:**
- Alternative fuels: natural gas (CNG/LNG), renewable diesel
- Electric trucks: battery-electric and hydrogen fuel cell
- Aerodynamic improvements: trailer skirts, gap reducers, tail fairings
- Idle reduction technologies and APUs (auxiliary power units)

## Industry Trends

### Driver Shortage

The trucking industry faces an ongoing driver shortage:

- Estimated shortage of 80,000+ drivers in 2023
- Aging workforce: average driver age 46-55
- High turnover rates (85-95% at large truckload carriers)
- Competition from other industries
- Lifestyle challenges: extended time away from home

**Retention Strategies:**
- Increased driver pay and benefits
- Sign-on and referral bonuses
- Improved equipment and amenities
- Regional and local routes
- Predictable home time schedules
- Enhanced training programs

### Autonomous Trucks

Self-driving technology is advancing in trucking:

**Current Development:**
- SAE Level 4 autonomous trucks in testing
- Long-haul highway applications with remote monitoring
- Hub-to-hub models with human drivers for first/last mile
- TuSimple, Waymo Via, Aurora, Plus.ai, Kodiak Robotics
- Platooning: connected trucks in convoy formation

**Implementation Timeline:**
- 2024-2026: Limited autonomous operations in ideal conditions
- 2026-2030: Expanded deployment on major freight corridors
- 2030+: Broader commercial availability

### E-Commerce Impact

Online retail growth drives trucking demand:

- Faster delivery expectations (2-day, next-day, same-day)
- Smaller, more frequent shipments
- Final-mile congestion and parking challenges
- Urban delivery optimization
- Micro-fulfillment and dark stores

### Technology Adoption

Digital transformation across trucking:

**Freight Matching Platforms:**
- Digital freight matching (Uber Freight, Convoy, Transfix)
- Load boards (DAT, Truckstop.com)
- Dynamic pricing and spot market visibility

**Visibility and Tracking:**
- Real-time GPS tracking
- Predictive ETAs with machine learning
- Automated check-calls and exception management
- Shipper and carrier portals

**Automation:**
- Robotic process automation (RPA) for back-office
- Automated invoicing and payment processing
- AI-powered load optimization and route planning
